# Things to Handle Around Childbirth: A Timeline

The book is split by topic; pregnancy, birth, and infant care are spread across several sections. This checklist orders them by time, from planning a pregnancy through the child’s first birthday. Each line states only what to do; why and how strong the evidence is are in the entry cited in parentheses. Start at the heading for the stage you are in.

## I. When planning a pregnancy

1. Take 0.4 mg folic acid daily, starting three months before conception. See [Section 27, item 1](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (folic acid).
2. Call social insurance or medical insurance once and ask whether maternity-insurance contribution months are sufficient. See [Section 27, item 11](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (maternity insurance).
3. Agree with household members that no one smokes indoors from now on. See [Section 2, item 2](../../../book/en/02-Do-Not-Die-Slowly.md) (no smoking at home or in the car).
4. If someone at home depends on your income, buy term life insurance for the earner first. See [Section 5, item 40](../../../book/en/05-Do-Not-Waste-Money.md) (term life insurance).

## II. After pregnancy, before birth

1. Before week 13, register at a community health center for the Maternal and Child Health Handbook; free prenatal visits start there. See [Section 27, item 2](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (Maternal and Child Health Handbook).
2. At the first prenatal visit, test for HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B; you do not pay for the tests. See [Section 27, item 3](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B).
3. No smoking and no alcohol at all during pregnancy. See [Section 27, item 4](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (no cigarettes, no alcohol).
4. Tell your full medical history at prenatal visits. If you have high-risk factors for preeclampsia, let the doctor decide whether to start low-dose aspirin after week 12. See [Section 27, item 5](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (low-dose aspirin).
5. From week 24 onward, have gestational diabetes screening. See [Section 27, item 6](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (gestational diabetes screening).
6. After week 20 of pregnancy, do not take ibuprofen-type pain relievers on your own. See [Section 34, item 5](../../../book/en/34-Avoid-Serious-Harm-From-Home-Medicines.md) (ibuprofen-type pain relievers).
7. Learn the “go to the hospital now” list and make sure others at home know it too. See [Section 27, item 7](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (go to the hospital now).
8. At prenatal visits, ask whether the hospital offers painless labor. See [Section 27, item 9](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (painless labor).
9. Without a medical indication, do not ask for a cesarean section. See [Section 27, item 10](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (cesarean section).
10. If you will deliver outside your insurance locality, ask in advance whether you need cross-region maternity filing. See [Section 27, item 11](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (maternity insurance).
11. Maternity leave is at least 98 days; up to 15 days may be taken before birth. See [Section 18, item 2](../../../book/en/18-Is-Having-Kids-Worth-It.md) (maternity leave).
12. If the employer cuts pay or moves you because of pregnancy, keep written notices and chat records. See [Section 18, item 3](../../../book/en/18-Is-Having-Kids-Worth-It.md) (no pay cut for pregnancy, birth, or nursing).
13. Borrow a crib and stroller first; buy second-hand if you cannot borrow. Do not buy a used car seat or mattress. See [Section 20, item 11](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (borrow, second-hand, new).
14. Try diapers in a small pack for a week before stocking up. See [Section 20, item 10](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (diapers).
15. Choose the baby’s name early and confirm the household-registration system can print the characters. See [Section 27, item 12](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (birth medical certificate).

## III. During hospital birth

1. If membranes rupture, lie flat where you are, elevate the hips, call 120; do not walk around. See [Section 27, item 8](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (ruptured membranes).
2. Start breastfeeding within the first hour after birth. See [Section 20, item 4](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (breastfeeding only).
3. Give the first hepatitis B vaccine dose within 24 hours of birth. If the mother is hepatitis B surface antigen positive, give hepatitis B immune globulin at the same time. See [Section 20, item 2](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (first hepatitis B dose).
4. Give the vitamin K injection. If birthing at home or at an informal facility, ask explicitly. See [Section 20, item 7](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (vitamin K injection).
5. Do not refuse heel-prick screening or hearing screening. Before signing consent, see which items are optional paid add-ons. See [Section 27, item 13](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (heel-prick and hearing screening).

## IV. Before discharge

1. Complete the birth medical certificate; verify parents’ and child’s names and ID numbers character by character. See [Section 27, item 12](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (birth medical certificate).
2. Enroll the child in resident medical insurance online using the birth certificate; you do not need household registration first. If unsure, call 12393. See [Section 27, item 14](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (resident medical insurance).
3. Use a car seat on the way home: rear-facing under age one; do not hold the baby in your arms. See [Section 1, item 10](../../../book/en/01-Do-Not-Die-Early.md) (car seat).

## V. The first month after birth

1. Register the birth at the police station within one month. See [Section 27, item 15](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (birth registration).
2. After household registration, apply for the child-rearing subsidy where the child is registered: 3,600 yuan per child per year. See [Section 18, item 1](../../../book/en/18-Is-Having-Kids-Worth-It.md) (child-rearing subsidy).
3. Put the baby to sleep on the back, on a firm surface, same room but not same bed; no soft bedding in the crib. See [Section 20, item 1](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (back to sleep).
4. For formula, mix with water at 70 °C or above; discard leftovers. See [Section 20, item 5](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (mixing formula).
5. If a child under three months reaches 38 °C, go to the hospital. See [Section 20, item 8](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (fever at 38 °C).
6. When you are about to lose control, put the baby in the crib and step away for a few minutes. See [Section 20, item 9](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (never shake a baby).
7. The mother’s “go to the hospital now” list still applies for one year after birth. See [Section 27, item 7](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (go to the hospital now).

## VI. From one month through the first birthday

1. At 42 days postpartum, go for the check-up, which includes postpartum depression screening. See [Section 27, item 16](../../../book/en/27-Pregnancy-And-Birth.md) (42-day postpartum visit).
2. Follow the immunization schedule at the clinic; DTaP starts at two months. See [Section 20, item 3](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (national immunization program).
3. Breastfeed only for the first six months; from six months add complementary foods with iron-rich animal foods daily. See [Section 20, item 4](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (complementary feeding from six months).
4. Do not give honey before age one. See [Section 20, item 6](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (honey).
5. With severe eczema or egg allergy, see a doctor before deciding whether to introduce peanut early. See [Section 20, item 12](../../../book/en/20-Newborn.md) (do not avoid peanut entirely).
6. For colds in children under two, do not give combination cold medicines on your own. See [Section 34, item 4](../../../book/en/34-Avoid-Serious-Harm-From-Home-Medicines.md) (combination cold medicines).
7. Install window and balcony limiters; screens are not fall protection. See [Section 1, item 11](../../../book/en/01-Do-Not-Die-Early.md) (window and balcony limiters).
8. No phones or tablets for children 0–3. See [Section 30, item 5](../../../book/en/30-School-Age-Kids.md) (no screens).
9. After returning to work, if the child is under one year, you have one hour of nursing time per day. See [Section 18, item 3](../../../book/en/18-Is-Having-Kids-Worth-It.md) (nursing time).
10. Link your employee medical-savings account for family pooling so the child can use it for care and medicine. See [Section 5, item 13](../../../book/en/05-Do-Not-Waste-Money.md) (family pooling).
11. At annual individual income-tax settlement, claim the deduction for care of children under three. See [Section 5, item 2](../../../book/en/05-Do-Not-Waste-Money.md) (special additional deductions).
