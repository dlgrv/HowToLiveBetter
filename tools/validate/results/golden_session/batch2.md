### 28. Use condoms for the whole act, every time, and never share needles
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: one to two yuan apiece; the hard part is not money but using one every time, starting from the very beginning
- In plain terms: With a condom on for the whole act, every time, the odds of passing HIV between men and women drop by about 70% or more; "usually wearing one" is barely better than not wearing one at all. Syphilis and gonorrhea get blocked by the same layer.
- Benefit: systematic review and meta-analysis of 25 studies with 10676 HIV-serodiscordant heterosexual couples: consistent condom users had HIV transmission risk RR 0.29 versus never-users (95% CI 0.20 to 0.43), and RR 0.23 versus irregular users (95% CI 0.13 to 0.40). The authors' conclusion: although condoms are not perfect, "consistent use can reduce HIV transmission by over 70%". The same layer of protection also covers syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, and other sexually transmitted infections
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Giannou FK, Tsiara CG, Nikolopoulos GK, et al. (2016). Condom effectiveness in reducing heterosexual HIV transmission: a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on HIV serodiscordant couples. Expert Review of Pharmacoeconomics & Outcomes Research, 16(4), 489-499. <https://doi.org/10.1586/14737167.2016.1102635>
- Notes: The gap between "irregular use" and "no use" is small; the entire effect of this item lives in the words "every time, the whole act". Sharing needles (injecting drugs, tattoos, piercings included) is another independent and highly efficient transmission route with nothing to do with sex. People at high risk also have pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP): a meta-analysis of 18 studies showed that in trials with adherence above 70%, oral PrEP versus placebo gave a risk ratio of 0.30 for HIV infection (95% CI 0.21 to 0.45), while low-adherence trials showed no protective effect (Fonner VA et al., 2016, AIDS, <https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000001145>); PrEP is prescription-only — ask at an infectious disease clinic or the CDC, do not buy it online. VARIANT 1:
### 20. When a child is born and you cannot raise it, registration with civil affairs is the only lawful way out: taking money to hand a child over can be sentenced as child trafficking, and abandoning the child is the crime of abandonment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; the civil affairs department at or above county level is to be gone to for adoption registration, materials are to be submitted, and review and the adoption assessment are to be waited for
- In plain terms: it is the case that there is a way out when a child cannot be raised, but only one lawful way out is to be had: adoption registration through the civil affairs department — the adoptive relationship is had only from the day of registration; it is limited who may place a child out: the guardian of an orphan, a child welfare institution, and "birth parents with special difficulties who are unable to raise the child". When a child is handed over privately, especially for money, it can be brought about that "placing out" is turned straight into the crime of trafficking children, with a starting sentence of over 5 years and, in especially serious circumstances, the death penalty. The line that is drawn by the authorities is "whether there is a purpose of unlawful gain": selling right after birth, "placing" a child with someone for money while it is known that they have no intent to raise it or not caring at all whether they can, and taking large sums that clearly are not nutrition or gratitude fees — all of it is counted as selling one's own child. Conversely, when a child is placed out privately under genuine financial hardship while only a small nutrition or gratitude fee is taken, it is counted as private placement among the people and is not handled as trafficking. When one walks away and leaves the child, the crime of abandonment is had, up to 5 years; when the child is strangled or drowned, it is intentional homicide, and it is not made lighter by the child's being newborn.
- Benefit: Criminal Law article 240: "whoever abducts and traffics a woman or child is sentenced to 5 to 10 years of fixed-term imprisonment, plus a fine"; where aggravating circumstances are had, 10 years or more of fixed-term imprisonment or life imprisonment plus a fine or confiscation of property; "where the circumstances are especially serious, the death penalty plus confiscation of property". (《关于依法惩治拐卖妇女儿童犯罪的意见》 — Opinions on Punishing Crimes of Abducting and Trafficking Women and Children per Law), article 17: the key to the distinguishing of selling one's own child from private placement is "whether the actor has the purpose of unlawful gain"; three situations may be recognized as selling one's own child and punished as trafficking women or children — "making childbearing a means of unlawful gain and selling the child right after birth", "'placing' a child with another for money while knowing that person has no purpose of raising it, or without considering at all whether they have such a purpose", and "'placing' a child with another for sums of money clearly not 'nutrition fees' or 'gratitude fees'"; when a child is placed out privately, not for unlawful gain but forced by living hardship, while small nutrition or gratitude fees are taken, it is private placement and is not punished as trafficking. Criminal Law article 261: "one who has a duty to support the old, the young, the sick, or others without the ability to live independently and refuses to do so, where the circumstances are vile, is sentenced to up to 5 years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention, or surveillance". Civil Code article 1094: those by whom a child may be placed out are the guardian of an orphan, a child welfare institution, and birth parents with special difficulties who are unable to raise the child; article 1105: "adoption shall be registered with the civil affairs department of the people's government at or above the county level; the adoptive relationship is established from the date of registration"
