# 11. Common legal pitfalls for programmers and tech professionals

This section’s metrics are personal freedom and money — what you get back is “don’t get locked up” and “don’t pay damages.” People who write code or do technical work often treat the items below as “just technical problems.” In fact they map to specific criminal offenses, or to a concrete civil sum you may have to pay. Each item gives the numbers in the statutes, plus cases published on the websites of the Supreme People’s Procuratorate and the Supreme People’s Court. Criminal-law citations use the consolidated text “as amended by Amendment (XI) to the Criminal Law” republished by the Beijing Traffic Management Bureau. Item 1 is the general one: how to judge before you act, and what to do first if something goes wrong.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below; numbers in parentheses are item numbers.

**Before you act**: Before acting, ask whether anyone will file a police report (item 1).

**Cheats, scripts, and cracks**: Game cheats and aids (item 2); ticket-snatching, fake-order, and coupon-farming scripts (item 3); crawl only public pages that need no login (item 4); tools that auto-skip or block ads in other apps (item 18); crack patches and keygens (item 20).

**Touching others’ systems**: Do not run your programs on others’ computers (item 8); do not test others’ systems without written authorization (item 9); report vulnerabilities as required (item 10); DDoS traffic attacks (item 21).

**Side gigs and client work**: Development orders for gambling, fraud, or adult-content apps (item 5); circumvention tools and VPN accounts (item 11); AI relay sites (item 19).

**Leaving and ownership**: Hand over account access when you leave (item 6); do not take source code when you leave (item 7); non-compete agreements (item 12); code written with company resources belongs to the company (item 13); GPL and other open-source code (item 14).

**Shipping a product**: Collect only personal information the business needs (item 15); complete ICP filing before launch (item 16); generative-AI security assessment and algorithm filing (item 17).
### 1. Before acting, ask one question: will the vendor, platform, or company file a police report; if you are pursued, hire a criminal defense lawyer at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. Before acting, replace “can this be done technically?” with another question: will the vendor, platform, or company file a police report. The hard part is resisting the thought that “nobody will care about something this small,” because investigating authorities have the power to obtain server logs, payment records, and group-chat records
- In plain terms: Before you act, ask one question: will the vendor, platform, or company file a police report. Investigating authorities have the power to obtain server logs, payment records, and group-chat records. If you are criminally detained, the decision whether to arrest you comes within at most 37 days. From the first questioning you may hire a lawyer; the lawyer can argue to the procuratorate that arrest should not be approved, and can also apply for bail
- Benefit: Any unit or individual who discovers facts of a crime has the right to report it. If investigating authorities believe there are facts of a crime, they shall open a case. Courts, procuratorates, and public security “have the power to collect and obtain evidence from relevant units and individuals,” and those units and individuals must provide it truthfully. After criminal detention, public security must apply for arrest approval within 3 days (ask the procuratorate for formal arrest). In special circumstances this may be extended by 1 to 4 days. For circumstances such as crimes committed while roaming across places in series, it may be extended to 30 days. The procuratorate then has another 7 days to decide. Added up, the arrest decision comes within at most 37 days. When the procuratorate reviews arrest approval, “if defense counsel requests it, the defense counsel’s opinions shall be heard.” Counsel also has the right to apply to change compulsory measures — for example, replacing detention with bail pending trial — and the investigating authority must reply within 3 days. From the first interrogation, or from the time a compulsory measure is imposed (detention, arrest, and the like), you may entrust a lawyer. While the case is still being investigated by public security, you may only entrust a lawyer. When counsel requests a meeting, the detention center must arrange it within 48 hours at latest. If the likely sentence is control, criminal detention, or another penalty lighter than imprisonment, or only accessory penalties without a principal penalty, bail pending trial is available. Bail means posting a bond or finding a guarantor and going home while the case proceeds. Bail lasts up to 12 months (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:全国人大 (2018). 刑事诉讼法（2018 年修正，第三十四、三十九、五十四、六十七、七十九、八十八、九十一、九十七、一百一十、一百一十二条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>
- Notes: These 37 days count from detention to the procuratorate’s decision whether to approve arrest; they are not the upper limit on how long you can be held. After arrest approval there is still a detention period during investigation, which continues to run. Within these 37 days, what a lawyer can do is see the person, submit to the procuratorate an opinion that arrest should not be approved, and apply for bail pending trial. The author’s view is that waiting until after indictment to hire a lawyer means the arrest-approval stage has already passed. Every other item in this section is about the same thing: whether the other side has grounds to file a report.

### 2. Do not make or sell game cheats and aids, including AI aids that “only read the screen and never touch memory,” and do not sell modified builds that add features to software such as WeChat
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. The trade-off is giving up income from such orders and “little tools.” The hard part is that the money comes fast and it is unclear who will pursue you
- In plain terms: Selling a cheat to 20 person-times, or earning 5000 yuan, is enough for up to 3 years; especially serious cases get 3 to 7 years. Someone who made wall-hack and aim-assist cheats sold over 80000 yuan worth in 3 months, got 1 year suspended for 1 year and a 5000 yuan fine. Someone who took server-side code, ran a private server, and took over 20000000 yuan in top-ups got up to 4 years, with fines as high as 10000000 yuan
- Benefit: Providing programs or tools specially used to intrude upon or illegally control computer information systems, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years and a fine concurrently or alone. Concurrent means prison plus a fine; alone means fine only. Especially serious circumstances bring 3 to 7 years plus a fine. The threshold for “serious circumstances” is providing the tool to 20 person-times or more, or illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more. Illegal gains means the money earned from this. Intentionally bypassing or destroying technical protection measures set by the rights holder may also constitute copyright infringement, with up to 3 years, or 3 to 10 years in serious cases. Official case: Wang and others made and sold cheats that read and modified game memory for wall hacks and aim assist. In 3 months they sold over 80000 yuan and profited over 30000 yuan. Sentence: 1 year suspended for 1 year, fine 5000 yuan. Suspended for 1 year means a 1-year sentence that is not served yet; if you stay clean that year you need not go to prison. Xiang and others sold the “TZ” series of cheats with sales over 200000 yuan; Xiang got 3 years suspended for 3 years and a 20000 yuan fine. Liu and others obtained game server programs, changed parameters, ran private servers, and took over 20000000 yuan in top-ups. Sentences from 4 years to 2 years 6 months, fines from 10000000 to 1300000 yuan. Not only games. A Shanghai company made custom ROMs that added dozens of features to WeChat on the phone — auto-forward, like, mass message, and more. Those phones were sold as “marketing phones,” 2381 units. In 2019 the Shanghai Xuhui court convicted the company of copyright infringement, fined it 500000 yuan, and sentenced the person in charge to 1 year 6 months suspended for 1 year 6 months (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第三款、第二百一十七条第六项）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第三条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2026). 上海静安：严惩制售游戏外挂黑灰产共护清朗网络空间. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202608/t20260825_735480.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2021). 2020 年度检察机关保护知识产权典型案例（案例五）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202104/t20210425_516525.shtml>；国家版权局 (2020). 2019 年度上海十大版权典型案件发布（上海市版权局发布，案例八：上海昱宫网络科技有限公司等侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/ywxx/202004/t20200430_47269.html>
- Notes: Published cheat rulings all involve reading or writing game memory and bypassing anti-cheat. Aids that “only screenshot or only simulate key presses” have not yet appeared in official rulings. But the ticket-snatching case in item 3 shows that never touching memory and only sending requests can still support a conviction for illegally obtaining data. Whether it is a crime and under which charge is decided by investigating authorities from materials the vendor submits when reporting — not by the author. Selling to 20 person-times already meets “serious circumstances”; that bar is low.

### 3. Do not write or sell scripts for ticket snatching, flash sales, fake orders, or coupon farming — even if they merely “automate button clicks”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No expense and no time required
- In plain terms: Even if a script does not modify memory or crack anything and only sends requests faster than a person, it can still lead to a conviction; earning 5000 yuan or causing the other side 10000 yuan in losses is enough. Someone used bought 12306 accounts to snatch over 200 tickets, earned over 20000 yuan, and got 1 year 6 months suspended for 1 year 6 months plus a 5000 yuan fine. Someone who sold ticket-snatching software in an online shop paid 20000 yuan for unfair competition.
- Benefit: Illegally accessing computer systems or obtaining their data via any technical means can lead to imprisonment of up to 3 years, a fine, or both; more serious cases incur 3–7 years in prison. The threshold for “serious circumstances” is earning over 5,000 yuan illegally or causing losses exceeding 10,000 yuan. Official cases include a person who used a bot to submit numerous ticket‑purchase requests using purchased 12306 accounts; he snatched more than 200 tickets and earned over 20,000 yuan, resulting in the same sentence as above. Zheng sold ticket‑snatching software for a specific booking app and was ordered to pay 20,000 yuan for unfair competition (national precedent).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>；最高人民检察院 (2026). 上海静安：查办利用外挂软件批量代抢火车票牟利案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202607/t20260707_731426.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2025). 2024 年人民法院知识产权典型案例（案例七，抢票软件不正当竞争案）. <https://enipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-4205.html>
- Notes: Even scripts that do not alter memory or bypass security and merely operate faster than humans still fall under Article 285 of the Criminal Law. Both paid ticket‑snatching services and selling such scripts are considered profit‑generating activities. No official cases have been reported for individuals using such scripts solely for personal use.

### 4. Crawl only public pages that need no login; do not bypass anti-scraping, do not touch personal information, and do not sell the data you crawl
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. A crawler is a program that automatically flips through web pages and pulls down the data on them. Before crawling, glance at the robots file and any API authorization notes; robots is the file a site puts in its root directory listing which pages must not be scraped. Data that needs a login, a token (a temporary pass string), or a cracked signature to obtain — do not crawl it. The hard part is that technically these data are one grab away; holding back is all self-discipline
- In plain terms: Bypassing login, token, signature, and similar protections to take data, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years if especially serious. Crawling personal information is worse. Location tracks, communication content, credit, and property-type information at 50 records already counts as serious circumstances. Accommodation, health, and transaction types at 500 records, other types at 5000 records, or earning 5000 yuan, likewise
- Benefit: Taking system data by bypassing protections has the same sentencing range as item 3 (ticket-snatching, flash-sale, and fake-order scripts): up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years. Illegally obtaining, selling, or providing citizens’ personal information meets “serious circumstances” at any of the following: location tracks, communication content, credit, or property information of 50 records or more; accommodation, communication records, health, or transaction information of 500 records or more; other personal information of 5000 records or more; illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more. Serious circumstances bring up to 3 years and a fine concurrently or alone; especially serious cases bring 3 to 7 years. Official case: software developer Li disguised a function that stole phone albums as a “looks rating” app and obtained 1751 face photos. He also resold social-engineering database material and got 3 years suspended for 3 years. A social-engineering database is a database that aggregates personal information leaked from various places. A network technology company used a crawler to take store and order data from a food-delivery platform, caused over 40000 yuan in losses, and was prosecuted for illegally obtaining computer information system data (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款、第二百五十三条之一）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2017). 关于办理侵犯公民个人信息刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第三、四、五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201705/t20170509_190088.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 检察机关依法惩治侵犯公民个人信息犯罪典型案例（案例二）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202212/t20221207_594915.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例一）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: A 2017 judicial interpretation provides that “providing to others information lawfully collected, without the person’s consent” also counts as “providing.” Exporting a user table inside a company to an outside partner falls under the same sentence. 5000 records is only the size of a small table.

### 5. Refuse development orders for gambling, fraud, or adult-content apps; do not set up payment gateways or unblock accounts for such “clients”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No direct monetary cost. The trade-off is forgoing outsourcing projects that pay well but have vague requirements. The difficulty lies in the fact that these clients often offer significantly higher pay than normal projects.
- In plain terms: If you take a development order knowing it is a gambling app, you are treated as an accomplice in operating a casino. Operating a casino carries up to 5 years, or 5 to 10 years in serious cases — heavier than the at-most 3 years for merely helping. In one e-sports betting app case, 28 people were sentenced under operating a casino to between 1 year and 3 years 6 months. Someone who unblocked QQ accounts for a fraud ring got 1 year 6 months and a 20000 yuan fine.
- Benefit: Providing technical support or payment settlement services to known online criminals can lead to imprisonment of up to 3 years and/or a fine, especially when the circumstances are serious. If other crimes are also involved, the harsher penalty applies. Operating illegal gambling dens carries up to 5 years in prison plus a fine; serious cases bring 5–10 years. Official examples: Zhangsheng Group developed gambling games and handled “online distribution,” earning fees for website setup and reporting; the total illicit revenue reached nearly 7 billion yuan. Its leader was sentenced to 9 years and 9 months plus a 50-million-yuan fine. 79 developers, including Wang Qiang, received sentences ranging from 6 months to 9 years and 9 months. In another case, 28 developers in Hainan who built an e-sports betting app were convicted of operating illegal gambling dens and received similar sentences, plus fines. One individual who ran a studio unblocking QQ accounts for fraudsters got 1 year and 6 months in prison and a 20,000-yuan fine (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十七条之二、第三百零三条第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民检察院 (2023). 建赌博网站并出售，涉案近 70 亿元. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202307/t20230711_621248.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 上海闵行区：办理一起新型开设网络赌场案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202208/t20220805_569952.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 依法惩治帮助信息网络犯罪活动及相关犯罪典型案例（案例一）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202507/t20250728_702377.shtml>
- Notes: “Knowing intent” does not require the client to explicitly state the purpose. When a quote is far above market rates, the app includes top-up, cash-out, and betting features, and the client refuses to disclose usage, these facts are used to prove you were aware of the illegal nature. Developers of gambling apps are prosecuted as accomplices in operating illegal gambling dens, not under the “Crime of Assisting Information Network Criminal Activities” which caps at 3 years.

### 6. When you leave, hand over all account access; do not delete databases, leave backdoors, or change passwords and lock the system — even if the company owes you money
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. If the company owes you wages, get them back through labor arbitration; do not act yourself (labor arbitration is in Section 7). The hard part is holding back from touching the system while angry
- In plain terms: Account passwords the company gave you do not mean you may still use them after you leave. Illegally controlling a system, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years. Deleting data so the system cannot run, with serious consequences, brings up to 5 years, or 5 years or more if especially serious. An engineer who was dismissed logged in with the old admin account, deleted data and logs, caused over 120000 yuan in losses, and got 3 years suspended for 5 years plus a 30000 yuan fine
- Benefit: Illegally controlling a computer information system, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years and a fine concurrently or alone; especially serious cases bring 3 to 7 years. Deleting or modifying system functions or data so the system cannot run normally, with serious consequences, brings up to 5 years; especially serious cases bring 5 years or more. Official case: senior IT engineer Lü, after being dismissed, logged into the company shared server with the old admin account. He changed the password, deleted data and logs, and caused over 120000 yuan in losses. Sentence: 3 years suspended for 5 years, fine 30000 yuan. Software-company employee Bai deliberately inserted faulty code into an upgrade program so the client system could not log in, causing over 40000 yuan in losses. He was prosecuted for destroying a computer information system (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第二款、第二百八十六条）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一、十一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2024). 检察机关「依法惩治网络犯罪 助力网络空间综合治理」典型案例（案例六）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202402/t20240223_644505.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例七）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: Account passwords the company gave you do not mean you may still use them after you leave. Logging in with an account beyond the authorized scope counts as intrusion under Prosecutorial Example No. 36; see item 9 (do not test others’ systems without written authorization). In Lü’s case, after logging in he also changed the admin password and deleted data. The procuratorate found the system had not gone down as a result, so it did not charge destroying a computer information system and charged illegal control of a computer information system instead. Losses include necessary costs of restoring data. Under the 2011 joint judicial interpretation, losses of 10000 yuan or more meet “serious circumstances,” and 50000 yuan counts as especially serious. Lü’s losses of over 120000 yuan were found to be especially serious circumstances.

### 7. When you leave, do not take source code, client lists, or technical documents; do not upload them to a personal cloud drive; do not reuse them at the next employer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. Before leaving, delete company materials from your personal computer and cloud drives. The hard part is that some files are ones you wrote line by line, and you hate to let them go
- In plain terms: Copyright in code written on the job generally belongs to the company; client lists may also count as trade secrets — taking them is taking the company’s things. A former employee who took security-screening system source code to a new company got 5 years and a 500000 yuan fine; the new company was fined 4000000 yuan. Someone who exploited a system flaw to download over 33000 design files got 1 year 10 months. The company can also sue you separately for damages
- Benefit: Violating confidentiality duties by disclosing trade secrets or using them yourself, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years and a fine concurrently or alone; especially serious cases bring 3 to 10 years plus a fine. The Anti-Unfair Competition Law also lists two acts as infringement: obtaining trade secrets by “electronic intrusion” (technical intrusion into a system); and inducing or helping others to breach confidentiality. Official case: Sun and other former employees took security-screening system source code and core algorithms and used them in a new company’s product. Sun got 5 years and a 500000 yuan fine; the new company was fined 4000000 yuan. Tian exploited a system flaw to download over 33000 design files and got 1 year 10 months plus a 100000 yuan fine. Engineer Yang stored App module source code on a personal computer and in the cloud, then built a similar App after switching jobs, and was prosecuted for infringing trade secrets (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百一十九条）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 反不正当竞争法（2025 年修订，第十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699862.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关知识产权保护典型案例（案例七）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202504/t20250423_693691.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2020). 2019 年度检察机关保护知识产权典型案例（案例十六）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202004/t20200425_459623.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2025). 检察机关依法惩治侵害企业数据安全犯罪 服务保障企业创新发展（案例五）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>
- Notes: Even “code you wrote yourself” generally has copyright belonging to the company; see item 13 (code written with company resources belongs to the company). Taking it is taking the company’s things. Client lists are business information and may also constitute trade secrets. Besides a criminal sentence, the company can bring a separate civil claim for damages.

### 8. Do not run your own programs on others’ computers, servers, or cameras; do not use company machines for mining
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No expense and no time loss
- In plain terms: Controlling others’ computers, servers, or cameras without permission reaches up to 3 years at 20 devices, or 3 to 7 years if especially serious. Even below the criminal threshold, detention of up to 5 days still applies, and a fine of 50000 to 500000 yuan is possible. Anyone who has received a criminal penalty for this is barred for life from key posts in cybersecurity management and network operations.
- Benefit: Illegally controlling computer information systems is a crime. Serious cases result in up to 3 years in prison plus a fine; especially severe cases incur 3 to 7 years. Controlling 20 or more devices is deemed “serious.” Those not meeting criminal thresholds still face confiscation of illegal gains, up to 5 days of detention, and fines ranging from 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. More serious violations bring 5 to 15 days of detention and fines from 100,000 to 1,000,000 yuan. Anyone with a prior public security penalty is barred for 5 years, while those with a criminal record are barred for life from critical cybersecurity and network operation roles. Example: Li was sentenced to 3 years in prison suspended for 5 years and fined 20,000 yuan after using software to illegally control 105 surveillance cameras; Wu and others received 2 to 4 years and 6 months in prison for implanting malware into mobile phone motherboards to harvest verification codes from over 3.3 million devices.
- Evidence grade: A

### 9. Do not test others’ systems without written authorization; “good intentions” and “reporting afterward” are not defenses in the statute
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. Before joining a vendor SRC or a public bug-bounty test, first obtain a document that states the authorized scope, target list, and allowed testing window. An SRC is a vendor-run security response center that receives externally reported vulnerabilities. Stop once you have verified the vulnerability exists. The hard part is giving up the “quick try” impulse
- In plain terms: The statute looks at whether you had authorization and how much you obtained — not at what you meant. Obtaining 500 sets of ordinary account passwords, or earning 5000 yuan or causing the other side 10000 yuan in losses, is enough for up to 3 years. Exporting one user table “to prove the bug” often crosses 500 sets in a single pass. Having account passwords and using them outside the authorized scope also counts as intrusion
- Benefit: Systems of ordinary companies and individuals are computer information systems outside the fields of state affairs, national defense construction, and cutting-edge science and technology. Intruding into such systems, or using other technical means to obtain data stored, processed, or transmitted in them, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention, and a fine concurrently or alone. Especially serious cases bring 3 to 7 years plus a fine. “Serious circumstances” has several standards: obtaining 10 or more sets of identity-authentication information (account passwords and the like) for online financial services such as payment settlement, securities trading, or futures trading; obtaining 500 or more sets of other identity-authentication information; illegally controlling 20 or more computer information systems; illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more, or economic losses of 10000 yuan or more. Five times or more of those figures is “especially serious circumstances.” Official case: Prosecutorial Example No. 36 established one sentence — “using an account and password to log into a computer information system beyond the authorized scope is an act of intruding into a computer information system.” Meaning: you already have the account and password, but using them outside the authorized scope itself counts as intrusion. In that case three people used accounts, passwords, and tokens obtained at work to log into the company’s internal system from another place. They downloaded data outside their work scope, sold it online, and had illegal gains of 37000 yuan. They were sentenced to 4 years, 3 years 9 months, and 4 years respectively, each fined 40000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百八十五条第一款、第二款）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2011). 关于办理危害计算机信息系统安全刑事案件应用法律若干问题的解释（第一条）. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>（深圳市公安局转载）；最高人民检察院 (2017). 第九批指导性案例（检例第 36 号，卫梦龙、龚旭、薛东东非法获取计算机信息系统数据案）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/jczdal/201710/t20171017_202593.shtml>；央广网 (2016-06-29). 白帽子发布漏洞后被抓 世纪佳缘否认“钓鱼”使其上套（记者赵珂，实习记者刘香玺）. <https://m.cnr.cn/news/yctt/20160629/t20160629_522531823.html>（网页存档 <https://web.archive.org/web/20261005043859/https://m.cnr.cn/news/yctt/20160629/t20160629_522531823.html>）
- Notes: Prosecutorial Example No. 36 itself was a case of selling data for profit. It is cited only for the sentence that logging in beyond authorized scope counts as intrusion — not to say that good-faith testing will be sentenced the same way. Article 285 of the Criminal Law and the 2011 interpretation do not list good intentions or reporting afterward as reasons that it is not a crime. The author infers from that: what you meant to do, and whether you reported afterward, cannot cancel the offense; they may only affect how heavy the sentence is or whether to prosecute. A vendor thanking you first and filing a report later are not contradictory; thanks are not authorization. In 2016, a security researcher commonly called a “white hat” submitted a vulnerability in the Jiayuan site to a third-party vulnerability platform. Jiayuan later reported to police and he was arrested. Jiayuan told reporters the report was because data had been taken, and at the time they did not know the person who took the data was the one who submitted the vulnerability. This case has only media reports, no judgment and no official bulletin; there is no official account of how it ended. No good-faith testing case that can be checked word for word was found on the Supreme People’s Court or Supreme People’s Procuratorate websites, so this item is graded only from the statute and penalty standards. If you test, go through the vendor’s public SRC, or a written engagement, and put scope, targets, and time window in the document. Prove the bug exists with the least evidence; do not bulk-extract data. Conduct below the criminal threshold still faces public-security penalties and occupational bans; fine amounts and years match item 8 (do not run your programs on others’ machines). What to do after finding a vulnerability is in item 10 (report vulnerabilities as required).

### 10. Report vulnerabilities as required; before a patch, do not publish details, release exploit tools, or hand them to parties abroad
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. Report the vulnerability to the product provider, or to an official platform. Until the vendor has fixed it, do not publish details or proof-of-concept code
- In plain terms: Individuals are also covered by the (网络产品安全漏洞管理规定). Before the vendor has a fix, you must not publish the vulnerability, and must not publish vulnerability details for systems currently in use. You must not release programs and tools made to exploit the vulnerability, and when you publish you must also explain how to fix it. Unpublished vulnerabilities must not be given to organizations or individuals abroad other than the vendor
- Benefit: This regulation covers three kinds of subjects: domestic network product providers, network operators, and organizations or individuals engaged in discovering, collecting, publishing, and similar activities involving vulnerabilities. No organization or individual may use vulnerabilities for activities that harm cybersecurity, or illegally collect, sell, or publish vulnerability information. Publishing vulnerability information to the public must follow five rules. First, do not publish before the product provider offers a remediation measure. Second, do not publish details of vulnerabilities in networks, information systems, and equipment currently used by network operators. Third, do not deliberately exaggerate harm and risk, and do not use vulnerability information for malicious hype or fraud. Fourth, do not publish or provide programs and tools specially used to exploit vulnerabilities for activities that harm cybersecurity. Fifth, when publishing, publish remediation or prevention measures at the same time. Also, unpublished vulnerability information must not be provided to organizations or individuals abroad other than the product provider. The regulation encourages reporting vulnerabilities to four official platforms. One is the MIIT cybersecurity threat and vulnerability information sharing platform. Two is the National Cyber and Information Security Information Notification Center vulnerability platform. Three is the CNCERT/CC vulnerability platform. Four is the China Information Technology Security Evaluation Center vulnerability database. Collecting or publishing vulnerability information in violation is handled by MIIT and the Ministry of Public Security according to their duties. Conduct that meets circumstances under the (网络安全法) is punished under that law. Publishing system vulnerabilities and other cybersecurity information to the public shall comply with relevant state rules. Violations bring orders to correct and warnings from the competent authority, and may bring a fine of 10000 yuan or more and 100000 yuan or less. Refusal to correct or serious circumstances bring a fine of 100000 yuan or more and 1000000 yuan or less. The authority may also order suspension of related business, shutdown for rectification, or closure of the website or application. It may also revoke related business permits or the business license. Persons directly in charge and other persons directly responsible may be fined 10000 yuan or more and 100000 yuan or less (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:工业和信息化部、国家互联网信息办公室、公安部 (2021). 网络产品安全漏洞管理规定（工信部联网安〔2021〕66 号，第二、四、九、十、十四条，2021 年 9 月 1 日施行）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5641351.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十八、六十五条；2016 年文本为第二十六、六十二条，规定第十四条援引的是 2016 年文本的条号）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>

A lawfully found vulnerability can still draw a penalty for unlawful publication; a vulnerability found without authorization has problems on both the testing and the publishing sides. The regulation does not specially address “the vendor was notified but does not fix.” The author’s advice is to keep records of negotiation and reporting, and also report to the four official platforms above.

### 12. If you signed a non-compete, and after leaving the company does not pay monthly compensation, send a written demand; if unpaid for a full 3 months you may terminate; posts that never touched trade secrets may seek a ruling that the clause does not take effect
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money spent. When you leave, keep the original non-compete agreement and evidence that the company did not pay monthly
- In plain terms: A non-compete means for a period after leaving you may not work for a competitor — up to 2 years — and the company must pay compensation monthly. If no amount was agreed, pay 30% of average wages over the 12 months before leaving, not below the local minimum wage. If for the company’s reasons nothing is paid for 3 months, you may request termination. From 2025, posts that never touched trade secrets may request a ruling that the clause does not take effect
- Benefit: Non-competes may only bind senior managers, senior technical staff, and others with confidentiality duties. The term is at most 2 years. The company must pay economic compensation monthly during that term. If no compensation amount was agreed, pay 30% of average wages over the 12 months before leaving, and not below the local minimum wage. If for the company’s own reasons nothing is paid for 3 months, the worker may request termination of the non-compete. If the company terminates on its own initiative, it must pay an extra 3 months of compensation. From 2025, workers whose posts never knew or touched trade secrets may request a ruling that the clause does not take effect. If the agreed scope exceeds the secrets they actually knew, the excess is void. Official case: a pharmaceutical company sued CTO Zheng for 7100000 yuan in liquidated damages. The court found the new employer’s products were not substitutes for the former company’s, that the non-compete scope should be limited to secrets he actually knew, and dismissed the claim in full (nationwide)

### 13. Code written on work time with company resources belongs to the company; do personal open-source projects on your own time and equipment, and do not mix in company code
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money spent. When you join, read the intellectual-property clauses in the labor contract carefully. The hard part is that personal projects and company work often share one computer and must be kept apart
- In plain terms: Software written mainly with company equipment, intranet, or business data, for which the company bears responsibility, has copyright belonging to the company; you keep only the right of authorship — and the same if the contract so provides. Code written after hours may still count as the company’s if you used a company computer or business data. Inventions made mainly with company resources also have patents belonging to the company
- Benefit: Works created to complete the unit’s work tasks are works for hire (things made while doing the job). In the ordinary case copyright belongs to the author; the unit has priority to use them within its business scope, and for 2 years the author may not license third parties to use them in the same way. But two kinds of cases differ. One is engineering designs, software, and similar works created mainly with the unit’s material and technical conditions, for which the unit bears responsibility. The other is works the contract assigns to the unit. In both, the author keeps only the right of authorship. The unit’s material and technical conditions means company equipment, intranet, business data, and the like. Inventions completed while performing the unit’s tasks, or mainly using the unit’s material and technical conditions, are service inventions. The right to apply for a patent and the patent obtained both belong to the unit (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第十八条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；全国人大常委会 (2020). 专利法（2020 年修正，第六条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html>
- Notes: Software is written directly into the “copyright belongs to the unit” category, so programmers almost never fall into the “copyright belongs to the author, unit has two-year priority use” tier. “Written after hours” can still be treated as mainly made with the unit’s equipment and materials if you used a company computer, intranet, or company business data. To keep a personal project clean, use your own machine, do not touch company repositories, and do not overlap company business.

### 14. Using open-source code such as GPL in products: Open-source it per the license or switch licenses — don’t treat this as “no one cares”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: There is no cost involved. Before using someone else’s open-source code, check its license for any stated usage terms. The GPL family of licenses requires that any product built using it must also have its source code released, which conflicts with selling the product as closed-source software for profit. If such a conflict exists, you must switch to another license. The hard part is giving up a library you’ve already grown comfortable using.

- In plain terms: Copyright infringement requires you to compensate the copyright holder based on their actual losses or the profits you earned from the infringement. If you commit infringement intentionally and under aggravating circumstances, you may be ordered to pay damages ranging from 1 to 5 times the original amount. One company hired a former employee of a competitor and developed software that was substantially similar to the competitor’s product. The company argued that since the competitor’s software used GPLv2, it was required to be open-source anyway. The Supreme People’s Court ruled that whether a developer complies with GPLv2 or not has no bearing on their copyright rights, and ordered the company to cease infringement and pay 500,000 yuan in damages.

- Benefit: Copyright infringement demands compensation based on the rights holder’s actual losses or the infringer’s profits. If neither of these can be accurately calculated, damages are determined by how much it would cost to obtain a proper license for the same software. Intentional and aggravated infringement can also result in 1 to 5 times the standard compensation amount. If the infringement harms public interests, any illegal profits may be confiscated and fines imposed. A relevant official case is Supreme People’s Court Civil Appeal No. 51 of 2021. In this case, a defendant hired a former employee of a plaintiff and developed gateway software that was substantially similar to the plaintiff’s product. The defendant claimed the plaintiff’s software used GPLv2 and thus should have been open-source. The Supreme People’s Court reiterated that compliance with GPLv2 and copyright ownership are two separate issues, and ordered the defendant to cease infringement and pay 500,000 yuan in damages nationwide.

- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第五十三、五十四条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；最高人民法院知识产权法庭 (2022). 涉「开源软件」著作权侵权案（（2021）最高法知民终 51 号）. <https://ipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-3042.html>

- Notes: This case makes it clear that “the other party used GPL code” cannot serve as justification for copying their software. Conversely, there are already effective court rulings in China where GPL authors successfully sue commercial entities that fail to release source code. No original text of these rulings could be found on the Supreme People’s Court website, so they are not cited here. This item is graded B because compliance with open-source licenses is governed only by general contract and copyright laws, without any specific legal provisions dedicated to this area.

### 17. Before offering generative AI services to the public, complete the required security assessment and algorithm filing, and mark generated content
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent, but you must go through the cyberspace authority’s process, which takes some time. Tools that do not provide services to the domestic public are outside this measure
- In plain terms: Generative AI services aimed at the public that can shape opinion or mobilize people need a security assessment and algorithm filing before launch; changes or shutdown also need amendment or cancellation filings. - Benefit: Providing generative artificial-intelligence services with public-opinion attributes or social-mobilization capacity shall conduct a security assessment per state rules. ### 18. Do not make or distribute tools that automatically skip or block ads in other apps; even free ones may be held to be unfair competition

- In plain terms: Making software that skips or blocks ads in other apps for users may get you sued for unfair competition. Not charging money may still count; the law’s “operators” include individuals who provide services. A browser that by default blocked Mango TV’s pre-roll ads paid 800000 yuan. - Benefit: The law provides that operators must not use technical means to obstruct or disrupt the normal operation of network products or services lawfully provided by other operators. Market regulators may also fine 100000 to 1000000 yuan, or 1000000 to 5000000 yuan in serious cases. ### 20. Do not write, sell, or distribute crack patches, keygens, or cracked software; once sales hit the numbers, it is sentenced as copyright infringement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money spent. The trade-off is dropping fast money from selling cracks, registration codes, or cracked dongles. Do not post crack patches to forums or cloud drives either. The hard part is that cracking looks like technical work, not like theft
- In plain terms: Registration-code checks, dongles, and similar verification are a software’s technical measures; bypassing them without permission is infringement, and you must pay even if you charge nothing. Cracking or selling crack tools for profit — earning 30000 yuan or selling 50000 yuan — is enough for a criminal sentence, up to 10 years. Someone who made cracked dongles paired with pirated software for sale saw three co-defendants get between 1 year and 3 years 2 months
- Benefit: Civil side first. The Copyright Law allows rights holders to add technical measures to works. Technical measures are effective technology, devices, or components that prevent or restrict others from using a work without permission. Without the rights holder’s permission, no one may intentionally bypass or destroy technical measures. No one may manufacture, import, or provide to the public related devices or components for bypassing technical measures. No one may intentionally provide technical services to others for bypassing technical measures. Computer software is a work; doing any of these is infringement and requires stopping the infringement and paying damages. Damages are by the rights holder’s actual losses or what you earned. If that cannot be calculated, refer to what a normal license would cost. Intentional infringement with serious circumstances brings 1 to 5 times. If public interest is also harmed, the copyright authority may confiscate illegal gains. Illegal business volume of 50000 yuan or more may be fined 1 to 5 times that amount. Below 50000 yuan or if the amount cannot be calculated, a fine of 250000 yuan or less is possible. Criminal side next. Amendment (XI) to the Criminal Law wrote “intentionally bypassing or destroying technical measures” into the crime of copyright infringement, on the premise of profit. Illegal gains of 30000 yuan or more, or illegal business volume of 50000 yuan or more, bring up to 3 years, with a fine concurrently or alone. At 10 times those figures, the sentence is 3 to 10 years plus a fine. Someone who knows another person is committing copyright infringement and still provides devices, components, or technical services mainly used to bypass or destroy technical measures faces the same crime once the same thresholds are met. Official case 1: From March (3) 2019 to July (7) 2022, Liu made “dongles” that could bypass software technical measures for profit. He also copied pirated software and sold it together with the dongles. His illegal business volume was over 1060000 yuan. Shanghai No. 3 Intermediate People’s Court found especially serious circumstances and sentenced him for copyright infringement. The Supreme People’s Procuratorate said this was the first nationwide “intentionally bypassing or destroying technical measures” copyright-infringement case after Amendment (XI) took effect. In April (4) 2023, Shanghai courts sentenced the three defendants in this string of cases to between 3 years 2 months and 1 year, some suspended, with fines from 700000 to 80000 yuan. Official case 2: Medical-equipment repair worker Liu spent 80000 yuan in a QQ group to buy 50 cracked dongles, then resold them online for a few thousand yuan each. Plugging one in bypassed the medical equipment’s identity check and entered the maintenance software. Shanghai Putuo court sentenced him to 1 year 2 months, suspended, plus an 80000 yuan fine. Official case 3: From May (5) 2021, Li and others in Xuzhou had crack programs made for game machines and produced crack devices to sell. Illegal business volume was over 39000000 yuan. In November (11) 2024, 10 people were sentenced to between 3 years 6 months and 2 years 6 months, some suspended, with fines from 11000000 to 1500000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2020). 刑法修正案（十一）（第二十项，即刑法第二百一十七条第六项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202012/t20201227_503682.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2020). 著作权法（2020 年修正，第三条，第四十九、五十、五十三、五十四条）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2025). 关于办理侵犯知识产权刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2025〕5 号，第十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202504/t20250424_693977.shtml>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2025). 知识产权刑事保护典型案例（案例六：刘某生、刘某侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202504/t20250424_693978.shtml>；最高人民检察院 (2024). 最高检：指导办理全国首例「故意避开或者破坏技术措施型」侵犯著作权案. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/2024zgjtzwlft/202402/t20240228_645416.shtml>；检察日报 (2023-04-18). 售卖盗版加密狗 解锁医疗设备安全系统（最高检官网转载）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202304/t20230418_611586.shtml>；国家版权局 (2025). 2024 年度全国打击侵权盗版十大案件（案件六：江苏徐州李某某等人破坏技术措施侵犯著作权案）. <https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/ywxx/202508/t20250801_925366.html>
- Notes: The beneficiary is you yourself. ### 21. Do not help launch DDoS traffic attacks; do not sell or rent “stress-test” traffic platforms; if you lose a game, do not attack the other side’s servers either

- Cost: No money spent. DDoS means controlling many machines to send requests at a target at once until the server is unusable. The trade-off is dropping a per-hit or packaged traffic business, and also holding back a moment of anger. When conflict arises in a game or in business, use reports, complaints, and police reports — do not pay someone to attack
- In plain terms: Using DDoS so others’ websites, servers, or games cannot open is the crime of destroying a computer information system. Serious consequences bring up to 5 years; especially serious consequences bring 5 years or more. People who pay a platform to attack are also joint offenders. Someone who ran a DDoS site collecting membership fees and taking orders got between 10 months and 5 years 6 months
- Benefit: Interfering with the functions of a computer information system so it cannot run normally, with serious consequences, brings up to 5 years of fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention. Especially serious consequences bring 5 years or more. Courts treat DDoS as the “interference” here. “Serious consequences” has several standards. One is that the main software or hardware of 10 or more computer information systems cannot run normally. One is illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more, or economic losses of 10000 yuan or more. Another is that a system serving 10000 or more users cannot run normally for a cumulative 1 hour or more. Machine counts or money amounts at 5 times those figures count as especially serious consequences. Knowing someone is committing such destruction and still providing programs or tools for destruction is punished as joint crime. The threshold is illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more, or providing to 10 person-times or more. Providing server hosting, technical support, and similar help with illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more is the same. Providing programs or tools specially used to intrude upon or illegally control computer information systems — at 20 person-times or more, or illegal gains of 5000 yuan or more — brings up to 3 years and a fine concurrently or alone.
