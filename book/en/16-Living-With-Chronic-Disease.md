# 16. How to live after a chronic disease diagnosis

Scope: overall mortality and money. After a chronic diagnosis you often still have decades ahead; this section is about how to manage those decades.
### 1. Take medications as prescribed: do not stop just because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free—in fact, it saves money on treating future complications. Taking pills takes just a few minutes a day; the hard part is doing it consistently every single day without missing doses.
- In plain terms: People who take their medications as directed by their doctor have lower death rates than those who take them intermittently. However, this difference should be taken with a grain of salt. Even people who diligently took fake pills had lower mortality, showing that part of the benefit simply comes from the fact that people who stick to a routine already take better care of their health.
- Benefit: Pooled data from 21 studies and 46,847 people (meta-analysis): compared to those who took medication intermittently, people who adhered to their prescriptions had lower mortality (odds ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63). Looking only at treatments with proven efficacy, the odds ratio was 0.55 (0.49–0.62).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes: Disputed. This is one of the findings in this book that most needs to be taken with a grain of salt. In the same pooled analysis, even people who diligently took placebos (dummy pills) had lower mortality (0.56, 0.43–0.74). This indicates that a substantial portion of the benefit stems from the fact that people who adhere to medications are already more health-conscious. The author calls this confounding factor the "healthy adherer effect." Stopping tuberculosis medication on your own can cause drug resistance; see Section 1, Item 49 (Designated Tuberculosis Hospitals).

### 2. First get outpatient chronic special-disease certification, then file for care away from home, and hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy, dialysis, and anti-rejection treatment can be settled directly in another region
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. Before you set out, file once for care away from home on the yibao (basic medical insurance) app. It can be done on a phone.
- In plain terms: Hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy for a malignant tumor, dialysis for uremia, and anti-rejection treatment after an organ transplant: for these, outpatient care away from home can in most cases be paid directly by insurance. You do not pay first and then claim it back where you are insured. You must first get outpatient chronic special-disease certification where you are insured, then file for care away from home. It works only if both are done.
- Benefit: A document from the National Healthcare Security Administration (国家医保局) says this has already been reached. The original wording is: "Cross-province direct settlement of outpatient costs for 5 outpatient chronic special diseases, including hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy for malignant tumors, dialysis for uremia, and anti-rejection treatment after organ transplant, is basically accessible at county level." "Accessible at county level" (县域可及) means it can be handled at county level. On top of these 5, the document adds another 5
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: Both have to be done: first get outpatient chronic special-disease certification where you are insured, then file for care away from home. How far the 5 newly added diseases have opened differs by place; follow what the local insurance authority publishes. If the hospital does not stock the negotiated drug you need, see section 24, item 13 (dual-channel designated pharmacies).

### 3. Follow the follow-up intervals your doctor sets, and record each measurement in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each test costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. A hospital visit plus waiting for results takes some time. The hard part is going again and again on schedule without putting it off.
- In plain terms: Chronic disease is about change over a stretch of time, not a single reading. Measuring blood pressure at home alone does not show the pressure coming down because of that. Bring the readings to the doctor and let the doctor adjust the drugs from them, and the pressure falls more. When you change hospitals, test results marked for mutual recognition are ones the hospital generally should not make you repeat.
- Benefit: Chronic conditions require seeing how values shift over time; a single point is not enough. For hypertension, one meta-analysis pooled individual data from 15 randomized trials and 7,138 people. Compared with usual care, home self-measurement at 12 months meant mean change in office systolic blood pressure −3.2 mmHg (95% CI −4.9 to −1.6). Self-measurement alone without other support showed no clear effect: −1.0 mmHg (−3.3 to 1.2, difference may be chance). The most supported package lowered by 6.1 mmHg (3.2 to 9.0). Supported care means a doctor, pharmacist, or patient adjusting meds from readings, plus health education and lifestyle guidance. The author’s conclusion: measuring alone does not reliably lower pressure; pairing readings with dose adjustment does. These endpoints are blood pressure, not death. When changing hospitals, a joint measure from the National Health Commission «国家卫健委» and three other agencies states: if the patient’s existing test results meet mutual-recognition rules and satisfy clinical need, the hospital and clinician must not repeat tests. Recognized reports carry an “HR” mark, e.g. (全国HR) (national HR)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Tucker KL, Sheppard JP, Stevens R, et al. (2017). Self-monitoring of blood pressure in hypertension: A systematic review and individual patient data meta-analysis. PLoS Medicine, 14(9), e1002389. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002389>；国家卫生健康委、国家医保局、国家中医药局、中央军委后勤保障部卫生局 (2022). 关于印发医疗机构检查检验结果互认管理办法的通知（国卫医发〔2022〕6 号），第十三条、第十七条、第十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-02/19/content_5674575.htm>
- Notes: B only because direct evidence is for hypertension alone and stops at blood pressure, not mortality. No study was found on health outcomes from “keeping readings in one notebook” itself. Each time record at least four items: date, value, drug name, and dose. Photos of lab slips on your phone are fine, but keep everything in one place. Mutual recognition has exceptions: changed condition, rapidly changing results, before surgery or transfusion, and emergency rescue—hospitals may repeat tests. Copying and keeping medical records and imaging: see Section 24, item 5 (keep your own copy of records, reports, and imaging)

### 4. Do not stop standard medical treatment to try remedies or supplements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and saves money on supplements. Takes no extra time. The hard part is standing firm when others repeatedly urge you to try them.
- In plain terms: For treatable cancers, people who rely only on alternative therapies and skip standard care face 2.5 times the risk of dying compared to those receiving standard care. Patients who combine standard care with complementary therapies often refuse surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation, which is where the extra deaths come from. Some herbal remedies (such as St. John's wort) also weaken anti-rejection or blood-thinning medications, so always consult your doctor before taking them.
- Benefit: Two studies based on US National Cancer Database records examined patients with non-metastatic, curable breast, prostate, lung, and colorectal cancers. The first study looked at patients who chose only alternative therapies and refused all surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and hormone therapy. Compared to matched patients receiving standard care, their risk of death was 2.5 times as high (hazard ratio HR 2.50, 95% CI 1.88–3.27, about a 150% higher risk). By cancer type: breast cancer was 5.68 times (3.22–10.04), lung cancer was 2.17 times (1.42–3.32), and colorectal cancer was 4.57 times (1.66–12.61). The second study examined patients who used complementary therapies alongside conventional care. Compared to conventional-care controls, their refusal rates were 7.0% vs. 0.1% for surgery, 34.1% vs. 3.2% for chemotherapy, 53.0% vs. 2.3% for radiotherapy, and 33.7% vs. 2.8% for hormone therapy. Their 5-year survival rate was 82.2% vs. 86.6%, with a risk of death 2.08 times as high (hazard ratio HR 2.08, 1.50–2.90, about double the risk). After adjusting for treatment delays and refusals, complementary therapy itself showed no significant association with mortality (hazard ratio HR 1.39, 0.83–2.33). The authors concluded that the excess deaths were driven by refusing standard treatment. China's State Administration for Market Regulation requires health food labels to state: "Health foods are not drugs and cannot replace drugs in treating diseases." The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) notes that St. John's wort reduces the effectiveness of several medications, including the organ-transplant anti-rejection drug cyclosporine and the anticoagulant warfarin.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Use of Alternative Medicine for Cancer and Its Impact on Survival. Journal of the National Cancer Institute, 110(1), 121-124. <https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djx145>；Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Complementary Medicine, Refusal of Conventional Cancer Therapy, and Survival Among Patients With Curable Cancers. JAMA Oncology, 4(10), 1375-1381. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.2487>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2019). 市场监管总局就《保健食品标注警示用语指南》和《保健食品原料目录与保健功能目录管理办法》有关情况举办专题新闻发布会. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>；National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (2025). St. John's Wort. <https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort>
- Notes: Graded B only. Both studies looked only at cancer and were observational follow-ups of registry data rather than randomized trials; patients choosing alternative therapies may differ in other unmeasured ways. For the impact of discontinuing medications in conditions like hypertension or diabetes, see Item 1 (Take prescribed medication as directed). Always tell your treating physician before trying any supplement. On whether dietary supplements are worth the money, see Section 6, Item 10 (Supplements, herbal pastes, and tonics).

### 5. Get up to 12 weeks of medication at once from community health centers for stable chronic conditions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. Asking whether they can issue long-term prescriptions at the community health service center takes just a single sentence.
- In plain terms: If your condition is stable and your medications have not changed, you can get up to 12 weeks of medication at a time from a community health center. This spares you from losing half a day each month waiting in line at a major hospital.
- Benefit: Saves you from traveling back and forth each month to queue for prescriptions at major hospitals by getting up to 12 weeks of medication at once. Within the same health insurance coverage area (pool area), the reimbursement rate for inpatient care at primary care clinics is, in principle, about 10 percentage points higher than at the tier above.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；同文「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」，并要求上级医院在基层开设高血压、糖尿病、慢性阻塞性肺疾病等常见病慢性病门诊；同文「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」，「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: Eligibility is determined by a physician, typically for patients with a confirmed diagnosis and a stable drug regimen. The medication is exactly the same; what you save is the registration fee and the half-day spent making a trip every month. If the primary clinic lacks your specific medication, ask if they offer out-of-stock registration and home delivery. The same policy document requires tertiary hospitals to gradually scale down general outpatient clinics for such chronic diseases, shifting specialist teams' general clinics toward community centers. Relying on large hospitals simply to pick up refills will only make booking appointments harder over time. If your community center lacks your medication, or if a specialist must supervise dosage adjustments, you can book an online follow-up prescription with the hospital that diagnosed you; see Section 24, Item 14 (online follow-up consultations at internet hospitals).

### 6. Before signing a family doctor (家庭医生) contract at community level, ask which services are covered by yibao (医保) and which are out of pocket
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. One extra minute of questions before signing.
- In plain terms: The basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) as required. Personalized service packages are entirely self-paid. When someone sells an “upgrade package,” ask whether it is basic or personalized, whether it is filed with the county health authority, and the yearly price.
- Benefit: Basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) per rules. Personalized packages are fully self-paid and must be filed with the county-level health administration (县级卫生健康部门)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: The value of signing is long-term follow-up for your condition, not how many items are bundled. When offered an “upgrade package,” ask three things: basic or personalized, filed or not, and cost per year.

### 7. Once diabetes is diagnosed, have the back of the eye checked once, then on the interval the doctor sets; check the feet once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A dilated fundus exam costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. A foot exam is usually included in the outpatient visit and is not charged separately. The hard part is remembering to go when the eyes and the feet feel fine.
- In plain terms: When diabetes has damaged the back of the eye or the feet, there is almost no feeling until vision is gone or the foot starts to break down. Caught early, it can still be treated. Have a dilated fundus exam at the diagnosis of type 2 diabetes; for type 1, start 5 years after onset. If early checks were normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can widen to once a year or every other year. Check the feet once a year.
- Benefit: Damage from diabetes to the back of the eye is called retinopathy; damage to the foot is called diabetic foot. Both are almost without feeling before they go wrong. Caught early, they can still be treated. Once vision is gone or the foot has broken down, it is late
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care：「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: Type 2 diabetes needs the exam at diagnosis; type 1 starts 5 years after onset. The difference is that type 2 has often been quietly high for years. If early checks were normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can widen to once every 1 to 2 years. If sensation in the feet has dulled, look at the sole yourself every day; a mirror is fine. The kidneys should also be checked every year; see item 13 (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio).

### 8. After a kidney stone, drink 2.5–3 liters of water daily and keep salt under 6 grams
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Turn sipping water throughout the day into a habit. Drink even more on days with heavy sweating; the hard part is remembering to do it every day.
- In plain terms: Among people who form an unexplained calcium kidney stone for the first time, about a quarter develop another within 5 years if they change nothing. Drinking enough water to produce at least 2 liters of urine daily cuts that recurrence rate to just over 10%—more than halving the risk. British clinical guidelines recommend drinking 2.5 to 3 liters of water a day and limiting salt intake to no more than 6 grams daily.
- Benefit: In a 5-year randomized controlled trial of 199 patients with a first episode of unexplained calcium kidney stones (idiopathic calcium stone disease), one group increased water intake to achieve a daily urine volume of at least 2 liters, while the other received no intervention. Over 5 years, recurrence occurred in 12/99 (12.1%) in the increased water group versus 27/100 (27.0%) in the no-intervention group (P = 0.008; a smaller P value indicates the difference between groups is less likely due to chance). The average time to recurrence was 38.7 ± 13.2 months versus 25.1 ± 16.4 months (P = 0.016). Baseline 24-hour urine volume was significantly lower in stone formers than in healthy controls: 1,057 ± 238 mL versus 1,401 ± 562 mL in men (P < 0.0001), and 990 ± 230 mL versus 1,239 ± 440 mL in women (P < 0.001). NICE guideline NG118 (recommendation 1.8.1) advises adults to drink 2.5 to 3 liters of water daily, and children and adolescents 1 to 2 liters depending on age, with added fresh lemon juice, avoiding carbonated drinks, and restricting adult salt intake to no more than 6 grams per day. Recommendation 1.7.1 in the same guideline advises considering stone composition analysis for adults with a history of renal or ureteric stones. For recurrent stone formers whose stones contain more than 50% calcium oxalate, recommendation 1.8.3 advises considering potassium citrate. NICE found insufficient evidence to recommend routine 24-hour urine testing. The European Association of Urology (EAU) guideline takes a different approach: every stone patient should undergo reliable stone analysis. Recurrent stone formers are classified as high risk and should collect two consecutive 24-hour urine samples for specialized metabolic evaluation. The EAU general prevention target is drinking 2.5 to 3 liters of fluids daily with a daily urine output of 2 to 2.5 liters.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Bao Y, Tu X, Wei Q (2020). Water for preventing urinary stones（纳入研究 Borghi 1996 的干预目标为尿量至少 2 L/日）. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004292. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7012319/>；Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.7.1、1.8.1、1.8.3 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>；European Association of Urology (2026). EAU Guidelines on Urolithiasis, Chapter 4 Metabolic Evaluation and Recurrence Prevention（第 4.1、4.1.2 节，Table 4.5）. <https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/metabolic-evaluation-and-recurrence-prevention>（高危结石患者名单见同一指南 Table 3.3：<https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/guidelines>）
- Notes: With recurrence reduced by roughly 55% at zero financial cost, this measure ranks as the most cost-effective in this chapter by our calculation. The real metric to watch is 24-hour urine volume—the European guideline targets 2 to 2.5 liters daily—rather than just fluid intake alone. Those who sweat heavily or work in hot environments need to drink even more. The clinical trial focused on first-time idiopathic calcium stone formers. Stones come in different compositions; uric acid stones, infection stones, and cystine stones each require distinct strategies. Both British and European guidelines recommend having stone composition analyzed. For recurrent cases, the European guideline also calls for 24-hour urine testing before deciding on medication, whereas British guidelines consider evidence for that testing insufficient. Do not cut calcium out of your diet to prevent stones; see Chapter 6, Item 21. Severe sharp pain in the back or abdomen during an attack requires urgent medical care at a hospital, not trying to flush the stone out by chugging water. Painless blood in the urine requires a different workup; see Chapter 1, Item 27.

### 9. Take long-term uric-acid-lowering medication if you have tophi, bone erosion on imaging, or at least two gout flares a year, keeping serum urate below 360 µmol/L
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol costs a few to several tens of yuan per month. In the beginning, check blood uric acid every few weeks to adjust the dose, and every few months once it stabilizes. Take an anti-inflammatory flare-prevention medication concurrently for the first 3 to 6 months. The hardest part is continuing to take it even when you feel no pain.
- In plain terms: If you have tophi (gout nodules), bone erosion seen on scans, or 2 or more gout flares a year, take uric-acid-lowering medication long term to keep blood uric acid below 360 µmol/L. If flares are rare, discuss with your doctor whether to start. With nurse-led dose adjustments based on blood tests, 95% reached their target at two years, compared to only 30% under usual care. If you stop the medication after achieving good control, most people flare again within five years; diet alone lowers uric acid only by a modest amount.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial enrolled 517 adults who had experienced a gout flare within the previous 12 months. One group received nurse-led care, where nurses explained the disease and titrated urate-lowering therapy to target according to blood test results; the other group received usual care from general practitioners. At 2 years, the proportion achieving serum urate <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) was 95% versus 30%. The likelihood of achieving target was approximately 3.18 times that of the usual-care group (relative risk RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18), P < 0.0001. Secondary outcomes at year 2—including flare frequency, tophi presence, and quality of life—all favored the nurse-led group. The cost per additional quality-adjusted life year (QALY) gained was £5,066. A follow-up study of the same trial sent questionnaires to 438 participants, with an 82% response rate. Both groups self-reported their number of flares over the preceding 12 months: the target group had a median of 0 flares (interquartile range, IQR 0–0) versus 1 flare (IQR 0–3) in the usual-care group, P < 0.001. The proportion still taking urate-lowering medication was 1.19 times higher in the target group (adjusted RR 1.19, 95% CI 1.09–1.30). The 2020 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) gout guideline strongly recommends initiating urate-lowering therapy (ULT) in three groups: those with subcutaneous tophi, radiographic damage (erosion) attributable to gout, or frequent flares (≥2 per year). For patients with more than one prior flare but fewer than 2 per year, the guideline conditionally recommends starting ULT through shared decision-making between doctor and patient. For a first flare, it conditionally recommends against starting immediately, except for patients with stage ≥3 chronic kidney disease (CKD), serum urate >9 mg/dL, or a history of urolithiasis, where starting is conditionally recommended. The strategy is to monitor serum urate continuously and titrate the dose until the target (<6 mg/dL) is reached. Allopurinol is the preferred first-line agent, including in patients with stage ≥3 CKD, with a starting dose of ≤100 mg/day. Concomitant anti-inflammatory flare prophylaxis should be started at the same time and continued for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline also cited an observational cohort of patients who stopped ULT after long-term successful control and whose serum urate remained <7 mg/dL after stopping: only 13% (27/211) remained completely flare-free over a 5-year follow-up period. Regarding diet, people who restricted or abstained from alcohol had serum urate levels 1.6 mg/dL lower than non-abstainers, and each serving of beer raised serum urate by 0.16 mg/dL. Healthy diets, the Mediterranean diet, and the DASH diet had even smaller effects. Opinions diverge on the choice of first-line ULT. The ACR guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as first-line because it is effective when dosed appropriately, well-tolerated, safe, and inexpensive. It conditionally recommends switching patients on febuxostat who have a history of cardiovascular disease or develop a new cardiovascular event to an alternative agent if available. This recommendation aligns with the FDA black box warning on febuxostat. One piece of supporting evidence was the CARES trial, which randomized 6,190 gout patients with coexisting cardiovascular disease to febuxostat or allopurinol: the febuxostat group had a 1.22-fold higher risk of all-cause mortality compared to allopurinol (hazard ratio HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, roughly 22% higher) and a 1.34-fold higher risk of cardiovascular mortality (HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.03–1.73, roughly 34% higher). The European FAST trial reached a different conclusion: in 6,128 patients aged 60 and older with cardiovascular risk factors, febuxostat was not associated with an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events compared to allopurinol (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.70–1.03), and all-cause deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group versus 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For patients taking uricosuric agents (drugs that promote uric acid excretion via the kidneys), the ACR conditionally recommends against urinary alkalinization due to a lack of evidence of benefit (very low certainty evidence), while advising patients to drink plenty of fluids. Uricosurics should not be used in patients with known nephrolithiasis or stage ≥3 CKD. The same guideline notes that the HLA-B*5801 allele is most common in individuals of Han Chinese, Korean, and Thai descent (7.4%), followed by African Americans (3.8%), and White and Hispanic individuals (0.7% each). When taking allopurinol, Asian and African American patients have a 3-fold higher risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome compared to White patients. The guideline conditionally recommends HLA-B*5801 testing prior to initiating allopurinol in these populations, based on very low certainty evidence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. ### 10. If you have had gout, avoid gorging on meat and seafood at barbecues or gatherings, and cut back on alcohol

- Cost: Free. Order fewer meat skewers and seafood dishes during barbecues or group dinners, and drink less alcohol. The hard part is self-control at the table.
- In plain terms: For anyone who has had gout, the more purines you eat in the two days prior, the more likely a flare-up becomes—those in the highest intake tier face the greatest risk, even while taking uric-acid-lowering medication. Drinking two to four drinks the day before also triggers flares more easily, whether it is beer, spirits, or wine.
- Benefit: An online study tracked 633 gout patients for 1 year, comparing the two days before each person's flare-up with two flare-free days for the same person (a case-crossover study). Purines are food components that turn into uric acid in the body. When total purine intake over two days was divided into five tiers from lowest to highest, higher intake was tied to higher flare risk. Compared with the lowest tier, the odds ratios (OR) for a flare across the next four tiers were 1.17, 1.38, 2.21, and 4.76 (trend P < 0.001). Counting only animal-derived purines, the odds ratios were 1.42, 1.34, 1.77, and 2.41. Counting only plant-derived purines, they were 1.12, 0.99, 1.32, and 1.39. Another study by the same team examined alcohol consumed in the 24 hours prior to a flare among 724 gout patients. Compared with no alcohol, having >1 to 2 drinks had an odds ratio of 1.36 (95% CI 1.00–1.88), and >2 to 4 drinks had an odds ratio of 1.51 (1.09–2.09). Wine, beer, and spirits were each independently linked to flare-ups. In contrast, those consuming the most dairy products had an approximately 44% lower risk (0.56, 0.42–0.74). Purine-rich vegetables and total protein intake showed no clear association. Within the same cohort, each additional 12-ounce (about 355 mL) bottle of beer per day was linked to an approximately 49% higher risk of gout (relative risk 1.49, 1.32–1.70); one drink of spirits per day was linked to an approximately 15% higher risk (1.15, 1.04–1.28); wine showed no statistically significant difference (1.04, 0.88–1.22).

### 14. Join a 3-year guided exercise program after surgery and chemotherapy for colon cancer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Walking and similar activities cost nothing. Getting a fitness assessment and an exercise prescription from a hospital rehabilitation clinic or sports medicine department requires registration and testing fees; hiring a personal trainer costs extra. It requires several extra hours of exercise per week, sustained over three years. Those who exercised experienced slightly more muscle and joint injuries.
- In plain terms: For people who have completed surgery and chemotherapy for colon cancer, participating in a 3-year guided exercise program lowered the risk of cancer recurrence, developing a new cancer, or death by about 28%. The risk of dying was about 37% lower. After 8 years, roughly 90.3% of those in the exercise group were still alive, compared with 83.2% of those who received educational materials alone. Muscle and joint injuries were somewhat more common among those who exercised.
- Benefit: A randomized trial conducted across 55 centers (CHALLENGE) enrolled 889 patients with colon cancer. All participants had completed surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, had high-risk stage II or stage III disease, and had no metastases at enrollment. They enrolled 2 to 6 months after completing chemotherapy, and engaged in less than 150 minutes of physical activity per week before enrollment. 445 participants were assigned to a 3-year exercise program, and 444 received only general health education materials. During the first 6 months, participants met with a physical activity consultant every two weeks to discuss adherence strategies and completed one supervised exercise session. Between months 6 and 12, sessions transitioned to in-person visits or telephone calls, followed by minimal support through year 3. The target was to increase physical activity by an additional 10 metabolic equivalent task hours (MET-hours, a unit measuring exercise volume) per week above baseline, with any moderate-to-vigorous physical activity counting toward the goal; the protocol anticipated that most participants would choose walking. At 1 year, recreational physical activity increased by 15.6 MET-hours per week in the exercise group versus 5.1 in the control group. Over a median follow-up of 7.9 years, the risk of recurrence, new primary cancer, or death was about 28% lower in the exercise group than in the control group (hazard ratio HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55–0.94). At 5 years, the proportion free from these events was 80.3% versus 73.9%. The risk of death was about 37% lower (hazard ratio HR 0.63, 0.43–0.94). At 8 years, overall survival was 90.3% versus 83.2%. Musculoskeletal adverse events occurred in 18.5% versus 11.5%.
