# 16. How to live after a chronic disease diagnosis

Scope: overall mortality and money. After a chronic diagnosis you often still have decades ahead; this section is about how to manage those decades.
### 1. Take your medication as prescribed through the full course; do not stop because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost; it saves money on treating complications later. Daily pills take only a few minutes; the hard part is not missing a single day.
- In plain terms: People who take medication exactly as their doctor says have roughly half the chance of dying in the same period as people who take it on and off. Treat that figure with caution. Even people who stick to placebo pills die less often, which shows part of the benefit comes from “people who can keep taking meds tend to take better care of their health overall.” Stopping medication still does clear harm, especially blood pressure drugs, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: Pooled from 21 studies and 46,847 people (meta-analysis): compared with intermittent use, full adherence was associated with about 44% lower odds of dying in the same period. Raw odds ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (confidence interval). For treatments with proven efficacy only, about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49–0.62)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes: Controversial. This is one of the numbers in this book that needs the most discounting. In the same meta-analysis, people who fully adhered to placebo also had lower mortality (0.56, 0.43–0.74). That suggests a large share of the benefit comes from “people who can keep taking meds tend to take better care of their health overall.” The author calls this mixed-in factor the healthy adherer effect. Harm from stopping medication is nonetheless clear, especially for blood pressure drugs, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs

### 2. First complete outpatient chronic special-disease certification (门诊慢特病认定), then cross-region filing (异地就医备案)—hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo/radiotherapy, dialysis, and anti-rejection therapy can then be settled directly across regions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. Before travel, complete cross-region medical care filing once on the basic medical insurance (医保) app on your phone.
- In plain terms: For hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo/radiotherapy for malignant tumors, uremia dialysis, and anti-rejection therapy after organ transplant, outpatient care in another region can go straight through yibao (医保) without paying upfront and claiming back in your home insurance region. You must first get outpatient chronic special-disease certification where you are insured, then complete cross-region filing. Both steps are required for it to work.
- Benefit: National Healthcare Security Administration (国家医保局) policy documents state this step has largely been reached. The wording is: “Cross-region direct settlement of outpatient expenses for five chronic special-disease categories—hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo/radiotherapy for malignant tumors, uremia dialysis, and post-transplant anti-rejection therapy—is now largely accessible at county level.” “Accessible at county level” (县域可及) means it can be handled at the county tier. The documents also add five more disease categories on top of these five
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: Both steps are required: first outpatient chronic special-disease certification in your home insurance region, then cross-region filing. How far the five newly added categories have rolled out varies by locality; follow what your local healthcare security authority publishes

### 3. Follow the follow-up intervals your doctor sets, and record each measurement in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each test costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. A hospital visit plus waiting for results takes some time. The hard part is going again and again on schedule without putting it off.
- In plain terms: Chronic disease is about change over time, not one reading. Measuring blood pressure at home alone does not lower it by much. Bring readings to your doctor and let treatment be adjusted from them, and pressure falls more. When you change hospitals, test results marked for mutual recognition should not be repeated.
- Benefit: Chronic conditions require seeing how values shift over time; a single point is not enough. For hypertension, one meta-analysis pooled individual data from 15 randomized trials and 7,138 people. Compared with usual care, home self-measurement at 12 months meant mean change in office systolic blood pressure −3.2 mmHg (95% CI −4.9 to −1.6). Self-measurement alone without other support showed no clear effect: −1.0 mmHg (−3.3 to 1.2, difference may be chance). The most supported package lowered by 6.1 mmHg (3.2 to 9.0). Supported care means a doctor, pharmacist, or patient adjusting meds from readings, plus health education and lifestyle guidance. The author’s conclusion: measuring alone does not reliably lower pressure; pairing readings with dose adjustment does. These endpoints are blood pressure, not death. When changing hospitals, a joint measure from the National Health Commission «国家卫健委» and three other agencies states: if the patient’s existing test results meet mutual-recognition rules and satisfy clinical need, the hospital and clinician must not repeat tests. Recognized reports carry an “HR” mark, e.g. (全国HR) (national HR)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Tucker KL, Sheppard JP, Stevens R, et al. (2017). Self-monitoring of blood pressure in hypertension: A systematic review and individual patient data meta-analysis. PLoS Medicine, 14(9), e1002389. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002389>；国家卫生健康委、国家医保局、国家中医药局、中央军委后勤保障部卫生局 (2022). 关于印发医疗机构检查检验结果互认管理办法的通知（国卫医发〔2022〕6 号），第十三条、第十七条、第十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-02/19/content_5674575.htm>
- Notes: B only because direct evidence is for hypertension alone and stops at blood pressure, not mortality. No study was found on health outcomes from “keeping readings in one notebook” itself. Each time record at least four items: date, value, drug name, and dose. Photos of lab slips on your phone are fine, but keep everything in one place. Mutual recognition has exceptions: changed condition, rapidly changing results, before surgery or transfusion, and emergency rescue—hospitals may repeat tests. Copying and keeping medical records and imaging: see Section 24, item 5 (keep your own copy of records, reports, and imaging)

### 4. Do not stop standard treatment to try folk remedies or health supplements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost; you also save what you would spend on supplements. No extra time. The hard part is holding firm when others keep pushing you to try something.
- In plain terms: For cancers that are otherwise treatable, using only alternative therapy and skipping standard surgery, chemo, radiotherapy, and hormone treatment carries about 2.5 times the death risk of standard care. People who add complementary therapy while on standard treatment more often refuse chemo or radiotherapy mid-course, and the extra deaths show up there. Some herbs weaken anti-rejection or anticoagulant drugs; ask your treating doctor before taking anything.
- Benefit: Two US cancer-registry studies looked at non-metastatic, potentially curable breast, prostate, lung, and colorectal cancer. The first compared patients who used only alternative therapy and did none of surgery, chemo/radiotherapy, or endocrine treatment with matched standard-care patients: death risk 2.5-fold (HR 2.50, 95% CI 1.88–3.27, about 1.5 times higher). Breast cancer 5.68-fold (3.22–10.04), lung 2.17-fold (1.42–3.32), colorectal 4.57-fold (1.66–12.61). The second looked at standard treatment plus complementary therapy: refusal rates were surgery 7.0% vs 0.1%, chemo 34.1% vs 3.2%, radiotherapy 53.0% vs 2.3%, endocrine therapy 33.7% vs 2.8%. Five-year survival 82.2% vs 86.6%; death risk 2.08-fold (HR 2.08, 1.50–2.90, about double). After accounting for delay and refusal, complementary therapy alone was not linked to death (HR 1.39, 0.83–2.33). The author’s conclusion: extra deaths came from refusing standard treatment. China’s State Administration for Market Regulation requires health-food labels to state: “Health food is not medicine and cannot replace drugs to treat disease.” The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) states that St John’s wort (贯叶连翘) weakens several drugs, including cyclosporine for transplant rejection and the anticoagulant warfarin
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Use of Alternative Medicine for Cancer and Its Impact on Survival. Journal of the National Cancer Institute, 110(1), 121-124. <https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djx145>；Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Complementary Medicine, Refusal of Conventional Cancer Therapy, and Survival Among Patients With Curable Cancers. JAMA Oncology, 4(10), 1375-1381. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.2487>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2019). 市场监管总局就《保健食品标注警示用语指南》和《保健食品原料目录与保健功能目录管理办法》有关情况举办专题新闻发布会. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>；National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (2025). St. John's Wort. <https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort>
- Notes: B only. Both studies were cancer-only, registry follow-up without randomized groups; people who chose alternative therapy may differ in other ways. Stopping meds in hypertension or diabetes: see item 1 (full adherence). Tell your treating doctor before any supplement. Whether to pay for supplements: Section 6, item 10 (supplements, tonic formulas, tonics)

### 5. When a chronic condition is stable, a community health center can dispense up to 12 weeks of medication at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No cost. At the community health service center (社区卫生服务中心), ask in one sentence whether long-term prescriptions are available.
- In plain terms: If the illness is stable and the drug regimen unchanged, the community center can supply enough for 12 weeks in one visit. That saves half a day each month queuing at a large hospital for a refill. Within the same pooled insurance region (统筹地区), inpatient reimbursement at primary-level facilities is usually about ten percentage points higher than at the next tier up.
- Benefit: Avoids monthly trips to a large hospital just to refill. In the same pooled insurance region, inpatient reimbursement at primary-level facilities is in principle about ten percentage points higher than at the next tier
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；同文「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」，并要求上级医院在基层开设高血压、糖尿病、慢性阻塞性肺疾病等常见病慢性病门诊；同文「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」，「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: “Eligible” is judged by the doctor—usually clear diagnosis and stable regimen. Same drugs; what you save is half a day and registration fees each month. If the community site lacks your drug, ask about shortage registration and delivery. The same policy also asks tertiary hospitals to gradually cut routine outpatient for such chronic diseases and shift those specialists’ general clinics to community sites. Using big hospitals mainly as refill points will get harder to book over time.

### 6. Before signing a family doctor (家庭医生) contract at community level, ask which services are covered by yibao (医保) and which are out of pocket
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. One extra minute of questions before signing.
- In plain terms: The basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) as required. Personalized service packages are entirely self-paid. When someone sells an “upgrade package,” ask whether it is basic or personalized, whether it is filed with the county health authority, and the yearly price.
- Benefit: Basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) per rules. Personalized packages are fully self-paid and must be filed with the county-level health administration (县级卫生健康部门)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: The value of signing is long-term follow-up for your condition, not how many items are bundled. When offered an “upgrade package,” ask three things: basic or personalized, filed or not, and cost per year.

### 7. After a diabetes diagnosis, get a dilated eye exam once, then on the interval your doctor sets; check feet once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One dilated eye exam costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. Foot exams are usually part of outpatient visits without extra charge. The hard part is remembering when eyes and feet feel fine.
- In plain terms: Diabetes damage to the retina and feet rarely hurts or feels wrong until vision fails or ulcers appear. Early detection still allows treatment. Type 2 diabetes: dilated eye exam at diagnosis; type 1: start five years after onset. After one or two normal exams with blood sugar at target, spacing can widen to every one or two years. Feet: once a year.
- Benefit: Retinal damage from diabetes is diabetic retinopathy; foot damage is diabetic foot. Both are nearly silent until late. Early detection still allows treatment. By the time vision is lost or the foot breaks down, it is late
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care：「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: Type 2: exam at diagnosis; type 1: five years after onset, because type 2 often ran high for years unnoticed. After one or two normal exams with sugar at target, every 1–2 years may suffice. If foot sensation is dull, inspect the sole daily, mirror if needed.

### 8. After kidney stones, drink 2.5–3 liters of water daily and keep salt under 6 g
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost. Make drinking water a habit. Sweaty days need more; the hard part is remembering every day.
- In plain terms: After a first calcium stone, about one in four will get another within five years if nothing is done. Drinking enough daily cuts five-year recurrence to a bit over one in ten—roughly half less. UK guidance suggests 2.5–3 L water daily and salt under 6 g.
- Benefit: A 5-year randomized trial in 199 patients with a first calcium stone and no identified cause (idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis). One group drank at least 2 L water daily; the other had no intervention. Recurrence in 5 years: water group 12/99 (12.1%), control 27/100 (27.0%), P=0.008. Smaller P means the gap is less likely chance. Mean time to recurrence: 38.7±13.2 months vs 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. Baseline 24-hour urine volume in stone patients was clearly lower than in people without stones: men 1,057±238 mL vs 1,401±562 mL (P<0.0001), women 990±230 mL vs 1,239±440 mL (P<0.001). NICE guideline NG118 section 1.8.1 advises adults 2.5–3 L water daily; children and adolescents 1–2 L by age. Fresh lemon juice in water is fine; avoid carbonated drinks; adults keep salt under 6 g daily
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.8.1 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: Recurrence falls by about 55% at no cost—by this book’s ranking, the best value in this section. Track 24-hour urine volume, target 2–2.5 L or more, not just fluid swallowed. Heavy sweaters and hot environments need more. The trial was first-episode idiopathic calcium stones; uric acid, infection, and cystine stones need different plans. After recurrence, stone composition analysis and 24-hour urine are worth doing before adding drugs. Do not cut calcium to prevent stones—Section 6, item 21. Severe flank or abdominal colic needs hospital care, not flushing with water. Painless blood in urine has other workup—Section 1, item 27

### 9. After gout diagnosis, take urate-lowering therapy long term; keep serum urate below 360 µmol/L and maintain it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol (别嘌醇) costs a few to a few dozen CNY per month. At first check serum urate every few weeks to titrate dose; once stable, every few months. For the first 3–6 months add anti-inflammatory prophylaxis. The hard part is continuing when there is no pain.
- In plain terms: Gout needs long-term urate-lowering drugs to hold urate below 360 µmol/L; painkillers alone when flaring are not enough. In one trial, nurses titrated dose from labs: 95% at target at 2 years vs 30% with usual GP care. Do not stop once at target. Diet alone rarely reaches that line; stopping alcohol lowers urate only 1.6 mg/dL.
- Benefit: Randomized trial, 517 adults with a flare in the past 12 months. One group had nurse-led education and treat-to-target urate titration; the other usual GP care. At 2 years, proportion with serum urate <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL): 95% vs 30%. Likelihood of target vs usual care: RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18, P<0.0001. Secondary outcomes at year 2—flare frequency, tophi, quality of life—also favored the nurse-led group. Each extra quality-adjusted life year gained cost £5,066. Follow-up survey: 438 invited, 82% response; both groups self-reported flares in the past 12 months. Target group median 0 (interquartile range 0–0); usual care 1 (0–3), P<0.001. Still on urate-lowering therapy: adjusted RR 1.19 (1.09–1.30) in the target group. 2020 American College of Rheumatology gout guideline strongly recommends urate-lowering therapy for tophi, imaging bone erosion from gout, or ≥2 flares per year: monitor serum urate, titrate to target <6 mg/dL; first-line allopurinol, including CKD stage 3+, start ≤100 mg/day. Give anti-inflammatory prophylaxis at initiation for at least 3–6 months. The same guideline cites case series: after long good control, some stopped drugs yet kept urate <7 mg/dL; only 13% (27/211) had zero flares over 5 years. Diet: limiting or stopping alcohol vs not limiting lowered urate 1.6 mg/dL; one beer raised urate 0.16 mg/dL; healthy, Mediterranean, and DASH patterns had smaller effects. First-line drug choice is disputed. ACR strongly recommends allopurinol for efficacy at adequate dose, tolerability, safety, and cost; conditionally suggests switching febuxostat (非布司他) if cardiovascular disease or new cardiovascular events and alternatives exist—aligned with FDA boxed warning on febuxostat. CARES trial: 6,190 gout patients with cardiovascular disease randomized to febuxostat or allopurinol. All-cause death risk febuxostat vs allopurinol HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47 (about 22% higher); cardiovascular death 1.34 (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). European FAST trial differed: 6,128 patients ≥60 with cardiovascular risk factors; major cardiovascular events on febuxostat not higher than allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths febuxostat 7.2%, allopurinol 8.6%. For uricosurics, ACR conditionally advises against urine alkalinization—no evidence, very low certainty—while stressing more fluids; avoid uricosurics if known nephrolithiasis or CKD stage 3+
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>；FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180>；White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895>；Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0>；中华医学会内分泌学分会 (2020). 中国高尿酸血症与痛风诊疗指南(2019). 中华内分泌代谢杂志, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Notes: Controversy: first-line urate-lowering drug differs by guideline. US favors allopurinol and avoiding febuxostat with cardiovascular disease; FAST did not show extra febuxostat harm. China also sells benzbromarone (苯溴马隆), a uricosuric. Chinese Medical Association (中华医学会) guidelines (2019 and 2024 update) give separate drug and alkalinization advice. Whether to add sodium bicarbonate with benzbromarone: US guideline sees no evidence; follow your doctor; extra water stays mandatory. **Han Chinese (汉族) should consider HLA-B*5801 testing before allopurinol**—a lifelong immune-related genotype; carriers are 7.4% in Han, Korean, and Thai vs 0.7% in white and Hispanic groups; allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome risk is about triple vs whites, can cause severe skin detachment and death. ACR 2020 suggests considering screening in those groups. **Starting urate-lowering can trigger more flares early**—guidelines require 3–6 months of anti-inflammatory prophylaxis. “More pain after starting, so I quit” is the common failure mode; add anti-inflammatory per plan, do not stop urate-lowering. Target <6 mg/dL ≈ 360 µmol/L; Chinese labs often report µmol/L. Asymptomatic hyperuricemia without flares is different—Section 6, item 19. Sugary drinks and alcohol—Section 2, items 7 and 19 (avoid sugary drinks; drink little or no alcohol)
