# 16. How to live after a chronic disease diagnosis

Scope: overall mortality and money. After a chronic diagnosis you often still have decades ahead; this section is about how to manage those decades.
### 1. Take the medicine as prescribed for the full course; do not stop because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost; it saves the money that would later go to treating complications. Taking the medicine each day takes only a few minutes. The hard part is not missing a day.
- In plain terms: People who take the medicine for the full course, as the doctor said, have about half the chance of dying over the same period as people who take it on and off. Discount this figure. People who take even a dummy medicine for the full course also die less often, which shows that part of the benefit comes from "people who can keep taking medicine were already paying more attention to their health."
- Benefit: Pooled from 21 studies and 46847 people (meta-analysis): compared with people who take medicine on and off, people who take it as prescribed have about a 44% lower chance of dying over the same period. The original value is an odds ratio of 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (confidence interval). Looking only at treatments that truly work, about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49–0.62)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes: Disputed. This is the figure in this book that most needs discounting. In the same pooled analysis, people who took even placebo (a dummy medicine) for the full course also had lower mortality (0.56, 0.43–0.74). That shows a large part of the benefit comes from "people who can keep taking medicine were already paying more attention to their health." The author calls this mixed-in factor the healthy adherer effect

### 2. First get outpatient chronic special-disease certification, then file for care away from home, and hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy, dialysis, and anti-rejection treatment can be settled directly in another region
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. Before you set out, file once for care away from home on the yibao (basic medical insurance) app. It can be done on a phone.
- In plain terms: Hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy for a malignant tumor, dialysis for uremia, and anti-rejection treatment after an organ transplant: for these, outpatient care away from home can in most cases be paid directly by insurance. You do not pay first and then claim it back where you are insured. You must first get outpatient chronic special-disease certification where you are insured, then file for care away from home. It works only if both are done.
- Benefit: A document from the National Healthcare Security Administration (国家医保局) says this has already been reached. The original wording is: "Cross-province direct settlement of outpatient costs for 5 outpatient chronic special diseases, including hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemo or radiotherapy for malignant tumors, dialysis for uremia, and anti-rejection treatment after organ transplant, is basically accessible at county level." "Accessible at county level" (县域可及) means it can be handled at county level. On top of these 5, the document adds another 5
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: Both have to be done: first get outpatient chronic special-disease certification where you are insured, then file for care away from home. How far the 5 newly added diseases have opened differs by place; follow what the local insurance authority publishes. If the hospital does not stock the negotiated drug you need, see section 24, item 13 (dual-channel designated pharmacies).

### 3. Follow the follow-up intervals your doctor sets, and record each measurement in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each test costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. A hospital visit plus waiting for results takes some time. The hard part is going again and again on schedule without putting it off.
- In plain terms: Chronic disease is about change over a stretch of time, not a single reading. Measuring blood pressure at home alone does not show the pressure coming down because of that. Bring the readings to the doctor and let the doctor adjust the drugs from them, and the pressure falls more. When you change hospitals, test results marked for mutual recognition are ones the hospital generally should not make you repeat.
- Benefit: Chronic conditions require seeing how values shift over time; a single point is not enough. For hypertension, one meta-analysis pooled individual data from 15 randomized trials and 7,138 people. Compared with usual care, home self-measurement at 12 months meant mean change in office systolic blood pressure −3.2 mmHg (95% CI −4.9 to −1.6). Self-measurement alone without other support showed no clear effect: −1.0 mmHg (−3.3 to 1.2, difference may be chance). The most supported package lowered by 6.1 mmHg (3.2 to 9.0). Supported care means a doctor, pharmacist, or patient adjusting meds from readings, plus health education and lifestyle guidance. The author’s conclusion: measuring alone does not reliably lower pressure; pairing readings with dose adjustment does. These endpoints are blood pressure, not death. When changing hospitals, a joint measure from the National Health Commission «国家卫健委» and three other agencies states: if the patient’s existing test results meet mutual-recognition rules and satisfy clinical need, the hospital and clinician must not repeat tests. Recognized reports carry an “HR” mark, e.g. (全国HR) (national HR)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Tucker KL, Sheppard JP, Stevens R, et al. (2017). Self-monitoring of blood pressure in hypertension: A systematic review and individual patient data meta-analysis. PLoS Medicine, 14(9), e1002389. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002389>；国家卫生健康委、国家医保局、国家中医药局、中央军委后勤保障部卫生局 (2022). 关于印发医疗机构检查检验结果互认管理办法的通知（国卫医发〔2022〕6 号），第十三条、第十七条、第十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-02/19/content_5674575.htm>
- Notes: B only because direct evidence is for hypertension alone and stops at blood pressure, not mortality. No study was found on health outcomes from “keeping readings in one notebook” itself. Each time record at least four items: date, value, drug name, and dose. Photos of lab slips on your phone are fine, but keep everything in one place. Mutual recognition has exceptions: changed condition, rapidly changing results, before surgery or transfusion, and emergency rescue—hospitals may repeat tests. Copying and keeping medical records and imaging: see Section 24, item 5 (keep your own copy of records, reports, and imaging)

### 4. Do not stop standard treatment to try folk remedies or health supplements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost; you also save what you would spend on supplements. No extra time. The hard part is holding firm when others keep pushing you to try something.
- In plain terms: For a cancer that could otherwise be treated, people who use only alternative therapy and skip standard treatment have 2.5 times the risk of death of people on standard treatment. People who use complementary therapy while also being treated more often refuse surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, and the extra deaths come from that. Some herbs also weaken anti-rejection drugs and anticoagulants; ask the treating doctor before taking any.
- Benefit: Two US cancer-registry studies looked at non-metastatic, potentially curable breast, prostate, lung, and colorectal cancer. The first compared patients who used only alternative therapy and did none of surgery, chemo/radiotherapy, or endocrine treatment with matched standard-care patients: death risk 2.5-fold (HR 2.50, 95% CI 1.88–3.27, about 1.5 times higher). Breast cancer 5.68-fold (3.22–10.04), lung 2.17-fold (1.42–3.32), colorectal 4.57-fold (1.66–12.61). The second looked at standard treatment plus complementary therapy: refusal rates were surgery 7.0% vs 0.1%, chemo 34.1% vs 3.2%, radiotherapy 53.0% vs 2.3%, endocrine therapy 33.7% vs 2.8%. Five-year survival 82.2% vs 86.6%; death risk 2.08-fold (HR 2.08, 1.50–2.90, about double). After accounting for delay and refusal, complementary therapy alone was not linked to death (HR 1.39, 0.83–2.33). The author’s conclusion: extra deaths came from refusing standard treatment. China’s State Administration for Market Regulation requires health-food labels to state: “Health food is not medicine and cannot replace drugs to treat disease.” The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) states that St John’s wort (贯叶连翘) weakens several drugs, including cyclosporine for transplant rejection and the anticoagulant warfarin
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Use of Alternative Medicine for Cancer and Its Impact on Survival. Journal of the National Cancer Institute, 110(1), 121-124. <https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djx145>；Johnson SB, Park HS, Gross CP, Yu JB (2018). Complementary Medicine, Refusal of Conventional Cancer Therapy, and Survival Among Patients With Curable Cancers. JAMA Oncology, 4(10), 1375-1381. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.2487>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2019). 市场监管总局就《保健食品标注警示用语指南》和《保健食品原料目录与保健功能目录管理办法》有关情况举办专题新闻发布会. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>；National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (2025). St. John's Wort. <https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/st-johns-wort>
- Notes: B only. Both studies were cancer-only, registry follow-up without randomized groups; people who chose alternative therapy may differ in other ways. Stopping meds in hypertension or diabetes: see item 1 (full adherence). Tell your treating doctor before any supplement. Whether to pay for supplements: Section 6, item 10 (supplements, tonic formulas, tonics)

### 5. For a stable chronic disease, a community hospital can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medicine at a single visit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No cost. At the community health service center (社区卫生服务中心), ask whether a long-term prescription is possible. It is a single question.
- In plain terms: If the illness is stable and the drugs have not been changed, a community health service center can prescribe at most 12 weeks of medicine at a single visit. That saves the half-day each month spent going to a large hospital, queuing, and registering. In the same place where you are insured, the inpatient reimbursement rate at a primary-level hospital is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than at the next tier up.
- Benefit: You do not have to go back and forth to a large hospital every month and queue for a refill. In the same insured place (also called a pooled region, 统筹地区), the inpatient reimbursement rate at a primary-level hospital is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than at the next tier up
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；同文「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」，并要求上级医院在基层开设高血压、糖尿病、慢性阻塞性肺疾病等常见病慢性病门诊；同文「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」，「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: "Meets the conditions" is judged by the doctor, usually someone whose diagnosis is already clear and whose drug plan is stable. The drug stays the same drug. What you save is the half-day trip each month and the registration fee. If the primary-level site does not have the drug you need, ask whether they have a shortage register and delivery. The same document also requires tertiary hospitals to gradually cut ordinary outpatient clinics for this kind of chronic disease, and to extend the specialist team's ordinary clinics into the community. Using a large hospital as a refill point will get harder to book. If the community does not have your drug, or a specialist has to watch the dose changes, you can get the refill from an online follow-up at the hospital that made the diagnosis; see section 24, item 14 (online follow-up at an internet hospital).

### 6. Before signing a family doctor (家庭医生) contract at community level, ask which services are covered by yibao (医保) and which are out of pocket
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. One extra minute of questions before signing.
- In plain terms: The basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) as required. Personalized service packages are entirely self-paid. When someone sells an “upgrade package,” ask whether it is basic or personalized, whether it is filed with the county health authority, and the yearly price.
- Benefit: Basic service package is paid by yibao (医保) per rules. Personalized packages are fully self-paid and must be filed with the county-level health administration (县级卫生健康部门)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: The value of signing is long-term follow-up for your condition, not how many items are bundled. When offered an “upgrade package,” ask three things: basic or personalized, filed or not, and cost per year.

### 7. Once diabetes is diagnosed, have the back of the eye checked once, then on the interval the doctor sets; check the feet once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A dilated fundus exam costs from a few dozen to a few hundred CNY. A foot exam is usually included in the outpatient visit and is not charged separately. The hard part is remembering to go when the eyes and the feet feel fine.
- In plain terms: When diabetes has damaged the back of the eye or the feet, there is almost no feeling until vision is gone or the foot starts to break down. Caught early, it can still be treated. Have a dilated fundus exam at the diagnosis of type 2 diabetes; for type 1, start 5 years after onset. If early checks were normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can widen to once a year or every other year. Check the feet once a year.
- Benefit: Damage from diabetes to the back of the eye is called retinopathy; damage to the foot is called diabetic foot. Both are almost without feeling before they go wrong. Caught early, they can still be treated. Once vision is gone or the foot has broken down, it is late
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care：「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: Type 2 diabetes needs the exam at diagnosis; type 1 starts 5 years after onset. The difference is that type 2 has often been quietly high for years. If early checks were normal and blood sugar is at target, the interval can widen to once every 1 to 2 years. If sensation in the feet has dulled, look at the sole yourself every day; a mirror is fine. The kidneys should also be checked every year; see item 13 (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio).

### 8. After a kidney stone, drink 2.5–3 liters of water a day and cut salt to within 6 grams
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost. Make drinking whenever you can into a habit. On days you sweat a lot, drink still more. The hard part is remembering every day.
- In plain terms: Among people who grew a first calcium stone and no cause was found, about a quarter grow another within 5 years if they do nothing. Among people who drink enough water every day, a bit over a tenth have a recurrence within 5 years, fewer by more than half. The British guideline gives 2.5 to 3 liters of water a day, and salt no more than 6 grams.
- Benefit: A 5-year randomized controlled trial in 199 patients with a first calcium stone and no cause found (also called idiopathic calcium stones). The water group drank at least 2 liters of water a day; the other group had no intervention. Recurrence over 5 years: 12/99 (12.1%) in the group that drank more water, 27/100 (27.0%) in the group with no intervention, P=0.008. A smaller P value means the difference between the groups is less likely to be chance. Mean time until recurrence: 38.7±13.2 months versus 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. The 24-hour urine volume measured at the start was clearly lower in stone patients than in people without stones: men 1057±238 mL versus 1401±562 mL (P<0.0001), women 990±230 mL versus 1239±440 mL (P<0.001). Clause 1.8.1 of NICE guideline NG118 recommends that adults drink 2.5 to 3 liters of water a day, and that children and adolescents drink 1 to 2 liters by age. Add a little fresh lemon juice to the water, do not drink carbonated drinks, and keep salt for adults to no more than 6 grams a day. Clause 1.7.1 of the same NICE guideline recommends considering a stone composition analysis for adults who have had a kidney stone or a ureteral stone. For people with recurrence whose stone is more than half calcium oxalate, clause 1.8.3 recommends considering potassium citrate. NICE found no evidence on 24-hour urine testing and gives no recommendation. The stone guideline of the European Association of Urology (EAU) is written differently: every patient with a stone should have a reliable stone composition analysis. People with recurrence count as high risk and should give 24-hour urine twice in a row for a dedicated metabolic workup. Its general prevention target is 2.5 to 3 liters of water a day, and a daily urine volume of 2 to 2.5 liters
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.7.1、1.8.1、1.8.3 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>；European Association of Urology (2026). EAU Guidelines on Urolithiasis, Chapter 4 Metabolic Evaluation and Recurrence Prevention（第 4.1、4.1.2 节，Table 4.5）. <https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/metabolic-evaluation-and-recurrence-prevention>（高危结石患者名单见同一指南 Table 3.3：<https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urolithiasis/chapter/guidelines>）
- Notes: Recurrence was about 55% lower, and it costs nothing, so by this book's way of scoring value for money this is the highest-value item in the section. What to watch is 24-hour urine volume. The European guideline's target is 2 to 2.5 liters a day, not how much you drink. People who sweat a lot, and people who work in high heat, have to drink more. That trial enrolled patients with a first episode of idiopathic calcium stones. There is more than a single stone type: uric acid stones, infection stones, and cystine stones each have their own approach. Both the British and the European guidelines recommend a stone composition analysis. For people who have recurred, the European guideline also requires a 24-hour urine test before deciding whether to add a drug; the British guideline finds the evidence for that test insufficient. Do not cut out calcium to prevent stones; see section 6, item 21. Severe colic in the flank or abdomen during an attack means go to a hospital, not try to flush the stone out by drinking more. Painless blood in the urine has other things that need checking; see section 1, item 27

### 9. After a gout diagnosis, if tophi have grown, imaging shows bone eaten out, or flares come 2 or more times a year, take a urate-lowering drug long term and hold serum urate under 360 µmol/L
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol (别嘌醇) costs a few CNY to a few dozen CNY a month. At the start, check serum urate every few weeks to adjust the dose; once it is stable, every few months. For the first 3 to 6 months also take a drug that prevents flares. The hard part is keeping on with it when nothing hurts.
- In plain terms: If tophi have grown, bone has been eaten out, or flares come 2 or more times a year, take a urate-lowering drug long term and hold urate under 360 µmol/L. If flares are infrequent, whether to take it is something to settle with the doctor. Among patients whose dose a nurse adjusted from the lab results, 95% were at target after 2 years, and only 30% with usual care. After the target is reached the drug still cannot be stopped. Cutting foods lowers it only so much.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial of 517 adults who had flared in the past 12 months. The nurse-led group had the disease explained, then had urate adjusted to target from the lab results. The other group had usual care from a general practitioner. At 2 years, the share with serum urate <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) was 95% versus 30%. The chance of reaching target was 3.18 times that of the usual-care group (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18, this interval is the confidence range of the figure), P<0.0001. In year 2, flare frequency, tophi, and quality of life, these secondary measures, were also better than in the usual-care group. Each extra year of life adjusted for quality of life (a quality-adjusted life year) cost £5066. The same trial also ran a follow-up: questionnaires went to 438 people and 82% came back. Both groups reported their own flare counts over the past 12 months. The median in the target group was 0 (the middle half of people fell between 0 and 0). Usual care was 1 (0–3), P<0.001. The range in those parentheses is called the interquartile range. The share still taking a urate-lowering drug was 1.19 times the usual-care group (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The 2020 American College of Rheumatology gout guideline strongly recommends starting urate-lowering treatment for people with subcutaneous tophi, people whose imaging shows gout has eaten a defect into the bone, or people with ≥2 flares a year. For more than a single flare but fewer than 2 a year, the guideline conditionally suggests the drug; a conditional recommendation is settled between doctor and patient. For a first flare, it conditionally suggests not starting at once. The exception is chronic kidney disease of stage 3 or above, serum urate >9 mg/dL, or a past urinary stone; those are conditionally suggested to treat. The method is to keep watching serum urate and adjust the dose from the results until target, with a target <6 mg/dL. Allopurinol is the first choice, including for chronic kidney disease of stage 3 or above, at a starting dose ≤100 mg/day. At the start, also give an anti-inflammatory preventive drug for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline also cites a set of case observations. These patients stopped the drug after long good control, and after stopping, serum urate stayed <7 mg/dL. Among them, only 13% (27/211) had no flare at all over 5 years of follow-up. On diet, people who limited alcohol or stopped it had serum urate 1.6 mg/dL lower than people who did not limit it, and a serving of beer raised serum urate by 0.16 mg/dL. A healthy diet, a Mediterranean diet, and the DASH diet had smaller effects. Which urate-lowering drug to choose first is disputed. The American College of Rheumatology guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as the first choice, because at a high enough dose it works, is tolerated, is safe, and is cheap. It also conditionally suggests that a person on febuxostat (非布司他) who has a history of cardiovascular disease, or a new cardiovascular event, switch if another drug is available. That recommendation matches the boxed warning the FDA put on febuxostat. It draws on the CARES trial: 6190 gout patients with cardiovascular disease were randomized to febuxostat or allopurinol. All-cause death risk in the febuxostat group was 1.22 times the allopurinol group (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher). Cardiovascular death was 1.34 times (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). The European FAST trial came to a different conclusion. It enrolled 6128 patients aged over 60 with cardiovascular risk factors; major cardiovascular events on febuxostat were not higher than on allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group and 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For people taking a drug that promotes uric acid excretion through the kidney, the American College of Rheumatology conditionally suggests not alkalinizing the urine, because there is no evidence that it works and the certainty of the evidence is very low. It also requires telling those people to drink more water. People with known kidney stones, or with chronic kidney disease of stage 3 or above, should not use this class of drug. The same guideline notes that the HLA-B\*5801 genotype is most common in people of Han, Korean, and Thai ancestry, at 7.4%, in African Americans at 3.8%, and in white people and in Hispanic people at 0.7% each. For Asian people and African Americans taking allopurinol, the risk of allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome is 3 times the risk in white people. The guideline conditionally suggests testing these groups for HLA-B\*5801 before allopurinol; the certainty of the evidence is very low
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>；FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. ### 10. If you have had gout, do not eat a large amount of meat and seafood at a barbecue or a group meal, and drink less alcohol

- Cost: No cost. At a barbecue or a group meal, order fewer meat skewers and less seafood, and drink less. The hard part is holding yourself at the table.
- In plain terms: For people who have had gout, when purine intake over the pair of days before was at its highest, the chance of a flare was nearly 5 times the chance when intake was lowest, and the same held while taking a urate-lowering drug. After 2 to 4 drinks the day before, the chance of a flare was about half higher; beer, spirits, and wine all count.
- Benefit: A study followed 633 gout patients online for 1 year, comparing a pair of days before each person's flare with a pair of their own days when they did not flare (a case-crossover study). Purine is a component of food that the body turns into uric acid. Splitting the purine total over that pair of days from low to high into bands, and comparing with the lowest band, the chance of a flare in the higher bands was 1.17, 1.38, 2.21, and 4.76 times (OR, trend P<0.001). Counting only purine from animal sources, the figures were 1.42, 1.34, 1.77, and 2.41 times. Counting only purine from plant sources, they were 1.12, 0.99, 1.32, and 1.39 times. Split by sex, by whether the person drank, and by whether they took a diuretic, allopurinol, an anti-inflammatory pain drug, or colchicine, the result was the same. Another study by the same researchers looked at alcohol in the 24 hours before a flare in 724 gout patients. Compared with not drinking, more than 1 drink up to 2 drinks was 1.36 times (95% CI 1.00–1.88, confidence interval), and more than 2 drinks up to 4 drinks was 1.51 times (1.09–2.09). Wine, beer, and spirits were each linked to flares. For people who have not had gout, another follow-up study applies: 47150 men in the United States followed for 12 years, recorded only, not assigned to groups. The top fifth by meat intake had about a 41% higher risk of gout than the bottom fifth (RR 1.41, 1.07–1.86). Seafood was about 51% higher (1.51, 1.17–1.95). The top fifth by dairy intake was instead about 44% lower (0.56, 0.42–0.74). Vegetables high in purine, and total protein, showed no link. In the same men, an extra 12-ounce bottle of beer a day (about 355 ml) raised risk by about 49% (1.49, 1.32–1.70). A daily drink of spirits raised it by about 15% (1.15, 1.04–1.28). Wine showed no statistical difference (1.04, 0.88–1.22)

- Notes: Written mainly for people who have already had a flare. For people who have not had gout, what you eat moves uric acid less than most people think. So when someone nearby gets gout after barbecue and beer, diet is a trigger and constitution accounts for more. The studies looked at how much meat, seafood, and alcohol was eaten, not at whether it was grilled; cold seafood with beer counts the same. All of the studies only followed and recorded, with no assignment to groups; what was eaten at the time of a flare was reported by the patients online, the people studied were American, and no Chinese data were found. The dairy figure is also only a follow-up record; no trial has shown that drinking more milk prevents gout. Eating less cannot replace a urate-lowering drug, and cutting foods lowers uric acid only so much; see item 9 (once gout is diagnosed, take a urate-lowering drug long term). ### 11. If hepatitis B surface antigen is positive, have a liver ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein every half year, let the doctor decide whether to take an antiviral, and do not stop it on your own once you start

- Cost: Go to hepatology or infectious disease every half year, have blood drawn, and have an ultrasound; the fee differs by place. If an antiviral is needed, the National Healthcare Security Administration (国家医保局) says that after pooled procurement the yearly cost of entecavir fell to a bit over 100 CNY. The hard part is going on time when you feel nothing, and staying on the drug.
- In plain terms: People who carry hepatitis B virus, checked every half year with ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein, had nearly 40% fewer deaths from liver cancer in a Shanghai trial. The more virus in the blood, the more liver cancer later. People whose liver was already badly fibrotic or cirrhotic, once on an antiviral, had about half as much liver cancer. Most of these drugs have to be taken long term; if you stop on your own the virus readily rebounds.
- Benefit: A randomized trial in Shanghai of 18816 people aged 35 to 59, all infected with hepatitis B virus or with a history of chronic hepatitis. The screening group was invited every 6 months for alpha-fetoprotein and ultrasound; the control group was not screened. Alpha-fetoprotein is a blood marker that often rises when liver cancer is present. Deaths from liver cancer were 32 in the screening group and 54 in the control group. Liver-cancer mortality was 83.2 versus 131.5 per 100000 people. The screening group was 0.63 times the control group (95% CI 0.41–0.98, confidence interval), about 37% lower. Of the liver cancers found in the screening group, 46.5% were surgically removed, against 7.5% in the control group. A follow-up study in Taiwan of 3653 people aged 30 to 65 who were surface-antigen positive, followed for a mean of 11.4 years, recorded only, not assigned to groups. The more virus in the blood at entry, the more liver cancer later. Below 300 copies per milliliter, there were 108 liver cancers per 100000 person-years. Above 1000000 copies per milliliter, there were 1152. The cumulative share who developed liver cancer was 1.3% and 14.9%. After accounting for sex, age, smoking, alcohol, aminotransferases, and cirrhosis, more virus still meant more liver cancer. An Asian randomized trial of 651 people with chronic hepatitis B who already had severe liver fibrosis or cirrhosis. A lamivudine group and a placebo group were followed for a median of 32.4 months. Liver cancer was 3.9% versus 7.4%, about half the risk (HR 0.49, P=0.047). Worsening of disease was 7.8% versus 17.7% (HR 0.45, about 55% lower). The 2022 guideline of the Chinese Medical Association (中华医学会) states that people with chronic hepatitis B virus infection should have an abdominal ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein every 6 months to screen for liver cancer. People at high risk should have them at least every 3 to 6 months. Carriers who do not yet need drugs should have a blood count, liver function, viral markers, alpha-fetoprotein, and ultrasound every 6 to 12 months. Once the conditions for treatment are met, start at once. If virus is detectable in the blood, antiviral treatment is recommended after age 30, whether or not aminotransferases are high. The same applies if someone in the family developed cirrhosis or liver cancer from hepatitis B. The oral drugs the guideline recommends are the entecavir and tenofovir class. The guideline states that most people need long-term treatment, and that the share whose virus rebounds after stopping is high

- Cost: Having the inhaler technique watched costs nothing extra. Bring the medicine to the follow-up, use it once in front of the clinician, and it takes a few minutes. Pulmonary rehabilitation is supervised exercise training plus teaching on drugs and self-management. It means going several times on a schedule, and the fee differs by place. The hard part is still going to train after discharge, when the body is weak.
- In plain terms: Among people who use an inhaler, only about 30% get the technique fully right, and some errors keep the drug from reaching the lungs. Patients who made a critical error had about twice the share admitted or seen in emergency for an exacerbation. Pulmonary rehabilitation after an admission for an exacerbation cuts the chance of another admission by more than half, and quality of life and walking also tend to be better. Whether it means fewer deaths is not yet clear.
- Benefit: A national sample survey in China gave lung-function tests to 50991 adults aged 20 and over. The share with COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) was 8.6% (95% CI 7.5–9.9, confidence interval), about 99900000 people. At 40 and over it was 13.7%. Fully correct technique was 31%, barely acceptable 41%, and poor 31%. Compared with the prior 20 years, the later 20 years showed no improvement. With a press-and-breathe spray, failure to match the press with the breath was 45%. Not inhaling fast enough or deep enough was 44%, and not holding the breath afterward was 46%. A French survey only recorded, with no assignment to groups: doctors watched 2935 COPD patients use an inhaler. More than half the maneuvers had an error. Critical errors that keep the drug from being inhaled ran from 15.4% to 46.9% across devices. Admission or an emergency visit for an exacerbation in the past 3 months was 3.3% among patients with no error and 6.9% among those with a critical error (OR 1.86, 1.14–3.04). It compared pulmonary rehabilitation after a COPD exacerbation with usual care and no rehabilitation. 8 studies and 810 people looked at readmission. Readmission was about 56% lower with rehabilitation (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.21–0.91); evidence quality was moderate. But the studies differed a great deal. 6 studies and 670 people looked at death, OR 0.68 (0.28–1.67); the difference may be chance, and evidence quality was low. The gain in quality of life was larger than patients can feel; evidence quality was high. The 6-minute walk was on average 62 meters longer (38–86 meters); evidence quality was high

### 14. After surgery and chemotherapy for colon cancer, join a supervised exercise program and keep it up for 3 years

- Cost: Walking and similar exercise cost nothing. An assessment and an exercise plan at a hospital rehabilitation clinic or an exercise-prescription clinic take a registration fee and test fees; a private coach is extra. Several extra hours of exercise a week are needed, and it has to be kept up for 3 years. People who train have more muscle and joint injuries.
- In plain terms: After surgery and chemotherapy for colon cancer, people who joined a supervised exercise program for 3 years had about a 28% lower chance of recurrence, a new cancer, or death, and about a 37% lower chance of death. Of those alive at 8 years, about 90% were in the exercise group and about 83% had only received pamphlets. People who trained had more muscle and joint injuries.
- Benefit: A randomized trial at 55 centers (CHALLENGE) enrolled 889 people with colon cancer. All had finished surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, were high-risk stage II or stage III, and had no metastasis at enrollment. For the first 6 months of the program, they met an exercise coach every other week to talk about how to keep going, and also trained a session while the coach watched. From 6 to 12 months this became a meeting or a phone call, and from then until year 3 only minimal support remained. The target was 10 more metabolic-equivalent hours a week than before (a unit of exercise amount); anything of moderate intensity or above counted, and the program expected most people to choose walking. Death was about 37% lower, HR 0.63 (0.43–0.94). At 8 years, 90.3% versus 83.2% were alive. Musculoskeletal adverse events were 18.5% versus 11.5%. A cost analysis of the same trial, on Canadian public health care, put the exercise program at 2917 Canadian dollars per person up front, but with fewer recurrences the 5-year total cost was 1589 Canadian dollars less per person

- Notes: The trial enrolled colon cancer only, not rectal cancer, and only people whose surgery had cleared the tumor, who had finished chemotherapy, and who had no metastasis. It does not carry straight over to other cancers or to people with late disease. The main result is recurrence, a new cancer, or death. Death overall is a secondary result; the authors' wording is "consistent with living longer," and they do not treat it as settled. There is no ready-made program of the same kind in China; an oncology or rehabilitation department can write an exercise prescription. Have a fitness assessment before starting; everyone in the trial had an exercise test before enrollment. How to count walking: see section 2, item 11 (walk to 7000–8000 steps a day). Strength training: see section 2, item 16 (strength training). The person who benefits is mainly you.
