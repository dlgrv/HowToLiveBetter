# 29. After a Major Life Blow

Death of a close relative, being told you have a serious illness, losing a job, divorce — these events share one thing in the research: what takes the hit is not only your mood but also your body, and the highest-risk window is concentrated in the first few weeks to the first year. This section does not explain how to "get through" the experience; it covers which things someone must keep under control during this time, which decisions should be postponed, and when to book an appointment. Most items are measured in mortality; items 9, 11, 12, and 13, which deal with money and benefits, are measured in money — the two kinds of numbers are not converted into each other. If you have no relatives or friends to turn to, see item 6 for how to make "have someone watch over you" happen.

The paperwork right after a relative's death, the body, and the money to claim — Section 25; guardianship and wills for a living elderly parent — Section 17; what you can claim when unemployed — Section 7; exercise and light for low mood — Sections 3 and 22; if you have suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first — Section 1, item 25; the time scale of such thoughts — Section 1, item 30; what remains after a suicide attempt is survived — Section 1, item 31. This section does not repeat them.
### 1. Don't stay alone in the first days after a close relative dies; if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, or diabetes, keep taking your medication as usual, and call 120 if you have crushing chest pain
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — just don't be alone, and don't stop your medication
- In plain terms: in the 24 hours after a relative's death, the chance of a heart attack is 21.1 times the usual level, then falls day by day. Among people aged 60 to 89, 0.16% of those who lost a spouse had a heart attack or stroke within 30 days, versus 0.08% among matched peers who hadn't — roughly double, and it falls back after a month. The absolute numbers are not large: for someone whose ten-year heart attack risk was 5% to begin with, about 1 extra case per 1,394 bereaved people; for someone at 20% to begin with, 1 extra case per 320 — so this item is mainly aimed at people who already have cardiovascular disease. Don't stop your medication these days. Crushing chest pain, breathlessness, or weakness on one side of the body: call 120 immediately.
- Benefit: case-crossover analysis of 1,985 patients with acute myocardial infarction: 270 (13.6%) had lost a significant person within 6 months before onset, 19 of them within 1 day before the heart attack; the rate of acute myocardial infarction rose 21.1-fold (95% CI 13.1–34.1) within 24 hours after the death of a significant person, then declined day by day; absolute risk was 1 extra case per 1,394 exposed people at a ten-year heart attack risk of 5%, and 1 per 320 at 20%. In the UK, 30,447 people aged 60–89 who lost a spouse versus 83,588 matched controls: heart attack or stroke within 30 days of bereavement occurred in 50 (0.16%) versus 67 (0.08%), IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15) — heart attack 2.14 (1.20–3.81), stroke 2.40 (1.22–4.71) — weakening after 30 days; within 90 days, non-heart-attack acute coronary syndrome IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29), pulmonary embolism 2.37 (1.18–4.75)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Notes: both are observational studies and cannot strip out the component of "was about to fall ill anyway," but the direction and the window agree. What you can do is very concrete: hand the pill organizer to someone else to manage, don't rely on your own memory; don't drive long distances these days; don't prop yourself up with alcohol (the accounting for alcohol is in Section 2). Recognizing a heart attack or stroke and how to call for a ride is in Section 13. If there is no one to hand the pill organizer to and these days you are bound to be alone in the house anyway, see item 6 of this section.

### 2. In the week you're told you have a serious diagnosis, don't go collect the report alone, and put off decisions other than treatment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — find someone to go with you, and push the big decisions back a few days
- In plain terms: in the first week after a cancer diagnosis, the probability of suicide is 12.6 times that of people without a diagnosis, and the probability of dying from cardiovascular disease 5.6 times; by the end of the first year these fall to 3.1 and (for the first four weeks) 3.3, and the worse the prognosis of the cancer, the more pronounced it is. This is not a question of "mental fortitude" — it is a common reaction in the days after anyone receives bad news. So: bring someone with you on the day you collect the report, don't be alone at home afterward, and call 120 immediately for chest pain or breathlessness. Decisions about the treatment plan, whether to quit your job, whether to sell the house — leave all of them until after the next outpatient visit.
- Benefit: Swedish historical cohort of 6,073,240 people from 1991 to 2006: compared with people without a cancer diagnosis, relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8, incidence 2.50/1000 person-years), and 3.1 (2.7–3.5, 0.60/1000 person-years) in the first year; cardiovascular death was 5.6 (5.2–5.9, 116.80/1000 person-years) in the first week and 3.3 (3.1–3.4, 65.81/1000 person-years) in the first 4 weeks; the elevated risk fell back rapidly within the first year after diagnosis, with the largest elevations in cancers with poor prognosis; a self-controlled analysis of the same patients reached the same conclusions
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Notes: this is data on cancer; extrapolating it to other major diagnoses has no text of equal strength behind it. The absolute numbers are small (suicide rate of 2.50/1000 person-years in the first week); they are listed not to scare anyone but to show that "the first few days after finding out" require someone at your side. Long-term management, reimbursement, and follow-up after a diagnosis are in Sections 16 and 24; for when you cannot find anyone to go with you, see item 6 of this section.

### 3. After losing your job, first fix your daily routine, health insurance, and job-search rhythm, and don't stay home all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — first register as unemployed and claim unemployment benefits, and put the job search on a fixed schedule
- In plain terms: unemployed people are about 60% more likely to die in the following years than people in work, more pronounced among men and people in the early and middle stages of their careers, and highest in the first ten years after job loss. Nearly a quarter of this gap can be explained by behaviors like smoking and drinking — which is to say, part of it is something you can hold down yourself. The order of things is: first register as unemployed and claim unemployment insurance benefits (while you are drawing them, your employee health insurance is paid on your behalf by the unemployment insurance fund — see Section 7); don't let your health insurance lapse; then fix a set schedule for getting up, sending out résumés, and meeting people.
- Benefit: random-effects meta-analysis of 42 studies, 235 mortality risk estimates, and over 20 million people: after adjusting for age and other covariates, the average HR for all-cause mortality among the unemployed was 1.63; higher in men than women; a clear effect for people in the early and middle career stages, smaller in the late stage; risk was highest in the first 10 years of follow-up and declined afterward; in the subset of studies that controlled for health-related behaviors, the average HR was 24% lower
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Notes: observational evidence; reverse causation cannot be removed — people whose health was already worse are more likely to become unemployed. But the finding that "the effect is 24% lower after controlling for health behaviors" points to something actionable. What you can claim when unemployed, where to claim it, and how to keep social insurance going is in Section 7; how much compensation you are owed if dismissed and why not to sign a voluntary resignation is in Section 19.

### 4. If a relative died by suicide, an accident, or homicide, don't expect to tough it out; go find professional help on your own initiative
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: from 0 — calling 12356 is free; a psychology outpatient appointment costs the same as an ordinary clinic visit
- In plain terms: people whose spouse died by suicide are about 80% (men) and 70% (women) more likely to be diagnosed with a mental disorder within five years than the general population; compared with people whose spouse died of other causes, they are 70% to twice as likely, with more suicidal behavior, higher mortality, and a higher share needing social assistance. People bereaved by suicide also more often feel shunned and ashamed, and therefore speak up less — which is exactly the reason to go find someone proactively. Don't wait to "get better on your own."
- Benefit: national register cohort of 6.7 million Danish adults from 1980 to 2014, over 136 million person-years: among those widowed by a spouse's suicide, the IRR for a new mental disorder within 5 years was 1.8 (95% CI 1.6–2.0) for men and 1.7 (1.6–1.8) for women (versus the general population); versus those widowed by other causes of death, IRR was 1.7 (1.5–1.9) for men and 2.0 (1.9–2.2) for women, with suicidal behavior, mortality, and the share needing municipal assistance all higher, and elevated risk of somatic conditions such as liver cirrhosis and sleep disorders as well. A systematic narrative review of 57 studies: bereavement by suicide is associated with elevated risk of a partner's own suicide, elevated risk of psychiatric hospitalization in parents after a child's suicide, elevated risk of suicide in mothers after the suicide of an adult child, and elevated risk of depression in children after a parent's suicide; compared with people bereaved by other violent deaths, those bereaved by suicide more often report rejection and feelings of shame
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>
- Notes: after sudden, violent deaths (accidents, homicide, disasters), the rates of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and grief getting stuck are all higher than after deaths from illness, and recovery is slower (Kristensen P, et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>). What to do if you yourself have thoughts of suicide is in Section 1, item 25.

### 5. In the first six months after being widowed, put meals, sleep, chronic-disease medication, and follow-up visits under the watch of one fixed person
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — pick one specific person (child, sibling, friend) to visit or call on a set schedule
- In plain terms: within six months of being widowed, the probability of dying in the same period is about 40% higher than among people who haven't; after six months it drops to about 15% higher. For men it is about 23% higher; for women that figure is so small it may be coincidence. Another analysis pooling over 500 million people puts the overall figure at about 23% higher — men 27%, women 15%. This gap is not made up by "thinking positive"; it is made up by not stopping medication, eating on time, and going to follow-ups when due, so it has to land on one specific person. Don't count on remembering it yourself.
- Benefit: random-effects meta-analysis of 15 prospective cohorts and 2,263,888 people: RR for death when widowed less than 6 months was 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), and 1.14 (1.10–1.18) after 6 months; for men RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28), for women 1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant); no significant difference between those under 65 and those 65 and older. Another meta-analysis and meta-regression of 123 publications, 1,377 risk estimates, and over 500 million people: adjusted HR 1.23 (1.19–1.28), men 1.27 (1.19–1.35) higher than women 1.15 (1.08–1.22)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: observational studies; confounding cannot be removed (spouses' lifestyles and illnesses are similar to begin with). But the time shape — "the first six months far higher than afterward" — is consistent across two independent meta-analyses, and is worth treating as a care window. Men are at higher risk and also ask for help less often. If there is a newly widowed elderly parent at home, sort out accounts, guardianship, and the will together, per Section 17. If there is no such person to turn to (living alone, having lost an only child, or with the children gone too), hand this over to the community and to devices — see item 6 of this section.

### 6. If you have no relatives and no friends, replace "that person" with three things: a neighbor who can enter your home, the community's visit list, and emergency contacts in your phone
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — the price is having to speak up yourself and tell a neighbor or the residents' committee "I live alone and something just happened"
- In plain terms: the items above keep saying "find someone," but when you have no relatives and no friends that sentence is empty. "Having someone with you" is really doing two jobs, and you can swap them separately. First, if something goes wrong, someone will notice; second, someone watches that you take your medication, eat, and don't drink. The second job can only be yours anyway: write the medication and meals into a checklist, set alarms, don't rely on memory. It is the first job that needs a substitute, and three things are enough. Give a spare key or a temporary door-lock code to one neighbor or to the property management and say one sentence — "I'm alone these days" — no friendship needed, only someone who knows you are in the house. Go to the residents' committee, say that you live alone, and ask whether you can be put on the care-visit list: among the elderly, those living alone, empty-nesters, and parents who lost an only child are explicitly named service targets, and the policy goal is that these older people are visited every month, with a visit at least once a month for the disabled — ask at the same time whether your district has free installations such as a smart call button or a smart water meter. Set up emergency contacts and a medical ID card on your phone, and for these days don't mute it or switch it off. One number in passing: among people who already had cardiovascular disease and lived alone, the share who died within four years was about 3 percentage points higher than among those living with others, and about 24% higher in the 45-to-65 band.
- Benefit: the REACH international registry of 44,573 stable outpatients with atherothrombotic risk or history (enrolled December 2003 to December 2004, followed 4 years): 8,594 (19%) lived alone; 4-year all-cause death among those living alone 14.1% versus 11.1%, cardiovascular death 8.6% versus 6.8% (both log-rank P<0.01); age modified the effect (interaction P=0.03) — at 45–65, death for living alone versus with others was 7.7% versus 5.7%, adjusted HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51); at 66–80 it was 13.2% versus 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26); above 80, 24.6% versus 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06), no elevation seen. The 2022 opinion of the Ministry of Civil Affairs and nine other departments: care-visit services for older people who live alone, are empty-nesters or left behind, are disabled or severely disabled, or belong to special family-planning households, delivered by "regular home visits, telephone and video, remote monitoring"; townships (subdistricts) do the baseline survey and village (residents') committees assist, record basic information and the "willingness to receive care-visit services," and bring this into grid-based grassroots management; by the end of 2025 "the monthly visit rate for older people in special difficulty is to reach 100%," and "older people in special difficulty who are disabled are to be visited and cared for at least once a month"; households are encouraged to equip themselves with smart call systems, smart electricity and water meters, health-monitoring products, and eldercare monitoring devices, "so that once monitoring shows an anomaly an early warning can be issued in time and a prompt sent simultaneously to emergency contacts"; when service staff find an emergency, they "must immediately help call the emergency number"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>；民政部等十部门 (2022). 关于开展特殊困难老年人探访关爱服务的指导意见（民发〔2022〕73 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Notes: why B: the link between living alone and mortality is observational and cannot strip out the reverse explanation that "people in worse health and worse circumstances are more likely to live alone" — the original paper itself says the result needs confirmation. The care-visit document covers only older people; under 60 there is no matching dedicated system, and the closest thing is the requirement that grid workers and social workers "promptly detect psychological crisis risks such as family upheaval, unemployment, and dropping out of school" (item 11 of this section). Why "someone notices" matters is in Section 13, item 1: 79.2% of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests happen at home, survival to discharge with bystander CPR is 16.1% versus 3.9% without — no one in the house means landing in the lower tier. Two more things in passing: a funeral itself means running between the funeral home, community health services, the police, and the housing fund office (Section 25), so schedule it as one outing a day and the structure of leaving the house is already there; and the hotlines can be called again and again, not just once (item 11 of this section). "Living alone" is itself a long-term risk factor (odds ratio for death 1.32 for living alone; Section 22, item 10), but this item covers only the days right now — the long-term account is not settled here.

### 7. After a parent dies, tell the child the truth, let them attend the farewell, and don't send them elsewhere to "change the environment"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — the price is having to say things clearly to the child even when you are hurting yourself
- In plain terms: children who lose a father or mother before age 18 are about 50% more likely to die by early adulthood than people who haven't been through it; about 80% higher when the parent died of unnatural causes such as an accident or suicide, and about 30% higher when the parent died of illness. So this is not something "a small child forgets in a couple of days" — the effect lasts many years. What can be done: tell the truth, don't invent "they went away on a long trip"; let the child attend the farewell; don't interrupt schooling and don't send them to relatives "to change the environment"; put guardianship and support arrangements in writing.
- Benefit: entire populations born in Denmark 1968–2008 and Sweden 1973–2006, plus an 89.3% sample of those born in Finland 1987–2007, over 7.3 million people in total, of whom 189,094 lost a parent before 18: all-cause mortality 50% higher than among the non-bereaved (mortality rate ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58); 1.84 (1.71–2.00) when the parent died of unnatural causes and 1.33 (1.24–1.41) of natural causes; highest when the child's cause of death fell in the same category as the parent's
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Notes: the B is not because the numbers are weak — this is a three-country whole-population cohort, and the numbers themselves are A-grade — but because there is no direct trial evidence between this figure and the concrete practices of "how to talk to the child, whether to attend the farewell." The authors also state that data on parent-child relationship quality and lifestyle after bereavement are missing, and that the elevation contains both genetic susceptibility and the long-term impact of bereavement. Designating a guardian and property arrangements are in Section 17; child support and schooling are in Section 18.

### 8. If grief is still stuck in place after six months and daily life is not working, book an appointment in psychiatry or clinical psychology
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: the registration fee plus treatment fees; this kind of treatment is done as a course — in the original trials, 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks
- In plain terms: about one in every ten bereaved adults gets stuck: for more than six months, persistent longing so strong that they can't eat, can't work, can't look after their children; numbness, shock, a sense that life is meaningless, inability to accept it, avoiding everything connected to the deceased, no longer knowing who they are supposed to be. This is not "not being strong enough" — it has diagnostic criteria, and there is a treatment built for it: the targeted treatment works for 51%, compared with 28% for ordinary psychotherapy — on average, for every 4.3 people treated, one more person gets better — and it works faster.
- Benefit: systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 studies (non-psychiatric adults bereaved by non-violent loss): pooled prevalence of prolonged grief disorder 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0), with prevalence rising the higher the average age. A validity study of the diagnostic criteria (291 bereaved people, interviewed three times at 0–6, 6–12, and 12–24 months): the core symptom is intense longing for the deceased, plus at least five of the following nine — emotional numbness, shock, a sense that life is meaningless, difficulty trusting others, bitterness over the loss, difficulty accepting it, confusion about identity, avoidance of reality, and inability to move on with life — occurring daily or at a disabling level, and still present with functional impairment at least 6 months after the death. A randomized controlled trial in 95 people (16 sessions, average 19 weeks): response rate 51% for treatment targeting complicated grief versus 28% for interpersonal psychotherapy (P=0.02), faster onset, number needed to treat 4.3
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Notes: this diagnosis is called complicated grief or prolonged grief disorder in different publications, and different criteria require different durations (6 or 12 months); don't hold yourself to the month count — the criterion for judgment is "it affects eating, sleeping, working, or caring for children"; if it matches, go book an appointment. The 9.8% prevalence comes from 14 studies with substantial methodological differences, and the authors caution that external validity is limited. The department is called psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology; public psychiatric specialty hospitals all have it.

### 9. Don't pay for grief counseling right off the bat; first check whether you match the criteria in item 8
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 — you save the counseling fee instead
- In plain terms: arranging counseling universally for everyone who is bereaved has only a small effect at the end of the intervention, and by follow-up no benefit can be seen — the reason is that most people who receive no intervention recover on their own anyway. In a study that tracked 205 people from before to after widowhood, the most common pattern was precisely the "resilient" one; staying stuck the whole time is the minority. But for people who already show clear adjustment difficulties, the same treatment works as well as it does for other psychological problems, and keeps improving through the follow-up period. So the order is: first check yourself against item 8, and spend this money only if you match. Don't buy courses or counseling packages at the start.
- Benefit: meta-analysis of 61 controlled studies: psychological interventions for bereavement had a small effect size at treatment end and no statistically significant benefit at follow-up; interventions that enrolled only bereaved people "already showing clear adjustment difficulties" performed on a par with psychotherapy for other psychological problems; the poor results of studies without screening can be attributed to natural improvement over time in the control groups. Meta-analysis of 14 randomized controlled trials: therapeutic interventions were significantly better than control both after treatment and at follow-up, with the effect still growing during follow-up; preventive interventions were ineffective. A prospective study of 205 people from before widowhood to 6 and 18 months after: five patterns were identified (common grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, resilience), with the resilient pattern the most common and the common-grief pattern relatively rare
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>；Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>；Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>
- Notes: this item says "not everyone needs it," not "no one should do it" — the high-risk bereaved in item 4 and the people already stuck in item 8 are exactly the populations the evidence supports treating. A Lancet review reaches the same conclusion: grief is not an illness, most people do not need professional intervention, and intervention should target those at high risk and those who have already developed complicated grief, bereavement-related depression, and stress disorders (Stroebe M, et al. (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9>).

### 10. After separation or divorce, don't let your health insurance lapse and don't prop yourself up with alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — get the health insurance reinstated and stop the drinking
- In plain terms: in an analysis pooling 11 countries and 6.5 million people, separated or divorced people were clearly more likely to die early than the still-married, with a larger increase among men and younger people. The original authors did not treat this as causal — people who divorce may already differ in health and income. Of the four mechanisms the authors list, two are things you can hold down yourself: interruption of income and coverage (don't let health insurance or checkups lapse), and worsening health behaviors (alcohol and tobacco).
- Benefit: random-effects meta-analysis of 32 prospective studies, over 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, over 755,000 divorces, and 11 countries: compared with married adults, separated or divorced adults had a significantly elevated risk of early death; the increase was significantly larger in men and younger adults than in women and older people; the authors discussed four possible mechanisms — social selection, resource interruption, changes in health behaviors, and chronic psychological stress
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Notes: the original gives "significantly elevated" and subgroup comparisons; this item does not state a specific multiplier, and the benefit magnitude is set at medium per "direction only, with no relative reduction to which a threshold can be applied." Property, debts, and bride-price in divorce are in Sections 8 and 10; arrangements for the children's care are in Section 18.

### 11. Call 12356 when you need someone to talk to, 12355 for minors and teenagers, and book a psychology outpatient appointment to see a doctor
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 — both hotlines are free; the psychology outpatient clinic charges an ordinary outpatient registration fee
- In plain terms: two free lines: 12356 is the national psychological assistance hotline; minors and teenagers have a separate service, the 12355 comprehensive youth service platform; the two lines transfer to each other, and 12356 is also linked with 110. To see a doctor, book a psychology appointment in psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology — under the 2026 plan, by 2030 public psychiatric specialty hospitals at every level and at least one hospital in every county (district) are to provide psychology outpatient services, and over 80% of villages and communities are to set up psychological counseling rooms or social work stations. After a family upheaval, job loss, or dropping out of school, community grid workers and social workers are supposed to detect it proactively and make visits on their own initiative.
- Benefit: 2026 plan by the National Health Commission and 25 departments: improve the working system of the 12356 psychological assistance hotline, guarantee stable operation and raise the connection rate, promote the establishment of provincial-level 12356 information platforms, and strengthen the linkage mechanism with 110 and other service hotlines; build up and make good use of the 12355 comprehensive youth service platform, with coordination and collaboration with 12356; the goals are that by 2030, over 80% of administrative villages and urban communities will have psychological counseling rooms or social work stations providing psychological services, public psychiatric specialty hospitals at every level and at least 1 hospital in every county (district) will provide psychology outpatient services, and every province will have built a provincial-level 12356 psychological assistance platform; the psychological crisis intervention and psychological assistance workforce is to consist of psychiatrists, psychotherapy technicians, psychological counselors, social work professionals, and volunteers; grid workers, social work professionals, and volunteers are required to carry out front-line daily screening and promptly detect psychological crisis risks such as family upheaval, unemployment, and dropping out of school (nationwide, issued March 2026)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 关于印发健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案的通知（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>

### 12. In the first three months after a life upheaval, push every irreversible major decision back
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 — the decision is only deferred; the price is possibly missing certain opportunities
- In plain terms: the period right after an upheaval is when "opportunities" and scammers are most concentrated: people claiming they can contact the deceased, claiming they can help you win your money back, urging you to quit your job and start over in another city, urging you to sell the house quickly, pulling you into a partnership. Many decisions made in this period are irreversible — selling the house, quitting the job, investing a compensation payment in one lump, a hasty remarriage, guaranteeing a loan for someone. Set yourself one hard rule: anything costing more than one month's income, or carrying a heavy price to undo, gets pushed back three months without exception, and is first told to someone with no stake in the matter — if there is no such person, call 12356 and tell them, or write it down yourself and reread it three days later; all you need is to not decide the same day.
- Benefit: avoids irreversible property dispositions made during a period of acute stress — selling the house, quitting the job, investing a death gratuity or compensation payment in one lump, a hasty remarriage, guaranteeing a loan for someone. The amounts in such decisions usually run from tens of thousands up to the level of real estate, which is why the benefit of this item is rated medium. The official position likewise lists "family upheaval, unemployment, dropping out of school" as psychological crisis risks that communities should screen for proactively — that is, one's state during this period is considered to require outside support
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；「家庭变故、失业、失学」作为心理危机风险的表述见国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Notes: the "three months" is a threshold set by the author; no literature supports this specific duration, which is why this item is grade C. Common scams targeting the bereaved and the elderly are in Section 17 (retirement investment schemes, housing-for-pension schemes) and Section 6 (fortune-telling); the traps in guarantees and IOUs are in Section 8. Money you should actively go claim — the housing fund balance, social insurance entitlements, the three work-death payments — is in Sections 25 and 19; those are not on the "push it back" list.

### 13. Don't treat death as a way to pay off debts: life insurance won't pay out within two years, work injury won't be recognized, and debts still come off the estate first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 — the point is simply to get this accounting straight
- In plain terms: for insurance with death as the condition of payment, if the insured dies by suicide within two years of the contract being formed or of coverage being restored, the insurer does not pay, and only refunds the cash value of the policy as the contract provides (except where the insured lacked capacity for civil conduct at the time). The work-injury route is also closed: self-harm or suicide is not recognized as a work injury, and not a penny of the three work-death payments — the funeral subsidy, the dependent's pension, and the lump-sum work-death compensation — is paid. And debts do not vanish with the person: an estate must first pay the taxes and debts owed, and heirs pay within the actual value of the estate they receive — what you owe is deducted first from what you leave behind, and the family gets whatever is left after that. What you leave them is not a sum of money but a shortfall, plus the health cost in item 4 of this section.
- Benefit: Article 44 of the Insurance Law: for a contract with the death of the insured as the condition for paying insurance benefits, if the insured dies by suicide within two years of the date the contract was formed or of the date coverage was restored, the insurer bears no liability to pay (except where the insured lacked capacity for civil conduct at the time of suicide), and shall refund the cash value of the policy as agreed in the contract — life-insurance coverage is usually in the range of hundreds of thousands of yuan, which is the basis for rating this item's benefit as large. Article 16, item 3 of the Regulations on Work-Related Injury Insurance: self-harm or suicide may not be recognized as, or treated as, a work injury, and with it all three work-death benefits under Article 39 fall away (the funeral subsidy of 6 months of the preceding year's average monthly wage of employees in the coordinated area, the dependent's pension, and the lump-sum work-death compensation of 20 times the preceding year's national per-capita disposable income of urban residents). Article 1159 of the Civil Code: in dividing an estate, the taxes and debts the decedent was legally required to pay must be settled; Article 1161: heirs pay within the actual value of the estate they receive, and an heir who renounces the inheritance may bear no liability for repayment
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2015 修正). 中华人民共和国保险法第四十四条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811>；国务院 (2010 修订). 工伤保险条例（国务院令第 586 号）第十六条、第三十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>；全国人大 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典第一千一百五十九条、第一千一百六十一条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: this item only settles the money account — the other items in this section and in Section 1 are about not getting to that point at all. Article 44 of the Insurance Law covers only the first two years; it is written out here to state the provisions completely, not to offer a timetable. The health cost on the family's side is in item 4 of this section (those whose spouse died by suicide run a 70–80% higher risk of a new mental disorder within 5 years); what to do when the thought surfaces is in Section 1, items 25 and 30; the after-effects of being rescued are in Section 1, item 31; the standards for the three work-death payments are in Section 19, item 16, and the concrete procedures for estates and debts in Section 25, item 9.
