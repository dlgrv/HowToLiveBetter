# 29. After a major blow

Losing a relative, being told of a serious illness, losing a job, or a divorce. In the research these events share a finding: what goes wrong is not only the mood, but the body. The stretch of highest risk sits in the first weeks through the first year. This section does not explain how to "get over it." It only says which things someone has to watch during this stretch, which decisions have to be put off, and when to go and register at a clinic. Most items are scored on the chance of death. The items that talk about spending and about benefits are scored on money. They are: do not start by paying for grief counseling (item 9), call 12356 and register at a mental-health clinic (item 11), push irreversible big decisions back (item 12), and do not treat death as a way to pay a debt (item 13). These kinds of figures are not converted into each other. If there is no relative and no friend to turn to, how "find someone to watch" actually gets done is in item 6. If you yourself went through a traffic crash, an assault, or a disaster and the nightmares and flashbacks are still there, see item 14 (trauma-focused psychological treatment).

The paperwork at the time a relative dies, the body, and the money to collect are in section 25. Guardianship and a will for an elder while alive are in section 17. What you can receive after losing a job is in section 7. Exercise and daylight when mood is low are in section 3 and section 22. If suicidal thoughts appear, call 12356 first; see section 1, item 25. The time scale of the thoughts is in section 1, item 32. Lasting harm after a person is brought back is in section 1, item 33. This section does not repeat any of that.
### 1. In the first days after a relative dies, do not stay alone; if you have heart disease, hypertension, or diabetes, keep taking the medicine; if the chest feels pressed and hurts, call 120
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Do not stay alone these days, and keep taking the medicine.
- In plain terms: On the day after a relative dies, the chance of a heart attack is 21.1 times the usual chance, and each day after that it is lower. Among people aged 60 to 89, the share who have a heart attack or a stroke within 30 days after a spouse dies is about double, and it falls back after a month. If you already have cardiovascular disease, do not stop the medicine these days. If the chest feels pressed and hurts, you cannot catch your breath, or an arm or a leg on a single side has no strength, call 120 at once.
- Benefit: 1985 patients with an acute heart attack, comparing different stretches of time in the same person, before and after the attack. Of them, 270 (13.6%) had lost an important person in the 6 months before the attack, and 19 had lost that person within 1 day before the heart attack. Within 24 hours after an important person died, the rate of acute heart attack was 21.1 times higher (95% CI 13.1–34.1, and the true multiple sits roughly between 13 and 34). After that it fell day by day. In actual counts: among people with a decade-long heart-attack risk of 5%, each 1394 people who had just lost a relative produced 1 extra case. Among people with a risk of 20%, each 320 people produced 1 extra case. Another study in Britain compared 30447 widowed people aged 60 to 89 with 83588 controls in similar circumstances. Within 30 days after the death of the spouse, a heart attack or a stroke occurred in 50 people on the widowed side (0.16%) and in 67 people on the control side (0.08%). The IRR was 2.20 (1.52–3.15), that is, the chance of the event was about 2.2 times the control group. Of that, heart attack was 2.14 (1.20–3.81), about 2.1 times. Stroke was 2.40 (1.22–4.71), about 2.4 times. After 30 days it weakened. Within 90 days they also looked at further illnesses. The first was acute coronary syndrome that was not a heart attack (an acute problem with the blood supply to the heart that still falls short of a heart attack), IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29). The other was pulmonary embolism (a clot blocking a blood vessel in the lung), IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75). The British National Health Service (NHS) lists the signs for which you should call the emergency number at once. A heart attack is pain in the chest as if it were being pressed or squeezed. The pain can spread to the arm, the neck, or the jaw, and there is also shortness of breath. A stroke is a side of the face drooping, an arm or a side of the body weak or numb, and slurred speech.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>；NHS. Heart attack: symptoms. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/>；NHS. Symptoms of a stroke. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/>
- Notes: Hand the medicine box to someone else to manage. Do not rely on remembering it yourself. Do not take a long trip these days. Do not get through it on alcohol. The account of alcohol is in section 2. How to recognize a heart attack or a stroke, and how to call for a car, is in section 13. Both studies looked back through population records. They cannot separate out how many of these people were going to fall ill anyway. Both point the same way, and both point at the same stretch of time. If there is nobody you can hand the medicine box to, and these days you are bound to be alone in the room, see item 6 of this section.

### 2. On the week you receive a cancer diagnosis, don’t go alone to pick up the results — postpone non‑treatment decisions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Simply arrange for someone to accompany you when you collect the results, and put any major decisions unrelated to treatment off for a few days.

- In plain terms: In the first week after receiving a cancer diagnosis, the risk of suicide is roughly 12.6 times higher than among people without a diagnosis, while the risk of dying from cardiovascular disease is about 5.6 times higher. By the end of the first year this drops to roughly 3.1 times; the effect is especially pronounced for harder‑to‑treat cancers. The greatest risk exists during those first few days, after which it falls quickly. Therefore, bring a companion when picking up the results and avoid being alone at home. Decisions such as quitting your job or selling your house can wait until your next appointment.

- Benefit: Data from Sweden’s national registers tracked over 6.07 million people (6,073,240 individuals) from 1991 to 2006. Compared with cancer‑free controls, the relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8), meaning roughly 12.6 times higher. The true value lies somewhere between 8.6 and 17.8. The incidence rate during that week was 2.50 per 1,000 person‑years — about 2.5 cases per 1,000 people each year. By the end of the first year this fell to 3.1 (2.7–3.5; 0.60 per 1,000 person‑years), or roughly 3.1 times higher. Cardiovascular mortality in the first week was 5.6 times higher (5.2–5.9; 116.80 per 1,000 person‑years), and over the first four weeks it remained at 3.3 times higher (3.1–3.4; 65.81 per 1,000 person‑years). This elevated risk declines rapidly within the first year; the effect is more pronounced for cancers with poorer prognoses. Comparisons of the same patients over time yielded consistent results.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>

- Notes: These figures apply specifically to cancer patients. No comparable data exist for other serious diagnoses. The actual number of people affected remains low: a total of 6,073,240 individuals were included in the study, and the first‑week suicide rate is 2.50 per 1,000 person‑years, i.e., only 2.5 cases per 1,000 people each year. This information is provided not to frighten, but to emphasize the importance of having someone present during those initial days. Long‑term management, insurance coverage, and follow‑up care are discussed in Sections 16 and 24. For those unable to find a companion, see point 6 in this section, which suggests enlisting neighbours or community contacts instead.

### 3. After losing a job, it’s important to set a regular sleep schedule, keep yibao (basic medical insurance) active, and plan job searches methodically — don’t just stay at home all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: There’s no expense involved. First, register as unemployed and apply for unemployment benefits; then schedule job searches at set times. The real challenge is waking up on time when no one is there to push you.

- In plain terms: People who have lost a job later die at a rate about 60% higher than people still in work. The gap is clearer for men, and for people who are not old, and it is highest in the first 10 years. Once smoking and drinking are taken out, the rise is about a quarter smaller, and that part is in your own hands. Register as unemployed first, and claim the unemployment insurance benefit.

- Benefit: Data from 42 studies involving over 20 million participants and 235 mortality risk estimates show that, after adjusting for age and other factors, unemployed individuals face an average HR of 1.63 for all causes of death — meaning their risk is about 63% higher than that of employed people. This effect is more pronounced in men and in people early or midway through their careers; it diminishes after the first ten years. When lifestyle factors like smoking and drinking are included in the analysis, the average HR drops by 24%, reflecting a similar reduction in overall risk.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>

- Notes: These findings come from observational studies, so a direct cause‑and‑effect relationship cannot be proven; it’s also possible that poorer health contributes to unemployment. Nevertheless, the 24% reduction in risk after adjusting for health behaviors offers a useful direction for intervention. Details on eligibility for unemployment benefits, how to apply, and how to maintain social insurance coverage are provided in Section 7. Information on severance pay and why you should avoid voluntarily resigning can be found in Section 19.

### 4. If a relative died by suicide, accident, or homicide, do not try to tough it out; go and ask for professional help
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Calling 12356 costs nothing. If you need to see a doctor, a psychology clinic is the price of an ordinary outpatient visit. The hard part is opening your mouth and asking.
- In plain terms: People whose spouse died by suicide have a higher chance of being diagnosed with a mental disorder within 5 years than other people do: about 80% higher for men, about 70% higher for women. Compared with people whose spouse died of another cause, the chance is also 70% higher up to twice as high. People bereaved this way are more likely to feel kept at a distance, and to feel shame. That is the reason to go and ask, rather than waiting to feel better on your own.
- Benefit: A national registry in Denmark followed 6700000 adults, from 1980 to 2014, more than 136000000 person-years in all (the years each person was followed, added up). For people widowed because a spouse died by suicide, the risk of a newly diagnosed mental disorder within 5 years, compared with other people, was an IRR of 1.8 for men (95% CI 1.6–2.0), that is, about 80% higher. For women it was 1.7 (1.6–1.8), that is, about 70% higher. Compared with people widowed by other causes of death, the IRR was 1.7 for men (1.5–1.9) and 2.0 for women (1.9–2.2), that is, 70% higher up to double. In this group, suicidal behavior, mortality, and the share who needed municipal assistance were also higher. Physical illnesses such as cirrhosis and sleep disorders were higher as well. Another 57 studies were summarized separately, without pooling the arithmetic. Bereavement by suicide is linked to the following. A partner's risk of suicide is higher. After a child dies by suicide, the parents' risk of admission to a psychiatric ward is higher. After an adult child dies by suicide, the mother's risk of suicide is higher. After a parent dies by suicide, the child's risk of depression is higher. Compared with people bereaved by other violent deaths, people bereaved by suicide more often say they are kept at a distance and feel shame.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>；Kristensen P 等 (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>（备注里那份综述）
- Notes: A sudden, violent death means an accident, a homicide, or a disaster. After this kind of death, the share with post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and grief that stays stuck is higher than after a death from illness. Recovery is also slower. If a month has passed and you are still having nightmares, flashbacks, or avoiding what you dare not touch, see item 14 of this section (trauma-focused psychological treatment). What to do when you yourself have suicidal thoughts is in section 1, item 25.

### 5. In the first half year after losing a spouse, hand meals, sleep, chronic-disease medicine, and follow-up visits to a fixed person
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Find a specific person, a child, a sibling, or a friend, and have them come by or call on a regular schedule. The hard part is opening your mouth and handing this over.
- In plain terms: In the first half year after losing a spouse, the chance of dying in that same stretch is about 40% higher than for people who did not lose a spouse. After half a year it falls to about 15% higher. For men it is about 23% higher. For women the gap is small enough that it may only be a coincidence. What to do is put the medicine, the meals, and the follow-up visits on a specific person. Do not count on remembering them yourself.
- Benefit: 15 studies that registered people first and then followed them, 2263888 people taken together. When the spouse had been dead for less than 6 months, the mortality RR was 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), that is, the chance of dying in that same stretch was about 40% higher than for people who had not lost a spouse. After 6 months it was 1.14 (1.10–1.18), down to about 15% higher. For men the RR was 1.23 (1.18–1.28), about 23% higher. For women it was 1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant, that is, this small gap may only be a coincidence). Under 65 and 65 or older showed no clear difference. Another analysis pooled 123 papers, 1377 risk estimates, and over 500000000 people. After the differences in circumstances were leveled, the HR was 1.23 (1.19–1.28), that is, about 23% higher overall. Men were still higher than women. Men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), 27% higher. Women 1.15 (1.08–1.22), 15% higher.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: These studies looked back through population records. Other factors come in and cannot be removed. A couple's way of living and their illnesses were already similar. "The first half year is much higher than what follows" is what the first pooled analysis got by splitting on time since the death. The second did not split on time since the death. It only split on how long the study followed people. Studies that followed only 6 months, taken together, had an HR of 1.58 (1.32–1.88). Studies that followed 16 to 20 years had 1.22 (1.02–1.47). The authors take this to mean the risk is highest in the first years after the death, and they also state that most studies were not starting the follow-up in the moment just after the death. Both point the same way, so the first half year is worth treating as a window of watching. Men have a higher risk, and they are also less likely to open their mouths and ask. If there is a widowed elder in the family, sort out accounts, guardianship, and the will together, as in section 17. If no such person can be found (living alone, a child already lost, or the children gone as well), hand this to the community and the phone; see item 6 of this section.

### 6. With no relatives and no friends, replace the person watching you with a neighbor who can enter the home, a place on the community visit list, and an emergency contact in the phone
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is opening your mouth first, and saying to a neighbor and to the residents' committee (居委会), "I live alone, and something has just happened at home."
- In plain terms: When there is no relative and no friend, what has to be covered is this: if something happens, someone finds you, and on ordinary days you take medicine and eat on time. Medicine and meals are on you. Write them as a list, and set an alarm. Do not rely on memory. Someone finding you rests on a neighbor being left a spare key, on asking the residents' committee whether you can be put on the visit-and-care list, and on the phone having an emergency contact set, and not being switched off.
- Benefit: 44573 outpatients, each with a risk or a history of atherothrombosis (plaque in the vessels, the kind of trouble that a clot can block). Doctors in many countries registered them into the international project REACH and followed them. They were enrolled from December (12) 2003 to December (12) 2004, and followed for 4 years. Of them, 8594 (19%) lived alone. Over those 4 years, death from all causes together was 14.1% for people living alone and 11.1% for people living with others. Cardiovascular death was 8.6% against 6.8%. Both log-rank P<0.01, so the difference is unlikely to be chance. The gap was not the same at every age (interaction P=0.03). From 45 to 65, death for living alone against living with others was 7.7% against 5.7%. After other circumstances were leveled, the HR was 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51), that is, about 24% higher. From 66 to 80 it was 13.2% against 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26), about 10% higher. Over 80 it was 24.6% against 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06), and no rise was seen. A 2022 opinion of the Ministry of Civil Affairs (民政) and the other departments with it says visit-and-care services are to be carried out. The people covered are elders who live alone, elders in an empty nest, elders left behind, elders who have lost the ability to care for themselves, elders with a severe disability, and elders in a special family-planning household. The service is "regular visits into the home, telephone or video, and remote monitoring." The township (or subdistrict) does the survey, and the villagers' committee or the residents' committee assists. They keep basic information and "willingness to receive visit-and-care services," and bring the person into grassroots grid management (a named person watches each patch). By the end of 2025, "make sure the monthly visit rate for elders in special difficulty reaches 100%," and "for elders in special difficulty who have lost the ability to care for themselves, visit-and-care is no less than once a month." The document also encourages the household to set up a smart call system, a smart electricity meter and water meter, health-monitoring products, and an elder-care monitoring device, so that "once monitoring is abnormal, a warning can be given in time, and a prompt is sent to the emergency contact at the same moment." If service staff find an urgent problem, they "shall help call the emergency number right away."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>；民政部等十部门 (2022). 关于开展特殊困难老年人探访关爱服务的指导意见（民发〔2022〕73 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Notes: How to do the practical parts. First, give a spare key, or the temporary code for the door lock, to a neighbor or to the property manager, and tell them "I am alone these days." No friendship is required. Someone only needs to know you are in the room. Second, go to the community residents' committee, say you live alone, and ask whether you can be put on the visit-and-care list. Among elders, living alone, an empty nest, and having lost a child are service targets the official text names. Also ask whether the locality has a smart caller or a smart water meter that is installed free of charge. Third, set an emergency contact and a medical emergency card on the phone, and do not silence it or switch it off these days. The grade is B for a pair of reasons. The link between living alone and mortality comes from studies that looked back through population records. They cannot remove the reverse reading that people who were already in worse health, and in worse circumstances, are more likely to live alone. The original paper itself says this result still awaits confirmation. The visit-and-care document covers elders only. Under 60 there is no matching special system. What can be counted on is a line in another document. It requires grid workers and social workers to "find in time the psychological-crisis risks of a family blow, job loss, dropping out of school, and the like" (see item 11 of this section: if you need someone to talk with you, call 12356). Why "someone in the room" is worth something: see section 13, item 1 (pressing on the chest). Of cardiac arrests that happen outside a hospital, 79.2% happen at home. When someone nearby does compressions, 16.1% leave the hospital alive. When nobody does, only 3.9% do. Nobody in the room means you fall in the lower band. Further things are already at hand. The funeral itself means trips to the funeral home, the community health service, the public security bureau, and the housing provident fund (公积金), several windows (see section 25). Set those trips up as a fixed schedule of going out once a day, and the outings for these days are already there. The hotline can be called again and again. It is not limited to a single call (see item 11 of this section: if you need someone to talk with you, call 12356). "Living alone" is also a long-run risk factor. The mortality ratio for living alone is 1.32, about 30% higher; see section 22, item 9 (treat meeting people on a regular schedule as a health expense). This item only covers these days in front of you. The long-run account is counted separately.

### 7. After a parent dies, tell the child the truth, and let the child attend the farewell
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is that you are hurting too, and you still have to say it clearly to the child.
- In plain terms: A child who loses a father or a mother before 18 later dies at a rate about 50% higher than a child who did not go through that. If the parent died by accident or by suicide, it is about 80% higher. If the parent died of illness, it is about 30% higher. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends telling the child what happened, truthfully, and supporting the child in attending the funeral and the farewell.
- Benefit: Everyone born in Denmark from 1968 to 2008, and everyone born in Sweden from 1973 to 2006, was counted in. Added to that was 89.3% of people born in Finland from 1987 to 2007. Together, over 7300000 people. Of them, 189094 lost a parent before 18. Mortality from all causes together in these children was 50% higher than in people who had not been bereaved (mortality ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58, that is, about 1.5 times). When the parent died of an unnatural cause such as an accident or suicide, it was 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about 80% higher. When the parent died of a natural cause such as illness, it was 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about 30% higher. It was highest when the child's cause of death and the parent's cause of death were the same class. A 2016 clinical report from the American Academy of Pediatrics, written for pediatricians, requires that a talk with a bereaved child state clearly what happened and what it means, and correct wrong information and misunderstandings the child has heard. The report also gives advice on how to support the child in attending the funeral and other farewell rites.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>；Schonfeld DJ, Demaria T, Committee on Psychosocial Aspects of Child and Family Health, Disaster Preparedness Advisory Council (2016). Supporting the Grieving Child and Family. Pediatrics, 138(3), e20162147. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2016-2147>
- Notes: When you tell the child, do not invent "they went away on a long trip." Guardianship and the arrangement for raising the child have to be put on paper. Whether to change schools, or to send the child to live somewhere else, has no direct study or guideline text that was found. The author's view is that fewer changes in the child's life are better. The grade is B not because the numbers are soft. This is a follow-up of the whole population of Denmark, Sweden, and Finland, and the numbers themselves would be enough for grade A. The grade is B because, from this number to the specific practice of how to tell the child and whether the child should attend the farewell, there is no direct trial evidence. The authors also state that data on the parent-child relationship after the death, and on the child's way of living, are both missing. The rise includes a part that is inborn constitution, and a part that is the long effect left by the bereavement. Naming a guardian and arranging property are in section 17. The child's support and schooling are in section 18.

### 8. Still stuck in grief after half a year, unable to move on — when to see a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: This includes registration fees and treatment costs. Treatment is delivered in sessions; on average, 16 sessions are given over 19 weeks in clinical trials. The main challenge is simply mustering the courage to make that first appointment.

- In plain terms: About 1 in 10 bereaved adults gets stuck. After more than half a year they still miss the person so much that they cannot eat, cannot go to work, and cannot look after a child. They feel numb, and life seems to have no point. This is not a lack of strength. It has diagnostic criteria, and it has a treatment made for it. Targeted treatment works in 51% of cases, ordinary psychotherapy in 28%, and the targeted treatment also starts to work sooner.

- Benefit: Fourteen studies involving adults whose loved ones died of non‑violent causes and who had not previously sought mental‑health care reveal that prolonged grief disorder affects 9.8% of this group (95% CI 6.8–14.0), meaning about 10 out of every 100 bereaved adults are impacted. The rate rises with age. Another study tested the diagnostic criteria on 291 bereaved individuals through interviews at 0–6, 6–12, and 12–24 months after loss. The core symptom is intense longing for the deceased; at least five of nine additional criteria must also be present. The first five criteria are emotional numbness, shock, feeling life lacks meaning, distrust of others, and distress caused by the loss. The remaining four are difficulty accepting the loss, trouble defining one’s own identity, avoidance of reality, and an inability to carry on daily life. All these symptoms must appear daily or be severe enough to impair functioning, and they must persist for at least six months after the death. A randomized trial of 95 participants receiving 16 therapy sessions over 19 weeks found a 51% success rate for specialized treatment versus 28% for standard interpersonal psychotherapy (P = 0.02, a statistically significant difference). Consequently, only 4.3 patients need to be treated for one additional person to experience improvement.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>

- Notes: Don’t fixate on exact time limits. The real indicator is whether grief interferes with eating, sleeping, work, or childcare — if so, it’s time to seek help. The appropriate clinics are psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology departments, all of which are available at public psychiatric hospitals. This condition is known as prolonged grief disorder or complex grief in various sources; required duration varies from six to twelve months across different guidelines. The 9.8% prevalence figure stems from 14 studies with differing methodologies, so authors caution that it may not apply universally to all populations.

### 9. Do not start by paying for grief counseling; first see whether your grief is really stuck (the signs in item 8)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The other way around, you save a counseling fee.
- In plain terms: Arranging psychological counseling for every bereaved person brings a small benefit, and after a while it can no longer be seen, because most people would have eased on their own. For people who already clearly cannot adapt, treatment works as well as treatment for other psychological problems. So check item 8 first (grief that is stuck, go to psychiatry). Spend this money only if it matches.
- Benefit: 61 controlled studies taken together. ### 10. After a separation or a divorce, do not drop yibao (医保 — basic medical insurance), and do not get through it on alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Keep yibao going, and stop the alcohol. The hard part is that this stretch makes it easy to get through the days on alcohol.
- In plain terms: People who are separated or divorced have a clearly higher chance of dying early than people who are still married. The rise is larger for men, and for people who are younger. What you can hold yourself is this: do not let yibao lapse, and do not get through it on tobacco and alcohol.
- Benefit: 32 studies that registered people first and then followed them, taken together, covering over 6500000 people, 160000 deaths, over 755000 divorces, and 11 countries. ### 12. In the first months after a blow, push back every big decision that cannot be undone
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You only push the decision back, and you may miss some opportunities because of that. The hard part is that at the time it feels as if the opportunity will not wait.
- In plain terms: In this stretch your judgment of every "opportunity" is at its worst. In an experiment, sad people were willing to take a bit over 10% less money, and up to a bit over 30% less, so they would not have to wait 3 months. Anything larger than a month of income, or anything that would cost a lot to undo, gets pushed back for 3 months. Tell it first to a person who has no stake in the matter. Do not decide the same day.
- Benefit: Avoid making a property disposal you cannot undo, in the stretch just after the blow, when judgment is at its worst. This kind of thing includes selling a home, quitting a job, putting a pension or a compensation payment out as a lump sum, remarrying in a hurry, and standing guarantee for someone. The amount of this kind of decision is usually on the scale of several tens of thousands of yuan up to a home, so the benefit is set at medium. Official documents likewise list "a family blow, job loss, and dropping out of school" as psychological-crisis risks that the community should go and look for on its own initiative. The state of this stretch is already treated as a state that needs an outsider to lend a hand. The grief guide of the US National Institute on Aging (NIA) lists "making decisions is hard" as a common feature of grief. It suggests: if you can choose, push major life decisions back until you feel better. While you are grieving and may not be able to think clearly, it is better not to make a large change such as selling a home or quitting a job. A study ran a set of experiments. The researchers assigned people at random, put some of them into a sad state, and then had them choose "take the money now" or "take the money after 3 months." Compared with people in a calm mood, sad people were willing to take 13% to 34% less money so they would not have to wait 3 months. This was sadness brought on in an experiment, not a real bereavement. In 2023 the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warned about a scam: someone poses as a funeral home and tells the family that if they do not pay more at once, the funeral will be cancelled. The first step it gives is to resist the pressure to "do it right now." A proper business will give you time to decide.
- Evidence grade: B

- Notes: If there is no such person with no stake in the matter beside you, call 12356 and talk it through once (see item 11 of this section: if you need someone to talk with you, call 12356). ### 13. Do not treat death as a way to pay a debt: life insurance within 2 years does not pay, a work injury is not recognized, and the debt is still taken from the estate first

- Benefit: Article 44 of the Insurance Law governs contracts that pay the insurance money on the condition that the insured person dies. ### 14. After a traffic crash, an assault, or a disaster, do not count on a single-session psychological debriefing done right afterward; if a month later the nightmares, flashbacks, and constant dread are still severe, go to a psychiatry department for trauma-focused psychological treatment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A registration fee plus a treatment fee, usually 8 to 12 sessions. The hard part is that the treatment has to go back and touch the stretch of experience you most fear remembering.
- In plain terms: After a serious event, nightmares and being easily startled in the first weeks are common, and most people ease on their own. If it is still severe after a month, it may be post-traumatic stress disorder, and psychological treatment aimed at the trauma has a clear effect. Pulling someone, right after the event, to tell the whole course once does not prevent this illness. In a trial, the people who had it done were worse 3 years later.
- Benefit: The National Health Commission's Standard for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Mental Disorders (2020 edition) states that on average about 8% of people who go through a psychological trauma go on to post-traumatic stress disorder. The core features are these. Nightmares and flashbacks: the scene of the event keeps coming back. Avoidance: staying away from topics, places, and people connected with the event. Constant watchfulness: easily startled, and insomnia. The diagnosis is made only when the symptoms last more than 1 month and seriously affect family, study, or work. In the section on acute stress disorder, the standard says most people return to normal through their own adjustment or through crisis intervention in the acute period. The treatment effect is in a 2020 pooled analysis: 114 trials that assigned people by lot, 8171 people. Trauma-focused cognitive behavioral treatment, compared with waiting for treatment or with usual care, had an SMD of -1.32 (95% CI -1.57 to -1.08, the range the true value likely sits in), and the evidence quality was moderate. Eye-movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) was -1.23 (-1.69 to -0.76), and the evidence quality was low. SMD and d are the same ruler. Above 0.8 counts as large, and both of these are past that line. Ordinary supportive counseling, compared with waiting, showed no difference (-0.43, -0.90 to 0.04). A 2013 Cochrane review by the same group of authors had 70 trials and 4761 people, and pointed the same way. Trauma-focused cognitive behavioral treatment was -1.62 (-2.03 to -1.21). EMDR was -1.17 (-2.04 to -0.30). The evidence quality rated that time was very low. The British NICE 2018 guideline arranges it this way. For an adult whose symptoms are already severe within a month of the event, offer individual trauma-focused cognitive behavioral treatment. For someone diagnosed after a month, or whose symptoms are obvious, offer the same, usually 8 to 12 sessions. If the trauma was not caused by war and it is still not better after a full 3 months, also offer EMDR. If the symptoms are light and do not reach a diagnosis, watch through the first month, and arrange a review within that month. The psychological treatments listed in the domestic standard are the same kinds: prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, and EMDR. A single-session debriefing done right after the event is covered in the material that follows. A 2002 Cochrane review pooled 11 trials. A single individual debriefing did not lighten the distress, and it did not prevent post-traumatic stress disorder. At 3 to 5 months the OR was 1.22 (0.60–2.46), no difference. A trial among them followed people to 1 year, and the people who had the debriefing had a higher risk of the illness instead. The OR was 2.88 (1.11–7.53), about 2.9 times. The authors' conclusion was: stop forcing a debriefing on people who have been traumatized. Another trial enrolled patients hospitalized after a traffic crash, assigned by lot. At 3 years, the debriefing group was clearly worse on psychiatric symptoms, anxiety while riding in a car, pain, physical problems, overall ability to live, and money problems. People whose flashbacks and avoidance were already severe at the start still had symptoms 3 years later if they had the debriefing. Those who did not have it recovered. The NICE guideline states plainly: do not offer a psychologically led debriefing to prevent or to treat post-traumatic stress disorder. The reason is that neither doing it individually nor doing it in a group showed a benefit, and there are signs of being worse than not doing it.
