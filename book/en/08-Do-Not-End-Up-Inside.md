# 8. Don't Ruin Yourself: Legal and Property Safety

This section calculates money and personal liberty rather than converting outcomes into life expectancy. It focuses on three things: what legal liabilities you bear, how much money following the rules can save you in payouts, and what procedural rights you have when trouble strikes. All legal provisions and article numbers have been verified against official source texts.

The items in this chapter are organized by topic below, with item numbers shown in parentheses.

**Avoiding scams**: Ask for an immediate payment freeze the moment you realize you've been scammed (Item 2), hard rules for anti-fraud (Item 3), seeing a face on video does not count as identity verification (Item 4), never lend your bank cards or SIM cards (Item 8), check your credit report for free (Item 9), getting scammed in second-hand online trading (Item 22), ask for the price before on-site repairs (Item 27), never carry luggage or items for strangers (Item 29), and helping someone scan your face is an "AB loan" trap (Item 45).

**Signing, borrowing, and debts**: Read the document completely before signing (Item 17), write a clear IOU when lending money (Item 18), protecting your rights has a statute of limitations (Item 19), report your assets truthfully under court enforcement (Item 20), getting blacklisted as a dishonest judgment debtor (Item 21), never serve as a nominal legal representative (Item 28), and when a family member incurs gambling debts (Item 44).

**Conflicts and grievances**: Call the police first when a conflict breaks out (Item 10), fight back against unavoidable attacks (Item 11), don't seek revenge over past grudges (Item 12), never take it out on unrelated bystanders (Item 13), having thoughts of mutually assured destruction (Item 14), when someone close says "no one gets out of this alive" (Item 15), and the loophole of insuring someone before harming them is completely closed (Item 38).

**Romance and marriage**: Think twice before handing over large monetary gifts (Item 23), pay bride price via bank transfer (Item 24), put premarital property in writing (Item 25), verify marital status before getting married (Item 26), and call the police first if subjected to domestic violence (Item 43).

**Reporting to police, criminal allegations, and evidence**: Hire a lawyer first when summoned (Item 5), step forward and clarify the facts voluntarily after an incident (Item 6), being reported with fabricated allegations (Item 33), insufficient evidence must lead to acquittal (Item 34), apply for state compensation (Item 35), request a case acceptance receipt on the spot when reporting a crime (Item 39), never bribe case investigators (Item 40), record conversations that might turn hostile (Item 41), and take wide-angle establishing shots of the scene first (Item 42).

**Online speech and cyberbullying**: Do not insult others or spread rumors online (Item 16), and turn on anti-harassment protections first when facing cyberbullying (Item 37).

**Sex and extortion**: Never engage in sexual activity if the other party is not fully conscious (Item 31), call the police directly when blackmailed over nude chats (Item 32), and victims seeking compensation should never meet the other party alone (Item 36).

**Driving, dog walking, and theft prevention**: Never flee the scene of a traffic accident (Item 1), never touch the steering wheel after drinking (Item 7), always keep dogs on a leash (Item 30), and install proper locks on doors and windows (Item 46).
### 1. In a traffic accident, stop, help the injured, and call the police—do not flee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Stopping, turning on hazard lights, helping the injured, calling the police, and waiting for traffic police takes at most an hour or two. In a minor accident with no injuries and no dispute over the facts, take photos first. Then write down the other party's name, phone number, license plate, driver's license number, insurance policy number, and the impact locations. After both parties sign, you can leave the scene and negotiate privately.
- In plain terms: If you flee, you are presumed fully at fault for the accident by default. Your driver's license will be revoked, and if fleeing constitutes a crime, you will be barred from ever testing for a license again for life. There is an even more practical reality: vehicle damage and third-party liability insurance will not pay a cent for hit-and-runs, leaving you to cover hundreds of thousands in compensation out of your own pocket. Staying at the scene to help the injured and call the police takes at most an hour or two.
- Benefit: The fleeing party must assume full liability for the accident; liability can only be reduced if you can prove the other party was also at fault. Fleeing also results in driver's license revocation, and if the flight constitutes a crime, a lifetime ban on reapplying for a driver's license. If the incident constitutes the crime of causing a traffic accident, fleeing brings a sentence of 3 to 7 years in prison; if fleeing leads to someone's death, the sentence is 7 years or more. Insurance will not help either: commercial auto insurance for vehicle damage and third-party liability explicitly excludes coverage for "hit-and-run traffic accidents" and "deliberately damaging or fabricating the scene, or destroying evidence," leaving you to pay hundreds of thousands in compensation entirely out of pocket (nationwide).

- Notes: If someone is injured, help the injured first, then call the police. If the scene must be disturbed to rescue someone, mark the original positions of the vehicle and person first. Driving away thinking "I barely felt a bump" can still be classified as a hit-and-run after the fact; when in doubt, call the police. The insurance rule cites model clauses, which are the insurance industry's standardized template; what is actually covered depends on the terms in your specific policy.

### 2. If you realize you've been scammed, immediately call 110 or 96110 to request a stop-payment—don't try to investigate on your own first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Make a phone call and clearly provide the transfer time, amount, and recipient's account number. Keep screenshots of the transfer and your chat records.
- In plain terms: The brief window right after money leaves your account matters most. Police can issue an emergency stop-payment and quickly freeze the funds, and banks are required to comply. Once scammers move and scatter the money across multiple accounts, the chances of recovering it drop sharply. Call 110 or 96110 right away instead of trying to check or investigate things on your own first. If you get a call from 96110, it is the police trying to warn you—make sure to answer.
- Benefit: Public security authorities have the power to conduct real-time inquiries, emergency stop-payments, and rapid freezes on illicit funds. Banks and payment institutions must cooperate. Frozen funds are returned to victims through established legal procedures. Once money is transferred away and dispersed, the recovery rate drops substantially, making early stop-payments far more effective. 96110 is the unified national anti-fraud warning hotline; an incoming call means police are trying to intervene, so always answer it (nationwide, from December 2022).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2022). 反电信网络诈骗法（第二十、三十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>；福建省公安厅 (2023). 96110 来电，请务必接听. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202303/t20230306_6126156.htm>（省级公安机关页面）
- Notes: Anyone claiming to be from the police, procuratorate, or courts who tells you to transfer money to a "secure account" is running a scam. The law establishes a "stop-payment and freeze mechanism," which simply means directing banks to withhold the funds. How much can be recovered depends entirely on whether the money is still in the account when you report it. Descriptions of the National Anti-Fraud Center app and 96110 are cited from the Fujian Provincial Department of Public Security, as the original page on the Ministry of Public Security website was inaccessible.

### 3. Remember the hard rules against fraud: never trust unknown callers, never share private info, never click unverified links, verify before transferring money; the seven most common scams share this exact shape
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Memorize the "three nevers and one verify" rule, read through the seven scam types below, install the National Anti-Fraud Center app, and always pick up calls from 96110. Whenever someone asks you to transfer money, share your screen, download an unfamiliar app, click a link, or read out a verification code, hang up first and verify through official channels. The real challenge is that scammers push hard and create panic; you have to resist the pressure and hang up first.
- In plain terms: If someone asks you to transfer money, share your screen, download an unknown app, click a link, or read out an SMS code, hang up immediately and call back using an official public number to verify. Order-brushing rebates, fake investment platforms, bogus online loans, impersonating customer service, impersonating law enforcement, romance investment scams, and escort-task traps all boil down to asking you to send money first. Spotting them beforehand is far cheaper than chasing lost funds later.
- Benefit: The law requires the government and relevant departments to publicize the common methods used in telecom and online fraud. Public security organs work alongside financial institutions, telecom operators, and cyberspace administrations to build an early warning and deterrence system that intervenes whenever someone is suspected of being scammed. 96110 is the national hotline for these early warnings and intervention calls; if it rings, pick it up. In June 2026, the Ministry of Public Security reported that the top 10 scam types accounted for 85% of all telecom fraud cases. Order-brushing rebates were the most frequent, making up 25%. Fake online investment and wealth management caused the largest financial losses, accounting for about 40% of all stolen funds. The Fujian Provincial Public Security Department broke down the key features of each type as follows. Order-brushing rebates: "Anyone inviting you to make easy money together almost certainly wants to take yours"; if you cannot finish the assigned tasks, your principal will never be returned. Fake investment and wealth management: fake accounts in chat groups brag about huge returns, pay out small initial profits, lure you into depositing larger sums, and then demand "taxes" when you try to withdraw. Online loans: "Legitimate loans never require upfront security deposits or fabricated bank transaction records; whenever you encounter this, it is definitely fake." Impersonating customer service: claiming your VIP membership renewed automatically, will trigger recurring charges, or will hurt your credit score, pressuring you to transfer money to cancel it. Scammers in this category often instruct you to download an online meeting app and enable screen sharing; once shared, everything on your phone screen—"including pop-up notifications, SMS verification codes, and password entries—is clearly visible to them." The Shanghai Anti-Fraud Center warns: "Any mention of 'meeting ID', 'screen sharing', or 'remote control' is a scam." Impersonating law enforcement: "Public security, procuratorates, and courts have no such thing as a 'secure account'; anyone claiming to handle cases or take depositions over the phone, QQ, WeChat, or the internet is an imposter." Abroad, scammers also impersonate Chinese embassies and consulates, claiming you are involved in money laundering, have urgent documents to collect, or have issues with your passport or visa, then transfer your call to domestic "police." The Chinese Embassy in the United States specifically warned international students: "Anyone claiming to be staff from an embassy, consulate, telecom operator, or customs who offers to transfer your call directly to public security agencies on grounds of suspected crimes is a scammer!" Romance investment scams (pig-butchering): "Never trust an online 'lover' you have never met in person and have only seen in photos or videos"; they refuse real video calls or face-to-face meetings yet constantly have urgent emergencies requiring money. Escort-task traps: promising in-person hookups only after you complete "tasks," demanding more and more money along the way. Catching these scams beforehand is far cheaper than requesting an emergency payment freeze afterward. How much an emergency freeze can recover depends entirely on whether the funds are still in the scammer's bank account when you report it. For how to freeze payments, see item 2 (call 110 or 96110 to request an emergency freeze) (nationwide, from December 2022).

- Notes: Anyone claiming to be police, prosecutors, judges, customer service, your company executive, or platform risk control demanding that you "cooperate with an investigation," "cancel a membership," "brush orders for commission," or "access insider investments" is running the same playbook. Real loans never require security deposits, and judicial organs do not maintain "secure accounts"; remembering just these two facts will block the majority of scams. This is graded B because the slogan is official promotional guidance rather than an intervention with precise trial data showing "how much fraud decreases after memorizing it." The rules and breakdowns come from provincial public security department notices. The Ministry of Public Security reported 10 high-incidence fraud categories in 2026. The remaining categories include impersonating e-commerce or logistics customer service, loan and credit reporting scams, fake online gaming transactions, online matchmaking scams, impersonating leaders or acquaintances, and fake flight cancellation or change alerts. All these follow the identical pattern: in the end, they all demand that you transfer money. If you are abroad and receive a call claiming to be an embassy, consulate, or domestic police saying you are involved in a crime, have documents waiting, or have passport issues, hang up immediately. International students are prime targets for this scam. If an acquaintance asks you in person to "help by scanning your face" to process a loan—not asking for your money, but saddling you with debt—see item 45 (helping someone by scanning your face). For outsourced store-management scams encountered when doing business, see chapter 12, item 24 (outsourced online store operations).

### 4. Seeing a face on video or hearing a familiar voice is not verification: if money is involved, hang up first and call back using an old number saved in your contacts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. It takes one extra phone call and waiting a few extra minutes. Avoid posting front-facing videos and long voice recordings on public social media accounts. The hard part is that scammers often create intense urgency; you must resist transferring money on the spot.
- In plain terms: Seeing a face or hearing a familiar voice does not prove who is on the other end. Whenever money or bank transfers come up, hang up immediately, then call back using the old phone number already saved in your contacts, or check with a mutual acquaintance. If they claim bad reception or say they are in a meeting to refuse a live video call, treat it straight away as a scam.
- Benefit: The Cyber Security Bureau of the Ministry of Public Security outlines three countermeasures. The first is movement verification: ask the other person to turn their head smoothly from side to side, or fully cover their face with an open palm and then quickly pull it away. The official guidance notes that "current AI technology is prone to stuttering, blurring, or distortion when simulating these complex facial occlusions and shifting light and shadow." The second is listening for vocal details: "AI-generated voice clips often lack natural breathing pauses or emotional inflection, or carry a slight robotic feel and abnormal background noise." The third is multi-channel verification. The official text calls this the "core defense": "Hang up and immediately call a familiar, frequently used number (never dial back the incoming suspicious number), or verify indirectly through a mutual third-party contact." The operational slogan is: "Never trust video or voice blindly; verify through movement and multiple channels." Legally, deepfake face-swapping and voice synthesis have long been outlawed for fraud. For face generation, face swapping, synthetic speech, and voice cloning services, platforms are legally required to display prominent labels. No organization or individual may maliciously delete, alter, forge, or conceal these labels. However, legitimate platforms obey rules while criminals ignore them, so the absence of an AI label does not prove the person is real. In one case released by the Criminal Investigation Bureau of the Ministry of Public Security, a 14-person criminal ring was caught with over 50,000 synthetic dynamic facial videos, over 500,000 pieces of personal citizen information, and 60 seized cloud servers (national, labeling requirement effective September 1, 2025).
- Evidence grade: B

If the other person makes excuses like "bad signal" or "in a meeting" and refuses a live video call or refuses to perform the requested movements, treat it directly as a scam. Physical motion tests are only an auxiliary check—AI models keep improving, so multi-channel callback verification remains the only foolproof defense. If money has already been sent, see Item 2 in this section (call 110 immediately to request an emergency stop-payment). To prevent your own face and voice from being harvested, see Section 14. ### 5. If accused or summoned, get a lawyer first: never settle privately, never delete records
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Lawyer fees start at several thousand yuan. Those facing financial hardship can apply for legal aid to get a lawyer free of charge. When questioned, answer only questions directly related to the case and leave the rest to your lawyer. The hardest part is staying calm on the spot and not rushing to explain yourself.
- In plain terms: You can hire a lawyer from the very first time you are brought in for questioning. Once your lawyer requests a meeting, the detention center must arrange it within 48 hours at the latest, and no one is allowed to listen in. You do not have to answer questions that have nothing to do with the case.
- Benefit: From the very first interrogation or from the moment compulsory measures are taken (restricting personal liberty), you have the right to retain a lawyer. While the case is still under investigation by public security organs, you may retain only a licensed lawyer, not anyone outside the legal profession, as your defense counsel. When the lawyer requests a meeting, the detention center must arrange it within 48 hours at the latest, and the meeting cannot be monitored. No one may be forced to prove their own guilt. You have the right to refuse to answer questions unrelated to the case. A single summons or compulsory appearance can last at most 12 hours, or at most 24 hours for exceptionally major or complex cases. A summons notifies you to come in for questioning, whereas a compulsory appearance brings you in by force. Successive summonses may not be used to detain someone under disguised custody. Anyone who fabricates facts to falsely accuse another person with the intent of subjecting them to criminal liability faces up to 3 years in prison, or 3 to 10 years if the consequences are serious. If a case is withdrawn, dropped without prosecution, or results in an acquittal after detention or arrest, one can apply for state compensation. Daily compensation is calculated based on the national average daily wage of enterprise employees from the previous year (nationwide).

Chat histories, call logs, and travel records are the evidence that proves your innocence; deleting them is destroying your own defense. Misremembering facts or filing a report that later turns out to be unfounded does not constitute the crime of false accusation and framing. The procedure is the exact same regardless of whether the accused is a man or a woman. ### 6. Turn yourself in when trouble strikes: voluntary surrender cuts prison time by up to 40%, minor crimes can get even more or full exemption, and running the clock on the statute of limitations is a dead end
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Go to the police station yourself to turn yourself in, or stay put, call the police, and truthfully recount what happened. The hard part is overcoming the urge to "wait and see—maybe nobody will investigate."
- In plain terms: Turning yourself in can cut a prison sentence by up to 40%, and for minor offenses, punishment can be waived entirely. Confessing truthfully only after police track you down usually cuts the sentence by at most 20%. Running away or denying everything gets you none of these reductions. Hoping to hide out until the statute of limitations expires is a dead end: once a case is formally filed, running away removes all time limits, so authorities can prosecute no matter how many years pass.
- Benefit: Article 67 of the Criminal Law: Voluntarily turning oneself in after committing a crime and truthfully confessing one's crime constitutes voluntary surrender (*zishou*). Those who turn themselves in may receive a lighter or mitigated penalty; where the crime is minor, punishment may be exempted. Those who do not turn themselves in but truthfully confess (*tanbai*) may receive a lighter penalty; if a truthful confession prevents particularly serious consequences, the penalty may be mitigated. Sentencing guidelines from the Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate establish explicit deduction ranges. The base sentence is calculated from case facts, and the percentages below are deducted from it. Voluntary surrender can reduce the base sentence by up to 40%; for minor crimes, by more than 40% or exempt punishment according to law. Under confession, truthfully admitting one's crime reduces the base sentence by up to 20%. Truthfully confessing a more serious offense of the same type not yet known to judicial authorities reduces it by 10% to 30%. If truthful confession prevents particularly serious consequences, it reduces the sentence by 30% to 50%. Pleading guilty and accepting punishment reduces it by up to 30%. Combining voluntary surrender, major confession, restitution/compensation, and victim settlement can reduce the base sentence by up to 60%; for minor crimes, by more than 60% or exempt punishment according to law. The statute of limitations is tiered into four brackets based on the statutory maximum penalty: 5 years, 10 years, 15 years, and 20 years (Criminal Law Article 87). But Criminal Law Article 88 sets an exception: once procuratorates, public security organs, or state security organs have formally opened an investigation, or a court has accepted the case, fleeing to evade investigation or trial removes all statute-of-limitations restrictions (nationwide; sentencing guidelines effective July 1, 2021).

### 10. Call the police instead of throwing punches in a conflict: striking first makes the other person's retaliation count as self-defense

- Cost: Free. Step back into a crowded area to call the police, and let surveillance cameras catch their actions. The hard part is holding back from talking back or shoving.
- In plain terms: Hitting someone brings 5 to 10 days of detention and a fine of 500 to 1,000 yuan. - Benefit: Anyone who assaults another person or intentionally inflicts injury is detained for 5 to 10 days and fined 500 to 1,000 yuan. Gang brawls are handled as "picking quarrels and provoking trouble" (creating disturbances or causing commotions without cause), carrying up to 15 days of detention and a fine of up to 2,000 yuan. ### 11. Fight back only when you cannot escape, target only the active attacker, and stop the moment they stop
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Lawyer fees start at several thousand yuan; those facing financial hardship can apply for legal aid. The primary cost comes afterward: police often initially open a case for intentional injury, requiring you to cooperate with the investigation or even spend over a dozen days in detention first. The hardest part is staying calm and holding steady through that stretch.
- In plain terms: When someone is actively attacking you or someone else, stepping in to stop them and injuring the attacker does not carry criminal liability as long as your response does not clearly go too far. If the attacker is committing aggravated assault, murder, robbery, rape, or kidnapping, injuring or even killing them is not considered excessive defense. However, you may only strike the person who is actively attacking, and you must stop the moment they stop. Even if investigators ultimately rule in your favor, a criminal case may still be opened against you first, and you may spend days in detention before you are cleared.
- Benefit: The Criminal Law defines what constitutes justifiable self-defense. When national or public interests, or the personal, property, or other rights of yourself or others are undergoing an active unlawful attack, taking action to stop it and injuring the active attacker is justifiable self-defense and carries no criminal liability. The same article provides: "Where justifiable self-defense clearly exceeds the necessary limits and causes major harm, criminal liability shall be borne, but punishment shall be mitigated or remitted." Another clause adds: "Where defensive acts are taken against ongoing assault, murder, robbery, rape, kidnapping, or other violent crimes that severely endanger personal safety, causing injury or death to the unlawful attacker, it does not constitute excessive defense and carries no criminal liability." Guiding opinions jointly issued by the Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, and Ministry of Public Security define these boundaries further. Establishing excessive defense requires meeting two conditions at the same time: it must "clearly exceed the necessary limits" and "cause major harm"—neither can be missing. "Causing major harm" means inflicting severe injury or death on the unlawful attacker; minor injuries or lesser harm do not count. The unlawful attack is considered over only when the attacker has genuinely lost the capacity to continue or has genuinely abandoned it. Defensive action must be directed exclusively at the unlawful attacker themselves. If the other party's attack is manifestly minor and you can tell as much, yet you respond directly with force sufficient to cause severe injury or death, it will not be recognized as a defensive act. Procedural costs are documented in official guiding cases. On September 1, the Kunshan Public Security Bureau determined it was justifiable self-defense under Article 20, Paragraph 3 of the Criminal Law and dropped the case. Public security authorities placed him under criminal detention for suspected intentional injury. The procuratorate determined it was justifiable self-defense and refused to approve the arrest, after which public security released him. Once a suspect is detained, if public security deems an arrest necessary, it must submit a request to the procuratorate for review and approval within 3 days. Under special circumstances, this may be extended by 1 to 4 days. For major suspects committing crimes on the run, repeatedly, or in gangs, it may be extended up to 30 days. The procuratorate then makes its decision within 7 days (nationwide).

### 32. If someone demands money after sex or nude chat by threatening to call the police, leak photos, or inform your employer: do not pay a cent, do not delete any records, and report it to the police immediately

- Cost: Free. Take screenshots of their account, chat history, and payment QR codes, and bring your phone to the police station. The hard part is resisting the impulse to "pay to make it go away."
- In plain terms: Demanding money under threats like "I'll call the police" or "I'll send your photos to your workplace" is extortion. Do not pay a single cent, do not delete any chat logs, and call the police on the spot. If they actually leak your photos, they have additionally violated your right to privacy. Extorting between 2,000 and 5,000 yuan or more carries a prison sentence of up to 3 years; 30,000 to 100,000 yuan or more carries 3 to 10 years.

If the amount is huge or there are other serious circumstances, the penalty is fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years but not more than ten years, and a fine. If the amount is especially huge or there are other especially serious circumstances, the penalty is fixed-term imprisonment of not less than ten years, and a fine. ### 34. Insufficient evidence legally requires an acquittal, coerced confessions must be excluded, and appeals and retrials remain after sentencing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Going through the legal procedures does not incur official fees, but appeals and retrials take a great deal of time. You usually need to hire a lawyer, with legal fees starting from several thousand yuan. Those facing financial hardship can apply for legal aid (see item 5 in this section, "hire a lawyer first when accused"). The real difficulty is enduring round after round of waiting without giving up.
- In plain terms: The threshold for conviction is written into the Criminal Procedure Law: evidence used to decide a case must be verified through legal procedures, and all evidence across the case must be weighed together to rule out reasonable doubt. If that standard is not met, the court must issue an acquittal. Confessions obtained through torture or statements extracted by violent threats must be excluded and cannot serve as the basis for a ruling. Even after a judgment takes effect, you, your legal representative, and your close relatives can still file an appeal or petition.
- Benefit: Article 55 of the Criminal Procedure Law: evidence is reliable and sufficient when it meets these conditions—all evidence used to determine a case has been verified as true through statutory procedures, and, considering all evidence across the whole case, reasonable doubt has been excluded regarding the established facts. Ruling out reasonable doubt means that when looking at all evidence together, the established facts leave no plausible alternative explanation. Article 56: suspect or defendant confessions collected through illegal methods such as torture to extract a confession, and witness testimony or victim statements collected through illegal methods such as violence or threats, must be excluded. If evidence that should be excluded is discovered during investigation, review for prosecution, or trial, it must be excluded in accordance with the law and cannot serve as the basis for a recommendation for prosecution, a prosecution decision, or a judgment. Article 200, item (3): if the evidence is insufficient and the defendant cannot be found guilty, an acquittal verdict must be rendered on the grounds of insufficient evidence and unsustainable charges. Article 252 provides the avenue for appeal: the parties, their legal representatives, and their close relatives may file an appeal or petition with a people's court or people's procuratorate against a legally effective judgment or ruling, though filing an appeal does not suspend the execution of the judgment or ruling. Article 253 lists the circumstances under which a people's court must conduct a retrial (national).

- In plain terms: If you were unlawfully detained, held past statutory detention limits, or arrested, and your case was later dismissed, non-prosecuted, or resulted in an acquittal, you can apply for state compensation. Days spent in detention are compensated on a daily basis at 495.94 yuan per day, effective May 18, 2026. If the consequences were severe, an additional solace payment for emotional distress is awarded. No compensation is paid if prosecution was dropped merely because the offense was minor, nor if the statute of limitations had run out, if you were under the legal age of criminal responsibility, or if you intentionally gave a false confession.
- Benefit: Article 17 of the State Compensation Law outlines circumstances in which victims have the right to compensation. The first is detention: where detention measures are imposed on a citizen in violation of the Criminal Procedure Law, or where lawful detention exceeds the statutory time limit, followed by a decision to dismiss the case, non-prosecute, or acquit, terminating criminal proceedings. The second is arrest: where arrest measures are taken against a citizen, followed by a decision to dismiss the case, non-prosecute, or acquit, terminating criminal proceedings. The third is retrial: where a case is retried under trial supervision procedures resulting in an acquittal, and the original criminal penalty has already been executed. Trial supervision procedures refer to reopening and retrying an already legally effective judgment. Article 33: Where personal liberty is infringed, daily compensation is calculated according to the state's average daily wage of urban non-private sector employees for the preceding year. Article 35: Where emotional distress is caused, the organ must eliminate negative effects, rehabilitate reputation, and offer apologies within the scope of the infringement's impact; where severe consequences result, it must pay an appropriate solace payment for emotional distress. Article 19 lists six situations where the state pays no compensation: 1. Where an individual is detained or sentenced because they intentionally made a false confession or fabricated other evidence of guilt. 2. Where individuals not held criminally responsible under Articles 17 and 18 of the Criminal Law are detained, such as those below the age of criminal liability, or individuals with mental illnesses who caused harm when unable to recognize or control their conduct. 3. Where individuals exempt from criminal liability under the Criminal Procedure Law are detained, including cases where the offense was clearly minor and caused little harm and is not deemed a crime, where the statute of limitations has expired, or where the suspect has died. This also includes cases where the offense was minor and the procuratorate decided against prosecution (discretionary or relative non-prosecution), as well as conditional non-prosecution of minors after the probation period ends, and non-prosecution following settlement with the victim. 4. Personal acts of case officers unrelated to the exercise of their official powers. 5. Harm resulting from intentional acts such as self-harm or self-mutilation. 6. Other circumstances prescribed by law. Article 7 of the 2015 Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate judicial interpretation on criminal compensation reaffirmed: the state does not compensate for detention of individuals who bear no criminal liability or who receive relative non-prosecution; however, if after prosecution a court erroneously sentenced them to criminal detention, fixed-term imprisonment, or life imprisonment and executed it, the period of continued confinement served after that judgment became legally effective must still be compensated. ### 38. Taking out insurance on someone before killing them is legally a total dead end: not a single cent is paid, and you face combined penalties for intentional homicide and insurance fraud
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Secretly buying life insurance on an adult family member without their consent makes the contract completely void. If the policyholder intentionally causes the insured person's death, the insurance company will not pay a dime, and intentionally killing someone you stand to inherit from strips you of all inheritance rights. Criminal law explicitly classifies this fraud as a specific offense, punished concurrently with intentional homicide, carrying penalties up to the death penalty. You will not get a single cent.
- Benefit: Article 34 of the Insurance Law: A contract that conditions the payment of insurance benefits on death is void without the insured's prior consent and approval of the insured amount. The insured is the person covered. Parents insuring their minor children are exempt from this restriction. Article 39: The policyholder must obtain the insured's consent when designating a beneficiary. The policyholder is the person paying for the policy, and the beneficiary is the person who collects the payout after an incident. Article 43 governs intentional harm: If the policyholder intentionally causes the death, disability, or illness of the insured, the insurer bears no liability to pay insurance benefits. If the policyholder has paid premiums for two years or more, the cash value of the policy is refunded to other entitled parties. If a beneficiary intentionally causes the death, disability, or illness of the insured, or attempts to murder the insured, that beneficiary forfeits all beneficiary rights. Article 27, Paragraph 2: If the policyholder or insured intentionally causes an insured incident, the insurer has the right to terminate the contract, bears no liability for compensation or payment, and does not refund premiums except as provided under Article 43. Article 1125 of the Civil Code: A person who intentionally kills the decedent forfeits all inheritance rights. Article 198, Paragraph 1, Item 5 of the Criminal Law classifies "a policyholder or beneficiary intentionally causing the death, disability, or illness of the insured to fraudulently obtain insurance money" as insurance fraud. If the amount involved is relatively large, the penalty is fixed-term imprisonment of not more than 5 years, plus a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. If the amount is huge, imprisonment of 5 to 10 years. If the amount is especially huge, imprisonment of 10 years or more. Paragraph 2: If committing this act concurrently constitutes another crime, punishment is imposed according to the provisions for combined punishment of multiple crimes (where separate penalties for each crime are calculated together). Article 232: Whoever intentionally kills another person is sentenced to death, life imprisonment, or fixed-term imprisonment of not less than 10 years. ### 40. Never give money or gift cards to investigators or law enforcement: paying bribes is punishable by law, and bribing supervisory, enforcement, or judicial officers carries aggravated penalties
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Zero cost. Just refrain from doing it.
- In plain terms: Trying to "spend money to settle things" is an independent crime under criminal law, and the person giving the money will also be sentenced. Offering a bribe carries up to 3 years of imprisonment, or 3 to 10 years for serious circumstances, and 10 or more years or life imprisonment for especially serious circumstances. Bribing supervisory, administrative law enforcement, or judicial personnel is explicitly designated as an aggravated offense that receives harsher penalties. If you are extorted into giving money and do not obtain any illegitimate benefit, it is not considered bribery.
- Benefit: Article 389 of the Criminal Law defines bribery: "Whoever gives property to state personnel to seek illegitimate benefits commits the crime of offering a bribe." The same article also states: "Whoever, in economic interactions, gives property to state personnel in violation of state regulations in relatively large amounts, or gives kickbacks or handling fees under various names in violation of state regulations, shall be punished for offering a bribe." It also provides an exception: "Whoever gives property to state personnel because of extortion and obtains no illegitimate benefits does not commit the crime of offering a bribe." Sentencing is specified in Article 390 of the Criminal Law (amended by Criminal Law Amendment XII, effective March 1, 2024): "Whoever commits the crime of offering a bribe shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years or criminal detention, and shall also be fined; if the circumstances are serious because of seeking illegitimate benefits through bribery, or if major losses are caused to national interests, the sentence shall be fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years but not more than ten years, and a fine; if the circumstances are especially serious, or if especially major losses are caused to national interests, the sentence shall be fixed-term imprisonment of not less than ten years or life imprisonment, and a fine or confiscation of property." There are seven circumstances requiring aggravated punishment: "(1) Offering bribes multiple times or to multiple persons; (2) Offering bribes by state personnel; (3) Offering bribes in key national projects or major undertakings; (4) Offering bribes to seek job promotion, rank adjustment, or organizational reassignment; (5) Offering bribes to supervisory, administrative law enforcement, or judicial personnel; (6) Offering bribes to carry out illegal and criminal activities in fields such as ecological environment, finance and banking, production safety, food and drugs, disaster prevention and relief, social security, education, or healthcare; (7) Using illegal gains to offer bribes." Having a recording does not guarantee winning; the court must evaluate authenticity and relevance alongside other evidence. A recording only addresses whether you can prove what happened; deciding whether to sue and how long it will take is a separate calculation. For materials to preserve before leaving a job, see Chapter 19, Item 8 (Save your pay stubs and attendance records before quitting). The legal basis cited above applies to civil litigation; criminal and administrative procedures follow separate evidentiary rules.

### 42. After an incident, photograph the wide view first, then relative positions, and finally damage and injuries; do not delete original files
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Take a few tens of seconds to several minutes of footage with a phone. Ensure your own safety first, without delaying rescue or emergency handling.
- In plain terms: If you only take close-ups of a scratch on a car or an object on the ground, no one will know where it originally was a few days later. First shoot a wide-angle view capturing the intersection, street numbers, vehicles, people, and surroundings, then capture their relative positions, and finally record the details. If vehicles or objects must be moved, photograph them first whenever possible. Keep the original photos and videos on your phone; do not rely solely on compressed copies forwarded through messaging apps.
- Benefit: Article 66 of the Civil Procedure Law lists physical evidence, audio-visual materials, and electronic data as evidence. Article 14 of the Supreme People's Court Evidence Provisions classifies images, audio, and video as electronic data. Article 15 requires submitting original electronic data, and presenting video recordings as evidence requires providing the original storage medium. Photos and video recordings preserve transient scene conditions that can corroborate police reports, repair records, medical charts, and witness statements. The sequence of "wide view, relative positions, details" is practical best practice; it is not prescribed by statutory law (China-wide).
- Evidence grade: C

Graded C because the shooting sequence is empirical practical experience; the statutes only govern that photos and video recordings are admissible evidence and that originals must be provided. If someone is injured, or if there is fire, a gas leak, or other active hazards, rescue people and clear the area first—never delay emergency response just to take photos. ### 43. Handle domestic violence: call the police first to file an incident report, then apply for a court protection order—no divorce needed and free of charge
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. Applying for a personal safety protection order requires no court fees and no security bond. The main cost is the time spent calling the police, getting injuries documented, and writing the application. The hardest part is speaking out against family.
- In plain terms: Multi-country data show that more than a third of murdered women are killed by an intimate partner, often as the culmination of long-term abuse. Among abused women, those who have been choked face a far higher risk of being killed later on. If you are hit, call the police right away, then apply to the court for a protection order. The court must generally rule within 72 hours.
- Benefit: First, the risks. A systematic review combining data from 66 countries found that intimate partners committed 38.6% of female homicides, compared to 6.3% of male homicides. The authors noted that intimate partner homicides are often the culminating event of long-term abuse. Another study compared 506 cases of femicide and attempted femicide against 427 abused women who survived without such incidents. Women previously choked by an intimate partner had significantly higher odds compared to those who were not choked: the odds ratio (OR) for attempted homicide was 6.70 (95% CI 3.91–11.49), and for completed homicide, the OR was 7.48 (95% CI 4.53–12.35). Because homicide and attempted homicide are rare outcomes, the odds ratio closely approximates relative risk. Next, the law. Victims and close relatives can report abuse to the police. Public security organs must respond promptly, stop the domestic violence, investigate and collect evidence, and assist in obtaining medical care and injury evaluations. For minor offenses, police can issue a formal written warning (admonition letter) to the abuser. Anyone who suffers domestic violence or faces an imminent threat of domestic violence can apply to the court for a personal safety protection order. Once accepted, the court must issue a ruling within 72 hours, or within 24 hours in emergency situations. A protection order can prohibit the perpetrator from committing domestic violence; prohibit them from harassing, stalking, or contacting you and your close relatives; and order them to move out of your residence. Protection orders are valid for up to 6 months and can be extended before expiration. Filing does not require filing for divorce. The court can grant the order if it finds a "high likelihood" that domestic violence occurred. If the other party violates the protection order, the court can fine them up to 1,000 RMB, detain them for up to 15 days, or prosecute them for the crime of refusing to execute a court judgment or ruling if it constitutes a criminal offense. Repeated insults, threats, stalking, and harassment, as well as deliberately depriving someone of food or warmth, also legally count as domestic violence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2015). 反家庭暴力法（第二、十三、十五、十六、十九、二十三、二十八、二十九、三十、三十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7f9d60889>；最高人民法院 (2022). 关于办理人身安全保护令案件适用法律若干问题的规定（法释〔2022〕17 号，第一、三、六、十、十二条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/366021.html>；最高人民法院 (2016). 关于人身安全保护令案件相关程序问题的批复（法释〔2016〕15 号，第一、二项）. 平原县人民法院转载全文 <http://qdzy.sdcourt.gov.cn/dzpyfy/393141/393283/1461545/index.html>；Stöckl H 等 (2013). The global prevalence of intimate partner homicide: a systematic review. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)61030-2>；Glass N 等 (2008). Non-fatal strangulation is an important risk factor for homicide of women. Journal of Emergency Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2007.02.065>
- Notes: The primary beneficiaries are you and your family. The homicide statistics come from international studies; China has no publicly available statistics under identical metrics. Preserve every piece of evidence: police incident reports, formal written warnings, injury evaluations, hospital records, written guarantee letters from the abuser, chat logs, and audio recordings are all valid. For audio recordings, see item 41 of this section (Audio recording). You do not need to hire a lawyer to apply for a protection order, and rulings are usually issued within a few days. Seeking a divorce or claiming civil compensation is a separate lawsuit; under ordinary civil procedure, the first instance is required to conclude within 6 months, which can be extended, and you pay your own legal fees. Victims of domestic violence can apply for legal aid, and courts can defer, reduce, or waive court costs. A protection order is not an absolute physical guarantee of safety; if you face immediate danger, call 110 first. If a victim is coerced or terrified and unable to apply personally, close relatives, public security organs, the women's federation, the residents' committee, or the villagers' committee can apply on their behalf. If the victim is a friend or colleague, you can accompany them to report to the police or visit a hospital. If you witness a stranger being beaten inside their home, calling 110 is enough. Do not intervene physically yourself; for the reasoning, see Section 13, Item 37 (Coming across a group fight). For drafting child custody and visitation arrangements during a divorce, see Section 10, Item 23 (Child support and visitation).

### 44. If a family member runs up gambling debts, do not rush to pay them off: gambling debts have no legal protection, and loans taken to gamble are not marital debt
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is holding your ground and refusing to pay when creditors show up at your door or family members beg in tears. If a creditor actually sues, you must appear in court to defend yourself.
- In plain terms: Debts incurred at the gambling table have no legal protection in court. If a creditor lent someone money while fully aware it would be used for gambling, the court does not protect that debt either. Money borrowed by one spouse to gamble cannot be counted as a joint debt for both partners to repay. Without going through a formal trial, the court cannot hold the spouse who never borrowed the money liable.
- Benefit: A 2017 Supreme People's Court notice establishes four points: debts incurred by one party through illegal or criminal activities such as gambling or drug use receive no legal protection. Debts where the creditor knew or should have known that the borrowed funds were for gambling or drug use receive no legal protection. If one party borrows money in their own name to engage in illegal or criminal activities and asks the court to treat it as joint marital debt, the court will not support it. Without formal judicial trial proceedings, the party who did not borrow the money cannot be required to assume civil liability (nationwide, from 2017).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:最高人民法院 (2017). 关于依法妥善审理涉及夫妻债务案件有关问题的通知（法〔2017〕48 号，第二、四条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/36982.html>
- Notes: Rated B because this is an internal judicial notice rather than empirical data, and whether a creditor had prior knowledge depends on specific evidence. This notice was issued under the Marriage Law in effect at the time. For how joint marital debt is determined following the implementation of the Civil Code, see Section 10, Item 12 (Large loans taken out by a spouse). If the creditor genuinely did not know the money was used for gambling, the loan must still be repaid, but solely by the person who borrowed it. To prove that the money went toward gambling, you need bank transfer records, gambling platform logs, or public security case documents—preserve these early. If a creditor sues, ordinary first-instance civil proceedings are required by rule to conclude within 6 months (which can be extended), while summary proceedings take 3 months. Each side covers their own attorney fees; the losing party does not pay them. If creditors show up at your door to block or detain anyone, see Section 9, Item 15 (No detaining people over debt) and call the police immediately. If the gambler has reached the point of feeling suicidal, see Section 1, Item 37 (Call 12356 first when borrowing to fuel gambling). The beneficiaries are primarily spouses and immediate family members.

### 45. Never agree when an acquaintance asks you to "just scan your face to witness" a loan or read out an SMS code: this is an "AB loan" scam where you are the actual borrower, not a guarantor
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is saying no to friends or relatives to their faces. Even if they claim you are "just witnessing" or that "you bear no responsibility," refuse anyway. Never hand your phone, national ID card, or bank card to a broker, and do not scan your face or sign documents at a broker's direction. SMS verification codes are for your personal use only; never read them out even if asked by an acquaintance.
- In plain terms: An acquaintance asking you to "just scan your face to witness a loan" is actually taking out a bank loan in your name. You become the actual borrower, not a guarantor. Once the borrowed money is transferred to the acquaintance, the bank will still legally hold you responsible for repaying it. Even if the loan broker tricked you, as long as the bank acted in good faith without knowing about the fraud, you cannot simply cancel the loan agreement. A written promise from your acquaintance saying "I will pay it back" has no legal power over the bank.
- Benefit: A 2022 risk alert from the Fujian Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration detailed this scheme. Customer A lacked the creditworthiness to obtain a loan. The broker advised adding a guarantor and instructed A to find Person B, who had good credit standing. During the actual application, the broker used B's identity information to apply for the loan while handing the proceeds to A. The regulatory text stated that "the borrower is essentially B" and B "only discovered they were the actual borrower rather than a guarantor when contacted by bank debt collectors." A 2024 risk alert from the Shandong Regulatory Bureau noted that brokers often promise B that they "will bear zero liability," and coach B beforehand on how to answer the bank's pre-loan verification questions, while secretly listing B as the formal applicant. In 2024, the Shanghai Putuo District Court publicized a case where an agent took B's phone, downloaded the bank app, and directly applied for a loan under B's name. The funds were disbursed into B's bank account, B transferred them to A, and A paid over 80,000 yuan in service fees to the broker. An 8-person broker gang was sentenced to prison terms ranging from 10 months to 4 years for contract fraud. However, even if brokers are criminally convicted, the loan debt does not automatically shift away from the victim. Article 149 of the Civil Code states that when a third party commits fraud, the defrauded party may request revocation only if the other contracting party "knew or should have known" about the fraud. Here, the other contracting party is the bank; if the bank had no knowledge of the fraud, the contract cannot be revoked. Where revocation is legally permitted, the party must petition the court within 1 year from the date they knew or should have known of the fraud (Article 152). A borrower must repay the loan within the agreed term (Article 675). A contract is binding only on its signatories (Article 465). Transferring a debt to someone else requires the creditor bank's consent (Article 551). Therefore, a private promise letter written by A cannot bind the bank, and your only recourse is to sue A separately to recover the money. Furthermore, courts uphold contracts even if you scanned your face without reading the terms. In a case concluded by the Shizhong District Court of Neijiang in 2025, an individual named Jiang failed to qualify for a loan from a microfinance firm alone. Zhang's son took Zhang's identity credentials without her full knowledge and provided them to Jiang to list her as a co-borrower. The loan procedure required Zhang's personal facial recognition verification; she scanned her face but failed to read the contract. The court ruled that she "ought to have foreseen the legal importance and consequences of facial recognition" and ordered her to repay the remaining principal of over 60,000 yuan jointly with Jiang. The Electronic Signature Law specifies that "a reliable electronic signature has equal legal validity with a handwritten signature or seal." The Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law prohibits any individual from "providing real-name verification assistance to others," punishable by fines or administrative detention in severe cases. First-instance ordinary civil proceedings are legally scheduled to conclude within 6 months, subject to statutory extensions. Attorney fees are not included in statutory court costs and must be borne out of pocket (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家金融监督管理总局福建监管局（原福建银保监局） (2022). 关于防范不良贷款中介新型套路的风险提示（2022 年 9 月 14 日）. <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/branch/fujian/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=1072844&itemId=1104&generaltype=0>；国家金融监督管理总局山东监管局 (2024). 风险提示：警惕非法贷款中介AB贷风险（2024 年 3 月 14 日）. <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/branch/shandong/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=1154326&itemId=1269&generaltype=0>；上海市普陀区人民法院 (2024). 以案释法：征信不好仍可办理大额贷款？小心「AB贷」陷阱. <https://www.shpt.gov.cn/fyzx-fy/20240920/953017.html>（上海市普陀区人民政府网转载）；唐逸飞 (2025). 有必要对「助贷中介套路欺诈」予以刑事规制. 检察日报理论版. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/llyj/202506/t20250607_697711.shtml>（最高人民检察院官网）；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百四十九、一百五十二、四百六十五、五百五十一、六百七十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2023 修正). 民事诉讼法（第一百五十二条）. <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>（上海市发展改革委转载）；国务院 (2006). 诉讼费用交纳办法（国务院令第 481 号，第六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6929.htm>；四川长安网（中共四川省委政法委员会主办，来源四川法治报）(2025). 一次「刷脸」让她背上数万元贷款 法院：当事人应当预见人脸识别的重要性及法律后果（2025 年 9 月 23 日）. <https://www.sichuanpeace.gov.cn/azsf/20250923/2992615.html>；山西省忻州市中级人民法院（转载人民法院报）(2021). 一女子「被刷脸」办卡贷款后被起诉还钱（2021 年 8 月 19 日）. <https://sxxzzy.shanxify.gov.cn/article/detail/2021/08/id/6215123.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2019). 电子签名法（2019 年修正，第三、十三、十四、十五条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f2163e4261aa1>；全国人大常委会 (2022). 反电信网络诈骗法（第三十一、四十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>

### 46. Put good locks on doors and windows, add an outdoor sensor light, and actually lock up when leaving: real burglary prevention relies on these basics; a standalone alarm shows no benefit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Upgrading to keyed deadbolts, adding window locks, and installing an outdoor motion-sensor light typically costs a few dozen to several hundred yuan. Replacing an entire security door costs over a thousand yuan. The real challenge is consistently locking all doors and windows every time you leave the house, and remembering to latch open windows in summer.
- In plain terms: Data from the UK shows that homes with both door and window locks have only about one-twelfth the risk of being burgled compared to homes with no security measures at all. Adding an outdoor sensor light drops that risk to roughly one thirty-fourth. Installing an alarm alone without physical security shows no protective benefit. This applies strictly to burglary, not to robberies where an intruder confronts people at home.
- Benefit: The Crime Survey for England and Wales surveyed 37,416 households between 2008 and 2012 regarding installed security devices, comparing them against burgled homes. Households with no security accounted for only 4.9% of all homes, but made up 36.6% of those that were burgled. The authors calculated a "Security Protection Factor" (the odds of being burgled relative to having no security). Door locks combined with window locks achieved a protection factor of 12.54 times, reducing burglary risk to roughly one-twelfth. Here, door locks refer to double locks or deadbolts (where the bolt can only be retracted with a key). Combining door locks, window locks, and an outdoor sensor or timer light reached 34.41 times. Adding an indoor timer light pushed this to 49 times. For single measures alone, an outdoor light had a factor of roughly 3 times and door locks roughly 2.8 times, while window locks alone showed no noticeable difference. Having five security measures offered no clear advantage over having the right three. Having an alarm alone with no other security was associated with a higher burglary risk than having nothing at all. Properties with dummy alarm boxes also experienced higher rates of attempted burglaries. Locks only work if they are actually engaged: between 1993 and 2006/07, forced-entry burglaries (picking locks, breaking windows, kicking in door panels) dropped 77%, from 31 to 7 per 1,000 households. Meanwhile, unforced entries (walking in through unlocked doors or windows) remained completely unchanged at 7 per 1,000 households (England and Wales).
- Evidence grade: B
