# 23. Which skills are worth learning?

This section deals with costs and time investment; the second item also examines mortality rates. The first six items address one main question: whether it still makes sense to pursue further education. Item 1 outlines which options the law effectively rules out. Item 2 explores how education influences life expectancy. Item 3 shows what educational levels most people in China have attained. Item 4 lists available financial aid options when education proves unaffordable. Item 5 describes alternative pathways after failing to get into regular high school. Item 6 frames the “education versus work” dilemma as a calculable problem and provides a simple method for readers to work out their own figures. The next seven items tackle another question: once you decide to learn something new, how do you pick the right skill? Reliable official statistics on wages broken down by occupation are hard to verify in China, so this book only covers a few well-documented facts: roughly how much extra income an extra year of schooling brings (known as the return on education), how to verify whether a certificate is legitimate, where to find training subsidies, and which criteria help ensure a chosen skill stays in demand. Exact monthly earnings figures for any specific job are not provided here. Finally, the last five items answer a third question: after deciding what to learn, how can you minimize the time required to acquire it? These items focus solely on time, not money, and no conversion between the two is attempted.
### 1. Under 16? You can’t legally work: any employer who hires you faces a fine of $5,000 per month, and those willing to do so are breaking the law
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: Zero. It takes just a minute to figure out how old you are.  
- In plain terms: Most kids finish middle school at 15 or 16, right at this age threshold. Hiring anyone under 16 is illegal; each month an employer uses a child worker, they must pay $5,000 in fines. That means any employer willing to hire you is knowingly breaking the law. If anything goes wrong, all medical bills and compensation must come from that employer — if they refuse to pay or go out of business, there’s no one else to cover those costs.  
- Benefit: Article 11 of the Compulsory Education Law states: “All children aged six or older must be enrolled and complete compulsory education by their parents or legal guardians.” Article 14 adds: “Employers are prohibited from hiring children and teens who should still be in compulsory education.” Article 58 says that if parents or guardians fail to enroll their kids without valid reasons, local authorities must “issue criticism and order them to correct the behavior within a set timeframe.” Article 2 of the Regulations on Banning Child Labor explicitly bans all state agencies, social organizations, enterprises, private non‑profit groups, and individual business owners from hiring anyone under 16. Article 6 imposes a fine of $5,000 per month for each child worker used; if the violation continues, the penalty rises to $10,000 per month. Article 7 levies a $5,000 fine for each child referred for employment. Article 10 requires employers to “send injured or sick child workers to medical care at their own expense and cover all related costs”; in cases of permanent disability or death, additional compensation must be paid. The only exception appears in Article 13: with parental consent, cultural or sports organizations may employ under‑16 professionals.  
- Evidence grade: A  
- Notes: You’re the one who benefits here. After turning 16, working is perfectly legal. Yet jobs that accept a 16‑year‑old with no qualifications are exactly the type described in Section 10 — roles with fixed procedures that machines can easily replace. The overtime pay, vacation entitlements, and workers’ compensation discussed in Section 19 all require a formal employment relationship and proof of it; illegal workers get none of those protections, so they also miss out on those benefits.
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2018). 义务教育法（2018 年第二次修正，第十一、十四、五十八条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>（教育部刊登）；国务院 (2002). 禁止使用童工规定（国务院令第 364 号，第二、六、七、十、十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>

### 2. Adding “education’s value” to mortality statistics: each extra year of schooling lowers adult death risk by roughly 1.9%
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: There’s no direct monetary cost. What you do spend is extra time and effort on studying; tuition fees are covered in section 4 of this chapter (vocational school tuition and grants). The real challenge is staying in school long enough without dropping out.

- In plain terms: When data from 603 studies conducted in 59 countries are pooled together, every additional year of schooling is linked to an average 1.9% drop in adult death risk. This effect is strongest for people aged 18–49, where each extra year cuts risk by about 2.9%. For those over 70, the drop is just 0.8%. In other words, the difference between finishing high school and only finishing junior high goes far beyond simple wage gaps.

- Benefit: This conclusion comes from a comprehensive meta-analysis that brought together all relevant prior research. The search covered studies published between January 1980 and May 2023, ultimately including 603 studies from 70 locations across 59 countries and 10,355 observations. There is a clear dose‑response relationship between education level and overall adult mortality: the more years of schooling, the lower the risk. On average, each extra year of schooling reduces death risk by 1.9% (95% confidence interval: 1.8–2.0). For 18‑ to 49‑year‑olds, the reduction is 2.9% per year (2.8–3.0); for those over 70 it is 0.8% per year (0.6–1.0). No significant gender differences were found, nor any variation linked to a country’s level of development (as measured by the Social Demographic Index).

- Evidence grade: A (controversial)

- Notes: This finding remains controversial. The authors themselves reported a significant publication bias (p < 0.0001), meaning studies with more favorable results were far more likely to be published. Moreover, the results varied widely across individual studies. All included data are observational, not from randomized trials that would isolate education as a single variable. Because years of schooling are closely tied to family background, region, and occupation, it is hard to attribute the full 1.9% risk reduction solely to education itself. Nevertheless, the evidence strongly supports the idea that more schooling is associated with longer life — though it does not guarantee that any individual will gain exactly 1.9% extra longevity by studying more. The ultimate beneficiaries are the individuals themselves.
- Sources:IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>

### 3. Before determining whether “education is losing its value,” first look at the national education structure: only 15,467 people per 100,000 have a college education or higher.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Just glance at the numbers below.

- In plain terms: According to the 2020 census, only 15,467 people per 100,000 nationwide have completed junior college or higher education — that’s roughly 15%. Meanwhile, 34,507 people per 100,000 have only a junior high school education, making it the most common level. On average, people aged 15 and older have attended school for just 9.91 years, barely enough to finish junior high. Therefore, a junior high education in China is far from “sufficient”; it is the baseline level for most people.

- Benefit: The reference date for the seventh national census was November 1, 2020. At that time, 218,360,767 people nationwide had a college education or higher, while 213,005,258 had completed high school (including vocational school). The number of people with only a junior high education was 487,163,489, and those with only elementary school education totaled 349,658,828. Compared to the sixth census in 2010, the number of college-educated individuals per 100,000 rose from 8,930 to 15,467, while those with a high school education rose from 14,032 to 15,088. Conversely, the number of people with only junior high education fell from 38,788 to 34,507 per 100,000, and those with only elementary education dropped from 26,779 to 24,767. The average years of schooling for people aged 15 and older also increased from 9.08 to 9.91 years, while the illiteracy rate fell from 4.08% to 2.67%.

- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: This entry only addresses how “scarce” a college education is within the general population; it does not determine whether pursuing education is worthwhile for any specific individual. For that question, you must apply the method described in section 6 of this chapter (“Should you study or work?”). Keep in mind that census data includes the entire population, from children to the elderly. Among young adults, the proportion with a college education is much higher than the national average, so comparing yourself to this figure would overestimate your relative advantage. Conversely, this data effectively counters the claim that “education is useless,” since the denominator represents the entire labor force competing with you for jobs. The ultimate beneficiaries of this information are individuals themselves.
- Sources:国家统计局、国务院第七次全国人口普查领导小组办公室 (2021). 第七次全国人口普查公报（第六号）——人口受教育情况. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>

### 4. Run the policy check first for “can’t afford it”: most vocational school tuition is already waived, with a grant of 2,300 yuan and student loans up to 20,000 yuan per year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Just visit the school’s student aid office before classes start and submit the required paperwork.
- In plain terms: At public vocational schools, tuition is waived for rural students, urban students in agriculture-related majors, and those from low‑income families. Starting spring 2025, all vocational and regular high school students receive a yearly grant of 2,300 yuan. For university students, the grant rises to 3,700 yuan per year, and they can borrow up to 20,000 yuan annually through student loans. If funds are still short, repayment can be deferred or an alternative path chosen — this rarely forces a student to drop out and work immediately.
- Benefit: The tuition‑waiver policy for public vocational schools has been in place since fall 2012, covering all full‑time first‑, second‑, and third‑year students who are rural (including those in county towns), study agriculture‑related subjects, or come from financially struggling families (except for performance majors in the arts). In 2024 the student aid system was revised: the national grant for undergraduates rose from 3,300 to 3,700 yuan per year, and the National Inspirational Scholarship increased from 5,000 to 6,000 yuan, both effective fall 2024. Grants for vocational and regular high school students also rose from 2,000 to 2,300 yuan per year, starting spring 2025. Student loan limits were raised from fall 2024: undergraduates may now borrow up to 20,000 yuan per year (previously 16,000) and graduate students up to 25,000 yuan (previously 20,000). The interest rate equals the applicable LPR minus 70 basis points, i.e., 0.7 % lower than market rates nationwide.
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: The benefits apply to the student and their children. Exactly which groups qualify for tuition waivers and how many grant tiers exist is decided by each province. Private schools receive subsidies based on local public‑school standards, so any remaining balance must be paid by the student; therefore, check local regulations before enrolling. Student loans are loans, not grants, and must be repaid after graduation. They help separate the immediate problem of lacking funds from a broader cost‑benefit analysis, so a shortfall today should not be taken as a definitive reason to drop out. Section 7 discusses training subsidies and emergency aid for periods of unemployment; families facing sudden income loss can explore those options first.
- Sources:财政部、发展改革委、教育部、人力资源社会保障部 (2012). 关于扩大中等职业教育免学费政策范围 进一步完善国家助学金制度的意见（财教〔2012〕376 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>；财政部、教育部、人力资源社会保障部有关司局负责人 (2024). 就调整学生资助政策有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>；财政部、教育部、人民银行、金融监管总局 (2024). 关于调整完善国家助学贷款有关政策的通知（财教〔2024〕188 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>

### 5. Failing to get into regular high school doesn’t mean your path ends: vocational schools offer integrated enrollment programs and separate exams, and employers can lower educational requirements for skilled roles.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost involved. Before submitting your middle school exam application, check your province’s vocational education enrollment platform once.

- In plain terms: The 2022 Vocational Education Law states that vocational education holds equal status to general education. Vocational high schools can enroll students through integrated programs with higher vocational institutions, which in turn must reserve enrollment quotas or hold separate exams specifically for vocational high school graduates. Employers cannot set barriers against vocational school graduates based on their educational background. Not making it into a regular high school means switching to an alternative path, not staying at a junior high school level.

- Benefit: Article 3 of the Vocational Education Law (effective May 1, 2022) specifies: “Vocational education is a type of education that holds equal importance to general education.” Article 37 allows secondary vocational schools to “carry out integrated enrollment and training with higher vocational education in relevant majors in accordance with national regulations.” Higher vocational institutions may “adopt assessment methods combining cultural literacy and vocational skills to enroll students per national rules.” Provincial-level and higher education authorities must establish unified vocational education enrollment platforms to publish information on schools, majors, and enrollment plans, as well as provide application query services. Article 53 states: “Vocational school students enjoy equal opportunities as students from regular schools at the same level in terms of further education, employment, and career development.” Higher vocational institutions and regular universities offering vocational education must “set aside a specific proportion of their enrollment quotas or hold separate exams to enroll vocational school graduates exclusively.” “Employers must not impose application, hiring, or employment conditions that hinder equal access and fair competition for vocational school graduates.” For positions requiring specific vocational skill levels in public institution recruitment, “educational requirements can be appropriately lowered” nationwide.

- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: These benefits apply to you and your children. These are legally mandated pathways, but they do not guarantee admission. Which majors are included in integrated training programs, what proportion of enrollment quotas are reserved for separate exams, and what subjects are tested in vocational college entrance exams are all determined by each province. Before applying, you must check your province’s current vocational education enrollment platform and plans — do not rely on experiences from other provinces. When selecting a vocational high school major, evaluate it using the criteria outlined in Section 10 of this chapter (choosing skills that are less prone to automation): majors requiring on-site work in non-standard environments are less likely to be phased out. To pick a school, confirm it is listed in your province’s current vocational education enrollment plan. Verify any certificates issued by the school using the three-step process in Section 8 (checking whether a certificate is on the official list). Avoid any enrollment promotions promising “guaranteed job placement” or “assured admission.”
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2022). 职业教育法（2022 年修订，第三、三十七、五十三条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html>（教育部刊登）

### 6. Treating “college vs. working” as a calculation problem: the three years of early earnings versus decades of future income
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: Zero. It takes about half an hour to sit down and do the math once.

- In plain terms: Lay both paths out on paper and compare them. Working early yields only three years of wages from low‑skill jobs — that’s it. Continuing school, on the other hand, adds a steady yearly income boost for the next three or four decades. In Chinese cities, estimates show that each extra year of schooling raises average earnings by 4.0% (1988) up to 10.2% (2001). These figures are a bit dated, so they’re best used only to gauge the order of magnitude.

- Benefit: This figure represents an estimate for Chinese urban areas between 1988 and 2001. The percentage increase in earnings per extra year of schooling is known as the return on education. The original text notes that this rate rose sharply from 4.0% per year in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001, with most of the jump occurring after 1992. It reflects how much more people with a college education earn compared to those without — essentially the wage premium for higher education. This number provides a locally relevant benchmark for converting “one extra year of schooling” into a projected lifetime income difference. The global average is discussed in item 7 of this section (average worldwide return on an extra year of schooling).

- Evidence grade: B

- Notes: Before doing the calculation, subtract any tuition waivers or grants mentioned in item 4 (vocational‑school fees and aid), as well as the mortality impact described in item 2 (extra year of schooling and reduced death risk). The data are limited to the year 2001 and cover only urban areas, so they shouldn’t be taken as current figures. The same caveat applies to the 9% global average cited in item 7. This method offers a framework, not a definitive answer; actual results will vary from person to person. For individuals facing high tuition, mismatched majors, or lengthy study periods, the calculation might indeed show a net loss. That simply means “this particular path isn’t profitable,” not that “education is worthless” — the two ideas must not be conflated. Finally, several other factors can’t be captured by the formula but still matter: legally mandated educational requirements (see item 5 on vocational‑school admissions and separate exams), resistance to automation (see item 10 on choosing less automatable skills), and the ability to switch careers. The ultimate beneficiaries are the individuals themselves.
- Sources:Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>

### 7. Keep this baseline in mind: on average, each additional year of formal education yields a roughly 9% annual increase in personal income worldwide.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Memorize this figure and use it as a benchmark for evaluating any “learning program.”
- In plain terms: A comprehensive review of 1,120 estimates from 139 countries shows that, on average, each extra year of schooling raises an individual’s income by about 9% per year. Women consistently see higher returns than men. This figure represents the average effect of “one extra year of formal education,” not the benefit of enrolling in a single course. Treat it as a yardstick: if a program costs 30,000 yuan and lasts half a year, ask whether its expected income boost equals that of one full year of schooling.
- Benefit: This same review of 1,120 estimates spanning 139 countries from 1950 to 2014 confirms that the global average private return on “one additional year of education” is roughly 9% per year. The private return is precisely the portion of that gain that ends up as higher personal income — i.e., on average, an extra year of schooling translates to a 9% rise in earnings. Returns on higher education are on the rise, while the broader social returns to education remain substantial. Women continue to enjoy higher average returns than men.
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: This is an average figure for “one extra year of formal schooling,” not the benefit of any single course or a specific figure for China; it should be used only as a rough reference. Its main purpose is to serve as a universal benchmark: if a program demands a 30,000‑yuan investment and six months of effort, you can gauge whether its income impact matches that of one year of regular schooling. Keep in mind that the global average masks considerable differences across fields and countries.
- Sources:Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>；世界银行政策研究工作论文 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>

### 8. Before spending money on a certification, first verify whether it appears in the national list of occupational qualifications or in the roster of evaluation agencies registered with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. It takes just a few minutes to check once.
- In plain terms: Certain organizations misuse the concept of “new occupations” or “skill levels” to run training programs, charge excessive fees, and issue dubious certificates. Some even impersonate authoritative bodies. The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security has taken steps to curb such practices. Before enrolling, follow three simple checks: first, confirm whether the occupation is listed in the national list of occupational qualifications; second, verify that the issuing agency is registered with the ministry; third, ensure the certificate number can be found on the official verification platform. If any of these checks fail, the enrollment fee is essentially wasted.
- Benefit: The ministry has launched nationwide efforts to address these issues. Official documents explicitly condemn “unauthorized training, evaluation, and certification activities carried out by certain organizations under the guise of new occupational qualifications or skill levels, including arbitrary fee collection and issuance of illegitimate certificates.” Enforcement actions involve collaboration with internet regulators, police, and market oversight authorities to “severely punish any activities involving counterfeit evaluation agencies or unregistered certification bodies.” Illegal promotional content on websites and social media is promptly shut down. The ministry also urges the public to learn how to verify certificates so they can assess their legitimacy and real value.
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: Follow this sequence when checking. First, confirm whether the occupation appears in the National List of Occupational Qualifications; if not, it is not a nationally recognized qualification. Next, verify that the issuing agency is registered with the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security. Finally, use the official platform for skilled‑worker qualification verification to see if the certificate number can be located. Any certificate that fails any step should not be pursued. Certificates bearing terms such as “international,” “advanced,” or “registered,” or those promising “guaranteed success” or “income through certificate registration” pose the highest risk.
- Sources:人力资源社会保障部 (2022). 关于开展技术技能类「山寨证书」专项治理工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>

### 9. It’s best to use government subsidies for training — don’t jump straight to paying for private courses.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: There’s little or no expense involved; in many cases costs are significantly reduced. Training does require time and effort. The real challenge is resisting the urge to sign up for paid courses right away — first, check the local list of subsidized programs.
- In plain terms: From 2025 through late 2027, the government plans to provide subsidized training to over 30 million participants. Funding comes from multiple sources, including employment assistance funds, unemployment insurance funds, and corporate education budgets. The more in-demand a profession is and the higher its training costs, the greater the subsidy. Employees should ask their employers first; everyone else should consult their local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau’s subsidy list before deciding what to study.
- Benefit: The national target is clear: over 30 million subsidized training slots will be offered from 2025 to 2027 for workers in high-tech industries, critical sectors, and priority employment groups. Multiple funding streams — employment assistance funds, unemployment insurance, sector development funds, East-West cooperation funds, and corporate education budgets — have been pooled for this effort. Subsidy amounts vary by occupation based on how urgently needed that skill is and how costly the training is. Companies must also allocate at least 60% of their mandated education budgets to frontline worker training.
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: Exact eligibility criteria and subsidy amounts are determined by each province. The main portals are the “Subsidized Vocational Training List” on local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau websites and public training centers. Section 7 covers unemployment-related training subsidies (up to three applications per individual) and social insurance benefits. Employees should first inquire about their employer’s education budget before making any decisions. Checking subsidy options first can save considerable money compared to choosing a course first and then searching for funding.
- Sources:国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>

### 10. When picking a skill to learn, prioritize those requiring hands-on work and on-the-spot judgment — these are the hardest for automation to replace.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Before making any choice, run through the skills you’re considering using this very criterion.

- In plain terms: A study evaluating 702 occupations in the U.S. found that roughly 47% of them fall into the high-risk category for automation. Three key factors currently hold machines back: manual dexterity, creative problem-solving ability, and social intelligence. That is why jobs that demand physical work, take place in unstructured environments, and require real-time decision-making are far less likely to be automated, while roles with rigid processes and heavy information processing carry the highest risk. Don’t fixate on that 47% figure — focus instead on these three core barriers.

- Benefit: This study produced a calculable probability assessment for each of the 702 U.S. occupations, indicating that about 47% of jobs have a high likelihood of being automated. The three main engineering bottlenecks identified as responsible for this risk are: first, perception and manipulation, which includes finger and hand dexterity, as well as the ability to work in cramped spaces or awkward postures; second, creative intelligence, or the capacity to devise novel solutions; and third, social intelligence, or the ability to interact effectively with people. Consequently, occupations involving physical labor, unpredictable surroundings, and on-site judgment consistently show lower automation risk — this pattern is not limited to just a few specific professions. Conversely, jobs with highly standardized workflows and heavy reliance on information processing face the greatest threat of automation.

- Evidence grade: B

- Notes: This study remains somewhat controversial. It originated as an unpublished manuscript from 2013, and its rating reflects the overall likelihood of an entire occupation being automated. Later research instead evaluated individual tasks within those occupations, arriving at a significantly lower high-risk percentage. The study itself does not specify any timeline for when automation might occur. The proper way to interpret it is not to fixate on the 47% figure, but to focus on those three fundamental barriers it identifies. This framework helps explain why hands-on roles such as installation, maintenance, and caregiving are far more resistant to automation than purely clerical work, and why when choosing a skill to develop, it makes sense to prioritize those requiring physical engagement in unstructured environments.
- Sources:Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>

### 11. Check first whether a skill has an official grading system and if those grades can translate into pay raises
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost involved. A single check is all that’s needed.
- In plain terms: The government is promoting a new eight-tier vocational skill grading system, which requires employers to link both job pay and skill levels to each other, with extra emphasis on hiring and promoting workers at the senior levels. Eligible employees can even have their skill levels recognized directly, regardless of their years of service or prior qualifications. However, this is still a policy direction rather than actual, guaranteed pay increases — implementation varies widely across regions and companies. The only reliable way to gauge this is to see if a skill has an official national standard and a formal grading pathway; only then can it realistically lead to pay raises based on skill level.
- Benefit: The government is actively rolling out this new eight-tier vocational skill grading framework. Official documents explicitly state that skill levels must be tied to compensation, with wording such as “strengthen a pay distribution system oriented around skill levels… guide enterprises to link job pay to both job value and skill grade.” Regions are also instructed to “increase the pace and scale of hiring and promotion for senior-level workers.” Eligible employers can independently evaluate their employees’ skill levels, and outstanding workers can have their skill grades recognized outright, even if they don’t meet standard criteria like years of service or prior experience.
- Evidence grade: B
- Notes: This is still a policy direction, not guaranteed pay increases that are already in effect; actual implementation differs greatly across regions and companies. The only actionable takeaway is to check whether a skill has a corresponding national standard and grading pathway — only those skills can realistically lead to pay raises based on skill level. For skills without any official grading system or industry entry requirements, learning them won’t guarantee any pay bump; you’ll still need to find work on your own.
- Sources:国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>；人力资源社会保障部 (2021). 「技能中国行动」实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/06/content_5622619.htm>

### 12. Don’t take “high wages reported online” as proof of labor shortages — check local lists of in‑demand occupations and subsidy programs instead.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: Zero. All you need is to look up two local documents.  
- In plain terms: The amount of subsidies each region offers is essentially the government’s way of “pricing” which trades are in short supply; those amounts are set based on urgency and training costs. Differences in subsidy levels for the same trade across cities directly reflect how badly that trade is needed locally. Figures you see online about monthly earnings for a given trade usually come from job boards or personal reports — they highlight only the top earners and ignore working hours, commuting, occupational hazards, and seasonal fluctuations.  
- Benefit: Subsidy amounts serve as an official indicator of which trades are in demand. Policies call for “differentiated subsidy mechanisms based on how urgently a trade is needed and its training costs,” and each region uses this to decide which vocational training programs qualify for support. Thus, the gap in subsidies for the same trade between cities mirrors the actual shortage of workers in each place.  
- Evidence grade: C  
- Notes: TODO (need to verify: reliable national sources for average wages by trade and official rankings of in‑demand occupations; we couldn’t locate relevant pages on the Human Resources and Social Security Bureau site). To gauge true demand, consult your local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau website for subsidy listings and check public employment service rankings of needed roles. Finally, speak with current workers on construction sites, in factories, or at retail outlets.

### 13. For the same money and time, prioritize short-duration programs that lead directly to a job
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: These short-term courses require tuition, which isn’t very expensive. They also take up a fair amount of time. The difficulty lies in stopping the program at the agreed-upon point, even when progress isn’t yet evident halfway through.
- In plain terms: Government policies encourage “quick-to-implement” training programs that lead to short-term employment, as well as micro-specializations offered by vocational colleges. When choosing a program, there’s only one key criterion: does it provide a specific job opening you can apply to after completion? Programs that require a year or more of study and then demand additional “experience building” after graduation actually cost far more than their listed price. It’s essential to set clear exit terms upfront: specify at what point of completion you can stop the program if you haven’t secured a job yet.
- Benefit: Government policy aligns with this approach: it supports “private training institutions running market-oriented, high-quality vocational training programs and ‘quick-to-implement’ programs that lead to short-term employment.” It also encourages vocational colleges to “focus on knowledge and skills urgently needed by the job market... and offer micro-specializations and vocational training courses.”
- Evidence grade: C
- Notes: The sole deciding factor is whether a specific job opening exists after completing the program. Programs that require a year or more of study and then additional “experience building” after graduation end up costing far more than their listed price. The exit conditions outlined in Section 4 also apply here: clearly define upfront at what point of completion you can stop the program if you haven’t found a job.

### 14. After studying, close the book and test yourself — don’t just reread it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No money is required, and you won’t need extra time. Use the time you’d otherwise spend rereading to write down or answer questions from memory. The difficulty lies in how uncomfortable it feels to confront what you’ve forgotten right away, compared to the relative ease of rereading.

- In plain terms: Rereading a passage makes it feel very familiar, but that doesn’t mean you’ve actually retained it. Closing the book and testing yourself yields noticeably better results. A meta-analysis of 222 classroom studies involving nearly 48,500 students showed that adding brief self-tests raised grades by a moderate amount. In a separate review that ranked ten common study methods, self-testing earned the top rating. Approximately 50,000 students participated in these studies overall.

- Benefit: This meta-analysis of 222 independent studies with 48,478 participants found that classroom self-tests boosted academic performance by a moderate effect size (g = 0.499). The magnitude of this effect depends on several factors: the baseline study method used in the control group, how closely the test format matches the final exam, whether the content aligns, whether corrective feedback is provided, how often the tests are administered, when and where they occur, how long the intervention lasts, and the overall experimental design. Another review that evaluated ten widely used learning strategies rated “practice testing” and “spaced practice” as the most effective methods overall, because they benefit learners of all ages and abilities and have been proven effective across diverse assessment formats and real-world educational settings.

- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: Self-testing isn’t about assigning yourself a score; it’s a way to force information out of your memory. To apply it: after finishing a section, write down its key points from memory, then check them against the text. Providing corrective feedback further improves outcomes, so after checking your answers, be sure to review exactly where you went wrong. The “large” benefit rating here is based on both the effect size and the overall evidence grade; these figures reflect academic performance gains rather than time saved, and they aren’t calculated using any specific time‑savings threshold. This strategy, together with the approach described in item 15 (spreading study time across several days), represents the two most effective methods identified in the same body of research.
- Sources:Yang C, Luo L, Vadillo MA, Yu R, Shanks DR (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 147(4), 399–435. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>

### 15. Spread the same study time across several days instead of cramming it all at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: There is no cost involved, and the total time spent stays the same. The only difference is the schedule: those same 3 hours are divided into one hour per day over three days. The challenge is that you must start planning days in advance — you can’t wait until the night before the test.
- In plain terms: Spending the same amount of time on studying across multiple days helps you retain information far better than cramming it all at once. This conclusion is supported by 317 separate experiments, all of which reached the same result. There is also a practical rule to keep in mind: the longer you want to remember something, the longer the interval between review sessions should be. If a test is tomorrow, review the material that day; if it’s a month away, schedule a review session a few days later.
- Benefit: This is a meta-analysis that brings together data from 184 papers, 317 experiments, and 839 separate evaluations of distributed practice effects. It compares two key factors: studying continuously versus spreading it out over time (the spacing effect), and short versus long review intervals (the delay effect). The results clearly show that both the desired retention period and the spacing between review sessions jointly determine how well information is retained. In fact, the optimal spacing increases as the desired retention period gets longer. Another comprehensive review that assessed ten common learning techniques rated distributed practice as one of the most effective methods overall (high utility).
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: This tip works best when combined with Tip 14 (self-testing without looking at notes): you can also spread those self-test sessions out across several days. It is worth noting that most of the materials studied in Cepeda’s meta-analysis involved simple tasks such as memorizing word lists; therefore, no universal number of days can be given for optimal spacing when dealing with more complex content. The principle that “the longer the test is in the future, the longer the spacing should be” comes directly from the original research and is not a prescriptive schedule. The problem with cramming the night before a test is not that it provides no benefit at all, but that the information is quickly forgotten afterward.
- Sources:Cepeda NJ, Pashler H, Vul E, Wixted JT, Rohrer D (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>

### 16. Don’t treat highlighting, rereading, or summarizing as primary study methods
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No cost at all; in fact it saves time. These methods are easy to apply and give you that “I’m actually studying” feeling.
- In plain terms: Students most often rely on highlighting and rereading, yet a review that evaluated ten different study methods ranked both of them at the very bottom. Summarizing, keyword mnemonics, and visualizing texts also ended up at the lowest tier. That doesn’t mean they’re useless; it simply means they only work under very specific conditions. Spending the same amount of time on self-testing would be far more beneficial.
- Benefit: This review assessed ten common study methods based on how well their effectiveness holds across different learning contexts, student profiles, materials, and assessment types. The highest rating went to practice testing and spaced practice. Methods such as elaborative questioning, self-explanation, and interleaved practice received a moderate rating. The five methods at the lowest tier were summarizing, highlighting (or underlining), keyword mnemonics, visualizing texts, and rereading. The review notes that although most students claim to use rereading and highlighting, these methods don’t reliably improve academic performance; they should be replaced with other approaches, such as practice testing instead of rereading.
- Evidence grade: A
- Notes: This isn’t a ban on highlighting. Highlighting can be useful if it helps you identify sections to test yourself on later. The problem arises when highlighting is treated as a learning activity in itself. The review also clarifies that a low rating doesn’t mean these methods have zero value; it simply indicates they work only under limited conditions and require further research. For suitable alternatives, see item 14 (self-testing after closing the book) and item 15 (spreading study time over several days).
- Sources:Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>

### 17. Mixing different types of exercises — don’t do twenty of the same kind in a row  
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Simply change the order of exercises in your workbook from strictly by type to a random mix. The downside is that accuracy drops at first when mixing them, which can feel like a step backward.  
- In plain terms: Doing many similar problems back‑to‑back makes them feel easy at the moment, but later you may struggle to recall which method to apply. Mixing several types of exercises yields better long‑term results — the effect size is moderate. This technique works best with visual material such as drawings and categorization tasks; it has a smaller impact on math problems, and for vocabulary memorization it’s actually better to practice in blocks.  
- Benefit: This finding comes from a large meta‑analysis that included 59 studies, 238 effect sizes, and 158 samples. Overall, interleaved practice produces a moderate benefit compared to blocked practice (Hedges’ g = 0.42). By material type, visual tasks benefit the most (g = 0.67); math tasks show a smaller effect (g = 0.34). For explanatory texts and taste‑related material there is no significant difference, while vocabulary learning is better with blocked practice (g = −0.39). Regression analysis indicates that the advantage grows when categories are more similar, when items within a category are less alike, and when the material is more complex.  
- Evidence grade: A  
- Notes: The review cited in item 16 (don’t rely solely on highlighting) rated interleaved practice as having a moderate effect, noting that research at the time was limited. Taken together, these studies suggest that interleaving works well for material that requires you to identify a category and choose the right method — e.g., different math problem types, medical cases, or legal statutes — whereas factual memorization such as vocabulary or definitions is better practiced in blocks.
- Sources:Brunmair M, Richter T (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin, 145(11), 1029–1052. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209>

### 18. Don’t pick learning methods based on “I’m a visual learner and he’s an auditory learner”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. You simply stop using this classification to select materials or courses, and you also avoid spending money on style assessments.

- In plain terms: The idea that “everyone has their own learning style and teaching should be tailored to that style” is widely believed. To prove this claim, researchers would need to group students by learning style, randomly assign each group to different teaching methods, and then test them all with the same exam. Yet almost no studies have produced results that support this notion. People certainly have preferences, but there is no evidence that teaching to those preferences actually improves learning outcomes.

- Benefit: A comprehensive review evaluating the evidence on learning styles concluded that to demonstrate the effectiveness of teaching according to learning styles, one must first group students by style, then randomly assign them to various teaching methods and finally test them with the same assessment. The key indicator of effectiveness would be a specific interaction effect — where the most effective teaching method for one style is not the most effective for another style. The review found virtually no such interaction effects. Although there is a great deal of literature on learning styles, very few studies employ proper experimental designs; some of those that do exist even contradict the “matching hypothesis.” Based on this evidence, the authors argue that current research does not justify incorporating learning style assessments into educational practice.

- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: The review also notes that this does not mean every version of learning style theory has been tested and disproven; many have never been examined at all. This recommendation is aimed at preventing unnecessary spending on learning style assessments and on selecting training programs based on such assessments. Criteria for choosing training programs are outlined in Item 9 (prioritize government‑subsidized training) and Item 13 (prefer short‑duration programs that lead directly to employment).
- Sources:Pashler H, McDaniel M, Rohrer D, Bjork R (2008). Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105–119. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x>

### 19. Test yourself using questions based on how you’ll use the material later; don’t just recite it verbatim
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: There’s no expense and it doesn’t require much extra time. The only change is the practice method: replace “reciting it verbatim” with “testing yourself using questions based on future usage.” The real challenge is figuring out how you’ll actually use the material later.

- In plain terms:  
  Testing yourself after closing the book offers benefits beyond simply memorizing a few words. A meta-analysis of 192 comparisons involving over 10,000 participants shows this method works reasonably well for different question types, application problems, and reasoning tasks. However, it’s largely ineffective in two cases: when the question–answer pairing is reversed, and when the content has only ever appeared in the book without ever being tested.

- Benefit:  
  This meta-analysis examined over 40 years of research, including 67 published and unpublished papers, 122 experiments, and 192 measures of transfer effect involving 10,382 participants. Compared to simply reviewing material without testing, practice testing led to meaningful learning transfer, with an effect size of d = 0.40 (95% CI 0.31–0.50). Transfer was strongest when participants answered questions of different types, application problems, reasoning tasks, or medical diagnosis questions involving similar concepts. Transfer was weakest when the stimulus–response pairing was reversed, when material had only been seen but never tested, and for example‑type questions. Factors such as whether the practice and test formats matched, whether participants received detailed feedback during practice, and whether they answered correctly on the initial test all strongly influenced whether positive transfer occurred. The authors applied two methods to correct for publication bias; while these adjustments slightly reduced effect sizes, they did not eliminate them. In fact, when none of these favorable conditions were met, positive transfer was rarely observed at all.

- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: The term “memorizing” usually combines two distinct activities: repeatedly reading or highlighting material, and actively recalling it from memory after closing the book. The former receives the lowest effectiveness rating in a review comparing ten learning methods; see item 16 for details (highlighting, repeated reading, and summary writing are not among the most effective strategies). The latter is what this item emphasizes: actively recalling information after closing the book, as described in item 14 (self‑testing). This practice should also be spread across several days, as noted in item 15. Begin by considering how you’ll actually use the material—whether you’ll need to select answers on a test or make judgments in real‑world situations—and then create questions reflecting those scenarios. It’s also valuable to reverse the question–answer pairing during practice, since this meta-analysis shows transfer is weakest in that direction; both orientations must be practiced. Failing to adapt the testing format or apply the material in practice means all that memorizing was essentially wasted effort.
- Sources:Pan SC, Rickard TC (2018). Transfer of test-enhanced learning: meta-analytic review and synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710-756. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151>
