# 23. Which Skills Are Worth Learning

The frame here is monetary return versus time cost; item 2 additionally factors in mortality. The first six items answer "should I keep studying or not" — the law first rules out a portion of the options, then come the longevity accounting, how scarce each education level is within the population, subsidies for those who cannot afford school, the pathways for people who never entered general academic high school, and finally a method for running the numbers yourself. The last seven answer "once you have decided to learn something, how to choose it". Wage statistics broken down by occupation in China are difficult to verify item by item through public official channels, so this chapter covers only what has a traceable source: the evidence on returns to education, how to verify whether a certificate is legitimate, where training subsidies come from, and which dimensions of a skill are more resistant to replacement. As for how much a specific trade earns per month, this book gives no numbers.
### 1. Under 16 there is no "go get a job" option: whoever hires you is fined 5,000 yuan per month, and anyone willing to hire you is off the books
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. Spend one minute working out how old you are.
- In plain terms: People usually finish junior high at 15 to 16, right on this line. Under 16, an employer hiring you is against the law — 5,000 yuan in fines per month for each child laborer used. So the only ones willing to take you are units that know it is illegal and use you anyway. If something really goes wrong, the medical bills and compensation can only be chased from that unit; if it refuses to pay or disappears, nobody is left to cover for it.
- Benefit: Article 11 of the law on compulsory education: "children who have reached the age of six must have their parents or other legal guardians send them to school to receive and complete compulsory education"; Article 14: "employers are prohibited from hiring school-age children and adolescents who should be receiving compulsory education"; Article 58: where there is no justifiable reason for failing to enroll school-age children or adolescents in school as required by the law, the local township people's government or the education administrative department of the county-level people's government shall "give criticism and education and order correction within a time limit". The state regulation prohibiting child labor, Article 2: state organs, social organizations, enterprises and public institutions, private non-enterprise entities, and individually owned businesses "may not hire minors under the age of 16"; Article 6: penalties are imposed "at the standard of a fine of 5,000 yuan per child laborer per month", and if not corrected after the deadline, 10,000 yuan per person per month; Article 7: for brokering child-labor employment "a fine of 5,000 yuan is imposed for each person introduced"; Article 10: if a child laborer falls ill or is injured, the employer "must take responsibility for sending them to a medical institution for treatment and bear all medical and living expenses during the treatment period", with a one-time compensation payment additionally owed in case of disability or death. Article 13 leaves a single exception: with the consent of parents or other guardians, culture and arts organizations and sports organizations may hire professional performers and athletes under 16 (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 义务教育法（2018 年第二次修正，第十一、十四、五十八条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>（教育部刊登）；国务院 (2002). 禁止使用童工规定（国务院令第 364 号，第二、六、七、十、十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiary is yourself. After 16, working is legal, but the jobs willing to take a 16-year-old with no credentials are exactly the category covered in item 10 of this section: fixed routines, the easiest for machines to displace. The overtime pay, annual leave, and work-injury benefits discussed in section 19 all presuppose an employment relationship and evidence; off-the-books jobs have neither, and that money cannot be claimed

### 2. Put "is schooling useful" into the mortality ledger too: each additional year of education lowers adult death risk by about 1.9%
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. What it costs is the years of time and energy spent on the extra schooling; for tuition, see item 4 of this section. The hard part is keeping on studying and not giving up halfway.
- In plain terms: Pooling 603 studies from 59 countries, each additional year of schooling means an average of about 1.9% lower risk of death in adulthood. The effect is most visible from 18 to 49, about 2.9% lower per extra year; past 70 it is only 0.8%. In other words, the difference between finishing high school and stopping at junior high is not just wages.
- Benefit: Gathering the earlier studies and pooling them (a systematic review and meta-analysis). The search ran from January 1980 to May 2023 and finally included 603 studies, 70 sites in 59 countries, and 10,355 observations. Education and adult mortality (deaths from all causes added together) follow a dose-response relationship — the more schooling, the bigger the drop. Each additional year of education lowers the risk of death by an average of 1.9% (95% uncertainty interval 1.8–2.0, meaning the true value roughly falls within this range). For people aged 18 to 49, each extra year lowers it by 2.9% (2.8–3.0). For people over 70, by 0.8% (0.6–1.0). No difference was seen between men and women. Broken down by country development level (the sociodemographic index), no difference there either
- Evidence grade: A (contested)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Notes: Contested. The authors themselves report publication bias (p<0.0001), meaning studies with good-looking results get published more easily. Results also vary widely from study to study. All the included studies are follow-up observation, not trials that assigned people into groups. Years of schooling are tangled up with family circumstances, region, and occupation, and this kind of research cannot separate how much of the 1.9% comes from schooling itself and how much from "people who manage to keep studying were better off to begin with". So it strongly shows that "more schooling and living longer move in the same direction", but it does not mean "go study and you will live 1.9% longer". The beneficiary is yourself

### 3. Before judging "has a degree lost value", first look at the national education structure: only 15,467 people per 100,000 have a college-level education
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One look at the numbers below.
- In plain terms: The 2020 census found that per 100,000 people nationwide, only 15,467 studied up to junior college or above, about 15%. People with a junior high education number 34,507, the largest bracket of all. People aged 15 and over have spent 9.91 years in school on average, just past junior high graduation. So in China a junior high education is not "enough" — it is the most ordinary bracket there is.
- Benefit: The seventh national population census had a reference time of November 1, 2020. Among the national population, 218,360,767 people have a college education (junior college or above), 213,005,258 have senior high (including vocational secondary school), 487,163,489 have junior high, and 349,658,828 have primary school. Compared with the 2010 sixth census, per 100,000 people those with a college education rose from 8,930 to 15,467; senior high rose from 14,032 to 15,088; junior high fell from 38,788 to 34,507; primary school fell from 26,779 to 24,767. Average years of schooling for those aged 15 and over rose from 9.08 to 9.91 years, and the illiteracy rate fell from 4.08% to 2.67%
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家统计局、国务院第七次全国人口普查领导小组办公室 (2021). 第七次全国人口普查公报（第六号）——人口受教育情况. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>
- Notes: This item answers only "how scarce is each education level in the population", not "is schooling worth it for one particular person" — the latter you must calculate yourself by the method in item 6 of this section. One more thing to note: the census counts the entire national population, old people and children included. The proportion of college-educated among young people is far higher than this figure, so comparing yourself against your own age group with it overstates your advantage. Conversely, using it to rebut "schooling is useless" is valid, because the denominator is precisely the whole labor force competing with you for jobs. The beneficiary is yourself

### 4. Run "can't afford school" through the policy ledger first: vocational school tuition is mostly waived, grants are 2,300 yuan, student loans up to 20,000 yuan a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Ask once at the school's student aid office before the semester starts and submit the paperwork once.
- In plain terms: At public vocational secondary schools, tuition is already waived for rural students, for urban students in agriculture-related programs, and for students from families in financial difficulty. The grants for vocational secondary school and academic senior high school are 2,300 yuan a year from spring 2025. Once at university, the national grant for undergraduates and junior college students is 3,700 yuan a year, and a student loan can be borrowed up to 20,000 yuan a year. Money being short can mean postponing or taking another path, but it rarely adds up to "must drop out and work right now".
- Benefit: The waiver of vocational school tuition is a policy in force from the fall semester of 2012. The original text is "tuition is waived for all rural students (including those from county towns), urban students in agriculture-related programs, and students from families in financial difficulty among formally enrolled full-time first-, second-, and third-year students at public secondary vocational schools (excluding students in related performing programs in the arts)". Student aid policies were adjusted in 2024. The national grant for undergraduate and junior college students rose from 3,300 yuan to 3,700 yuan per student per year, and the national encouragement scholarship from 5,000 yuan to 6,000 yuan; both take effect from the fall semester of 2024 (the encouragement scholarship from 2024). The national grants for secondary vocational schools and for academic senior high schools both rose from 2,000 yuan to 2,300 yuan per student per year, effective from the spring semester of 2025. National student loans were also adjusted, from the fall semester of 2024: for undergraduates and junior college students "the annual loan application limit per person rises from no more than 16,000 yuan to no more than 20,000 yuan", and for graduate students "from no more than 20,000 yuan to no more than 25,000 yuan". The interest rate is the same-term, same-tier loan prime rate (LPR) minus 70 basis points, that is, another 0.7 percentage points lower (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 财政部、发展改革委、教育部、人力资源社会保障部 (2012). 关于扩大中等职业教育免学费政策范围 进一步完善国家助学金制度的意见（财教〔2012〕376 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>；财政部、教育部、人力资源社会保障部有关司局负责人 (2024). 就调整学生资助政策有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>；财政部、教育部、人民银行、金融监管总局 (2024). 关于调整完善国家助学贷款有关政策的通知（财教〔2024〕188 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. Who exactly the tuition waiver covers and how many tiers the grants have are decided by each province. Private schools are subsidized at the local public rate for the same type of program and the shortfall is still paid out of pocket, so verify against local documents before enrolling. A student loan is a loan, not a grant, and must be repaid after graduation. But it splits "can't pay tuition right now" from "are these years worth it", so don't treat the lack of money as the conclusion. Section 7 covered training subsidies and temporary assistance during unemployment; if the family's income truly stops, take that route first

### 5. Failing to get into academic senior high does not mean the road is cut: vocational schools have through-track admissions and separate exams, and skill-job hiring can lower degree requirements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Check your province's vocational education admissions platform once before submitting senior high school preferences.
- In plain terms: The 2022 law on vocational education states plainly that vocational education and general education are of equal standing. Secondary vocational schools can run through-track admissions with higher vocational colleges, and higher vocational colleges must also set aside enrollment plans or hold separate exams specifically for secondary vocational graduates. Employers may not set up barriers against you just because you graduated from a vocational school. Scoring too low for academic senior high is a switch to another road, not a stop at a junior high education.
- Benefit: Law on Vocational Education (in force from May 1, 2022), Article 3: "vocational education is a type of education of equal importance with general education". Article 37: secondary vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, implement through-track admissions and training with higher vocational school education in relevant majors"; higher vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, admit students through an assessment method combining academic attainment and vocational skills"; education administrative departments at the provincial level and above must establish a unified vocational education admissions platform that aggregates and publishes schools, program offerings, and admissions information and provides inquiry and application services. Article 53: "vocational school students enjoy equal opportunities with same-level ordinary school students in further study, employment, and career development"; higher vocational schools and ordinary universities implementing vocational education "must set a corresponding proportion in their enrollment plans or adopt separate examination methods to recruit vocational school graduates specifically"; "employers may not set application, hiring, or recruitment conditions that obstruct equal employment and fair competition for vocational school graduates", and in open recruitment by public institutions, positions with vocational skill level requirements "may appropriately lower the degree requirement" (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2022). 职业教育法（2022 年修订，第三、三十七、五十三条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html>（教育部刊登）
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. These are statutory pathways, not guaranteed admission. Which majors run through-track training, what share separate admissions reserve, and what subjects the vocational education exam covers are all decided by each province. Before submitting preferences you must check your own province's vocational education admissions platform and enrollment plan for that year, and not extrapolate from another province's experience. When picking a secondary vocational major, run it through the dimensions in item 10 of this section — majors requiring presence on site and in non-standard environments are harder to displace. For choosing a school, just check whether it is in your province's vocational education enrollment plan that year. Then verify the certificate it issues with the three steps in item 8 of this section. Treat any admissions pitch promising "guaranteed job placement" or "guaranteed pass" as an automatic no

### 6. Turn "study or work" into a problem you compute: three years of early wages set against decades of annual income difference
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Sit down and compute it once, about half an hour.
- In plain terms: Lay both roads out on paper. Working early earns the wages of three years in low-barrier jobs, and that happens only once. Keeping on studying buys the extra slice you earn every year after, multiplied by the remaining thirty or forty years. China's urban estimates: one more year of schooling means income on average 4.0% higher (1988) to 10.2% higher (2001). The data are old and usable only as an order of magnitude.
- Benefit: These are estimates for urban China 1988 to 2001. How much income one more year of schooling adds is called the return to education. The original text reads "rising sharply from 4.0% per year of education in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001". The rise happened mainly after 1992. It reflects the wage premium for higher education — the extra earnings of college graduates over non-graduates — getting bigger. This is the locally available Chinese order of magnitude for converting "one more year of schooling" into an annual income difference. For the global average, see item 7 of this section
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>
- Notes: Before computing, first subtract the tuition waivers and grants in item 4 of this section, and there is also the mortality ledger in item 2 of this section. The data end in 2001 and cover only urban areas, so they cannot be used directly as today's numbers; the same applies to the 9% global average in item 7. This item gives you the method, not the answer: computed this way, the conclusion varies from person to person. High tuition, a mismatch between major and job, and a long duration of study can genuinely come out as not worth it. That only means "this specific program isn't worth it" and does not amount to "schooling is useless" — don't conflate the two. A few more things exist that won't fit in the formula but are real. One is statutory degree thresholds, see item 5 of this section. Another is resistance to replacement, see item 10. A third is whether you can switch industries. The beneficiary is yourself

### 7. Memorize the baseline first: each additional year of education yields a global average private return of about 9% a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Remember this number and use it as the ruler for every "learn something" plan.
- In plain terms: A review covering 139 countries and 1,120 estimates works out that each additional year of schooling means personal income about 9% higher on average per year. The return for women is consistently higher than for men. Note this is the average for "one more year of formal education", not the payoff of "signing up for a course". Use it as a ruler: a course asks you for 30,000 yuan and half a year — ask whether the income change it brings matches a year of formal education.
- Benefit: A review covering 1950 to 2014, 139 countries, and 1,120 estimates finds: "the global average private return to an additional year of education is 9% a year". The private return is the part that lands on your own income — that is, one more year of schooling means income about 9% higher on average. The private return to higher education is rising. The social return to education (the part landing on society as a whole rather than on the person) remains high. The average return to education for women is consistently higher than for men
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>；世界银行政策研究工作论文 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>
- Notes: This is the average for "one more year of schooling", not the payoff of "signing up for a course", and not a China-specific number — it serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference. Its use is to give every training plan a ruler: a course asking 30,000 yuan and half a year of your time should be asked whether it can deliver an income change equivalent to a year of formal education. Also note that the global average masks enormous differences across fields and across countries

### 8. Before paying for a certificate, first check whether it is in the national occupational qualification catalog or on the MOHRSS roster of registered assessment bodies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One check, a dozen or so minutes.
- In plain terms: Some organizations, under the names of new occupations and skill levels, run courses haphazardly, charge fees arbitrarily, and hand out certificates indiscriminately, and some impersonate authoritative bodies — the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security has run dedicated crackdowns on this. Before signing up, check three things: whether the occupation is in the national occupational qualification catalog, whether the issuing body is an assessment body registered with the human resources and social security departments, and whether the certificate number can be looked up on the official platform. If it can't be found, the registration fee is money thrown away.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security has run a nationwide special crackdown for this reason. The named abuses are "some institutions and units, under the names of new-occupation qualifications and vocational skill levels, arbitrarily conduct training, assessment, and certificate-issuing activities, charging fees indiscriminately and issuing certificates indiscriminately; some institutions and units engage in false or exaggerated publicity, and even organize training, assessment, and certificate issuance in the name of authoritative bodies". Measures included enforcement jointly with cyberspace, public security, and market regulation departments, "severely cracking down on certificate-issuing and assessment activities impersonating appraisal institutions and assessment bodies registered with human resources and social security departments", and lawfully shutting down non-compliant promotion on websites and new media. It also required "guiding the public to learn the channels and methods for verifying certificates and to judge whether a certificate is lawful, compliant, and worth anything"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 人力资源社会保障部 (2022). 关于开展技术技能类「山寨证书」专项治理工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>
- Notes: Verify in this order. First check whether the occupation is in the national occupational qualification catalog — if it is not in the catalog, it is not a national occupational qualification. Then check whether the issuing body is an assessment body registered with the human resources and social security departments. Finally, look up the certificate number on the official skill-talent assessment certificate verification platform. If it can't be found, do not enroll. Certificates bearing the words "international", "senior", or "registered", or promising "guaranteed pass" or "rent it out to an employer for income", carry the highest risk

### 9. For training, take the government-subsidized channel first; don't start by paying out of pocket for a commercial course
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free, or a large reduction. The training itself takes time. The hard part is holding back from impulsively paying for a course out of pocket — check the subsidy catalog first.
- In plain terms: From 2025 to the end of 2027, the state plans to run subsidized training for more than 30 million person-times. The money comes through multiple channels — employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, enterprise employee education funds, and others. The more short-handed the job type and the higher the training cost, the bigger the subsidy. If you're employed, ask your employer first; everyone else should read their local human resources and social security bureau's subsidy catalog before deciding what to learn.
- Benefit: The national scale is explicit: from 2025 to the end of 2027, "carry out subsidized training for more than 30 million person-times, focusing on high-end, cutting-edge industries and high-demand sectors, key employment groups, and so on". Funding sources are consolidated into one package: employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, industry development funds, east-west collaboration funds, enterprise employee education funds, and so on. Subsidy levels are set by how urgently and critically in demand the occupation (job type) is and by training cost, and differ from one job type to another. On the enterprise side it is also required to "withdraw and use employee education funds in full as prescribed, ensuring that more than 60% goes to the education and training of front-line workers"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: Claim procedures and amounts are set by each province. The entry points are the "vocational training subsidy catalog" on the local human resources and social security bureau website and the public practical training bases. Section 7 covered vocational training subsidies during unemployment (a maximum of 3 per person) and social insurance subsidies; employed people should ask about their employer's employee education funds first. Checking the subsidy catalog before choosing what to learn saves far more money than choosing first and then looking for a subsidy

### 10. When picking a skill, prioritize "does it need hands-on work and on-the-spot judgment" — these are the hardest to displace with automation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Run the work you want to learn through this dimension before choosing.
- In plain terms: In an assessment of 702 US occupations, about 47% of jobs fell in the high-risk band. What holds the machines back is three things: manual dexterity, the ability to come up with new things, and the ability to deal with people. So work that requires being on site, in non-standard environments, and making judgment calls on the spot is harder to displace, while jobs with fixed routines centered on processing information carry the highest risk. Don't fixate on the 47% figure — look at those three dimensions.
- Benefit: An assessment scored 702 US occupations one by one on "how likely is this work to be handed to a computer" (the computerization probability assessment), and about 47% of employment is in the high-risk band. In the model, the level of risk is decided by three bottlenecks machines cannot yet get past, which the authors call "engineering bottlenecks". One is perception and manipulation, including finger dexterity, manual dexterity, and working in confined spaces and awkward positions. Two is creative intelligence, the ability to come up with new things. Three is social intelligence, the ability to deal with people. So work in non-standard environments requiring manual skill and on-the-spot judgment is consistently less likely to be replaced — not an accident of a few occupations. Jobs with fixed routines centered on information processing carry the highest risk
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>
- Notes: Contested. This research is a 2013 working paper never formally published, and it scored "whether an entire occupation can be handed to the machine". Later research split jobs into specific tasks and estimated one by one, coming out with much lower high-risk shares. It also does not say in which year any of this will happen. The correct way to use it is not the 47% figure but the three places where machines get stuck that it pointed out. This explains why on-site installation, repair, and care work resists replacement better than pure paperwork, and why "learning a skill" should first mean the kind that requires being on site, facing non-standard environments

### 11. Before learning, confirm whether the skill has a graded assessment and whether the grade can be converted into money
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One check.
- In plain terms: The state is promoting the "new eight-grade worker" system, requiring enterprises to link position pay and skill grades together and to expand the evaluation and appointment of senior workers and above; qualifying employees can even have their grade determined directly, without regard to years of service or seniority. But this is a policy direction, not a wage number already delivered — it varies a lot by region and by enterprise. There is only one usable judgment: only skills with a national occupational standard and a grading track can later involve pay raises tied to grades.
- Benefit: The state is promoting the "new eight-grade worker" vocational skill grade system. The document explicitly requires linking grades to pay: "strengthen the skill-oriented pay distribution system... guide enterprises to adopt distribution methods linking position pay to both position value and skill grade". Each locality is to "increase the intensity and batches of evaluation and appointment for senior workers and above". Qualifying enterprises may conduct employee skill grade evaluations on their own. For outstanding employees "the conditions of length of service, grade, and seniority may be set aside, and the corresponding vocational skill grade directly recognized"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>；人力资源社会保障部 (2021). 「技能中国行动」实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/06/content_5622619.htm>
- Notes: This is a policy direction, not a wage number already delivered, and the degree of implementation varies greatly by region and by enterprise. The actionable part: when choosing a skill, check whether it has a corresponding national occupational standard and grading track — only skills with a track can later involve pay raises by grade. A "skill" with neither grades nor an industry entry threshold leaves you finding work entirely on your own after finishing

### 12. Don't take "the internet says the pay is high" as evidence of labor shortage; look up your locality's in-demand occupation catalog and subsidy catalog
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Look up two local documents.
- In plain terms: How much subsidy each locality pays is itself the official pricing of "which job types are short of people", with amounts set by urgency of shortage and training cost. How differently the same job type is subsidized across cities directly reflects differences in how short-handed each place is. Online claims like "this trade makes X a month" mostly come from recruitment-platform listing prices or personal accounts; they overrepresent winners and leave out hours, commuting, occupational disease, and slack versus peak seasons.
- Benefit: The subsidy amounts themselves are the official pricing of "which job types are short of people". Policy requires "improving the differentiated subsidy mechanism determined by the urgency and shortage level of the occupation (job type), training costs, and similar factors", with each locality using this to determine the scope of its local subsidized vocational skills training programs. So the difference in subsidy amounts for the same job type across cities directly reflects the local degree of shortage
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: TODO (to be verified: official data sources for national average wages by occupation and rankings of in-demand occupations; the relevant MOHRSS pages could not be retrieved this time). Online claims of "this trade makes X a month" almost all come from recruitment-platform listing prices or personal accounts. What you get to see is mostly the people who made money, and none of it counts hours, commuting, occupational disease, or slack versus peak seasons. To see real demand, check the subsidy catalog on the local human resources and social security bureau website, then the job-demand rankings of public employment service agencies. Finally go to actual construction sites, factories, and shops and ask the people working there

### 13. For the same money and time, prefer short-cycle programs that lead directly to employable work
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Tuition for the short course, not much money. A stretch of time is taken up. The hard part is stopping at the agreed conditions when you're halfway through and nothing has paid off yet.
- In plain terms: What policy encourages is "quick, short, effective" training that leads to employment in a short time, plus micro-majors at vocational colleges. Screen a program on one question only: after finishing, is there a specific position to send a resume to. Something that takes a year and a half and still requires "accumulating more experience" after completion actually costs far more time than its sticker price. Write the exit condition before starting: at what point without finding work do you stop.
- Benefit: The policy line matches: support for "private training institutions offering market-oriented, high-quality vocational skills training programs and 'quick, short, effective' vocational skills training programs that can achieve employment in a short time", and for vocational colleges to "focus on the knowledge and skills the job market urgently needs... open micro-majors and vocational competency training courses"
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: The judging criterion is a single one: after finishing, is there a specific position to send a resume to. Programs that take a year and a half and still require "accumulating more experience" after completion cost far more than the stated price. The exit conditions from section 4 apply equally — write clearly before starting "at what point without finding work I stop"

### 14. After finishing a section, close the book and test yourself; don't go back and reread
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free, and total time does not increase either. Take the time you would have spent rereading and spend it instead on writing things down from memory or doing questions with the book closed. The hard part is that self-testing exposes on the spot what you haven't memorized, and that feels worse than rereading.
- In plain terms: Read something, then read it again — it feels familiar, but it doesn't stick. Close the book and test yourself once, and the effect is clearly better. Pooling 222 classroom studies and nearly fifty thousand students, classes that added quizzes raised their results by a medium-sized margin. In a review that ranked ten study methods, self-testing came in the top tier.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 222 independent studies and 48,478 students: classroom testing raises students' academic results, with an effect size in the medium range (g = 0.499). The size of the effect is shaped by several conditions: what study method the comparison group used, whether the test format matched the final exam, whether the material matched, whether corrective feedback was given, how many times the test was repeated, where and when it was taken, how long the intervention lasted, and what experimental design was used. Another review that evaluated ten common study methods one by one rated "practice testing" and "distributed practice" in the highest tier (high utility), on the grounds that they work for learners of different ages and different abilities and have been verified across multiple assessment formats and real educational settings.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yang C, Luo L, Vadillo MA, Yu R, Shanks DR (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 147(4), 399–435. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: Self-testing is not for grading yourself — it is for forcing the material out of your head. The method: finish a section, close the book, write out the key points from memory, then flip back and check. Corrective feedback improves the effect, so after checking, look closely at where you went wrong. Recording the benefit size as "large" follows from the effect size and the rating; both pieces of evidence measured grades, not hours, so this was not fitted to a time-saved threshold. This item and item 15 (spread the same time over several days) are the two highest-rated methods in the same review — use them together.

### 15. Spread the same time over several days; don't learn it all in one go
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free, and total time doesn't change. Only the scheduling changes: the same 3 hours, split into three days of 1 hour each. The hard part is starting several days ahead and not dragging it to the last night.
- In plain terms: The same amount of time, spread over several days, sticks better than learning it all at once. 317 experiments have tested this, and the conclusion is consistent. There is also a practical rule: the longer you want to remember something, the longer the review interval should be. Exam tomorrow, review today; exam in a month, go over it once every few days.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis covering 184 articles, 317 experiments, and 839 assessments of distributed practice effects. It compared two things: learning in one stretch versus split into several sessions (the spacing effect), and short intervals versus long ones (the lag effect). The conclusion: the review interval and "how far into the future you need to remember" jointly determine retention on the final test; specifically, the review interval that gives the best retention grows longer as the retention period grows longer. Another review that evaluated ten common study methods one by one rated distributed practice in the highest tier (high utility).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cepeda NJ, Pashler H, Vul E, Wixted JT, Rohrer D (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: This works best stacked with item 14 (self-testing with the book closed): spread the self-testing itself over several days. Note that the materials in the Cepeda meta-analysis were mostly word-list memory; no universal number of days is given for the best interval with complex content, and the "farther the exam, the longer the interval" above is the rule the paper itself gives, not a specific schedule. The problem with cramming the night before is not that it does nothing on the spot — it is that it fades fast.

### 16. Don't treat highlighting, rereading, and summarizing as your main study methods
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free — it actually saves time. The hard part is that these are the easiest to do and give the strongest feeling of "I'm studying".
- In plain terms: The methods students use most are highlighting and rereading, and the review that ranked ten study methods puts both in the lowest tier. Summarizing, the keyword mnemonic, and imagining pictures to go with the text are also in the lowest tier. This doesn't mean they are completely useless — it means the conditions under which they work are too narrow. The same time spent self-testing buys more.
- Benefit: A review evaluated ten common study methods and rated them on "whether the effect generalizes across learning conditions, across student characteristics, across materials, and across assessment formats". Rated in the highest tier: practice testing and distributed practice. Rated in the middle tier: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaved practice. Rated in the lowest tier, five methods: summarizing, highlighting (or underlining), the keyword mnemonic, imagining images for the text, and rereading. The review states plainly: most students say they use rereading and highlighting, but neither reliably raises grades, and they should be replaced with other methods — for example, replacing rereading with practice testing.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Notes: This item does not ban underlining. Underlining as "marking which spots I'll test myself on later" is useful; the problem is treating the underlining itself as the studying. The review also states that the lowest tier does not mean zero effect — only that the conditions for it to work are narrow and more research is needed. For the replacement actions, see item 14 (self-testing with the book closed) and item 15 (spread the same time over several days).

### 17. Mix several problem types when practicing; don't do twenty of one kind in a row
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. Change your workbook from doing problems in order by type to doing them shuffled. The hard part is that mixed practice lowers your accuracy on the spot, which feels like going backwards.
- In plain terms: Do a long run of the same problem type and it goes smoothly in the moment, but afterward you may not be able to tell which method to use. Shuffle several types together and the long-term effect is better, a medium-sized margin. But this trick is picky about material: it works best for images and classification, less for math problems, and for memorizing vocabulary, blocked practice is actually better.
- Benefit: A multilevel meta-analysis including 59 studies, 238 effect sizes, and 158 samples. The overall effect of interleaved practice over blocked practice is medium (Hedges' g = 0.42). By material: painting works best (g = 0.67), other visual materials also fairly well; math tasks show a smaller effect (g = 0.34); the overall effects for expository texts and taste materials are not significant; for vocabulary, blocked practice is actually better (g = −0.39). Regression analysis shows: the more similar the categories are to each other, the less similar the items within a category, and the more complex the material, the bigger the benefit of interleaving.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Brunmair M, Richter T (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin, 145(11), 1029–1052. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209>
- Notes: The review cited in item 16 (don't treat highlighting as your main study method) rated interleaved practice in the middle tier, on the grounds that there was not enough research at the time. Reading the two together, the usage is: content that requires "recognizing which type this is and which method to use" should be practiced mixed — math problem types, clinical cases, applying legal provisions; content that requires rote memorization should be practiced in blocks — vocabulary, term definitions.

### 18. Don't pick study methods on the grounds that "I'm a visual type, he's an auditory type"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. You simply stop choosing materials and courses by this classification, and stop paying for style assessments.
- In plain terms: "Everyone has their own learning style, and teaching to the style is what makes learning work" is widely believed. To prove it, you would have to first group students by style, then randomly assign each group a teaching method, and finally test everyone with the same exam — and almost no study has produced a result supporting it. People do differ in what they prefer, but there is no evidence that being taught according to your preference makes you learn better.
- Benefit: A review commissioned to assess the evidence on learning styles points out: to prove that "teaching to learning styles" works, you must first group students by style, then randomly assign the students in each group to different teaching methods, and finally test them all with the same exam. The criterion of effectiveness is the appearance of a specific interaction — the method most effective for one style is not the most effective for another. The review's conclusion: almost no such interaction has been found; the literature on learning styles is abundant, but studies using sound experimental designs are extremely rare, and the results of several of them run directly counter to the "matching hypothesis". The authors therefore hold that the existing evidence is insufficient to support using learning style assessments in educational practice.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Pashler H, McDaniel M, Rohrer D, Bjork R (2008). Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105–119. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x>
- Notes: The review also states that this does not mean every version of learning styles theory has been tested and refuted — many were never tested at all. What this item blocks is two actions: paying for style assessments, and picking training courses by style. For the criteria for choosing training, see item 9 (take the government-subsidized channel first) and item 13 (prefer short-cycle programs that lead directly to employable work).

### 19. Test yourself on memorized material in the form it will be used later; don't recite it verbatim once and call it done
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free, and not much extra time either. Only the practice changes: replace "recite it verbatim once" with "test yourself with questions shaped by how the material will be used later". The hard part is first thinking through how you will actually use this material.
- In plain terms: Testing yourself with the book closed has benefits that don't stop at the exact words you memorized. Pooling 192 comparisons and more than ten thousand people, it also works on questions with a changed format and on application and reasoning questions, with an effect from small to medium. But two situations are nearly useless: swapping the question and the answer around, and material you have only seen in the book and never been tested on.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled more than 40 years of research: 67 published and unpublished articles, 122 experiments, 192 transfer effect sizes, 10,382 people in total. Compared against a control of "look at it again but don't test", the learning from practice testing does transfer to other situations, with an effect size d = 0.40 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.50). The strongest transfer is seen in: testing with a changed question format, application and reasoning questions, medical diagnosis problems, and testing with close-in-meaning cue words. The weakest transfer is seen in: swapping the stimulus and the response around, material seen during initial learning but never tested, and worked-example problems. Among the moderators, whether the answer format at practice matches the one at test, whether retrieval practice with detailed feedback was done, and whether the first test was answered correctly all strongly affect whether positive transfer appears. The authors also corrected for publication bias in two ways: the moderator effect sizes were barely affected, but the intercept estimate was pushed down markedly — that is, when none of the conditions above hold, positive transfer often fails to appear
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Pan SC, Rickard TC (2018). Transfer of test-enhanced learning: meta-analytic review and synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710-756. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151>
- Notes: The word "memorize" usually mixes two things together: going over the material again (rereading, highlighting), and actively recalling it with the book closed. The first kind is rated lowest in that review ranking ten study methods — for the ratings themselves see item 16 (don't treat highlighting, rereading, and summarizing as your main study methods). The second kind is what this item is about: actively recalling with the book closed, in detail in item 14 (self-testing with the book closed), and it should be spread over several days, see item 15 (spread over several days). The method is to first think through how the material will be used later: does it need to be picked out on an exam paper, or judged on the spot in real life — then write your self-test questions in that form. Testing yourself with question and answer swapped around is also worth doing once, because in this meta-analysis transfer in the swapped direction is the weakest, so both directions need practicing. Memorized material that is never tested in a new form and never put to use is what is truly memorized in vain.
