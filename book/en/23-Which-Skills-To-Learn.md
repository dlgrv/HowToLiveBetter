# 23. Which Skills Are Worth Learning

The frame here is monetary return versus time cost; item 2 additionally factors in mortality. The first six items answer "should I keep studying or not" — the law first rules out a portion of the options, then come the longevity accounting, how scarce each education level is within the population, subsidies for those who cannot afford school, the pathways for people who never entered general academic high school, and finally a method for running the numbers yourself. The last seven answer "once you have decided to learn something, how to choose it". Wage statistics broken down by occupation in China are difficult to verify item by item through public official channels, so this chapter covers only what has a traceable source: the evidence on returns to education, how to verify whether a certificate is legitimate, where training subsidies come from, and which dimensions of a skill are more resistant to replacement. As for how much a specific trade earns per month, this book gives no numbers.
### 1. Under 16 there is no "go get a job" option: whoever hires you is fined 5,000 yuan per month, and anyone willing to hire you is off the books
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; first work out your own age
- In plain terms: People usually finish junior high at 15 to 16, exactly on this line. The law on compulsory education states that parents must send their children to school to receive and complete compulsory education, and forbids employers from hiring children who should still be in school; the state regulation prohibiting child labor adds another layer: state organs, social organizations, enterprises and public institutions, private non-enterprise entities, and individually owned businesses may not hire anyone under 16, with a fine of 5,000 yuan per child laborer per month, rising to 10,000 yuan per person per month if not corrected after the deadline, and a 5,000-yuan fine for brokering even one person into child labor. So the only work you can find under 16 will come from units that knowingly break the law to use you — no contract, no social insurance contributions, and if something goes wrong, compensation is negotiated privately rather than handled as a work injury.
- Benefit: Article 11 of the law on compulsory education: "children who have reached the age of six must have their parents or other legal guardians send them to school to receive and complete compulsory education"; Article 14: "employers are prohibited from hiring school-age children and adolescents who should be receiving compulsory education"; Article 58: where there is no justifiable reason for failing to enroll school-age children or adolescents in school as required by the law, the local township people's government or the education administrative department of the county-level people's government shall "give criticism and education and order correction within a time limit". The state regulation prohibiting child labor, Article 2: state organs, social organizations, enterprises and public institutions, private non-enterprise entities, and individually owned businesses "may not hire minors under the age of 16"; Article 6: penalties are imposed "at the standard of a fine of 5,000 yuan per child laborer per month", and if not corrected after the deadline, 10,000 yuan per person per month; Article 7: for brokering child-labor employment "a fine of 5,000 yuan is imposed for each person introduced"; Article 10: if a child laborer falls ill or is injured, the employer "must take responsibility for sending them to a medical institution for treatment and bear all medical and living expenses during the treatment period", with a one-time compensation payment additionally owed in case of disability or death. Article 13 leaves a single exception: with the consent of parents or other guardians, culture and arts organizations and sports organizations may hire professional performers and athletes under 16 (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 义务教育法（2018 年第二次修正，第十一、十四、五十八条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>（教育部刊登）；国务院 (2002). 禁止使用童工规定（国务院令第 364 号，第二、六、七、十、十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiary is yourself. After 16, working is legal, but the jobs willing to take a 16-year-old with no credentials are exactly the category covered in item 10 of this section: fixed routines and the highest replaceability. The overtime pay, annual leave, and work-injury benefits discussed in section 19 all presuppose an employment relationship and evidence; off-the-books jobs have neither

### 2. Put "is schooling useful" into the mortality ledger too: each additional year of education lowers adult death risk by about 1.9%
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: the extra years of time and energy spent studying; for tuition, see item 4 of this section
- In plain terms: Pooling 603 studies from 59 countries with more than ten thousand observation points gives this result: years of education and mortality have a dose relationship — each additional year of schooling means an average of about 1.9% lower death risk in adulthood. The effect is most visible at ages 18 to 49, about 2.9% lower per extra year, dropping to 0.8% past age 70. No difference was seen between men and women, or between poor and rich countries. In other words, the difference between finishing high school and stopping at junior high is not just wages.
- Benefit: Systematic review and meta-analysis, searching January 1980 to May 2023, finally including 603 studies, 70 sites in 59 countries, and 10,355 observations: education shows a dose-response relationship with all-cause adult mortality, with each additional year of education lowering the risk of death by an average of 1.9% (95% uncertainty interval 1.8–2.0); among people aged 18 to 49, each additional year lowers it by 2.9% (2.8–3.0), and among people over 70, by 0.8% (0.6–1.0); no differences in effect were found by sex or by sociodemographic index
- Evidence grade: A (contested)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Notes: Contested. The authors themselves report publication bias (p<0.0001) and high heterogeneity across studies; all included studies are observational, and years of education are highly entangled with family circumstances, region, and occupation — how much of this 1.9% comes from education itself and how much from "people who can keep studying in the first place already had better conditions" cannot be separated with this kind of research. So it is strong evidence that "more schooling and living longer move in the same direction", not a causal guarantee that "sending you to school extends life by 1.9%". The beneficiary is yourself

### 3. Before judging "has a degree lost value", first look at the national education structure: only 15,467 people per 100,000 have a college-level education
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; a look at the numbers
- In plain terms: The 2020 seventh national census found that per 100,000 people nationwide, only 15,467 have junior college or above (about 15%), 15,088 have senior high including vocational secondary school, 34,507 have junior high, and 24,767 have primary school; the average years of schooling for people aged 15 and over is 9.91 years, which is just past junior high graduation. A junior high education in China is not "enough" — it is the largest and most easily replaced bracket. "985 graduates delivering takeout" and "junior high dropouts becoming bosses" get repeated over and over precisely because they are unusual; being talked about a lot does not mean it happens a lot.
- Benefit: Seventh national population census (reference time 00:00 November 1, 2020): among the national population, 218,360,767 people have a college education (junior college or above), 213,005,258 have senior high (including vocational secondary school), 487,163,489 have junior high, and 349,658,828 have primary school. Compared with the 2010 sixth census, per 100,000 people those with a college education rose from 8,930 to 15,467, senior high rose from 14,032 to 15,088, junior high fell from 38,788 to 34,507, and primary school fell from 26,779 to 24,767; average years of schooling for those aged 15 and over rose from 9.08 to 9.91 years, and the illiteracy rate fell from 4.08% to 2.67%
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家统计局、国务院第七次全国人口普查领导小组办公室 (2021). 第七次全国人口普查公报（第六号）——人口受教育情况. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>
- Notes: This item answers only "how scarce is each education level in the population", not "is schooling worth it for one particular person" — the latter you must calculate yourself by the method in item 6 of this section. One caveat: the census measures the stock of the whole national population, and the proportion among young people is far higher than this average, so comparing yourself against your own age group using it overstates your advantage; conversely, using it to rebut "schooling is useless" is valid, because the denominator is the entire labor force you are competing against. The beneficiary is yourself

### 4. Run "can't afford school" through the policy ledger first: vocational school tuition is mostly waived, grants are 2,300 yuan, student loans up to 20,000 yuan a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; one inquiry at the school's student aid office before the semester starts, one submission of paperwork
- In plain terms: Compulsory education already waives tuition and fees. On the vocational secondary school side, at public schools tuition is already waived for rural students (including those from county towns), for urban students in agriculture-related programs, and for students from families in financial difficulty. The national grants for vocational secondary school and academic senior high school rise from 2,000 yuan to 2,300 yuan per year starting from the spring semester of 2025. Once at university, the national grant for undergraduate and junior college students rises from 3,300 to 3,700 yuan, the national encouragement scholarship from 5,000 to 6,000 yuan, the national student loan cap for undergraduates and junior college students from 16,000 to 20,000 yuan a year and for graduate students from 20,000 to 25,000 yuan, with the interest rate at the same-term, same-tier LPR minus 70 basis points. Not enough money can mean postponing or changing paths, but run against these figures it rarely amounts to "must drop out and work right now".
- Benefit: From the fall semester of 2012, "tuition is waived for all rural students (including those from county towns), urban students in agriculture-related programs, and students from families in financial difficulty among formally enrolled full-time first-, second-, and third-year students at public secondary vocational schools (excluding students in related performing programs in the arts)". The 2024 adjustment of student aid policies: the national grant for undergraduate and junior college students rises from 3,300 yuan to 3,700 yuan per student per year, the national encouragement scholarship from 5,000 yuan to 6,000 yuan, effective from the fall semester of 2024 (the encouragement scholarship from 2024); the national grants for secondary vocational schools and for academic senior high schools both rise from 2,000 yuan to 2,300 yuan per student per year, effective from the spring semester of 2025. For national student loans from the fall semester of 2024, for undergraduate and junior college students "the annual loan application limit per person rises from no more than 16,000 yuan to no more than 20,000 yuan", and for graduate students "from no more than 20,000 yuan to no more than 25,000 yuan", with the interest rate at the same-term, same-tier LPR minus 70 basis points (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 财政部、发展改革委、教育部、人力资源社会保障部 (2012). 关于扩大中等职业教育免学费政策范围 进一步完善国家助学金制度的意见（财教〔2012〕376 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>；财政部、教育部、人力资源社会保障部有关司局负责人 (2024). 就调整学生资助政策有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>；财政部、教育部、人民银行、金融监管总局 (2024). 关于调整完善国家助学贷款有关政策的通知（财教〔2024〕188 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. The exact coverage of tuition waivers and the tiering ratios of grants are set by each province; private schools are subsidized at the local public rate for the same type of program and the shortfall is still paid out of pocket, so verify against local documents before enrolling. A student loan is a loan, not a grant, and must be repaid after graduation — but it splits "can't pay tuition right now" from "are these years worth it", so don't treat the funding problem as the conclusion. Section 7 covered training subsidies and temporary assistance during unemployment; when the family's income truly stops, take that route first

### 5. Failing to get into academic senior high does not mean the road is cut: vocational schools have through-track admissions and separate exams, and skill-job hiring can lower degree requirements
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; one check of your province's vocational education admissions platform before submitting senior high school preferences
- In plain terms: The law on vocational education, revised in 2022, defines vocational education as "a type of education of equal importance with general education", and gives explicit pathways upward: secondary vocational schools can run through-track admissions and training with higher vocational colleges in relevant majors, higher vocational colleges can admit students through a combination of academic attainment plus vocational skills, and higher vocational colleges and ordinary universities that run vocational education must also set aside a proportion of their enrollment plans, or hold separate exams, specifically for secondary vocational graduates. Employment has explicit provisions too: employers may not set application, hiring, or recruitment conditions that obstruct equal employment for vocational school graduates, and in open recruitment by public institutions, positions requiring vocational skill levels may appropriately lower their degree requirements. Scoring too low for academic senior high means switching to another road, not stopping at a junior high education.
- Benefit: Law on Vocational Education (in force from May 1, 2022), Article 3: "vocational education is a type of education of equal importance with general education". Article 37: secondary vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, implement through-track admissions and training with higher vocational school education in relevant majors"; higher vocational schools "may, in accordance with relevant state provisions, admit students through an assessment method combining academic attainment and vocational skills"; education administrative departments at the provincial level and above must establish a unified vocational education admissions platform that aggregates and publishes schools, program offerings, and admissions information and provides inquiry and application services. Article 53: "vocational school students enjoy equal opportunities with same-level ordinary school students in further study, employment, and career development"; higher vocational schools and ordinary universities implementing vocational education "must set a corresponding proportion in their enrollment plans or adopt separate examination methods to recruit vocational school graduates specifically"; "employers may not set application, hiring, or recruitment conditions that obstruct equal employment and fair competition for vocational school graduates", and in open recruitment by public institutions, positions with vocational skill level requirements "may appropriately lower the degree requirement" (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2022). 职业教育法（2022 年修订，第三、三十七、五十三条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html>（教育部刊登）
- Notes: The beneficiaries are yourself and your children. These are statutory pathways, not guaranteed admission: the range of majors in through-track training, the proportion of separate admissions, and the subjects of the vocational education exam are all set by each province — before submitting preferences you must check your own province's vocational education admissions platform and enrollment plan for that year, and not extrapolate from another province's experience. When picking a secondary vocational major, screen it with the dimensions in item 10 of this section — majors requiring presence on site and in non-standard environments are more resistant to replacement. Before choosing a school, first verify its operating credentials with the three steps in item 8; treat any admissions pitch promising "guaranteed job placement" or "guaranteed pass" as an automatic no

### 6. Turn "study or work" into a problem you compute: three years of early wages set against decades of annual income difference
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; sit down and compute it once, half an hour
- In plain terms: Lay both roads out on paper. The work-now road: the gain is three years of wages from a low-barrier job, and the cost is the slice you lose from every year after. The keep-studying road: the cost is tuition plus living expenses, minus the tuition waivers, grants, and student loans from item 4 of this section, plus the wages not earned during those years; the gain is the income difference brought by each extra year of education, multiplied by the remaining thirty or forty years of working life — plus the mortality ledger from item 2 of this section. The key is that the early earnings are one-off, while the annual income difference gets multiplied by your years of work. China's urban estimates rose from 4.0% a year in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001; the data are old and usable only as an order of magnitude.
- Benefit: Estimates for urban China 1988 to 2001: the return to education "rose sharply from 4.0% per year of education in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001", with the increase occurring mainly after 1992, reflecting the rise in the wage premium for higher education. This is the locally available Chinese order of magnitude for converting "one more year of schooling" into an annual income difference; for the global average, see item 7 of this section
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>
- Notes: The data end in 2001 and cover only urban areas, so they cannot be used directly as today's numbers; the same applies to the 9% global average in item 7 of this section. This item gives a method, not an answer: computed this way, the conclusion varies from person to person — high tuition, a mismatch between major and job, and a long duration of study can genuinely come out as not worth it, which does not amount to "schooling is useless", only to "this specific program isn't worth it". Don't conflate the two. Beyond the income difference, several items exist that won't fit in the formula but are real: statutory degree thresholds (item 5 of this section), resistance to replacement (item 10), and the ability to switch industries. The beneficiary is yourself

### 7. Memorize the baseline first: each additional year of education yields a global average private return of about 9% a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; use it as the baseline for comparing any "learn something" plan
- In plain terms: A review covering 139 countries and 1,120 estimates works out that each additional year of schooling means personal income about 9% higher on average per year, and the return for women is consistently higher than for men. This is the average for "one more year of formal education", not the payoff of "signing up for a course". Use it as a ruler: a program asks you for 30,000 yuan and half a year — ask whether the income change it brings matches a year of formal education.
- Benefit: A review covering 1950 to 2014, 139 countries, and 1,120 estimates: "the global average private return to an additional year of education is 9% a year"; the private return to higher education is rising, and the social return to education remains high; the average return to education for women is consistently higher than for men
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>；世界银行政策研究工作论文 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>
- Notes: This is the average for "one more year of schooling", not the payoff of "signing up for a course", and not a China-specific number — it serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference. Its use is to give every training plan a ruler: a course asking 30,000 yuan and half a year of your time should be asked whether it can deliver an income change equivalent to a year of formal education. The global average masks enormous differences across fields and across countries

### 8. Before paying for a certificate, first check whether it is in the national occupational qualification catalog or on the MOHRSS roster of registered assessment bodies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; one check, a dozen or so minutes
- In plain terms: The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security has run dedicated crackdowns on exactly this mess — organizations using the names of new occupations and skill levels to run courses haphazardly, charge fees arbitrarily, and hand out certificates indiscriminately, even impersonating authoritative bodies; enforcement was carried out jointly with cyberspace, public security, and market regulation authorities, shutting down non-compliant advertising. Before signing up, check in three steps: whether the occupation is in the national occupational qualification catalog, whether the issuing body is an assessment body registered with the human resources and social security departments, and whether the certificate number can be looked up on the official platform. If it can't be found, the registration fee is money thrown away.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security has run a nationwide special crackdown for this reason: "some institutions and units, under the names of new-occupation qualifications and vocational skill levels, arbitrarily conduct training, assessment, and certificate-issuing activities, charging fees indiscriminately and issuing certificates indiscriminately; some institutions and units engage in false or exaggerated publicity, and even organize training, assessment, and certificate issuance in the name of authoritative bodies". Measures included enforcement jointly with cyberspace, public security, and market regulation departments, "severely cracking down on certificate-issuing and assessment activities impersonating appraisal institutions and assessment bodies registered with human resources and social security departments", and lawfully shutting down non-compliant promotion on websites and new media. It also required "guiding the public to learn the channels and methods for verifying certificates and to judge whether a certificate is lawful, compliant, and worth anything"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 人力资源社会保障部 (2022). 关于开展技术技能类「山寨证书」专项治理工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>
- Notes: Verification order: first check whether the occupation is in the national occupational qualification catalog — if it is not in the catalog, it is not a national occupational qualification; then check whether the issuing body is an assessment body registered with the human resources and social security departments; finally, look up the certificate number on the official skill-talent assessment certificate verification platform. If it can't be found, do not enroll. Certificates bearing the words "international", "senior", or "registered", or promising "guaranteed pass" or "rent it out to an employer for income", carry the highest risk

### 9. For training, take the government-subsidized channel first; don't start by paying out of pocket for a commercial course
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan or a large reduction; the training itself takes time
- In plain terms: From 2025 to the end of 2027, the state plans to run subsidized training for more than 30 million person-times, with the money coming through multiple channels — employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, enterprise employee education funds, and others — and subsidy levels set by how short-handed the occupation is and by training cost. Enterprises are also required to spend more than sixty percent of their employee education funds on front-line workers. If you're employed, ask your employer first; everyone else should read their local human resources and social security bureau's subsidy catalog before deciding what to learn — cheaper than choosing first and hunting for a subsidy afterward.
- Benefit: The national scale is explicit: from 2025 to the end of 2027, "carry out subsidized training for more than 30 million person-times, focusing on high-end, cutting-edge industries and high-demand sectors, key employment groups, and so on". Funding sources are consolidated into one package: employment subsidy funds, the unemployment insurance fund, industry development funds, east-west collaboration funds, enterprise employee education funds, and so on; subsidies are set differentially by how urgently and critically in demand the occupation (job type) is and by training cost. On the enterprise side it is also required to "withdraw and use employee education funds in full as prescribed, ensuring that more than 60% goes to the education and training of front-line workers"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Notes: Claim procedures and amounts are set by each province; the entry points are the "vocational training subsidy catalog" on the local human resources and social security bureau website and the public practical training bases. Section 7 covered vocational training subsidies during unemployment (a maximum of 3 per person) and social insurance subsidies; employed people should ask about their employer's employee education funds first. Checking the subsidy catalog before choosing what to learn saves far more money than choosing first and then looking for a subsidy

### 10. When picking a skill, prioritize "does it need hands-on work and on-the-spot judgment" — these are the hardest to displace with automation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; screen candidates against this dimension before choosing
- In plain terms: In an assessment of 702 US occupations, about 47% of jobs fell in the high-risk band, and what holds the machines back is three things: manual dexterity, creativity, and the ability to deal with people. So work that requires being on site, in non-standard environments, and making judgment calls on the spot is harder to displace, while jobs with fixed routines centered on processing information carry the highest risk. This research scored entire occupations; later estimates that split jobs into specific tasks come out much lower — don't fixate on the 47% figure, look at those three dimensions.
- Benefit: Assessment of the computerization probability of 702 US occupations: about 47% of employment is in the high-risk band. In the model, the level of risk is decided by three kinds of "engineering bottlenecks" — perception and manipulation (finger and hand dexterity, working in confined spaces and awkward positions), creative intelligence, and social intelligence. That is, work in non-standard environments requiring manual skill and on-the-spot judgment is systematically less likely to be replaced, while jobs with fixed routines centered on information processing carry the highest risk
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>
- Notes: Contested. This research is a 2013 working paper that scored "whether an entire occupation can be automated"; later research (such as estimates splitting by task rather than occupation) finds far lower high-risk shares; it also does not predict in which year anything will happen. The correct way to use it is not the 47% figure but the three bottleneck dimensions it identified — these explain why on-site installation, repair, and care work resists replacement better than pure paperwork, and why "learning a skill" should first mean the kind that requires being on site, facing non-standard environments

### 11. Before learning, confirm whether the skill has a graded assessment and whether the grade can be converted into money
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; one check
- In plain terms: The state is promoting the "new eight-grade worker" system, requiring enterprises to link both position pay and skill grades, and to expand the evaluation and appointment of senior workers and above; qualifying employees can even have their grade determined directly without regard to years of service or seniority. This is a policy direction, not a wage number already delivered — it varies a lot by region and by enterprise. The usable judgment: only skills with a national occupational standard and a grading track can later be discussed in terms of pay raises tied to grades.
- Benefit: The state is promoting the "new eight-grade worker" vocational skill grade system and explicitly requires linking grades to pay: "strengthen the skill-oriented pay distribution system... guide enterprises to adopt distribution methods linking position pay to both position value and skill grade"; each locality is to "increase the intensity and frequency of evaluation and appointment for senior workers and above"; qualifying enterprises may conduct employee skill grade evaluations on their own, and for outstanding employees "may set aside conditions such as length of service, grade, and seniority, and directly recognize the corresponding vocational skill grade"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>；人力资源社会保障部 (2021). 「技能中国行动」实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/06/content_5622619.htm>
- Notes: This is a policy direction, not a wage number already delivered, and the degree of implementation varies greatly by region and by enterprise. The actionable part: when choosing a skill, check whether it has a corresponding national occupational standard and grading track — only skills with a track can later involve pay raises by grade; a "skill" with neither grades nor an industry entry threshold leaves you finding work entirely on your own after finishing

### 12. Don't take "the internet says the pay is high" as evidence of labor shortage; look up your locality's in-demand occupation catalog and subsidy catalog
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; look up two local documents
- In plain terms: How much subsidy each locality pays is itself the official pricing of "which job types are short of people" — subsidy amounts are set by urgency of shortage and training cost. How differently the same job type is subsidized across cities directly reflects differences in how short-handed each place is. Online claims like "this trade makes X a month" mostly come from recruitment-platform listing prices or personal accounts; they overrepresent winners and exclude hours, commuting, occupational disease, and slack versus peak seasons.
