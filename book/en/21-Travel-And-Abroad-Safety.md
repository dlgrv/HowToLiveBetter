# 21. Traveling abroad, trips, and safety overseas

This section deals with both financial matters and personal freedom, and several points also relate to physical safety. Once you’re overseas, it’s the laws of the host country that apply — not Chinese law. There are clear limits to what China’s embassies and consulates can do to assist you. This section explains those limits in detail.
### 1. Before you leave, check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs safety advisory once, and do not go anywhere marked "Do not travel for now"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Open the China Consular Service website and read through that page. It takes a few minutes.
- In plain terms: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs sorts safety risks abroad into 4 levels. The highest is called "Do not travel for now." From September (9) 15, 2026, staying away from high-risk countries and regions is written into an administrative regulation. When you apply for documents and when you go through exit checks, staff will also remind you, and when needed they will urge you not to go. Reading that page before you leave takes a few minutes.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs publishes safety advisories for foreign countries, ranked by risk. From low to high there are about 4 levels: "Pay attention to safety," "Strengthen safety precautions," "Travel with caution," and "Do not travel for now." The new rule in force from September (9) 15, 2026 also writes a personal duty into an administrative regulation. The original text says: "Chinese citizens shall pay attention to safety advisories for foreign countries and to safety-risk notices for tourist destinations, and shall avoid traveling to and remaining in high-risk countries or regions." The new rule also requires the immigration authorities (the departments that issue entry and exit documents and run border inspection), when handling documents and at exit border inspection, to remind people who are heading to high-risk areas. The original text of that part says: "For a Chinese citizen preparing to go to a country or region whose risk level is the highest, or where cases that seriously endanger personal safety have suddenly become frequent, they shall, when necessary, dissuade that person from going."

A package-tour contract has to be in writing. Before you join a group, you can ask them to write down the risks at the destination. That last point is the author's suggestion.

### 2. Save the 12308 hotline number and your local embassy/consulate’s consular protection number in your phone, then write them down and keep a copy in your wallet — don’t wait until an emergency to look them up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Saving two numbers and writing one copy down takes just a few minutes.

- In plain terms: When something happens, call the Ministry of Foreign Affairs global consular-protection hotline first. If you are in China, dial 12308. If you are abroad, dial +86-10-12308. Since this hotline opened, it has taken more than 3000000 calls. Over the same period, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and its missions abroad handled more than 500000 consular-protection cases.

- Benefit: The Ministry of Foreign Affairs operates a 24-hour global emergency hotline for consular protection and services. Inside China, call 12308; abroad, call +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308. Since its launch in 2014, this hotline has answered over 3 million calls, while the Ministry and its overseas missions have resolved more than 500,000 consular protection incidents.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:外交部、中国驻缅甸使领馆 (2023). 关于当前缅北地区安全局势的领事提醒. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>；国务院新闻办 (2023). 国务院政策例行吹风会介绍领事保护与协助有关情况. <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2023zccfh/11/index.htm>

- Notes: Your local embassy or consulate also maintains its own consular protection hotline, which is often quicker to reach than the main hotline. Before traveling, look up that number on the China Consular Service website for your destination country and save it alongside the main hotline number. However, if your phone is lost or runs out of battery, those saved numbers become useless. That’s why it’s wise to write down both numbers and keep one copy in your wallet, or give a copy to a family member.

### 3. Know what consular protection can and cannot do: it can visit you, it cannot get you out, and you still pay the costs
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Spend a few minutes and read this line clearly.
- In plain terms: If you are arrested abroad, what the embassy or consulate can do is check the facts with the local side, ask that you be handled fairly and according to law, come to see you, and sit in when the court hears the case. It cannot change the judgment for you, and it cannot take you away. Food, lodging, transport, and medical bills that someone else paid first are still yours to pay in the end. So get your money and your insurance ready before you leave, and do not treat "if something happens, the state will cover it" as the plan.
- Benefit: If you are arrested, the embassy or consulate can do these things. It can check the situation with the relevant department of the host country and ask that you be handled fairly and according to law. The original text of the regulations says: "visit that person or contact that person in accordance with the law of the host country and the international treaties China and the host country have concluded or both joined, learn what that person needs, and require the relevant department of the host country to give that Chinese citizen humanitarian treatment and fair treatment." In everyday words, the embassy or consulate visits you, contacts you, and asks what you need, following local law and the treaties both countries signed, and it asks the local side to treat you by a humane and fair standard. When the court sits, the embassy or consulate may attend and may ask that your rights in the case be protected. If you are injured or caught in a disaster, it can press the local side for emergency help and can help your family with the aftermath. On money, the regulations are just as clear: "Where a Chinese citizen, legal person, or unincorporated organization, in the course of consular protection and assistance, receives goods and services such as food and lodging, transport, and medical care provided by a third party, that person or organization shall pay the expenses that it should bear itself." Food, lodging, transport, and medical care that someone else advanced, if they are yours to pay, are still yours to pay.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第九、十、十四、二十六条）
- Notes: The embassy or consulate will not pay your fine, will not pay your medical bill, will not buy your plane ticket, and cannot change the conclusion of the host country's court. What it can give you is information on local lawyers, translators, medical care, and funeral homes, and it can help you contact relatives and friends and help you find a channel for relief. So get your money and your insurance ready yourself before you leave, and do not treat "if something happens, the state will cover it" as the plan.

### 4. Buy a policy that covers overseas medical care and medical evacuation, and do not buy only flight-delay insurance
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A short-term overseas travel policy costs a few dozen to a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: Medical bills abroad come out of your own pocket. China's basic medical insurance almost never pays them, and the embassy or consulate will not advance the money either. What can really drag a family under is not the outpatient fee. It is the hospital stay after a serious injury, and getting the person home, which takes a special flight or a medical escort. When you buy travel insurance, look mainly at 2 limits: overseas medical expenses, and emergency medical evacuation and return.
- Benefit: Medical bills abroad are yours to pay. Domestic yibao (医保 — basic medical insurance) basically does not reimburse care abroad. Consular protection does not advance this money either. See item 3 of this section (what consular protection can and cannot do). The basis is Article 26 of the regulations. What can really drag a family under is not the outpatient fee. It is the hospital stay after a serious injury, and medical evacuation. Moving a person from a hospital abroad back to China takes a special flight or a medical escort, and that is among the most expensive items you pay for yourself. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says that emergency transport of a patient from a remote place to a good hospital, if you pay it yourself, can cost more than 100000 US dollars. When you buy insurance, look first at 2 limits. The first is the limit for overseas medical expenses. The other is the limit for emergency medical evacuation and return, which is how much can be paid, at most, for a transfer after something happens and for sending the person home.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第二十六条）；美国疾病控制与预防中心. Travelers' Health: Travel Insurance：「Medical evacuation insurance covers emergency transportation from a remote area to a high-quality hospital, which could otherwise cost more than $100,000.」. <https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/insurance>；其余为作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: 100000 US dollars is a floor, not a range of costs. No published official or industry range of costs was found, so this book does not write a specific range. Before you buy, confirm 3 things. First, whether the country you are going to is inside the coverage. Second, whether high-risk activities such as diving, skiing, and riding a motorcycle are excluded. Third, whether the insurer settles directly with the hospital, or you pay first and claim it back later. Buy it for a visa-free country as well.

### 5. Treat every "high-paying job overseas" as a scam; if you are tricked into telecom fraud and come back, you may also be barred from leaving the country
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is personally turning down a job that looks very good.
- In plain terms: The monthly pay is absurdly high, they do not ask about education or experience, they do not sign a proper contract, they tell you to gather first in a border city, and they keep your passport for you. If you take that kind of work, neither you nor the passport is in your own hands. If the papers are false when you exit, border inspection will stop you and will not let you through. If you break the law abroad, or harm national security and national interests, you may also be barred from leaving the country for 6 months to 3 years after you come back.
- Benefit: The official description of this kind of case is direct. In recent years there have been people who "leave the country because they were deceived, or illegally leave the country on a made-up pretext, to engage in cross-border gambling, telecom and network fraud, and other illegal activity, seriously harming the safety of the public's lives and property." That means being tricked into going, or inventing a reason and slipping out, and then gambling and running telecom fraud over there. The new rule tightens both ends of this. On the way out: "The reason given for an application to exit or enter, or to stay or reside, shall be true and lawful." If you hand in false materials or tell a lie, the immigration authority has the power not to issue you documents and not to let you leave. On the way back: "Where a Chinese citizen engages in illegal or criminal activity abroad and harms national security and national interests, a decision may be made... that the person shall not leave the country for a period of not less than 6 months and not more than 3 years from the date of return." A person placed in administrative detention for obtaining documents by fraud, or for illegal exit or entry, is likewise barred from leaving the country for 6 months to 3 years.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、四条）；司法部、公安部、国家移民局 (2026). 就《国务院关于出境入境管理的规定》答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7077270.htm>
- Notes: The signs are basically fixed: monthly pay far above the same job, no requirement for education or experience, no proper contract, a first gathering in a border city, and someone else arranging the visa and keeping your passport. Northern Myanmar already has a specific consular advisory. If you actually go, your personal liberty and your passport are not in your own hands. Do not assume you can leave whenever you want to.

### 6. Overseas cash withdrawals in a year cannot exceed 100000 yuan, counted together across every card in your own name; if you carry cash in or out, RMB is at most 20000 yuan each time, and foreign currency over 5000 US dollars must be declared to customs
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before you leave, estimate how much cash this trip will need, and put the rest on a card. The hard part is changing the habit: if you can pay by card, do not withdraw cash. If you need to carry a large amount of foreign currency in cash out of the country, you first have to go to a bank and get a permit, which is an extra trip.
- In plain terms: Overseas cash withdrawals are counted together across every bank card in your own name, with a cap of 100000 yuan a year. Once you go over it, you cannot withdraw again for the rest of that year or the next year. Card spending is not affected. If you carry cash in or out, RMB is at most 20000 yuan each time, and foreign currency over 5000 US dollars must be declared to customs. If you do not declare it, you may be fined. Hiding it and carrying it counts as smuggling, and the cash can be confiscated.
- Benefit: The basis is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange notice on regulating large overseas cash withdrawals with bank cards, Hui Fa [2017] No. 29, in force from January (1) 1, 2018. Article 1: "Where an individual withdraws cash overseas with a domestic bank card, the bank cards in that person's own name (including supplementary cards), taken together, shall not exceed the equivalent of 100000 yuan in each calendar year. If the annual quota is exceeded, overseas cash withdrawal with a domestic bank card will be suspended for the current year and the following year." Paragraph 2 of the same article: "An individual shall not evade, or help someone else evade, the controls on overseas cash withdrawal by borrowing another person's bank card or by lending out his or her own bank card." Article 2: every day the foreign-exchange authority sends the suspension list to the card-issuing banks, and a card-issuing bank shall, no later than 17 o'clock Beijing time that day, suspend overseas cash withdrawal with that bank's cards for the people on the list. Article 3: a person placed on the suspension list may take a valid identity document to a branch of the foreign-exchange authority and look up his or her own overseas cash-withdrawal record. Article 4: the per-card daily cap for a foreign-currency card was adjusted from the equivalent of 1000 US dollars to the equivalent of 10000 yuan; for an RMB card, the cap stays at no more than the equivalent of 10000 yuan per card per day. Article 6: where a card-issuing bank, a domestic RMB-card clearing organization, or an individual violates this notice, the penalty follows the relevant provisions of the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration. A press Q&A from the foreign-exchange authority also makes a few points clear. The notice does not affect an individual's card spending overseas. Ordinary transactions for food, lodging, transport, and shopping on an overseas trip, a business trip, or study abroad can all be paid by bank card. That spending also does not use up the annual 50000 US dollar facilitation quota for buying foreign exchange. The annual quota is calculated by the foreign-exchange authority from the data of all the card-issuing institutions, because real-time control would slow the response of the transaction, and "the foreign-exchange authority uses a delayed control method" (it does not block the transaction on the spot). Separately, statistics show that in 2016, 81% of overseas cash withdrawals on domestic bank cards were below 30000 yuan, nationwide. Carrying cash in or out has a separate set of rules. The new customs declaration form in use from April (4) 1, 2025 says on the back: "The RMB each person carries in or out each time shall not exceed 20000 yuan." For foreign-currency cash on the way in, "an amount exceeding the equivalent of 5000 US dollars shall be declared to customs in writing." On the way out, if you carry the equivalent of more than 5000 US dollars and up to 10000 US dollars, you first get a Permit for Carrying Foreign Exchange out of the Country from a bank, then declare it to customs. Above 10000 US dollars, you get that permit from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. The foreign-exchange authority's measures state that a person leaving the country "in principle shall not carry foreign-currency cash exceeding the equivalent of 10000 US dollars out of the country." The customs measures on supervision of luggage and articles provide that currency cash above the set amount must be declared to customs in writing both on the way in and on the way out. If the written declaration is still not finished when customs starts the inspection, it counts as not declared. If you did not declare it, but you did not hide it, customs gives a warning and may impose a fine of up to 20% of the value of the goods. If you hide it, disguise it, or under-declare it, that counts as smuggling: the cash is confiscated, and a fine of up to the same value may also be imposed.

If you really need a large amount of cash overseas, buy the foreign exchange first under the Measures for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange, then carry the foreign-currency cash out. Above 5000 US dollars, remember to get the carrying permit first and declare it in the red channel at customs. The destination country has its own declaration threshold too. Check it once before you leave. ### 7. Take photos of your passport, visa, and ID card and store them in the cloud; if lost, report to police first and then apply for a travel document
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost involved. Simply take a few photos before you depart.
- In plain terms: If your passport is lost, the embassy or consulate can issue you a travel document for the trip home, but you have to prove who you are. If you have photos of the data page and the visa page, and your entry and exit records, the replacement is much faster. You also have to report the loss to the local police first and get the receipt. Exit checks and insurance claims in many countries ask for that paper. A phone is often lost together with the passport, so do not keep the photos only on the phone.
- Benefit: When a passport is lost, foreign embassies or consulates can issue a travel document for repatriation, but only after you verify your identity. The speed of this process depends heavily on whether you possess clear photos of the passport data page, visa pages, and entry/exit records. Additionally, obtaining a police report is mandatory for border checks and insurance claims in many countries. The contact numbers for embassy assistance are the same two emergency hotlines listed in item 2.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十六条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: Store the photos in two separate locations — for instance, upload one set to cloud storage and send another to a trusted family member. Also print a hard copy and keep it apart from the original documents. Photograph your bank cards and prescriptions as well. Since phones and passports are frequently lost together, relying only on phone storage is insufficient. Cloud services often require SMS verification, which becomes impossible if your phone is lost; test access from another device before travel to ensure you can retrieve the files. Photos of official documents are sensitive information; encrypted storage is safer, but if you forget the password, no one can access them, so the copy sent to family members remains essential.

### 8. Before you drive yourself abroad, first confirm whether the local side accepts a Chinese driver's license; most "international driver's licenses" sold online are worthless paper
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Check the destination's rules once before you leave.
- In plain terms: An "international driver's license" arranged online is not valid in mainland China. That document is a translation mutually recognized among parties to the 1949 Geneva Convention, and mainland China is not among the 103 parties. So whoever arranges it for you inside China, it has no legal effect. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the destination's own rules. If you have not confirmed that clearly, do not drive.
- Benefit: A so-called "international driver's license" (IDP) is a translation mutually recognized among the parties to the 1949 Convention on Road Traffic. That convention was concluded in Geneva on September (9) 19, 1949, and entered into force on March (3) 26, 1952. There are now 103 parties (countries and regions that signed). Mainland China is not on that list. So China does not issue this kind of international driver's license. Any agency that "arranges an international driver's license" inside China produces no legal effect. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the rules of the destination country or region. Some accept a Chinese license plus a notarized translation, some require you to exchange it for a local license, and some do not accept it at all.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:United Nations Treaty Collection. Convention on Road Traffic, Geneva, 19 September 1949 — status of treaties. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsV.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-1&chapter=11&clang=_en>
- Notes: If the local side does not accept the license you are driving on, the insurance may refuse to pay when something happens, and that is the real cost. This sentence is the author's experience. The policy terms and local law are what decide it. There are 2 ways to check: look up "Information for Chinese citizens" for the destination country on the China Consular Service website, or ask the rental company directly for a written confirmation. Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, and some countries have separate arrangements.

### 9. Before you use an agency for a visa, study abroad, or immigration, ask whether it has filed with the immigration authority
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask a further question about whether it has filed, and keep the written papers.
- In plain terms: From September (9) 15, 2026, an agency that handles visas, study abroad, or immigration has to file with the immigration authority. Drumming up clients with exaggerated claims, and providing or helping to provide false materials, are both expressly forbidden. If you hear "guaranteed to pass," "visa guaranteed," or "we will prepare the materials for you," be careful.
- Benefit: In 2018 the entry permit for private entry-exit agencies was cancelled (opening an agency no longer requires an approval document first). After that the number of agencies grew quickly, and there was also the problem that "the true number of agencies is unclear, some agencies handle business in violation of the rules, and the lawful rights and interests of people exiting and entering are harmed." The authorities could not even count how many there were. From September (9) 15, 2026, this kind of agency is managed by filing. An agency shall report to the immigration authority where it is located within 15 days from the day it is established. Those already operating have to complete the filing within 90 days from the day the rule takes effect. Several kinds of conduct are expressly forbidden. The first is "publishing false information, or soliciting clients by exaggerated publicity, misleading publicity, or similar means." The second is "providing or assisting in providing false materials, and assisting others in handling, in violation of the rules, visas, stay or residence documents, passports, or other exit-entry documents or procedures." The third is "leaking, selling, or illegally providing ... personal privacy or personal information." The fourth is "organizing or assisting others in cross-border illegal or criminal activity." Overseas enterprises and institutions must not provide this kind of agency service inside China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第七、八、十、十二、十三条）；United States Code, Title 8, §1182(a)(6)(C)(i) Misrepresentation. <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1182>（康奈尔大学法律信息研究所转载）
- Notes: An agency that keeps "guaranteed to pass," "visa guaranteed," or "we will prepare the materials for you" in its mouth is already doing something that is expressly forbidden. Once it is found out, the person who bears the consequences is the applicant. An unintentional mistake when you fill in the form yourself is generally a refusal this time. That is the author's experience. If false materials are used and it is found to be fraud, the consequences are much heavier. Take the United States as an example. Its immigration law says that a foreigner who applies for a visa by fraud or by a willful misrepresentation of a material fact "shall be inadmissible." Policy advice and information lookup that are not done to make money do not count as agency services.

### 10. Do not issue an invitation letter for a stranger, and do not let someone handle entry and exit procedures in your name
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is turning down a personal favor face to face.
- In plain terms: If you issue an invitation letter or application materials for someone else and the contents are not true, the person fined is the person who issued them. An individual is fined 5000 yuan to 10000 yuan, and any illegal gains are confiscated as well. An organization is fined 10000 yuan to 50000 yuan, and the person who handled it is fined another 5000 yuan to 10000 yuan. A favor, and what you get in return is paying this money yourself.
- Benefit: The person who issues an invitation letter is responsible for whether the contents are true. The original text says: "Where an individual issues a false invitation letter or other application materials for another person applying for exit or entry, or for stay or residence, the immigration authority shall impose a fine of not less than 5000 yuan and not more than 10000 yuan; where there are illegal gains, the illegal gains shall be confiscated." Where an organization issues false materials, the fine is not less than 10000 yuan and not more than 50000 yuan. At the same time, the person in charge who is directly responsible and the other persons directly responsible (the leader in charge and the person who handled it) are each fined not less than 5000 yuan and not more than 10000 yuan.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、十一条）
- Notes: This is the same kind of risk as section 8, "do not carry things for strangers": the materials are issued in your name, so the consequences land on you. If a public employee or a member of the military wants an agency to arrange a foreign nationality or an overseas residence qualification in violation of the rules, the agency must not do it, and must also report it to the supervisory organ.

### 11. Before you leave the country, send your itinerary, where you will stay, and who is traveling with you to a family member, and agree how often you will make contact
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Send a message before you leave.
- In plain terms: If someone loses contact overseas, the family can ask the embassy or consulate for help. The embassy or consulate will tell the family how to report it to the local police, and after a case is opened it will also press them to act. That depends on someone in China knowing where you are supposed to be and when you are supposed to make contact. Sending a family member the flight number, the address, the companions' phone numbers, and how often you agreed to make contact is enough.
- Benefit: When a Chinese citizen's whereabouts overseas are unknown, relatives may ask a Chinese diplomatic mission abroad for help. The original text of the regulations says: "A diplomatic mission abroad shall provide the local way to call the police and other information on how to get help." After the local police open a case, the embassy or consulate will press them to handle it in time. Whether this help can start depends on whether someone in China knows where you were supposed to be and when you were supposed to make contact.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十三条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: Writing down these 4 things is enough: the flight number, the address where you will stay, the name and phone number of the people traveling with you, and how often you agreed to make contact. If you are going somewhere with a weak signal or poor public safety, also say plainly how long with no news before they call the police. The cost of this is close to nothing, but the help procedures above can start only after a family member knows your itinerary.

### 12. Before you take medicine abroad, check the destination's rules: cold and cough medicines that contain pseudoephedrine or codeine, and sleeping pills, psychiatric drugs, and strong painkillers, should be in the original pack, with a prescription or a doctor's note, and only in the amount you will use yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Check the destination's official site once before you leave. It takes a few minutes. If you need to take a controlled drug, you also make an extra trip to the hospital for a certificate, and you apply about 2 weeks ahead.
- In plain terms: Cold and cough medicines with more than 10% pseudoephedrine or more than 1% codeine are regulated in Japan as a stimulant raw material or as a narcotic, and you have to apply for permission ahead of time. If you take more than a month's supply of sleeping pills, Japan also requires you to get a permit in advance, and Singapore requires an advance application for a medicine that contains a controlled ingredient. Carry the original pack and a doctor's note, and carry only what you will use yourself, and you can avoid having the medicine confiscated, a fine, or even a criminal sentence.
- Benefit: Japan regulates medicines by ingredient. A medicine with more than 10% pseudoephedrine counts as a "stimulant raw material." A medicine with more than 1% codeine counts as a narcotic. Both require a permit from the Narcotics Control Department of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare before entry, at least 14 days ahead. You must carry the medicine yourself. You cannot mail it, and you cannot have a family member carry it for you. For a psychiatric drug such as diazepam or triazolam, a supply of up to a month does not need an application. Ordinary medicines have limits too: a prescription medicine within a month's supply, and other medicines within a 2-month supply. Above that, you first get an "import confirmation certificate." The reminder from the Chinese embassy in Japan says, in the original wording, that illegally carrying medicines and similar items into Japan means "the violator will be fined, detained, or even sentenced, and the prohibited items will be confiscated." The rule of Singapore's Health Sciences Authority (HSA) is: a medicine that does not contain a controlled ingredient, at most a 3-month supply. Carry a copy of the prescription or of a doctor's letter. Keep the medicine in the original pack, with the pharmacy label on it. A medicine that contains a controlled ingredient needs approval applied for at least 2 weeks ahead. A supply of more than 3 months may not be brought in at all, and neither may a medicine in the form of chewing gum. Carrying medicine for someone else is limited to a direct relative's own prescription medicine. Medicine that does not comply will be confiscated, and you may also face enforcement. On the Chinese side, the Customs Law requires that articles an individual carries in or out be "limited to personal use and a reasonable quantity." Narcotic drugs and the first category of psychotropic drugs may be carried, within the maximum amount of a single prescription, on a hospital diagnosis certificate and an identity document. Customs releases them on the principle of personal use and a reasonable quantity.
- Evidence grade: A

### 13. When you enter China on the way back from abroad, do not bring meat products (vacuum-packed ham and jerky count too), cheese, fresh fruit, or seeds; if you are carrying an animal or plant product, declare it in the red channel
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. When you buy local specialties abroad, skip these kinds, and go through the luggage once before you fly back. If you are carrying something you are not sure about, take the red channel at customs and wait in line a bit longer.
- In plain terms: On the way back, meat cannot be brought in whether it is raw or cooked. Ham, jerky, and sausage are the same no matter how tightly they are packed. Cheese, butter, fresh eggs, fresh fruit and vegetables, and seeds are on the banned list too. If you bring them, they will be sent back or destroyed. If you carry an animal or plant product and do not declare it, you may also be fined, up to 5000 yuan.
- Benefit: In 2021 the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs and the General Administration of Customs published a banned list. It covers what you carry with you, what you check in, what you mail, what you send by courier, and what you buy directly by cross-border e-commerce. The kinds that matter most to an ordinary traveler are these. The first is meat. The original text is "(raw or cooked) meat (including offal) and products made from it," and the list does not make an exception by packaging. The second is milk and eggs. Cream, butter, cheese, and condensed milk are included, and so are preserved eggs, salted eggs, and mayonnaise. The third is "fresh fruit and vegetables" and fresh cut flowers. The fourth is seeds, seedlings, and other plant material that can grow. The fifth is live animals, except cats and dogs. Bird's nest is also banned, with the only exception canned bird's nest that has been treated to commercial sterility. Article 29 of the Law on the Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine states that bringing in something on the list is "handled by sending it back or destroying it." An animal or plant product that is not on the list may be brought in, but it still has to be declared. The customs measures on supervision of luggage and articles state that a person carrying animals, plants, or animal or plant products shall declare them to customs in writing. If the written declaration is still not finished when customs starts the inspection, it counts as not declared. The measures also say, in the original wording: "A person who does not know the customs rules, or does not know which channel to choose, shall choose the 'red channel.'" The penalty for not declaring is in Article 39 of the quarantine law: a person who "failed to apply for inspection" is fined. Article 59 of the implementing regulations of the quarantine law sets this kind of fine at 5000 yuan or less.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:农业农村部、海关总署 (2021). 农业农村部 海关总署公告第 470 号（中华人民共和国禁止携带、寄递进境的动植物及其产品和其他检疫物名录）. <https://www.moa.gov.cn/nybgb/2022/202202/202203/t20220324_6393815.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2009 年修正). 进出境动植物检疫法（第二十九、三十、三十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf6ab3604e7>；国务院 (1996). 进出境动植物检疫法实施条例（国务院令第 206 号，第四十一、五十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f3cbb3c016f4086afae03c1>；海关总署 (2025). 中华人民共和国海关进出境行李物品监管办法（海关总署令第 276 号，第九、十一、十二、二十五、二十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7005697.htm>；海关总署 (2025). 关于进出境人员行李物品申报有关事宜的公告（海关总署公告 2025 年第 43 号，第一、三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017195.htm>
- Notes: The list was checked against the announcement on the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs website on October (10) 14, 2026. Both departments say they will "adjust it dynamically." Check again before you leave. There are a few exceptions. Dried or cooked aquatic products are not on the banned list, for example dried squid and dried shrimp, but they still have to be declared. Something that has been approved, and that also has an official quarantine certificate from the exporting country, is not limited by the list. Each person may bring 1 cat or dog, and must have an official quarantine certificate and a vaccination certificate from the exporting country.
