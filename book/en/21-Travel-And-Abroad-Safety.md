# 21. Going Abroad, Travel, and Safety Overseas

This section deals with money and personal freedom; a few entries touch on personal safety. The rules abroad are set by the host country, and what Chinese missions abroad can do has clear limits. This section first makes clear where that boundary lies.
### 1. Check the foreign ministry's safety warnings once before departure, and do not go anywhere marked "do not travel"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; open the Chinese Consular Service Network and go through it, a few minutes
- In plain terms: The foreign ministry grades security risks abroad into four levels, and the highest one is "do not travel." From September 15, 2026, avoiding high-risk countries and regions is written into administrative regulations; you will also be reminded when applying for documents and at exit border checks, and people heading to the highest-risk areas will be dissuaded where necessary. Going through that page before departure takes only a few minutes.
- Benefit: The foreign ministry publishes safety warnings for other countries by level, which from low to high are roughly "exercise caution," "strengthen safety precautions," "travel with caution," and "do not travel." New rules taking effect September 15, 2026 write the citizen-side duty into administrative regulations: "Chinese citizens shall pay attention to safety warnings for foreign countries and risk alerts for tourist destinations, and avoid traveling to and staying in high-risk countries or regions"; they also require the immigration authorities (the bodies that issue exit-entry documents and run border checks) to remind people heading to high-risk areas when they apply for documents and at exit border checks, and "where citizens are preparing to travel to countries or regions at the highest risk level, or where incidents seriously endangering personal safety occur suddenly and frequently, Chinese citizens shall be dissuaded from traveling there when necessary"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第二条）；国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十九、二十一条）；外交部领事司. 安全提醒. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/>
- Notes: The warnings change with the situation; this book does not copy the list — check that page yourself once before travel. As of September 7, 2026, the "do not travel"-type warnings still listed in that section include Eswatini (2026-08-25), Palestine (2023-10-10), Sudan (2023-04-17), Afghanistan (2023-02-17), Syria (2023-01-04), Peru (2022-12-29), and Somalia (2022-11-24), plus dedicated warnings for northern Myanmar and certain provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Travel agencies also have a duty to inform; before joining a group tour you can ask them to state the destination's risks in writing

### 2. Save 12308 and the local embassy's consular protection number into your phone before anything happens
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; save two numbers
- In plain terms: There is one helpline for when things go wrong — dial 12308 inside China, and +86-10-12308 from abroad. Since the hotline opened it has taken more than 3 million calls, and over the same period the foreign ministry and missions abroad handled more than 500,000 consular protection cases. A number that lives only in your phone is as good as not saved: lose the phone or let the battery die and you lose both.
- Benefit: The foreign ministry's global 12308 hotline for consular protection and services runs around the clock — dial 12308 inside China, or +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308 from abroad. Since it opened in 2014 the hotline has taken more than 3 million calls in total, and over the same period the foreign ministry and missions abroad handled more than 500,000 consular protection cases of various kinds
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 外交部、中国驻缅甸使领馆 (2023). 关于当前缅北地区安全局势的领事提醒. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>；国务院新闻办 (2023). 国务院政策例行吹风会介绍领事保护与协助有关情况. <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2023zccfh/11/index.htm>
- Notes: The embassy or consulate in your destination also has its own consular protection number, usually faster than the main line; look it up by country on the Consular Service Network before departure and save it together with the main number. If the phone is lost or dead these numbers are useless, so copy a set by hand onto a slip for your wallet, or write the numbers down for your family

### 3. Know what consular protection can and cannot do: it can visit you, it cannot get you out, and the costs are still yours
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; spend a few minutes reading this boundary until it is clear
- In plain terms: If you are detained, what the embassy or consulate can do is verify the situation with the local authorities, demand you be handled fairly under the law, visit you, and sit in on hearings — it cannot get your sentence changed or take you home. Food, lodging, transport, and medical costs that someone fronted on your behalf are still yours to repay in the end. So sort out your money and insurance before going abroad, and do not treat "the state will bail me out" as a plan.
- Benefit: If you are detained, what the embassy or consulate can do is: verify the situation with the relevant authorities of the host country, demand you be handled fairly under the law, and "in accordance with the laws of the host country and international treaties concluded or jointly acceded to by China and the host country, visit you or contact you, learn your needs, and demand that the relevant authorities of the host country grant this Chinese citizen humanitarian treatment and fair treatment" — in plain words, under local law and the treaties the two countries have signed, they can visit you, contact you, ask what you need, and demand that the local side treat you humanely and fairly. At trial it can attend hearings and demand your litigation rights be protected. If you are injured or caught in a disaster, it can urge the local side to provide emergency assistance and help your family with aftermath arrangements. But the cost side is written just as clearly: "Where a Chinese citizen, legal person, or unincorporated organization receives goods and services such as food, lodging, transport, or medical care provided by a third party in the course of consular protection and assistance, that citizen or organization shall pay the expenses it ought to bear itself" — food, lodging, transport, and medical care that someone fronted for you are still yours to pay
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第九、十、十四、二十六条）
- Notes: The embassy or consulate will not pay your fines, medical bills, or airfare, and cannot change the judicial outcome of the host country. What it can provide is information on local lawyers, interpreters, medical services, and funeral providers, plus help contacting relatives and friends and accessing relief. The practical value of understanding this boundary is: prepare your money and insurance yourself before going abroad, and do not treat "the state will bail me out" as a plan

### 4. Buy a policy that covers overseas medical care and medical evacuation, not just flight delay cover
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: tens to a few hundred yuan for short-term overseas travel insurance
- In plain terms: Medical bills abroad come out of your own pocket — domestic health insurance basically does not reimburse them, and the embassy or consulate will not front the money either. What really breaks a family is not the clinic fee but the hospital stay after a serious injury and getting the person back home — that takes a chartered flight or medical escort. When buying travel insurance, look mainly at two limits: overseas medical expenses, and emergency medical evacuation and repatriation.
- Benefit: Medical costs abroad are your own to pay; domestic health insurance largely does not reimburse care received abroad, and consular protection does not front the money either (see the previous entry on what consular protection can and cannot do; the basis is Article 26 of the regulations). What can genuinely drag a family down is not the outpatient bill but the hospital stay after a serious injury and the medical evacuation — moving a person from a hospital abroad back home requires a chartered aircraft or medical escort, among the most expensive of self-paid items. When buying insurance, check two limits first: the overseas medical expenses limit, and the emergency medical evacuation and repatriation limit — that is, the most it will pay toward moving you to another hospital after something happens and getting you back home
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第二十六条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: TODO (to verify: typical cost ranges for overseas medical care and medical evacuation; needs figures on an official or industry statistical basis). Before buying, confirm three things: whether the destination is inside the covered area, whether high-risk activities (diving, skiing, motorcycles) are excluded, and whether claims are paid upfront or reimbursed after you pay first. Visa-free entry is not exemption from liability — you need cover wherever you go

### 5. Treat any "high-paying job overseas" offer as a scam — and if you are tricked into scam work abroad, you can be barred from leaving China when you return
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; turn down one job that looks too good
- In plain terms: A job with an absurdly high monthly salary, no questions about education or experience, no proper contract, asking you to gather first in a border city, and holding your passport for you — go there and both you and your passport end up in someone else's hands. Falsified documents get you stopped at the border and refused exit; if you took part in illegal or criminal activity abroad, you can also be barred from leaving China for 6 months to 3 years after you return.
- Benefit: The official characterization of these cases is blunt: in recent years people have been "tricked into going abroad or leaving the country illegally under false pretenses to take part in cross-border gambling, telecom and online fraud, and other unlawful activities, seriously endangering people's lives and property" — that is, tricked into going out there, or slipping out under a made-up excuse, to work in gambling and telecom fraud. The new rules tighten both ends of this. On exit: "the stated grounds for applying to exit or enter, stay, or reside must be true and lawful"; where false documents are provided or false statements made, the immigration authorities have the power to refuse to issue documents or refuse exit. After return: "where a Chinese citizen engages in unlawful or criminal activity abroad that endangers national security or interests, the authorities may... decide to bar that person from exiting for 6 months to 3 years from the date of return"; the same 6-month-to-3-year exit bar applies to anyone administratively detained for fraudulently obtaining documents or illegally exiting or entering
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、四条）；司法部、公安部、国家移民局 (2026). 就《国务院关于出境入境管理的规定》答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7077270.htm>
- Notes: The tell-tale signs are largely fixed: a monthly salary far above the same role anywhere else, no education or experience required, no proper contract, an instruction to gather first in a border city, visa handling done for you plus custody of your passport. There is already a dedicated consular warning for northern Myanmar. If you actually go, neither your freedom of movement nor your passport stays in your own hands — "I can leave whenever I want" does not hold

### 6. Cash withdrawals abroad are capped at 100,000 yuan a year, counted across every card in your own name
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Before departure estimate how much cash this trip needs and put the rest on the card. The hard part is breaking the habit — if you can pay by card, do not withdraw cash.
- In plain terms: The quota for cash withdrawals abroad is not one per card; it is counted across all the bank cards in your own name (supplementary cards included), 100,000 yuan per calendar year. Once it is used up, you cannot withdraw cash abroad for the rest of the year or the next. Only withdrawals stop; card payments are unaffected, and none of this touches your annual 50,000 USD quota for buying foreign exchange.
- Benefit: The basis is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's Notice on Regulating Large Cash Withdrawals Abroad with Bank Cards (Hui Fa 〔2017〕 No. 29, in force from January 1, 2018). Article 1: "When an individual withdraws cash abroad with a domestic bank card, the total across all bank cards in that person's own name (supplementary cards included) must not exceed the equivalent of 100,000 yuan per calendar year. Anyone who exceeds the annual quota will be suspended from withdrawing cash abroad with domestic bank cards for the current year and the following year." Paragraph 2 of the same article: "An individual must not circumvent, or help circumvent, the administration of cash withdrawals abroad by borrowing another person's bank card or lending out their own." Article 2: SAFE sends the suspension list to card-issuing banks every day, and no later than 17:00 Beijing time that day the bank must suspend overseas cash withdrawals with its cards by anyone on the list. Article 3: a person on the suspension list may take a valid ID to a branch of SAFE and check the details of their own overseas withdrawals. Article 4: the per-card daily limit for foreign-currency cards is lowered from the equivalent of 1,000 US dollars to the equivalent of 10,000 yuan; RMB cards keep the per-card daily cap of the equivalent of 10,000 yuan. Article 6: card-issuing banks, domestic RMB card clearing organizations, and individuals that violate the notice are penalized under the relevant provisions of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations of the People's Republic of China. SAFE's press Q&A also clarified several points. The notice does not affect paying by card abroad. Frequent-item transactions involved in tourism, business, or study abroad — food, lodging, transport, shopping — can all be paid by bank card, and these payments do not use up the individual annual quota of 50,000 US dollars for buying foreign exchange. The annual quota is computed by SAFE from data summed across the issuing institutions; because real-time control would slow transaction responses, "SAFE uses delayed control," meaning it does not block the transaction on the spot. By another statistic, 81% of domestic bank card cash withdrawals abroad in 2016 were below 30,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家外汇管理局 (2017). 关于规范银行卡境外大额提取现金交易的通知（汇发〔2017〕29 号，第一、二、三、四、六、七条，2018 年 1 月 1 日起实施）. <https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2017/1230/21873.html>；国家外汇管理局 (2017). 外汇局有关负责人就规范银行卡境外大额提取现金交易有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2017-12/31/content_5251958.htm>；中国银联国际. 境外ATM取款. <https://m.unionpayintl.com/wap/cn/serviceCenter/cardUsingInstructions/805.shtml>
- Notes: The quota runs on the calendar year and starts accumulating again on January 1. Only overseas cash withdrawals stop; card payments are unaffected. If you genuinely need a large amount of cash abroad, follow the Individual Foreign Exchange Administration Measures: buy foreign exchange first, then take the foreign currency banknotes out of the country. Withdrawing cash has two more traps, written out on UnionPay International's "Overseas ATM Withdrawals" page. One: on some ATMs in some countries, a dual-network card cannot select the UnionPay channel for withdrawal. Two: some ATMs abroad flash an extra fee (a surcharge) on the screen; that money is collected by the local acquirer and "has nothing to do with UnionPay." The same page also warns "since credit card withdrawals incur overdraft interest, cardholders are advised to use a UnionPay debit card for withdrawals abroad." What to do if the card is lost, swallowed by the machine, or skimmed: see unit 5 of section 14 (report the loss and freeze the card first, then call the police)

### 7. Photograph your passport, visa, and ID card and store copies in the cloud; if they are lost, report to the police first, then apply for a travel document
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; take a few photos before departure
- In plain terms: If your passport is lost, the embassy or consulate can issue you a travel document to get home — but only if you can prove who you are. With photos of the data page and visa pages plus your entry-exit records, the replacement goes far faster. You also have to report to the local police first and get a report receipt; exit checks and insurance claims both accept that piece of paper. The phone usually gets lost with the passport, so do not keep the photos on the phone alone.
- Benefit: When a passport is lost, the embassy or consulate abroad can issue a travel document for the trip home, but you need to prove your identity; with clear photos of the passport data page and visa pages plus entry-exit records, the replacement is vastly faster. You must also report to the local police first and obtain a report receipt — in many countries both exit checks and insurance claims require this document. The way to reach the embassy or consulate is the two consular protection numbers you saved above
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十六条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: How to store: after photographing, put the images in two different places (cloud drive plus a copy sent to one family member), and also print one paper copy kept separately from the originals. Photograph bank cards and prescriptions for regular medications as well. The phone and the passport are usually lost together — stored only on the phone is as good as not stored

### 8. Before driving abroad, confirm whether the destination accepts a Chinese driver's license — most "international driving permits" sold online are worthless paper
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; check the destination's rules once before departure
- In plain terms: The "international driving permit" sold through agents does not exist for mainland China: that document is a translation recognized between parties to the 1949 Geneva convention, and mainland China is not among the 103 contracting parties, so no version issued at home has any legal force. Whether you can drive on a Chinese license depends on the destination's own rules; do not drive until you have confirmed it.
- Benefit: The so-called "international driving permit" (IDP) is a translation recognized between states party to the 1949 Road Traffic Convention. That convention was concluded at Geneva on September 19, 1949 and entered into force on March 26, 1952; it currently has 103 contracting parties, meaning the countries and regions that signed it, and mainland China is not on the list. China therefore does not issue such a permit, and any agency offering to "arrange an international driving permit" domestically produces no legal effect; whether a Chinese license can be used to drive depends on the rules of the destination country or region: some accept a Chinese license plus a notarized translation, some require exchanging for a local license, and some do not accept it at all
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: United Nations Treaty Collection. Convention on Road Traffic, Geneva, 19 September 1949 — status of treaties. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsV.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-1&chapter=11&clang=_en>
- Notes: Driving without a valid license is a criminal or heavily penalized offense in most countries, and after an accident the insurer will most likely refuse to pay — that is the real cost. How to check: find the destination country's "Information for Chinese Citizens" page on the Chinese Consular Service Network, or ask the rental car company directly for written confirmation. Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, along with some countries, have separate arrangements

### 9. Before using an agent for visas, study abroad, or immigration, ask whether it is registered with the immigration administration
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; ask one extra question about registration and keep the written materials
- In plain terms: From September 15, 2026, agencies handling visas, study abroad, and immigration must register with the immigration administration. Phrases like "guaranteed approval," "guaranteed visa," or "we'll prepare the materials for you" are themselves on the explicitly banned list. If you fill in the forms yourself and make a mistake, the worst case is a visa refusal; use fake materials and it counts as fraud, and the stain stays on your own future entry-exit record.
- Benefit: The market-entry licensing for private exit-entry intermediary service agencies was abolished in 2018 — meaning such agencies no longer needed approval before opening. After that the number of agencies grew rapidly, and problems appeared such as "an unclear count of agencies, some agencies handling business in violation of laws and regulations, and harm to the lawful rights and interests of people exiting and entering"; officially, not even the number of agencies could be counted. From September 15, 2026, such intermediaries are placed under registration management: an agency shall register with the immigration administration at its location within 15 days of being established, and agencies already operating must complete registration within 90 days of the rules taking effect. Explicitly banned practices include "publishing false information, or soliciting clients through exaggerated or misleading publicity"; "providing or helping to provide false materials, or helping others handle visas, stay or residence documents, passports, or other exit-entry documents or formalities in violation of regulations"; "divulging, selling, or unlawfully providing... personal privacy or personal information"; and "organizing or helping others engage in cross-border unlawful or criminal activities." Foreign enterprises and organizations may not provide such intermediary services within China
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第七、八、十、十二、十三条）
- Notes: An agency that keeps saying "guaranteed approval," "guaranteed visa," or "we'll prepare the materials for you" is doing what is already explicitly banned. Once it is exposed, what gets stained is your own visa record. When you fill in the application yourself, a mistake only gets you refused this time; use fake materials and you can be found to have committed fraud, with far heavier consequences. Policy consultation and information lookups not conducted for profit do not count as intermediary services

### 10. Do not issue invitation letters for strangers, and do not let anyone handle exit-entry formalities in your name
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; the hard part is declining a favor face to face.
- In plain terms: If you issue an invitation letter or application materials for someone and the contents are untrue, the penalty falls on the issuer: 5,000 to 10,000 yuan for an individual, with any illicit gains confiscated; 10,000 to 50,000 yuan for an organization, plus 5,000 to 10,000 yuan for the individuals who handled it. What you trade for one favor is paying that money yourself.
- Benefit: Whoever issues an invitation letter is responsible for the truthfulness of its contents: "where an individual issues a false invitation letter or other application materials for another person's application to exit or enter, or to stay or reside, the immigration administration shall impose a fine of 5,000 yuan to 10,000 yuan; any illicit gains shall be confiscated"; for an organization the fine is 10,000 yuan to 50,000 yuan, and the directly responsible person in charge and other directly responsible personnel — the people who run this and the people who handle it — are each fined a further 5,000 yuan to 10,000 yuan
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 关于出境入境管理的规定（国令第 841 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>（第三、十一条）
- Notes: This is the same class of risk as "do not carry things for strangers" in section 8: the signature is yours, the stamp is yours, the guarantee is yours — the consequences land on you. Where public officials or military personnel entrust an intermediary to handle foreign nationality or overseas residence status in violation of regulations, the intermediary must not only refuse to do it but also report it to the supervisory authorities

### 11. Send your itinerary, accommodation, and travel companions to one family member before departure, and agree on how often to check in
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; send one message before departure.
- In plain terms: If you go missing abroad, your family can turn to the mission abroad for help; it will give them the local way to report to police and other channels for assistance, and once local police open a case the embassy or consulate follows up and presses for action. Whether that chain can start depends on whether someone back home knows where you are supposed to be and when you are supposed to make contact. Flight numbers, the address, your companions' phone numbers, and an agreed contact frequency — send it all to one person and that is enough.
- Benefit: When a Chinese citizen goes missing abroad, relatives can seek help from the diplomatic mission there; "the mission abroad shall provide the local means of reporting to police and other information on obtaining assistance," and after local police open a case the embassy or consulate will urge them to handle it promptly. Whether this chain can be set in motion depends on whether someone at home knows where you were supposed to be and when you were supposed to make contact
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2023). 领事保护与协助条例（国令第 763 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>（第十三条）；作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: Four things written down are enough: flight numbers, the accommodation address, companions' names and phone numbers, and the agreed contact frequency. If you are going somewhere with poor signal or poor public security, also make explicit "call the police if there is no news for X long." The cost of this is nearly zero, but it is the precondition for every assistance procedure above to be able to start
