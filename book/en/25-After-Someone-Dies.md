# 25. What to do after someone dies

Scope: money. This section only covers how to get the procedures done and how to collect the money. That includes what to do first right after the person dies, and which fees have a list you can check. It also covers which money sitting in which accounts can be taken back, and which matters close relatives have the right to handle in person. The first 5 items follow the order in which things happen: the scene, the death certificate, the body and cremation, what to do if you disagree with the cause of death, and canceling the hukou (户口 — household registration). Arrangements an older person should make while still alive, such as intended guardianship (意定监护 — a guardian named by agreement in advance), a will, and accounts, are in Section 17. The 3 benefits for a work-related death are in Section 19.

The funeral part follows the new Funeral Management Regulations, in force from March (3) 30, 2026. How the funeral allowance and the survivor allowance under the pension are calculated is in item 9 (housing provident fund balance and social insurance benefits).
### 1. If someone passes away at home or outdoors, distinguish between natural and unnatural death first: in accidents, unknown causes, or unattended deaths, call the police immediately and leave the body and scene untouched
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. It only takes a single phone call.
- In plain terms: When someone dies from an accident or an unknown cause without hospital care, the death certificate must be issued by the police. If the scene has been disturbed, what happened becomes impossible to clarify, making the certificate difficult to obtain. Without this piece of paper, everything downstream—cremation, household registration (*hukou*) cancellation, and insurance claims—grinds to a halt. If someone living alone is found long after passing away and you cannot tell how they died, call the police (110) first, and wait for officers to finish their investigation before getting the certificate issued.
- Benefit: Article 8, Paragraph 2 of the Regulations on Hukou Registration: "If a citizen dies of an accidental injury or of an unknown cause, the head of the household or the finder shall immediately report to the local public security police station or the township/town people's committee." This means that if an accident occurs or the cause of death is unknown, the head of the household and the person who discovers the body must immediately notify the police station or township government. Article 23 of the Regulations on Funeral and Interment Management: "Where a natural person dies a natural death, medical and healthcare institutions shall issue a death certificate in a timely manner. For deceased individuals involved in unnatural death cases or incidents handled by public security organs according to law and who have not received treatment from medical and healthcare institutions, the public security organs shall issue the death certificate." This means that for deaths from natural illness, the medical facility that treated the patient issues the certificate. For deaths without hospital treatment that fall under police jurisdiction, the police issue it. If the scene has been disturbed, the facts cannot be established and the certificate cannot easily be issued. Cremation, hukou cancellation, and insurance claims will all be blocked by this single document.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (1958). 中华人民共和国户口登记条例（第八条）. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html>（广东省人民政府门户网站刊登）；国务院 (2026). 殡葬管理条例（国务院令第 824 号，自 2026 年 3 月 30 日起施行，第二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>
- Notes: Passing away at home after a prolonged illness counts as a natural death. You can obtain the certificate from the medical institution that treated the person, or from the local community health service center or township health center. The principle that whoever provided medical care issues the certificate is covered in Item 2 (Death Certificate). If you are unsure which category applies, treat it as an unnatural death and call 110 first; the police will still issue the certificate once their on-site investigation is complete. If you clean up the scene before calling the police, no one will be able to help you. Do not move the body, wash or change their clothes, or remove medicine bottles or notes.

### 2. The death certificate is the key to everything that follows: whoever provided treatment issues it; for natural deaths at home, contact the local community health service center. It must be issued within one day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No cost at all.  
- In plain terms: If the person died in a hospital, get the death certificate from the place that handled the treatment. For a natural death at home, get it from the community health service for that area, or from the township health center. When the death is natural and the cause is clear, it is issued within one day. Canceling the hukou (户口 — household registration), cremation, withdrawing the housing provident fund, collecting social insurance benefits, an insurance claim, and handling the inheritance all use it. As soon as you have it, make several copies.  
- Benefit: According to the “Interim Regulations on Registration and Management of Population Death Information,” death certificates for patients who die in medical institutions are issued by those institutions; for deaths occurring at home or elsewhere, they are issued by the local community health service center or township health clinic. Cases deemed non‑natural by police or judicial authorities are handled under separate procedures. In 2026, five government agencies confirmed that electronic death certificates carry the same legal weight as paper versions; both are equally valid. Certificates for natural deaths with known causes must be issued within one day. If lost, a single replacement can be requested from the original issuer. Errors in basic information may be corrected by submitting supporting documents.  
- Evidence grade: A  
- Sources:国家卫生计生委办公厅 (2014). 人口死亡信息登记管理规范（试行）（国卫办规划发〔2014〕68 号，第九条）. <https://mzj.sz.gov.cn/szmz/pc/zwgk/jcxxgk/zcfg/byfw/content/post_2952215.html>（深圳市民政局转发）；国家卫生健康委、公安部、民政部、国家中医药局、国家疾控局 (2026). 关于加强居民死亡医学证明信息登记和电子证照管理工作的通知（国卫规划发〔2026〕5 号）. <https://wjw.fujian.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gjwj/202602/t20260228_7102810.htm>（福建省卫生健康委员会转发）
- Notes: This certificate is indispensable for deregistration, cremation, housing provident fund withdrawals, social insurance benefits, insurance claims, and estate settlement — so make plenty of copies right away. If electronic issuance is available, request it as well. Only one replacement is permitted, so keep the original safe. Any mistakes in name or ID number should be corrected on the spot; otherwise you’ll have to repeat the process at every office later on.

### 3. Transport, storage, and cremation of remains must be handled by funeral homes; hospital morgues cannot provide such services, and storage is generally limited to 3 days
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost involved.
- In plain terms: Transport, storage, embalming, cosmetic preparation, and cremation can only be done by a funeral home. A hospital morgue does not handle the funeral. At most, it lets the body stay there for a short while. A funeral home generally keeps the body for no more than 3 days. If you need more days, you have to apply for an extension. Cremation takes a death certificate and a cremation confirmation document. The cremation certificate they give you afterward is still needed for the inheritance and for an insurance claim.
- Benefit: Article 25 of the Funeral Management Regulations states: “Services such as transport, storage, embalming, body preparation, and cremation must be provided exclusively by funeral homes.” No other entities are permitted to offer these services. Article 24 adds: “Morgues operated by medical institutions may not subcontract or provide funeral services; they may only offer temporary storage for remains.” Hospital morgues are thus restricted to short-term storage only. Article 27 specifies: “Funeral homes may store remains for no more than 3 days; extensions must be formally requested by the family or body transferor.” Article 28 requires cremation to be performed only upon presentation of a death certificate and cremation authorization document, after which a cremation certificate is issued. Article 29 further mandates that in regions where cremation is mandatory, remains must be cremated at a nearby funeral home.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 殡葬管理条例（国务院令第 824 号，自 2026 年 3 月 30 日起施行，第八、九、二十四、二十五、二十七、二十八、二十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>
- Notes: Most “one-stop” services offered at hospitals are not affiliated with licensed funeral homes. Transport itself is a standard service with fixed pricing; see Section 6 for details. Keep the cremation certificate safe — it is required for inheritance, insurance, and other legal procedures. Whether cremation or burial is mandated locally is determined by provincial authorities. Article 9 safeguards the funeral customs of ethnic minorities. Any instance of cremation being required yet burial performed, or burial outside designated cemeteries or ecological burial zones, will prompt civil affairs bureaus to order immediate correction.

### 4. Disagreement over cause of death: request an autopsy within 48 hours and have medical records sealed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost involved. The hard part is remembering to make this request at such a time.
- In plain terms: If you doubt the cause of death, the autopsy has to be done within 48 hours after the person dies. If the hospital can freeze the body, that can be extended to 7 days. The autopsy needs a close relative's signed consent. If they do not consent, or the time is missed, the responsibility for a cause of death that cannot be sorted out falls on that side. When you ask for the autopsy, also ask to have the medical records sealed. Once the body is cremated, an autopsy can no longer be done.
- Benefit: The time limit for autopsies is stipulated in Article 26 of the Regulations on Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes. The original text reads: “In cases where a patient dies and both the patient and medical institution dispute the cause of death, an autopsy must be performed within 48 hours after death; if the hospital has facilities to freeze the body, this period may be extended to 7 days.” In other words, to determine the true cause of death, an autopsy must be carried out within 48 hours of death, or up to 7 days if the hospital has freezing capabilities. The same article further states: “An autopsy must be approved and signed off by the deceased’s close relatives; failure to sign is regarded as refusal of consent.” If family members do not sign, it is interpreted as a refusal. It also specifies: “If consent is denied or the deadline is missed, leading to uncertainty over the cause of death, the party responsible for the refusal or delay bears all related liability.” Article 24 states that sealing and unsealing of medical records “must be conducted in the presence of both the patient and medical institution.” Both you and the hospital must be present during both the sealing and later unsealing of records.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2018). 医疗纠纷预防和处理条例（国务院令第 701 号，第二十四、二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm>
- Notes: After this deadline passes, any later claims of “I always suspected something was wrong” will instead place liability on the party responsible for the delay. Once a body is cremated, no autopsy can ever be performed. Therefore, this decision must be made before contacting any funeral home. Details on how to seal medical records can be found in Section 24.

### 5. Legal deadlines for canceling hukou registration for the deceased: in cities, before burial; in rural areas, within one month
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No expense involved. Just bring the death certificate and household register to your local police station.
- In plain terms: In cities, you must cancel hukou registration before burial; in rural areas, you must do it within one month. Bring the death certificate and household register to the police station to complete the process. Failing to do so will block any inheritance transfers, benefit claims, or insurance cancellations due to the unresolved hukou status.
- Benefit: Article 8, Paragraph 1 of the Hukou Registration Regulations states: “When a citizen dies, in cities the household head, relatives, guardians, or neighbors must report the death to the hukou registration authority and cancel the hukou registration before burial; in rural areas, this must be done within one month. If a citizen dies while temporarily residing elsewhere, the local hukou registration authority must notify the permanent residence’s authority to cancel the registration.” In short, urban residents must act before burial, while rural residents have one month to do so. Any household head, relative, guardian, or neighbor may initiate the process at the police station, which serves as the hukou registration authority. If the deceased was living temporarily elsewhere, the local police station there must inform the permanent residence’s station to cancel the hukou registration.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (1958). 中华人民共和国户口登记条例（第八条）. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html>（广东省人民政府门户网站刊登）
- Notes: Leaving the hukou registration active will prevent any inheritance transfers, benefit claims, or insurance cancellations, as all these procedures rely on a cleared hukou status. If pension payments continue despite the death, they constitute fraudulent collection and must eventually be repaid. While at the police station, also ask how to suspend social insurance and yibao payments, as well as how to apply for funeral subsidies. Details on these financial matters are covered in Item 9 (housing provident fund balances and related social insurance benefits).

### 6. Distinguish between basic and non-basic funeral services: basic items follow an official list with government-regulated pricing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just take a few minutes before heading out to check the list.
- In plain terms: Body transport, storage, farewell ceremonies, cremation, ash storage, and eco-burials are all on the national basic services list. Columbarium niche burials provided by public institutions are included as well. Prices for these services are set by regulation, and other services must also be listed clearly, with no unapproved extra charges allowed. Before you go, ask which items count as basic services and what the local rate is before discussing anything else.
- Benefit: Body transport, body storage, farewell ceremonies, cremation, ash storage, and eco-burials are all on the national basic services list. Columbarium niche burials provided by government-run funeral service organizations are also included. Fee schedules for these items are established by law, and prices are determined by regulation rather than set arbitrarily by the funeral home. The catalogue of non-basic services is determined by the civil affairs bureau at or above the level of cities divided into districts (prefecture-level cities and above).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 殡葬管理条例（国务院令第 824 号，2025 年 11 月 14 日国务院第 72 次常务会议修订通过，「自2026年3月30日起施行」）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> 第五条：「殡葬服务分为基础项目和非基础项目，收费政策由国务院发展改革部门、财政部门会同国务院民政等部门制定。」「国家制定殡葬服务基础项目清单，将遗体接运、遗体存放、遗体告别、遗体火化、骨灰寄存、生态安葬以及政府举办的殡葬服务机构提供的骨灰格位安葬等纳入清单范围」；第五十条「殡葬服务实行清单化管理并动态调整，禁止在清单之外设立项目、收取费用。」
- Notes: Provinces, autonomous regions, and directly administered municipalities may add items to the national catalogue. Clarify first which items count as basic services and what the local regulated fees are before negotiating anything else. Basic service rates follow set rules; services outside the basic tier are elective, but should still be verified against the official list and price schedule. Distinguishing between the two gives you ground to challenge any overcharges. There is nothing wrong with wanting a dignified farewell for a loved one, and this is not about urging you to cut corners—how you spend your money is entirely up to you.

### 7. If you encounter forced bundling, extra charges beyond the listed price, or a single service being split into multiple line items for billing, make a note of it and file a complaint
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost involved. The real challenge is staying diligent in the moment.

- In plain terms: The price violations the regulations name are: not posting a price list, adding money on top of the marked price, bundling funeral goods and forcing you to buy them, and forcing you to accept another provider's paid service. Splitting one item into several charges also counts as a disguised price rise. On the spot, ask for the price list first, and keep the proof of the charges and the chat records. Complaining afterward is still in time. For the price, go to the market supervision administration. For funeral services, go to the civil affairs bureau.

- Benefit: Regulations identify five categories of price violations. Four of them are: not displaying prices clearly or adding extra charges beyond the listed price — that is, no posted price list or charging more than advertised; bundling services or imposing unreasonable conditions to force a purchase — meaning you can’t obtain a service unless you buy another one; forcing or indirectly forcing you to use paid third‑party services — essentially pushing another provider’s services on you; and indirectly raising prices by re‑labeling charges — such as charging more under a different name. Additionally, it is prohibited to “split services into multiple items or expand the scope of charges to indirectly raise prices.” This means breaking one service into several line items for billing or extending the range of charges beyond what was originally stated.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 殡葬管理条例（国务院令第 824 号，2025 年 11 月 14 日国务院第 72 次常务会议修订通过，「自2026年3月30日起施行」）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> 第四十二条：「殡葬服务机构不得违反有关规定擅自设立收费项目，或者采取分解项目、扩大范围收费等形式变相提高收费标准。」第四十三条列举的价格违法行为含「（一）不按规定明码标价，或者在标价之外加价或者收取未标明的费用」「（三）采取捆绑、附加不合理条件或者不合理限制等方式，强制或者变相强制销售丧葬用品、提供殡葬相关服务并收取费用」「（四）强制或者变相强制丧属接受第三方的有偿服务，或者利用相关服务网点重复收取费用」；第五十一条要求民政部门与政务服务便民热线建立信息共享监督机制，「对丧属反映的殡葬服务问题，应当会同有关部门及时予以处理并将处理结果告知丧属」

- Notes: Price‑violation investigations fall under the market regulation authority; funeral services themselves are under the civil affairs bureau. Ask for a price list right away, and be sure to keep all receipts, invoices, and chat records. Filing a complaint later is still possible.

### 8. Funeral agents, buyers of funeral goods, and planners and hosts must register with the county-level civil affairs bureau
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Ask whether they are registered.
- In plain terms: Funeral agents, buyers of funeral goods, planners and hosts, and information consultants have to register with the county-level civil affairs bureau. Those that are not registered are not in the funeral-service information system run by the civil affairs bureau. Whether you can look this up yourself differs from place to place. The surest way is to call the county-level civil affairs bureau and ask. Those who overcharge by trickery, or who resell information about the deceased, will be dealt with according to law.
- Benefit: An intermediary that is not registered is not in the funeral-service information system run by the civil affairs bureau. Those who charge in violation of the rules by deception, push a family into a large showy funeral, force people to accept a service, or resell information about the deceased will be dealt with according to law. They are also marked in the system and made public, and anyone can see it.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 殡葬管理条例（国务院令第 824 号，2025 年 11 月 14 日国务院第 72 次常务会议修订通过，「自2026年3月30日起施行」）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm> 第四十一条：「除殡葬服务机构外，从事殡葬服务代理、用品代购、策划主持、信息咨询等殡葬相关服务活动的组织和个人，应当向县级人民政府民政部门备案。」「对存在以欺骗等手段违规收费、诱导大操大办、强迫接受服务、倒卖逝者信息等损害丧属权益行为的依法处置，并在殡葬服务信息系统中进行标注，向社会公开。」
- Notes: Online tomb-sweeping platforms are regulated the same way. Article 45 of the regulations does not allow them to induce spending, and does not allow them to handle the deceased's information unlawfully.

### 9. Claim funds scattered across accounts: housing provident fund balances, social insurance, and work injury benefits
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free of charge, but requires visiting multiple service counters. Prepare the death certificate and proof of kinship before going.
- In plain terms: Heirs can withdraw both the housing provident fund balance and the remaining money in the personal pension account; housing provident funds are not in bank accounts and are easy to overlook. When someone covered by enterprise employee pension insurance dies of illness or non-work-related causes, family members can also receive a funeral subsidy and survivor relief payment. The survivor relief payment is paid for 3 to 24 months, with at least 9 months for those already retired. If death was work-related, there are three separate work injury payouts.
- Benefit: Heirs and legatees can withdraw the remaining balance of the housing provident fund account. A legatee refers to a person designated in a will to receive property. For work-related deaths, there are three separate payouts: a funeral subsidy, dependent survivor relief payment, and a lump-sum work-related death subsidy. The Social Insurance Law explicitly stipulates that the remaining balance in a personal pension account can be inherited. When an employee covered by urban employee basic pension insurance dies of illness or non-work-related causes, family members can also receive a funeral subsidy and survivor relief payment, collectively referred to as survivor benefits. The standard for enterprise employees follows a nationally unified formula. The funeral subsidy is 2 times the previous year's average monthly per capita disposable income of urban residents in the province. The survivor relief payment is also calculated based on this monthly per capita income, paying between 3 and 24 months depending on contribution years. Less than 5 years pays 3 months, 5 to 10 years pays 6 months, 10 to 15 years pays 9 months, and for over 15 years, each additional year adds 1 month, up to a maximum of 24 months. For those already retired, 1 month is deducted for each 1 year of pension already collected, with a minimum floor of 9 months.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2026). 住房公积金管理条例（国务院令第 844 号修改，2026 年 9 月 20 日起施行）第二十四条末款. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>：「职工死亡或者被宣告死亡的，职工的继承人、受遗赠人可以提取职工住房公积金账户内的存储余额；无继承人也无受遗赠人的，职工住房公积金账户内的存储余额纳入住房公积金的增值收益。」；工伤保险条例第三十九条的三笔待遇见第 19 节. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2018 修正). 社会保险法（第十四、十七条）：「个人死亡的，个人账户余额可以继承」「参加基本养老保险的个人，因病或者非因工死亡的，其遗属可以领取丧葬补助金和抚恤金」. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f210989b9179a>（国家法律法规数据库）；人力资源社会保障部、财政部 (2021). 企业职工基本养老保险遗属待遇暂行办法（人社部发〔2021〕18 号，2021 年 9 月 1 日起施行，第四、五条），原件未公开挂网，条文按广州市花都区人民政府行政复议决定书（花都府行复〔2022〕226 号）的逐字引用核对：「丧葬补助金的标准，按照参保人员死亡时本省……上一年度城镇居民月人均可支配收入的2倍计算」「缴费年限不满5年的，发放月数为3个月……缴费年限30年以上的，按照30年计算，发放月数最高为24个月」. <https://www.huadu.gov.cn/xxgk/xzfy/content/post_8672730.html>；福建省人力资源和社会保障厅. 常见问题：「每领取1年基本养老金减少1个月，发放月数最低为9个月」「累计缴费年限不足5年的，其遗属待遇（含丧葬补助金和抚恤金）标准不得超过其个人缴费之和」. <http://rst.fujian.gov.cn/wz/cjwt/czzgly/202311/t20231113_6294929.htm>
- Notes: The housing provident fund is the most commonly forgotten payout because it is not kept in a regular bank account and is not automatically distributed with the estate. The remaining balance in the personal pension account and survivor benefits are calculated and disbursed separately, and should be processed together at the local social insurance service window where contributions were made. Required documents vary by region. If contributions were made for less than 5 years, the total survivor benefits cannot exceed the total amount the deceased contributed. This formula applies to enterprise employee pension insurance; ask at the counter about survivor benefits for government/public institution schemes or urban and rural resident pension insurance. If funeral subsidies or survivor relief payments are deposited into the deceased's bank account, see Section 12 for withdrawal procedures. See Section 17 for formal requirements regarding wills and inheritance. If you plan to renounce inheritance to avoid inheriting debts, do not withdraw these funds yet; see Section 11 (written declaration of renouncing inheritance).

### 10. Close relatives can ask to access, copy, correct, and delete the deceased's account and personal information
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Only if the platform will not cooperate do you need to complain or to sue.
- In plain terms: After the person has died, close relatives, for their own lawful and proper interests, can ask the platform to access, copy, correct, or delete the deceased's personal information. If the platform refuses, it has to give a reason. You can also sue in court. There is one exception. If he made other arrangements while alive, for example by naming someone to inherit an account or by leaving a clear wish, follow his arrangements.
- Benefit: The law states it in so many words: when a natural person has died, close relatives, for their own lawful and proper interests, may exercise the rights to access, copy, correct, delete, and otherwise deal with the deceased's relevant personal information. Close relatives can ask the platform to bring his information out to be looked at, to make 1 copy, to correct an error, and to delete it. Article 50 requires the platform to set up a convenient mechanism for accepting and handling applications. "If it refuses a person's request to exercise a right, it shall state the reasons." If the platform refuses, "the individual may bring a lawsuit in a people's court according to law."

### 11. Repay a deceased relative's debts only up to what you inherit; waive inheritance in writing before division to avoid liability
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Drafting a written declaration costs nothing. The real cost is forfeiting the inheritance itself, including any real estate or bank deposits. If you accept the inheritance, creditors may sue you, requiring you to appear in court and present evidence of what the estate is actually worth.
- In plain terms: When someone dies, their debts do not simply vanish, but heirs—such as children or a spouse—only have to repay them up to the value of whatever assets they actually inherit; you do not have to pay shortfalls out of your own pocket. However, if a spouse co-signed the loan or the money went toward everyday household expenses, it is legally a joint marital debt, and the surviving spouse must personally repay it. If you want nothing to do with the debt, submit a written declaration waiving your inheritance before the estate is divided, which also means walking away from all inherited property. Remaining silent is legally treated as accepting the inheritance.
- Benefit: Article 1161 of the Civil Code provides: "An heir shall clear the taxes and debts that the decedent ought to pay according to law to the extent of the actual value of the estate obtained. This does not apply if an heir voluntarily pays an amount in excess of the actual value of the estate." This means you only repay the taxes and debts using the portion of the estate you inherit. You are not obligated to cover any shortfall unless you choose to do so voluntarily. Paragraph 2 of the same article specifies: "Where an heir disclaims an inheritance, he may be exempt from the liability for clearing the taxes and debts that the decedent ought to pay according to law." For a surviving spouse, Article 1064 of the Civil Code must also be considered: "Debts incurred based on the joint expression of intent by both spouses, such as joint signatures or subsequent ratification by one spouse, as well as debts incurred by one spouse in their own name during the marriage to meet everyday household needs, constitute joint marital debts." If both spouses signed for the loan or the funds were used for regular living expenses, the spouse is personally liable as a debtor, and disclaiming the inheritance does not eliminate this liability. Article 1159 provides that taxes and debts owed by the decedent must be cleared before the estate is partitioned. Article 1124 stipulates that anyone disclaiming an inheritance must "make a declaration to disclaim the inheritance in written form prior to the disposition of the estate; failure to make such a declaration shall be deemed an acceptance of the inheritance." Article 33 of the Supreme People's Court Interpretation (I) on the Succession Part of the Civil Code requires this written declaration to be delivered to the estate administrator or the other heirs. Article 35 of the same Interpretation provides that disclaiming after the estate has already been divided is treated not as a disclaimer of inheritance rights, but as a disposition of property already acquired. Article 1145 allows heirs to select an estate administrator, and if none is selected, they serve jointly. Article 1147 charges the estate administrator with taking inventory of the estate, compiling an inventory list, and settling outstanding claims and debts. In a case from Yuzhou, Henan, a borrower died of illness in March 2023, and his children did not submit a written disclaimer of inheritance. In May 2023, the court ordered the children to repay 95,000 yuan in principal and interest within the actual value of the inherited estate. Because the children argued that the inherited company could not be clearly valued, the judgment could not be executed smoothly; their personal bank accounts and vehicles were frozen or seized, and they were placed on the restricted high-spending list. The matter dragged on until February 2025, when they settled by paying 90,000 yuan. In a Chongqing case, a borrower died leaving 400,000 yuan in unpaid loan principal and interest. The heirs declared that they were waiving the inheritance; however, the decedent's spouse and daughter had already withdrawn his basic pension insurance funds and occupational annuity, which legally formed part of the estate. The procuratorate determined that they had not explicitly disclaimed the inheritance, deeming them to have accepted it, and in November 2025 the spouse reached a settlement with the creditor and cleared the debt. Because such liabilities typically range from 10,000 to several hundred thousand yuan, the potential financial benefit is rated Large.

Surviving spouses are treated under distinct rules: if a debt was signed jointly or incurred for everyday living expenses, it is a joint marital debt. In that situation, the spouse is personally a debtor and cannot escape liability by disclaiming the inheritance. When dividing an estate, half of the community property must first be allocated to the surviving spouse; only the remaining portion constitutes the estate. If you plan to waive the inheritance, do not withdraw money from the decedent's accounts beforehand. As noted in Item 9 of this chapter (housing provident fund balances and social insurance benefits), balances in a housing provident fund or personal pension account can only be withdrawn by legal heirs; the Chongqing case arose because heirs withdrew those funds before attempting to claim a waiver. A disclaimer of inheritance is legally invalid if it leaves you unable to fulfill statutory maintenance, support, or custody obligations. If debt collectors show up, first demand proof such as IOUs or contracts, and direct them to the estate administrator or the courts. Never sign a repayment guarantee for debt exceeding the estate's value, as voluntary promises to pay are legally enforceable. If you are sued, standard first-instance civil proceedings must conclude within 6 months of filing (subject to extension), while summary proceedings take 3 months. Attorney fees are not included in the statutory court costs borne by the losing party and must be paid out of pocket. Retain all estate inventory records and valuation documentation for real estate and savings, as enforcement courts rely on these to determine how much you are obligated to repay. For why using death to settle debt fails, see Chapter 29, Item 13 (Do not treat death as debt settlement).

### 12. Skip default notarization for inheritance: withdraw bank deposits under 50,000 RMB directly from each bank, and transfer real estate by having all heirs visit the property registration center together
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. You must visit each bank that holds deposits individually. For real estate, all heirs must assemble at the registration window on the same day. If anyone refuses to come or disputes the division, this route is blocked, leaving only notarization or litigation.
- In plain terms: If the deceased person's total deposits at a single bank do not exceed 50,000 RMB, the surviving spouse, children, or parents can withdraw the money directly with proof of identity and kinship, without notarization. The balance must be withdrawn all at once, and the account closed immediately. Real estate also does not strictly require notarization: all heirs can complete the title transfer simply by going to the real estate registration center together. Notarization costs money; in Fuzhou and Xiamen, for instance, notary fees for housing are charged by floor area, capped in principle at 10,000 RMB per property.
- Benefit: On bank deposits, Circular No. 18 (2021) issued by the CBIRC and the People's Bank of China established that notarization is no longer required for qualifying small-sum deposits. Three conditions must be met: first, the total balance across all accounts at the same bank must not exceed 10,000 RMB (individual banks were allowed to raise this limit up to 50,000 RMB); second, the applicant must be a spouse, child, or parent, or an heir or legatee designated in a notarized will; third, the entire balance must be withdrawn in one single transaction and the account closed. A spouse, child, or parent must present four items: a death certificate, proof of kinship such as a household register, marriage certificate, or birth certificate, their own valid identification, and a signed commitment letter. Under Document Jin Gui [2024] No. 6, the limit was standardized at 50,000 RMB starting June 1, 2024 (small and medium rural banking institutions may implement this by December 31, 2025). Gold accumulation products, as well as government bonds and wealth management products sold through the bank, can also be withdrawn this way upon maturity or redemption, with their proceeds counting toward the 50,000 RMB ceiling. Funeral allowances and survivor subsidies deposited into the account are exempt from the 50,000 RMB cap and can be collected by the spouse, children, or parents using the same set of documents. On real estate, Article 14 of the Detailed Rules for the Implementation of the Interim Regulations on Real Estate Registration provides that applicants for registration through inheritance or bequest "shall submit death certificates, a will or an agreement among all statutory heirs regarding the distribution of the real estate, and proof of kinship with the deceased, or may submit notarized documents or effective legal instruments." Notarization is merely one optional method. In 2016, the Ministry of Justice formally repealed the 1991 joint circular that had mandated notarization for inherited real estate registration. The Fuzhou Real Estate Registration and Transaction Center clarified the non-notarized procedure: all statutory heirs or legatees must appear in person at the registry to inspect documents and sign a written declaration guaranteeing their authenticity. Regarding costs, under Fujian standards: non-property inheritance notarization costs 0.8% for amounts up to 200,000 RMB, with a 300 RMB minimum. Property notarization is charged by area, capped at 70 RMB per square meter in Fuzhou and Xiamen, and generally capped at 10,000 RMB per residence. By these rules, notarizing a 50,000 RMB deposit costs 400 RMB, while notarizing a 90-square-meter apartment in Fuzhou costs up to 6,300 RMB. Bypassing notarization saves hundreds to thousands of yuan, rating the benefit as moderate.
