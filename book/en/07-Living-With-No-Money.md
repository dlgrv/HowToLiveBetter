# How to get by when you have no money

This section covers money and social safeguards. It lists how much you might receive, what services are available, and what requirements you must meet — without trying to convert these benefits into years of life expectancy. Policies change yearly and vary by region, so all amounts and eligibility criteria reflect what’s officially posted in your area at the moment. Items are ordered so that those requiring no cost, minimal paperwork, and quick results appear first; options needing long‑term qualification or showing large regional differences come later. Suggestions based purely on personal experience and lacking official backing are placed at the very end.

The topics are grouped as follows, with item numbers shown in parentheses.

**Losing a job**: Apply online for unemployment benefits (Item 1); get recognized as someone facing employment difficulties (Item 12); claim subsidies for vocational training (Item 13).

**Unpaid wages**: File a complaint with labor authorities first (Item 2); seek legal aid (Item 3); determine exactly who owes you money (Item 22).

**Emergency relief**: Visit a social assistance center (Item 4); apply for temporary aid (Item 6); apply for dibao (minimum subsistence allowance) (Item 7); qualify for the two disability subsidies (Item 8); replace a lost ID card right away (Item 11); find shelter if you have nowhere else to stay (Item 21).

**Medical and social insurance**: Keep your basic medical insurance active (Item 9); turn to medical insurance and medical aid for serious illnesses (Item 10); don’t panic if your social insurance payments are interrupted (Item 18); consider buying supplemental health or critical‑illness coverage (Item 20).

**Finding work and starting over**: Use public employment services and gig‑economy platforms (Item 5); adopt a systematic approach when job hunting (Item 14); avoid any employer demanding deposits or keeping your documents (Item 15); remember that even ex‑convicts or bankrupts can rebuild their lives (Item 19).

**Housing and food**: Join the queue for publicly subsidized housing (Item 16); keep both housing and food expenses as low as possible (Item 17).
### 1. Apply online for unemployment insurance benefits after losing a job
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no cost. You can submit the application via smartphone using any of three portals: the “12333 Mobile App”, the e-Social Security Card mini-program on WeChat or Alipay, or the National Social Insurance Public Service Platform. Having a social security card or ID card is enough; in most regions you won’t need to visit an office in person. The prerequisite is completing unemployment registration first.
- In plain terms: If both your employer and you have paid into the system for at least one year, and you did not resign voluntarily, you can receive benefits after finishing unemployment registration. If contributions total between one and five years, benefits last up to 12 months; between five and ten years, up to 18 months; and over ten years, up to 24 months. During this period, yibao (basic medical insurance) premiums are paid on your behalf by unemployment insurance funds.
- Benefit: All three conditions must be met. First, both your employer and you must have contributed to unemployment insurance for at least one year. Second, you must have lost the job through no fault of your own (legally termed “employment termination not due to personal choice”). Third, unemployment registration must be completed and you must be actively seeking work. Benefit duration depends on contribution history: up to 12 months for under five years of contributions, up to 18 months for five to ten years, and up to 24 months for over ten years. Monthly amounts are set by provincial authorities and vary by region; they are higher than the urban dibao threshold yet lower than local minimum wage levels. The national target is gradually raising these payments to 90% of minimum wage standards. While receiving benefits, you retain full coverage under employee medical insurance; the corresponding premiums are paid by unemployment insurance funds, so you pay nothing personally (this policy has been in effect nationwide since 1999).

Immediately register after leaving a job, using the termination certificate issued by your employer (see Section 19, Item 17). Questions regarding non-compete agreements or lack of compensation should be addressed in Section 11, Item 12. Exact benefit amounts differ widely by province; consult local regulations for precise figures. The “90% of minimum wage” benchmark originates from a 2017 guideline issued jointly by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security and the Ministry of Finance (Document No. 71 [2017]), which states that payments should “gradually be raised to 90% of minimum wage standards”; this is a long-term goal rather than an immediate requirement.

### 2. Filing a complaint with the labor inspection bureau or initiating one-instance arbitration are both free options; most cases yield results within a few months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: There are no fees for either option. You’ll need to gather your employment contract, pay stubs, attendance records, bank transfer statements, and any relevant chat logs, then visit the local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau or arbitration commission one to three times. For arbitration, there is a one-year statutory deadline (known as the arbitration limitation period). Employees still working for the employer are exempt from this deadline; former employees must file within one year of leaving. The main challenge is the repeated trips and evidence collection, which can be discouraging.

- In plain terms: Neither option costs anything. First, call 12333 or file a complaint with the labor inspection bureau; investigations must be completed within 60 working days, and if the employer fails to pay, it may be ordered to pay an additional 50%–100% as compensation. If that doesn’t work, you can initiate one-instance arbitration, which must be concluded within 45 days. However, “case closure” does not guarantee payment: if the employer has no assets or the owner has fled, you might still receive nothing even after winning.

- Benefit: The first step is filing a complaint. Call 12333 or visit your local labor inspection bureau. By law, investigations must be finished within 60 working days, extendable by another 30 days for complex cases. If violations are confirmed, the employer can be ordered to pay wages immediately; failure to do so triggers an extra penalty of 50%–100% of the owed amount. In 2024, labor inspection agencies nationwide handled 137,000 cases, securing 8.65 billion yuan for 654,000 workers. If you have a written IOU or clear wage records, you may also apply directly to a local court for a payment order, which must be issued within 15 days of acceptance. The second step is arbitration: legally required to conclude within 45 days, extendable by 15 days. Workers can request provisional enforcement even before the full ruling, and if the owed amount does not exceed 12 times the local minimum monthly wage, the arbitration decision is final and binding. In 2024, 4.257 million arbitration cases were filed, with 4.156 million resolved, totaling 93.47 billion yuan in recovered wages. The mediation success rate stood at 79.6%, while 98.2% of cases were closed and 73.7% resulted in final rulings. Some claim the process takes “six months to a year,” referring to cases that proceed to court after arbitration: a first-instance trial takes six months (or three months under simplified procedures) plus three months for any appeal. In 2024, courts resolved 88,000 wage-recovery cases, securing 28.54 billion yuan, and 998 individuals were convicted of refusing to pay wages.

### 3. Legal aid is available at no cost for cases such as wage disputes, alimony claims, and workplace injury compensation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. Legal aid services are provided free of charge to all applicants.
- In plain terms: Anyone seeking unpaid wages, alimony, social insurance benefits, dibao payments, or compensation after acting in defense of others can apply for free legal assistance. Simply call 12348 or visit your local legal aid center. You no longer need to obtain proof of financial hardship from your hometown — just describe your situation honestly. For defendants facing the death penalty, or those who are blind, deaf, mute, or minors, a lawyer is assigned regardless of their financial status.
- Benefit: According to national regulations, legal aid covers six main categories of civil cases: claims for state compensation; requests for social insurance or dibao benefits; applications for pensions or relief funds; demands for alimony, child support, or spousal maintenance; wage recovery claims; and civil claims arising from acts of bravery. In criminal cases, defendants facing capital punishment or those who are blind, deaf, mute, or minors are guaranteed legal representation irrespective of their financial circumstances.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2003). 法律援助条例（国令第 385 号，第十条、第十一条、第十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6944.htm>；司法部有关负责人 (2021). 就贯彻实施法律援助法答记者问. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2021-12/29/content_5665114.htm>
- Notes: The Legal Aid Law took effect on 1 January 2022, introducing one key change: applicants no longer need to submit official proof of financial hardship. A straightforward statement of one’s financial situation now suffices. Legal aid can be accessed via local legal aid centers, public legal service hubs, or by calling 12348.

### 4. Turning to a shelter when you have nowhere else to go – they provide food, lodging, and even a ticket home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no cost. You can go to your local civil affairs bureau or shelter on your own, or ask police or civil affairs officials to take you there. Upon arrival, you must truthfully provide your name and list any personal belongings. The hard part is simply admitting that you have no money and nowhere to stay.
- In plain terms: These shelters provide meals and a place to sleep. If you fall ill, they send you to a hospital and help you contact your family. Those who cannot afford a trip home receive a free ticket. Typically, stays are limited to 10 days. Entry and departure are completely voluntary – this is not a detention center, and no cash is handed out. Tickets are issued based on your household registration or current residence, so think carefully about where you want to go before arriving.
- Benefit: Shelters supply food and lodging that meet basic hygiene standards. Should you suffer a medical emergency while there, they arrange prompt hospital treatment. They also assist in reaching out to relatives or employers. For those unable to pay for travel back to their registered home or current residence, a free transport voucher – essentially a train or bus ticket – is provided. “Household registration” refers to your hometown, while “current residence” is wherever you currently live. Generally, stays are capped at 10 days; extensions are granted if family members are on the way, medical care is needed, or other arrangements must be made. Police and other government officials encountering homeless individuals in the course of their duties must inform them of this assistance option. Special care is given to disabled persons, minors, the elderly, and anyone with mobility issues – they are guided and escorted to shelters (nationwide, since 2003).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2003). 城市生活无着的流浪乞讨人员救助管理办法（国务院令第 381 号，第五、六、七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62246.htm>；民政部 (2003). 城市生活无着的流浪乞讨人员救助管理办法实施细则（民政部令第 24 号，第十一、十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62510.htm>
- Notes: Entry and departure are entirely voluntary; this is not a detention facility. Basic food and lodging are provided, but no cash is given. Transport vouchers are issued based on household registration or current residence – be sure to decide where you wish to go before seeking help. For shelter arrangements at night or during winter, see Section 21 (when there truly is nowhere else to sleep).

### 5. Use free public employment services and gig platforms instead of paid agencies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. You can register at any public employment service agency at the county level or higher — such as employment service centers or human resources markets. Local gig platforms also allow free registration. If you don’t know the exact location, call 12333 for help.
- In plain terms: Public agencies like employment service centers and human resources markets provide job referrals, career guidance, policy advice, and both employment and unemployment registration — all completely free of charge. In fact, the law explicitly forbids them from charging for these services. Registration and posting job listings on gig platforms is likewise free. It’s a good idea to complete your unemployment registration at the same time, since it’s required to receive unemployment benefits and qualify for assistance as someone facing employment difficulties.
- Benefit: The law guarantees that the following services must be provided at no cost: advice on employment policies; information about current job openings and labor shortages; career guidance and job matching; processing of both employment and unemployment registration; and targeted employment support for individuals facing employment difficulties. These agencies are prohibited from engaging in any commercial activities or profiting off these services. Gig platform job postings and registrations are also offered to the public free of charge, as part of the broader public employment information system. Special support is further extended to long-term unemployed workers and low-income gig workers facing financial hardship (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2007, 2015 修正). 就业促进法（第三十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/guoqing/2021-10/29/content_5647636.htm>；人力资源社会保障部等五部门 (2022). 关于加强零工市场建设 完善求职招聘服务的意见（人社部发〔2022〕38 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-07/09/content_5700177.htm>
- Notes: Unemployment registration is a prerequisite for receiving unemployment benefits and qualifying as someone facing employment difficulties — so it’s worth completing at the same time. For gig workers paid on a daily basis, always verify your earnings on the spot, and be sure to collect the employer’s contact details and a record of the work performed.

### 6. Apply for emergency assistance when unexpected events occur
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Simply submit an application to the local township government or subdistrict office, explaining what happened at home — such as a fire, traffic accident, sudden serious illness of a family member, or an unexpected spike in essential expenses. After review and public notice, the county-level civil affairs bureau approves the request. Prepare documents according to the local checklist; usually only one or two visits are required.
- In plain terms: If an unexpected crisis strikes — like a fire, car crash, or sudden serious illness of a family member — and it temporarily overwhelms your ability to cover basic living costs, you can apply for emergency assistance from the local township government or subdistrict office. You do not need to already be a dibao recipient. Most often, a one-time payment is issued; exact amounts vary widely by region.
- Benefit: Two categories of households qualify for this aid. The first includes families facing immediate hardship in covering basic needs such as food, shelter, and medical care due to unexpected events or sudden serious illnesses of family members. The second comprises dibao households experiencing an unexpected rise in unavoidable expenses that exceeds their financial capacity. What types of expenses qualify and how much aid is granted are determined and publicly announced by local governments at or above the county level (this nationwide policy has been in effect since 2014).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国务院 (2014, 2019 修订). 社会救助暂行办法（国务院令第 649 号，第四十七、四十八、四十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5468952.htm>
- Notes: You do not need to already be a dibao recipient. Typically, a one-time payment is provided; exact amounts differ greatly across regions and are set by local authorities each year.

### 7. Apply for dibao if household income falls below the local threshold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There are no fees. Household members living together must submit a written application to the local township government or subdistrict office based on their hukou registration. If handling it independently proves difficult, they may ask the villagers' committee or residents' committee to assist. The application requires verification of household income and assets. Typically, 2–3 visits are needed, bringing along ID cards, hukou registration books, and proof of income and assets. The main challenge lies in disclosing personal financial details for inspection and waiting for the public notice period to end.
- In plain terms: If the per-person household income is lower than the local dibao threshold and assets meet eligibility criteria, a dibao application can be filed. As of late 2024, the average monthly dibao standard stood at 798.1 yuan per person in urban areas and 593.9 yuan in rural regions. Approved recipients receive an amount equal to the difference between the local standard and their per-person monthly income. Owning property, vehicles, or sizable savings, or having children capable of providing support, may disqualify applicants.
- Benefit: Households whose per-person income remains below the local dibao threshold and whose assets comply with regulations qualify for dibao benefits. In late 2024, the average monthly standard was 798.1 yuan per person in cities and 593.9 yuan in rural areas. Approximately 6.25 million urban residents and 33.615 million rural residents received dibao support. Payments are issued monthly based on the difference between the local standard and per-person income. For instance, if the standard is 798.1 yuan and a household member earns 500 yuan monthly, the dibao payout per person amounts to 298.1 yuan, not the full 798.1 yuan. This threshold is determined by multiplying the previous year’s average per-person consumption expenditure by a fixed percentage, with periodic adjustments nationwide.

- Sources:国务院 (2014, 2019 修订). 社会救助暂行办法（国务院令第 649 号，第九、十、十一条；第十二条：「县级人民政府民政部门按照共同生活的家庭成员人均收入低于当地最低生活保障标准的差额，按月发给最低生活保障金」）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5468952.htm>；国家统计局 (2025). 社会事业向好发展 民生福祉不断增进——「十四五」以来社会民生统计报告. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202509/content_7042627.htm>；民政部等四部门 (2024). 关于进一步做好最低生活保障标准确定调整工作的指导意见（民发〔2024〕16 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202403/content_7007237.htm>
- Notes: Dibao serves as a final safety net, and applications must be submitted at one’s place of hukou registration. Exceeding local asset limits or having financially capable children may result in rejection. Upon approval, additional perks apply: financial aid for basic medical insurance (see Section 9), eligibility for medical assistance programs (see Section 10), and automatic qualification for legal aid regardless of financial status (see Section 3).

### 8. Apply for the two disability subsidies with a disability certificate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no cost. Simply bring your disability certificate and submit one application at your local subdistrict office or township government.
- In plain terms: Disabled individuals living in dibao households can receive a living subsidy. Those with a disability rating of level 1 or 2 who require long-term care can also qualify for a care subsidy. Both subsidies can be received simultaneously. Each province sets its own subsidy amounts, which vary widely, so it’s best to ask your local subdistrict office for exact figures. All you need to do is bring your second-generation disability certificate and file one application at the subdistrict office or township government.
- Benefit: The State Council has established these two subsidies. The first is the Living Subsidy for Disabled Persons in Need; the official wording specifies that it is intended for “disabled individuals living in dibao households, with eligible regions gradually expanding eligibility to low-income disabled persons and other disadvantaged groups.” In short, priority is given to disabled people in dibao households, though regions with sufficient funds may extend coverage further. The second subsidy is the Care Subsidy for Severely Disabled Persons; it is designated for “individuals with a disability rating of level 1 or 2 who require long-term care.” Both subsidies can be claimed at the same time.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2015). 关于全面建立困难残疾人生活补贴和重度残疾人护理补贴制度的意见（国发〔2015〕52 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2015-09/25/content_10181.htm>
- Notes: Subsidy amounts are determined by provincial governments and differ significantly across regions, ranging from just a few dozen yuan to several hundred yuan per month. To apply, you must present a second-generation disability certificate. If you are unable to apply in person, a family member may submit the application on your behalf.

### 9. Don’t let your annual dibao and yibao payments lapse — subsidies are available for low‑income households
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Individuals must pay at least 400 yuan per year. This is the national standard for 2024 and 2025; local authorities may set higher amounts. Payments must be made during the designated annual enrollment window. Extremely disadvantaged individuals receive full government funding, while those receiving dibao get a fixed government subsidy.
- In plain terms: Each person must contribute a minimum of 400 yuan per year; the government then adds roughly 700 yuan per person. Full funding is provided for the most vulnerable, while dibao recipients receive a set subsidy. Letting payments lapse carries a penalty: anyone who enrolls outside the window or fails to maintain continuous coverage must wait three months before receiving any benefits; each additional year without payment adds another month to that waiting period.
- Benefit: In addition to the individual contribution, the government contributes at least 670 yuan per person in 2024, a figure that rises to at least 700 yuan in 2025 — an increase of 30 yuan over the previous year. The minimum personal contribution remains 400 yuan (based on the 2025 Notice No. 22 from the National Healthcare Security Administration, as paraphrased from provincial documents). The 2026 Government Work Report projects a further 24‑yuan rise. For dibao recipients and the most disadvantaged, any medical expenses not covered by yibao can be reimbursed at a rate of at least 70 % through additional medical assistance programs. The main drawback of missed payments is the waiting period described above.
