# 5. Don't Waste Money

This section deals only in money: how much you can save, how much annual returns differ, and how much a sum of money returns on average over the long run. None of this is converted into lifespan or time. Items involving investing only cover how to run the numbers, rely solely on verified historical data, and do not constitute investment advice.

The items in this section are grouped into the following categories, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Everyday expenses**: Turn off auto-renewal (Item 1), recalculate phone and broadband plans (Item 4), decline extended warranties on electronics (Item 11), choose centralized procurement medications (Item 12), drink boiled tap water (Item 14), pay per visit at the gym (Item 21), set a cooling-off period for major purchases (Item 23), do not stockpile based on crossed-out reference prices (Item 24).

**Impulse spending and kids' expenses**: Do not buy lottery tickets (Item 5), do not tip livestreamers (Item 8), claim refunds when children top up or tip online (Item 9), anyone asking a child for a verification code is a scammer (Item 10), count prayer beads and designer toys as money spent (Item 33), accept only CMA inspection reports for jewelry and jade (Item 34), random sales like blind boxes and card packs (Item 35).

**Online shopping and consumer rights**: Sign written contracts for prepaid spending (Item 22), know platform rules when shopping online (Item 29), what to do when livestream purchases go wrong (Item 30), claim ten times the price for unsafe food (Item 31), check sampling inspections and certifications before buying major goods (Item 32), buy prepackaged goods if you want an ingredient list (Item 36), when tour groups steer you into shopping stops (Item 50).

**Taxes, housing provident fund, and medical insurance**: Annual individual income tax reconciliation (Item 2), withdraw from the housing provident fund (gongjijin) for rent and renovations too (Item 3), bind family accounts under yibao (basic medical insurance) (Item 13), personal pension accounts (Item 20), pay urban employee pension insurance at the base tier if paying out of pocket (Item 46).

**Saving, borrowing, and investing**: Walk away immediately from high advertised returns (Item 6), convert borrowing costs to an annualized interest rate first (Item 7), avoid frequent stock trading (Item 15), do not invest with borrowed money (Item 16), use broad-market index funds as a core holding (Item 17), choose funds with low expense ratios (Item 18), do not put all your money in one place (Item 19), build an emergency fund first (Item 27), compare options before prepaying a mortgage (Item 28), sell losing stocks according to set rules (Item 37), use QDII or Stock Connect to buy US-listed stocks (Item 38), stay away from cryptocurrencies (Item 45), verify wealth management products recommended by bank staff before buying (Item 51).

**Insurance**: Prioritize term or pure-protection insurance (Item 25), buy sufficient third-party liability coverage (Item 26), insure only against losses you cannot bear (Item 39), buy term life insurance for household earners first (Item 40), cancel policies within the cooling-off period (Item 41), distinguish bank deposits from insurance products at bank counters (Item 42), do not use third-party surrender agents to cancel policies (Item 43), designate specific individuals as beneficiaries (Item 44).

**Buying a car**: Inspect the vehicle on the spot before driving away (Item 47), specify in writing that a used car has no accident history (Item 48), do not pay financial service fees at 4S car dealerships (Item 49).
### 1. Turn off unused auto-renewals and renew manually when needed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Checking the automatic payment lists across Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android takes 10 to 20 minutes once. Each renewal after that just requires one extra tap yourself.
- In plain terms: What you save is money spent on subscriptions you stopped using long ago that still charge you every month. Checking the automatic payment lists across Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android takes under twenty minutes. By regulation, merchants must clearly notify you before charging and must provide an easy-to-find, hassle-free way to cancel at any time.
- Benefit: The money saved equals the sum of all subscriptions you no longer use but are still being charged for. This varies by individual, with no universal figure. Regulations require merchants to give you a noticeable reminder before renewing and to provide an easy-to-use option to cancel at any time at no extra charge (China, effective since 2021).
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:国家市场监督管理总局 (2021, 2025 修正). 网络交易监督管理办法（总局令第 37 号，第十八条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Notes: For services you use regularly, an annual plan is usually cheaper than paying monthly, so keeping auto-renewal can actually be more economical. What you want to cancel are the ones you forgot about long ago that keep deducting money. For how website operators selling memberships should give notice, see Section 26, Item 13 (Selling Auto-Renewing Memberships).

### 2. File your annual individual income tax reconciliation between March and June, and claim all eligible special additional deductions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. It takes ten to twenty minutes on the Individual Income Tax app.
- In plain terms: Filing usually results in a tax refund for several groups: people who worked only a few months during the year, changed jobs mid-year, or have unclaimed deductions for children's education, mortgage interest, rent, or elderly care. It takes ten to twenty minutes on a phone. You do not need to file if your annual comprehensive income is under 120,000 yuan or if the additional tax you owe is under 400 yuan.
- Benefit: Combine four categories of income—wages and salaries, remuneration for personal services, author's royalties, and royalties for the use of rights—to calculate the final balance. Any overpaid tax is refunded, and any underpaid tax must be settled. Filing is exempt under two conditions: your annual comprehensive income does not exceed 120,000 yuan, or the tax amount owed does not exceed 400 yuan. These two exemptions were established in a 2023 announcement by the State Taxation Administration and apply through December 31, 2027. Conversely, filing often yields a refund for people who earned income in only a few months of the year, changed jobs during the year, or have unclaimed deductions for children's education, mortgage interest, rent, or elderly care.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家税务总局 (2025). 个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴管理办法（第六条）及其解读. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm>；财政部、税务总局 (2023). 关于延续实施个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴有关政策的公告. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Notes: Anyone who owes tax must also file; failing to do so is illegal. There are seven special additional deduction items: children's education, continuing education, healthcare for serious illnesses, housing mortgage interest, housing rent, elderly care, and care for infants and young children under 3. Among these, mortgage interest and housing rent are mutually exclusive—you can choose only one, not both.

### 3. Use your housing provident fund beyond buying a home: rent, renovations, and property fees qualify starting September 20, 2026
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. It just takes filing a withdrawal application.
- In plain terms: You do not have to buy a home to use your housing provident fund. The new regulations list nine qualifying situations, including paying rent, renovating your home, and covering property management fees. Once you apply, the provident fund center must decide within 3 days whether to approve your withdrawal.
- Benefit: The revised regulations list nine qualifying withdrawal scenarios. These include: paying rent, renovating your own residence, and paying property management fees for your own home. The housing provident fund management center must make a decision approving or denying the withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application.

### 4. Recalculate your mobile and broadband plans once a year, downgrade unused services, and file a complaint if your carrier blocks number porting
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Checking your bill takes 10 to 15 minutes. The hard part is standing your ground when customer service resists downgrading your plan.
- In plain terms: Older mobile plans often cost several dozen yuan more each month than the new ones currently sold. That adds up to hundreds of yuan thrown away every year. By regulation, telecom carriers cannot refuse to let you port your number without a valid reason, drag their feet on processing it, or impose discriminatory pricing on porting users. If they stall, file a complaint.
- Benefit: Regulations from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology explicitly state that telecom carriers must not "refuse, obstruct, or delay the provision of mobile number portability services to users without a legitimate reason." They also must not "use technical measures such as interception or restrictions to affect communication quality for ported users," nor may they "create dedicated rate plans or marketing schemes specifically for ported users." Older plans are often tens of yuan more expensive than those currently on sale. Staying on an old plan means throwing away hundreds of yuan every year.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:工业和信息化部 (2019). 携号转网服务管理规定（工信部信管〔2019〕242 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Notes: First, check your carrier's app to see how much mobile data and voice minutes you actually used over the past six months, then pick a plan matching that real usage. If customer service claims that "downgrading requires visiting a physical store in person" or "you cannot change plans during a contract period," ask them to provide the written policy basis. If they cannot produce it, call 12300 to file a complaint with the industry regulator.

### 5. Skip the lottery
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. Skipping it just saves money.
- In plain terms: By regulation, the minimum prize payout as a share of sales is 50% for lotto/numbers games, 65% for instant scratch cards, and 73% for sports betting. For the Union Lotto (Shuangseqiu), for every 1 yuan spent, you only get back an average of 0.51 yuan over the long run—a steady loss of 49%. In 2024, national lottery sales reached 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion yuan went directly into public welfare funds and never entered the prize pool.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Finance sets minimum prize payout ratios for lottery sales: 50% for lotto and numbers games, 65% for instant scratch cards, and 73% for sports betting. The Union Lotto allocates 51% of sales to prizes, paying out only 51 yuan in prizes for every 100 yuan sold. For an individual player, for every 1 yuan spent, you only get back 0.51 yuan on average over the long run, losing 49%. In 2024, nationwide lottery sales reached 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion yuan went into public welfare funds rather than returning to ticket buyers (China, 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:财政部 (2015). 关于规范和加强彩票资金构成比例管理的通知（财综〔2015〕94 号）. <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm>；财政部 (2025). 财政部公告 2025 年第 8 号（2024 年彩票公益金筹集分配情况）. <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm>；中国福利彩票双色球游戏规则（第十四条）. <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>

### 6. Walk away whenever you hear "high return," "guaranteed principal," or "sure profit"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just pause whenever you hear these phrases.
- In plain terms: In 2018, China's banking and insurance regulator warned that any return over 6% calls for skepticism, over 8% is dangerous, and above 10% means you should be prepared to lose your entire principal. Regulated wealth management products are no longer allowed to promise guaranteed principal or returns. If you lose money in illegal fundraising schemes, regulations state that you bear the loss yourself—no one is coming to bail you out.
- Benefit: In 2018, the chairman of the former China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission stated at the Lujiazui Forum: "Question any return over 6%, consider over 8% dangerous, and be prepared to lose all your principal above 10%." Under the new asset management rules, financial institutions "must not promise guaranteed principal or guaranteed returns" and "when payout difficulties arise, financial institutions must not advance funds in any form." Legitimate wealth management no longer offers guaranteed principal. Losses from participating in illegal fundraising "shall be borne by the participants themselves"—no one compensates you when things collapse (China, 2018, 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:郭树清 (2018). 防范化解金融风险 奋力跨越重大关口（第十届陆家嘴论坛讲话，国家金融监督管理总局官网）：「在打击非法集资过程中，努力通过多种方式让人民群众认识到，高收益意味着高风险，收益率超过6%的就要打问号，超过8%的就很危险，10%以上就要准备损失全部本金」. <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=182418>；天津市地方金融管理局转载. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html>；中国人民银行等 (2018). 关于规范金融机构资产管理业务的指导意见（银发〔2018〕106 号，第二条、第六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5323101.htm>；国务院 (2021). 防范和处置非法集资条例（国务院令第 737 号，第二十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>
- Notes: The 6%, 8%, and 10% thresholds are regulatory rules of thumb based on experience, not statutory legal articles. The quote addressed crackdowns on illegal fundraising, targeting wealth management products and investment firms promising high returns. This does not constitute investment advice. For borrowing money or bringing in partners for your own business, see Section 12, Item 26 (raising funds for business).

### 7. Avoid minimum credit card payments and consumer installments; if
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
 you must borrow, always compare the APR

- Cost: Nothing. It requires having enough cash to pay off the full balance before
 the payment due date. The challenge is resisting the urge to spread out large expenses via installments. If borrowing is unavoidable, take a
 few minutes to ask for the annualized interest rate (APR) of each loan.

- In plain terms: "Minimum
 payment" options are loans that accrue interest daily—often at an annual rate of about 18% (based on pre-202
0 caps), and opting for them voids your interest-free grace period. Installment plans often advertise a "monthly fee of 0.6
%," but this works out to an annual rate of about 13.8%, not 7.2%. This
 happens because you are paying off the principal each month, yet the fee remains fixed to the original, larger amount. When comparing loans
, look only at the annualized interest rate; ignore daily or monthly rates.

- Benefit: Before 2020,
 credit card overdraft interest had a cap of 0.05% per day. Converted to a simple annual rate, this is 
18.25%. Since 2021, issuers set their own rates. When you pay only the minimum
, the remaining balance accrues interest daily, and the interest-free grace period is cancelled. The central bank now requires lenders
 to clearly state the annualized interest rate (calculated over a full year); they cannot show only daily rates or daily repayment amounts. All
 lenders must disclose the APR in contracts and advertisements; while daily or monthly rates can be listed, they cannot be more prominent than the APR
. The APR must include interest and all fees directly related to the loan. For installment loans, the APR is calculated based on the remaining
 principal after each payment. The People's Bank of China provided an example: borrowing 100,000 yuan over
 12 months with a 0.5% monthly fee and a 1,000 yuan service fee. The
 simple interest APR is about 12.80%, while the compound interest APR (Internal Rate of Return) is about
 13.58%. Looking only at the fee rate might lead you to believe the cost is 7% (0.5%
 × 12 + 1%), but the actual cost is nearly double because the principal decreases monthly while the fee is charged
 on the full 100,000 yuan. Using this logic, a 12-month installment plan with
 a 0.6% monthly fee—nominally 7.2%—actually has an APR of about 
13.8%, roughly 1.9 times the nominal figure. For terms between 6 and 36 months, the multiplier remains
 between 1.8 and 2. For private lending between individuals or non-financial entities, courts generally will not
 enforce interest rates exceeding 4 times the one-year Loan Prime Rate (LPR). As of August 20
, 2026, the one-year LPR is 3.0%, making the limit 1
2%. For loans where interest is deducted upfront, the court calculates the principal based on the actual amount received. This limit does
 not apply to licensed financial institutions like banks, consumer finance companies, or the seven types of local financial organizations (such as micro-
lending companies or pawn shops). Do not assume the 12% cap applies to these lenders; check the APR
 written in your contract (China, 2020-2021).

- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:中国人民银行 (2020). 关于推进信用卡透支利率市场化改革的通知（银发〔2020〕327 号）. <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>；中国人民银行 (2021). 中国人民银行公告〔2021〕第 3 号（第一、三、四条，附件《采用内部收益率法计算贷款年化利率示例》第三例）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-03/31/content_5597156.htm>；最高人民法院 (2020 年第二次修正). 关于审理民间借贷案件适用法律若干问题的规定（第一、二十五、二十六条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/94b6623974526df7d2430a3c73f050.html>（最高人民法院公报）；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于新民间借贷司法解释适用范围问题的批复（法释〔2020〕27 号）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/285171.html>；全国银行间同业拆借中心受中国人民银行授权 (2026). 2026 年 8 月 20 日贷款市场报价利率（LPR）公告. <https://www.chinamoney.com.cn/chinese/rdgz/20260820/3399885.html>

### 8. Don't tip streamers, don't buy in-game items, and don't impulse-shop
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. It takes a few minutes to do three things: turn off password-free payments, set transaction and daily limits, and remove linked payment methods from platforms. The hard part is resisting the urge to click that button in the moment.
- In plain terms: Gamers in China spend an average of about 480 yuan per person per year on games, and money spent is almost impossible to get back. The law only allows parents to reclaim money spent by minors on tips or games if the amount is disproportionate to their age and intelligence; there is no such provision for adults. The easiest way to protect yourself is to set up safeguards in advance: disable password-free payments, set transaction and daily limits, and unlink your payment methods.
- Benefit: This represents a significant expense. In 2024, the actual sales revenue of the domestic game market was 325.783 billion yuan with 674 million users, averaging about 480 yuan per person (calculated by dividing the total revenue by the number of users). Money spent is almost impossible to recover. The only exception is if a person with limited capacity for civil conduct (in this context, a minor) makes tips or purchases that are "disproportionate to their age and intelligence" without guardian consent. In such cases, the People's Court shall support the guardian’s request for a refund. There is no such regulation for an adult's own tips or purchases. Additionally, minors are restricted to playing online games only on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, and only during the one-hour window from 20:00 to 21:00 (China, 2020–2021).

### 9. Refunds for large in-app purchases by children
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 人群=孩子 -->

- Cost: Free, but requires time to collect evidence and negotiate with the platform. Platforms often reject initial requests before eventually issuing a refund. If negotiations fail, you must sue, which takes months and involves out-of-pocket legal fees. It is more efficient to prepare documentation and go through the platform’s support or the 12315 consumer protection hotline. The hardest part is getting the child to speak the truth; you must be patient.
- In plain terms: For children over eight, spending that is not appropriate for their age or cognitive level requires parental consent or retroactive approval to be valid. If parents do not consent or approve the purchase, the transaction is invalid, and a refund can be requested. Children under eight cannot make such spending decisions on their own. If the other party requests a decision, the transaction is considered rejected only if the parents fail to respond within 30 days of receiving the notice.
- Benefit: Minors aged eight or older have limited capacity for civil conduct, meaning they can only perform acts consistent with their age and intelligence. Civil legal acts inconsistent with their age and intelligence require consent or retroactive approval from a legal representative (a parent must agree beforehand or acknowledge the act afterward). Children under eight lack the capacity for civil conduct; they cannot make their own decisions, and their affairs must be handled by a legal representative. The other party may notify the legal representative to request approval; the representative has 30 days from receiving the notice to confirm. If no response is made within those 30 days, it is deemed a refusal.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典. 最高人民检察院转载全文. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> 第十九条：「八周岁以上的未成年人为限制民事行为能力人，实施民事法律行为由其法定代理人代理或者经其法定代理人同意、追认；但是，可以独立实施纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力相适应的民事法律行为。」第二十条：「不满八周岁的未成年人为无民事行为能力人，由其法定代理人代理实施民事法律行为。」第一百四十五条：「限制民事行为能力人实施的纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力、精神健康状况相适应的民事法律行为有效；实施的其他民事法律行为经法定代理人同意或者追认后有效。」「相对人可以催告法定代理人自收到通知之日起三十日内予以追认。法定代理人未作表示的，视为拒绝追认。」
- Notes: The key is the burden of proof: you must provide evidence that the child, not you, operated the phone. Useful materials include: transaction times concentrated during school or parental work hours, device usage logs, the child’s chat and game history, and the child's own explanation. There is no uniform monetary limit for what is "appropriate for age and intelligence"—spending a few dozen yuan on a game skin is different from donating tens of thousands. ### 11. Decline extended warranties on electronics
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The trade-off is that once the standard warranty expires, you must pay out of pocket if repairs are needed, though the odds of that happening are relatively low.
- In plain terms: Extended warranties typically cost 10% to 50% of the purchase price, and sellers make over a dozen times more profit on them than on standard goods—that markup is money coming straight out of your pocket. In China, key components like washing machine motors and refrigerator compressors are already covered by mandatory 3-year statutory warranties. If you come across a very cheap warranty, divide its price by the cost of a single repair; the resulting number tells you how high the chance of at least one breakdown over those years needs to be just to break even.
- Benefit: Extended warranties are usually priced at 10% to 50% of the product's original price. Industry estimates cited in research show an average gross margin of 50% to 60% on extended warranties: for every 100 yuan sold, 50 to 60 yuan is profit. This is roughly 18 times the gross margin of ordinary consumer goods. In 2003, extended warranties made up only 3% to 4% of Best Buy's revenue, yet contributed over 50% of its operating profits (US retail data, 2000s). If you encounter an extended warranty priced well below this range, you can calculate the threshold yourself: warranty price ÷ cost of a single repair = failure probability needed to break even. Buying it only makes financial sense if your estimated chance of at least one failure during the coverage period exceeds this threshold. For example, for a 25 yuan warranty, assuming a repair costs 300 yuan, the probability of at least one breakdown must exceed roughly 8% to break even (the 300 yuan figure is purely illustrative, not an empirical statistic). Before calculating, deduct whatever is already covered by statutory warranties. In China, mandatory statutory warranty periods run from the invoice date: 1 year for the entire unit on washing machines and refrigerators, and 3 years for major components. Major components include motors, timers, program controllers, and capacitors for washing machines, and compressors, thermostats, etc., for refrigerators. Any overlap between an extended warranty and statutory coverage amounts to paying for protection you already have by law (national policy, since 1995).

- Sources:Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? - Notes: The gross margin figure is an industry estimate cited by the study (BusinessWeek 2004), not an empirical measurement conducted by the paper itself. The calculation above applies to ordinary consumers who can comfortably afford an occasional out-of-pocket repair. People who rely on a single device for their livelihood and lack a backup need to assess things differently. ### 12. Prioritize consistency-evaluated generics and centralized procurement drugs

- Cost: Free. When a doctor writes a prescription or when you buy medicine at a pharmacy, simply ask: "Is there a centralized procurement generic available?"
- In plain terms: In the second round of national centralized procurement, drug prices dropped by an average of 53%, with the steepest drop reaching 93%. By April 2026, 11 rounds had been rolled out, covering 490 medications. When getting a prescription or buying medicine, take a second to ask if there is a centralized procurement generic available. The active ingredients are identical, but the out-of-pocket cost is far lower.
- Benefit: In China's state-organized centralized volume-based drug procurement, prices in the second batch dropped by an average of 53%, with a maximum decrease of 93%. As of April 2026, 11 procurement batches have been completed, covering 490 medications. A total of 1,695 drugs have passed or are deemed to have passed consistency evaluations, accounting for roughly two-thirds of commonly used clinical chemical drugs (China, official data from 2020 to 2026).

- Notes: Controversy: People often say that "consistency evaluation only compares chemical ingredients, not clinical effectiveness." That is half true. A bioequivalence trial recruits healthy volunteers, gives them the drug, and takes serial blood samples to track the drug's concentration curve in the bloodstream. If this curve falls within 80% to 125% of the original brand-name drug's curve, it passes. Drugs with narrow therapeutic windows face even stricter criteria. By December 2024, these covered 63 centralized procurement drugs, nearly 100 Class 3A tertiary hospitals, and over 300,000 patients. The clinical outcomes showed no significant differences from the original brand-name drugs. Two practical steps you can take: First, if you firmly prefer the original brand-name drug, you can usually still get it. Centralized procurement covers only 60% to 80% of a hospital's procurement quota; the rest is determined at the hospital's discretion. Original drugs are not banned—you simply pay more out of pocket. Second, if you switch chronic medications, do not judge the effect by personal feelings alone. Follow Section 16, Item 3 (log every measurement in the same notebook) and track objective markers like blood pressure, blood glucose, and HbA1c. If your numbers genuinely deteriorate, consult your doctor and ask them to file an adverse drug reaction report, rather than discontinuing the medication on your own.

### 15. Trade stocks infrequently, and stay especially still during wild market swings
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is holding back when news breaks and you itch to trade, or when everyone else is making money and you feel the urge to double down.
- In plain terms: Between 1991 and 1996, American households that traded most actively earned only 11.4% a year, compared with 17.9% for the broader market; the gap came almost entirely from trading costs. During China's 2014–2015 boom-and-bust cycle, the bottom 85% of households by account size traded far too actively. They ended up earning 250 billion yuan less than if they had simply held on and done nothing—missing out on roughly three-tenths of their starting capital.
- Benefit: This study analyzed 66,465 household accounts at a US discount brokerage from 1991 to 1996. The most active trading quintile had an annualized net return of 11.4% (earning an average of 11.4% per year). Over the same period, the overall market returned 17.9%, and the average household returned 16.4%. The average household had an annual turnover rate of 75%, turning over three-quarters of their stock holdings each year. Between the most active and least active traders, gross returns (returns before deducting transaction fees) differed very little. Thus, the performance gap came almost entirely from transaction costs incurred during trading (United States, 1991 to 1996).
Data from China: Researchers used daily account-level data from the Shanghai Stock Exchange covering all approximately 40 million accounts over an 18-month period from July 2014 to December 2015. During these 18 months, the Shanghai Composite Index surged by more than 150%, peaked at 5,166.35 points on June 12, 2015, and then fell 40% by the end of December. Household accounts were divided into four tiers by initial market value. The smallest tier accounted for 85% of all household accounts, while the largest tier accounted for 0.5%. The total starting stock value of the two tiers was comparable: 880 billion yuan and 808 billion yuan, respectively. The result was that the smallest 85% lost 250 billion yuan due to active trading, while the top 0.5% gained 254 billion yuan. Here, "loss" is measured relative to a buy-and-hold benchmark that did nothing over the exact same period. Both figures represented roughly three-tenths of each group's initial equity value (28% for the smallest tier, 31% for the largest). In contrast, during the calmer two and a half years prior to June 2014, the same gap over any random 18-month window was only 1% to 3% of initial market value. During this boom-and-bust cycle, the average Chinese household turned over their portfolio roughly every three weeks, or nearly 18 times a year (Shanghai, China, 2014 to 2015; control period 2012 to 2014).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Barber BM, Odean T (2000). Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth: The Common Stock Investment Performance of Individual Investors. ### 16. Do not borrow to invest, use leverage, or buy what you do not understand
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the urge to borrow a bit to make a bit more when the market is booming.
- In plain terms: If you borrow money to buy stocks, once the price drops past the required line and you cannot add cash, your broker will force-sell your holdings. Paper losses turn into real losses on the spot, leaving no chance to wait for the market to bounce back. Regulations bar anyone with under half a year of trading experience or an average of less than 500,000 yuan in assets over the last 20 trading days from opening a margin account. That threshold alone shows it is not meant for everyday people.
- Benefit: Leverage means buying with borrowed money, which magnifies losses. After buying on margin, the market value of your collateral can drop below the maintenance margin ratio set by your broker. If it drops below this line and you cannot deposit additional collateral in time, your position will be forcibly liquidated—the broker sells it directly on your behalf. Paper losses instantly become realized losses, leaving zero opportunity to wait for a rebound. Regulatory rules state that clients with less than six months of securities trading experience, an inadequate capacity to bear risk, or average daily securities assets under 500,000 yuan over the preceding 20 trading days may not open credit trading accounts. This bar itself shows it is not a tool designed for ordinary retail investors (China, 2015)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:中国证监会 (2015). 证券公司融资融券业务管理办法（证监会令第 117 号，第十二条）. <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c106256/c1654005/content.shtml>；上海证券交易所. 《上海证券交易所融资融券交易实施细则》解读（投资者教育材料）. <https://www.sse.com.cn/services/tradingservice/margin/edu/c/10074042/files/a1f1c4833302451fb9130dbb94116c56.pdf>

Using consumer loans or credit card cash advances to invest is simply hidden leverage; see Item 7 in this section (Do not pay only the credit card minimum) for their interest rates. ### 17. Use broad-market index funds instead of active funds for your long-term core holdings (money left untouched)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The trade-off is giving up the chance of picking a star fund manager. The hard part is riding out downturns when the index drops without bailing out halfway through.
- In plain terms: Taken together, all US active funds hold a basket of stocks very close to the entire market. But the extra management fees they charge come straight out of your pocket. When simulations control for pure luck, very few funds generate enough extra returns to cover their own fees.
- Benefit: The aggregate portfolio of US actively managed equity funds closely resembles the broad market portfolio: combined, their holdings mirror the entire market. Yet the high costs of active management turn directly into lower net returns for investors. Using bootstrap simulations that repeatedly reshuffled returns to see what pure luck could achieve, researchers found that very few funds produced enough excess returns to cover their costs (US, published 2010). S&P Dow Jones Indices reports annually: in 2024, 65% of US actively managed large-cap equity funds underperformed the S&P 500. Over 24 years of tracking, an average of 64% underperformed each year. Across the 15-year period ending in 2024, in no category did a majority of active funds beat their benchmark.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Fama EF, French KR (2010). Luck versus Skill in the Cross-Section of Mutual Fund Returns. The Journal of Finance 65(5):1915-1947. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01598.x>；S&P Dow Jones Indices (2025). ### 18. Choose lower-fee funds within the same category
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Before buying, take an extra look at the total sum of management fees, custodian fees, and sales service fees.
- In plain terms: For every 1 percentage point higher an annual fee is, you end up with about 18% less money after 20 years, and about 26% less after 30 years. Picking lower-fee funds in the same category leaves more money in your pocket instead of being eaten away by fees.
- Benefit: Fee differences compound over time and eat into your capital, compounding every year. An annual fee that is 1 percentage point higher reduces the final value by about 18% (specifically 18.2%, calculated as 1 − 0.99^20 ≈ 0.182) after 20 years and by about 26% after 30 years. Final value simply means the balance remaining in the account at that point. Starting in 2023, China rolled out mutual fund fee reforms, capping fee ceilings for actively managed equity funds (funds that mainly buy stocks hand-picked by a manager). As of July 7, 2023, newly registered funds of this type cap annual management fees at 1.2% and custodian fees at 0.2%. A total of 136 fund management companies have also lowered these two fees on their existing products below these caps.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:中国证监会 (2023). 证监会有关部门负责人就公募基金费率改革答记者问. <http://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7418692/content.shtml>；厦门证监局 (2024). 「新注册的主动权益类基金统一执行"管理费率不超过1.2%、托管费率不超过0.2%"的上限标准」. <http://www.csrc.gov.cn/xiamen/c105636/c7511608/content.shtml>；Fama EF, French KR (2010). 见第 17 条（宽基指数基金）. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01598.x>
- Notes: This calculation is purely mathematical, assuming both funds earn the same gross return before deducting fees. In practice, subscription fees, redemption fees, and sales service fees for Class C shares also need to be considered. Does not constitute investment advice.

### 19. Do not put all your money into a single stock, platform, or property
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The hard part is accepting that you won't always pick the single biggest winner.
- In plain terms: As long as different assets do not rise and fall in complete lockstep, spreading your money keeps your average return unchanged while smoothing out the account's swings. Conversely, putting everything into one stock, one platform, or one property means that when it runs into trouble, your entire net worth takes the hit.
- Benefit: As long as several assets do not move in perfect lockstep, spreading your money keeps expected return (what you earn on average) unchanged while lowering volatility (account ups and downs). This is the foundational conclusion of portfolio selection introduced by Markowitz in 1952, which has been standard practice for decades since. Conversely, if all your money is concentrated in a single stock or platform, its variance becomes your entire variance, and you absorb its swings completely. When trouble strikes, there is no hedge, meaning no other assets exist to help offset the loss.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Notes: Graded B because the foundational paper relies on pure mathematical derivation and does not provide an empirical figure for how much money diversification saves. Diversification only dampens account swings; it does not guarantee you will not lose money. Buying broad-market index funds (see item 17 in this chapter) is itself a low-effort way to diversify. This does not constitute investment advice.

### 20. Calculate tax savings and check withdrawal rules before opening an individual pension account; do not contribute just for tax deductions if you pay no income tax or are in the 3% bracket
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Opening an account is free, but contributed funds cannot be withdrawn at will. Beyond reaching the statutory basic pension age, withdrawals are permitted under qualifying medical expenses, unemployment, or dibao (minimum subsistence allowance), with specific thresholds in the notes. Without meeting these criteria, you may have to wait many years.
- In plain terms: Contributing the full 12,000 yuan within a single tax bracket yields a net tax saving of 840, 2,040, or 3,240 yuan for the 10%, 20%, and 30% brackets, respectively. This already accounts for the 3% tax paid upon withdrawal; crossing brackets requires recalculation. For those in the 3% bracket, the upfront tax saved is paid back at withdrawal, yielding at best a wash. Do not lock away money you might need soon, as early withdrawal is strictly limited to qualifying medical expenses, unemployment, or dibao.
- Benefit: Contributions up to 12,000 yuan per year can be deducted from comprehensive income or business income. Investment gains in the account are temporarily exempt from individual income tax, and withdrawals are taxed separately at 3% rather than merged into comprehensive income. Annual tax savings equal the total annual tax owed before the deduction minus the total annual tax owed after the deduction. Because a deduction can cross into a lower tax bracket, you cannot simply multiply the current marginal tax rate by the contribution amount. The marginal tax rate is the tax rate applied to each additional yuan earned. For example, after other deductions, if annual taxable comprehensive income before contribution is 40,000 yuan, the tax owed is 1,480 yuan. After contributing and deducting 12,000 yuan, taxable income becomes 28,000 yuan, the tax owed drops to 840 yuan, saving 640 yuan that year. Assuming no investment gains and ignoring the time value of money, withdrawing this 12,000 yuan principal later incurs a 360 yuan tax, yielding a net tax difference of 280 yuan. Only if you contribute the full 12,000 yuan and the entire deduction falls within a single bracket can you use that bracket's rate to calculate that year's tax savings. Still counting principal only and ignoring returns and the time value of money, the net tax differences for the 10%, 20%, and 30% tax brackets are 840, 2,040, and 3,240 yuan, respectively. These are tax differentials occurring at different times; they cannot be treated as an investment return rate, much less divided by the number of years to calculate an annualized return. Those who originally pay no individual income tax gain no current tax savings, yet must still pay 3% upon withdrawal. For those only subject to the 3% bracket, the current tax savings do not exceed 3% of the contribution. If annual comprehensive income after all deductions does not exceed 36,000 yuan, it falls into this bracket. Counting only principal and ignoring the time value of money, these tax savings do not exceed the future tax on the principal, so one cannot claim it is worthwhile solely for tax deductions. Having income tax withheld from a monthly wage does not mean your full year falls in the 10% bracket. Annual contribution quotas accumulate by calendar year and unused quota cannot be carried forward. The individual pension system rolled out nationwide on December 15, 2024. Starting September 1, 2025, early withdrawals are also allowed for qualifying medical expenses, unemployment insurance benefit claims, and dibao status. Funds cannot be withdrawn at will before meeting withdrawal conditions, making it unsuitable for emergency savings (Nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:财政部、税务总局 (2024). 关于在全国范围实施个人养老金个人所得税优惠政策的公告（财政部 税务总局公告 2024 年第 21 号，2024 年 12 月 12 日）. <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c102416/c5237110/content.html>；人力资源社会保障部等五部门 (2022). 个人养老金实施办法（第七、八、九、十二、十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-11/05/content_5724783.htm>；全国人民代表大会常务委员会 (2018). 中华人民共和国个人所得税法（2018 年第七次修正，个人所得税税率表一）. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/zgrdw/npc/xinwen/2018-09/05/content_2060671.htm>；人力资源社会保障部等五部门 (2025). 关于领取个人养老金有关问题的通知（人社部发〔2025〕39 号，第一、二、三、七项）. <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100013/c5242402/content.html>
- Notes: Currently, there are six withdrawal conditions: reaching basic pension age, complete loss of working capacity, settling abroad, and the following three. For medical expenses, look at the eligible out-of-pocket medical expenses within the catalog after yibao (basic medical insurance) reimbursement incurred by the individual, spouse, or minor children in the 12 months before applying. The cumulative amount must exceed the provincial per capita disposable income of residents from the previous year to qualify. For unemployment, look at whether unemployment insurance benefits were received for a cumulative total of 12 months within the 2 years before applying. For dibao, the individual must be currently receiving urban or rural minimum living allowance. If any condition is met, apply for verification through the account-opening bank or the national social insurance public service platform. Funds already invested in products must also follow product redemption rules. Do not deposit emergency funds here (see Item 27 for emergency funds). Whether it is worth contributing also depends on the investment horizon, product yield, fees, and risk—do not decide based solely on tax savings. You must select products to invest in yourself, and some products can lose money. For product selection, see Items 17 and 18 of this chapter (broad-based index funds, prioritize lower fees). Deduction vouchers are issued by the individual pension information management service platform. Income subject to cumulative withholding, such as wages and salaries, can be deducted during withholding in the current year or during the annual tax reconciliation the following year. Other income is deducted during annual tax reconciliation the following year according to regulations (see Item 2 for tax reconciliation).

### 21. Pay for the gym per visit or on a short plan unless you have over a year of steady attendance
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Paying per visit costs a bit more per session than the amortized cost of an annual membership. Be honest with yourself about how many times a month you will actually go.
- In plain terms: In three years of US data, members who chose contracts with monthly fees over $70 went an average of only 4.3 times a month, which worked out to over $17 per visit. A 10-visit pass cost just $10 per visit. Over their membership, these people overpaid by an average of $600. Unless you already have a track record of going steadily for over a year, paying per visit or buying short-term passes is more cost-effective.
- Benefit: This study analyzed 3 years of data from 7,752 members across 3 US health clubs. Members who chose monthly contracts over $70 visited an average of only 4.3 times per month, coming out to over $17 per visit, whereas a 10-visit pass cost $10 per visit. Over their full membership, these members overpaid by an average of $600. In addition, monthly members were 17% more likely than annual members to remain enrolled past one year without canceling (US, three-year panel data).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:DellaVigna S, Malmendier U (2006). Paying Not to Go to the Gym. American Economic Review 96(3):694-719. <https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.3.694>
- Notes: This is just one study using US data, but the underlying reason is universal: people overestimate their future self-control. If you already have a track record of going twice a week or more for a full year, an annual membership is indeed cheaper.

### 22. Sign a written contract when paying upfront; businesses in trouble must stop collecting funds, and you have the right to your unspent balance before they vanish
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is insisting on a written contract on the spot instead of giving in to "just top up first and we'll sort it out later."
- In plain terms: If a business takes your prepaid money, it must sign a written contract clearly stating how refunds work. If it runs into serious operational trouble, it must stop taking advance payments, and it must notify you in advance if it plans to close down or relocate. You can demand either continued service or a refund of your unspent balance. Businesses that break these rules can be fined.
- Benefit: Regulations stipulate that businesses collecting advance payments must sign a written contract that specifies refund methods. When facing major operational risks, a business must stop collecting advance payments. If it decides to suspend business or relocate, it must give consumers advance notice. Consumers are entitled to demand continued performance or a refund of the unspent balance. Violators can be fined 1 to 10 times their illegal gains, or up to 500,000 RMB if there are no illegal gains.

- Notes: Regulations give you legal grounds for a dispute after the fact, but they cannot prevent fraud beforehand. Once a merchant disappears, the money in the account is usually already gone. ### 23. Set a 24-hour cooling-off period for large non-essential purchases and use 7-day unconditional returns for online shopping
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. You get what you want one day later. You usually have to pay return shipping yourself. The hard part is holding back from placing the order that day.

A few categories cannot be returned: custom-made orders, fresh perishables, unsealed digital products, and newspapers or magazines.
- Benefit: The savings come from impulse purchases that "you no longer want the next day"; there is no single standardized percentage for this share. Goods purchased online may be returned without a reason within 7 days from the date of receipt, with four exceptions: custom-made items, fresh and perishable goods, unsealed digital products, and newspapers or periodicals (China, enacted in 2017).

### 24. Do not stockpile for strikethrough prices and mega-sales
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing. The trade-off is that you might miss a few genuine bargains. You need to remember the regular prices of the items you buy most often. The hard part is holding back from stocking up on the day of a big sale.
- In plain terms: When people judge whether an item is expensive, their mind gets anchored to the first number they see, and any adjustment after that is almost never enough. That is why "was 999, now 499" makes you systematically overestimate how much of a bargain it is. Yet by official rules, the benchmark for any discount is only the lowest price an item actually sold for in that store over the seven days before the sale. That crossed-out original price may never have been paid by anyone.
- Benefit: Human judgment gets anchored by the first number seen, and later adjustments are usually insufficient. A claim of "was 999, now 499" systematically inflates estimates of how much is being saved. Regulations require that the benchmark for a price reduction must be the lowest actual transaction price at the same merchant within the 7 days prior to the promotion; discounts must be calculated against the lowest price actually sold during those 7 days. That strikethrough price might never have been genuinely charged (China, effective since 2020).
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:Tversky A, Kahneman D (1974). Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases. Science 185(4157):1124-1131. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2020). 规范促销行为暂行规定（总局令第 32 号，第二十一条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_cae53a080be2401e8f91c6d6291539f8.html>
- Notes: The anchoring effect is an established laboratory finding, but no study has measured the exact financial loss from consumer stockpiling. Hoarding also carries several unbudgeted costs: expired goods, wasted storage space, and the tendency to consume more simply because it is in the house. Stockpile only when an item meets all three criteria: a long shelf life, certain future use, and a unit price that is genuinely lower than previous regular prices.

### 25. Prioritize pure protection insurance and treat returns and dividends as non-guaranteed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Pure protection policies do not return any money when they expire, which can feel like "money wasted." You have to handle saving money yourself. The hard part is accepting that this money is gone for good.
- In plain terms: Insurance companies do not guarantee dividends or cash returns. Product brochures are legally required to state in bold that "future policy dividends are non-guaranteed benefits." The extra premium you pay for a cash return is essentially handed to the insurer to save on your behalf: returns are not guaranteed, and getting your money out is difficult.
- Benefit: Regulatory rules require brochures for participating insurance to state in bold text: "Future policy dividends are non-guaranteed benefits, and dividend distributions are uncertain." In dividend illustrations (the projection sheet the insurer shows you), the interest rate spread is capped at 4.5% minus the policy's assumed interest rate. The assumed rate is the fixed baseline return locked into the contract. The extra premiums paid for return-of-premium or participating products are essentially savings managed by the insurer: returns are not guaranteed, liquidity is poor, and withdrawing the money is hard (China, effective 2023).
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:中国银保监会 (2022). 一年期以上人身保险产品信息披露规则（银保监规〔2022〕24 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2023-01/04/content_5735014.htm>
- Notes: Controversy: for people who truly cannot save money, return-of-premium insurance forces regular contributions, which has practical value. The advice above applies to ordinary people who can manage their own money. Exactly how much better "buying pure protection and investing the saved premiums yourself" is versus "buying return-of-premium insurance" depends on the specific product; this section cites no systematic data. This does not constitute investment advice.

### 26. Buy enough commercial third-party liability insurance: compulsory auto liability limits are nationwide and low, and anything above them comes out of your own pocket
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Commercial third-party liability insurance costs several hundred to a few thousand yuan per year. The higher the coverage limit you buy, the cheaper the premium becomes per yuan of coverage.
- In plain terms: When you are at fault, compulsory auto liability insurance covers up to three categories: 180,000 yuan for death or disability, 18,000 yuan for medical expenses, and 2,000 yuan for property damage. The three caps are calculated separately and cannot be pooled or transferred. Compensation for hitting and injuring someone can easily exceed these limits, and whatever is left over comes out of your family's own pocket, so make sure to buy enough commercial third-party liability coverage.
- Benefit: Compulsory auto liability insurance enforces uniform liability limits nationwide. The limits are calculated across separate categories: death and disability, medical expenses, property damage, and compensation when you bear no fault. Under the comprehensive auto insurance reform of 2020, the current total liability limit is 200,000 yuan. Within this 200,000 yuan, death and disability is 180,000 yuan, medical expenses are 18,000 yuan, and property damage is 2,000 yuan. When you are not at fault, death and disability is 18,000 yuan, medical expenses are 1,800 yuan, and property damage is 100 yuan. Any amount exceeding the limits is borne by the tortfeasor personally, meaning the driver at fault must pay out of pocket. ### 36. Care about ingredients? Buy prepackaged: bulk food is not legally required to list them

- Cost: Free. For the same item, prepackaged is usually slightly more expensive than bulk. Glancing at an ingredient list takes a few seconds.
- In plain terms: By law, only prepackaged foods are required to show an ingredient list; bulk displays are not. Bulk food counters only need to show the product name, production date, shelf life, and the producer or seller's name, address, and contact information. So "no additives listed" does not mean "none added" — it only means the law did not require them to be printed. If you care about what is inside, choose the prepackaged version, where the full ingredient list and additive names must be printed on the package.

### 37. Sell losing stocks by preset rules instead of averaging down to break even
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is admitting the loss and actually selling.
- In plain terms: People tend to sell their winners and hold on to their losers. Across seven years of records from 10,000 US brokerage accounts, winning stocks were over 50% more likely to be sold than losing ones. The same investors were also far more prone to buying more of their losing stocks. Yet the losing stocks they kept lagged behind the winning stocks they sold by an average of 3.4 percentage points over the following year.
- Benefit: This study examined the complete trading records of 10,000 accounts at a US discount brokerage from 1987 to 1993. The proportion of gains realized (PGR) was 0.148, while the proportion of losses realized (PLR) was 0.098—a difference of 0.050 (t-statistic > 35). On a daily basis, winning stocks were more than 50% more likely to be sold than losing ones. The same accounts were also notably more active in adding to losing positions: the rate of repurchasing losers was 0.135 versus 0.094 for winners (t = 19). The study also tested the idea that held losers will bounce back. The results showed the opposite: over the subsequent year, the excess return of sold winning stocks exceeded that of retained losing stocks by an average of 3.4 percentage points (excess return refers to performance above the market index). Based on average values, the author also calculated an example: selling $1,000 of a losing stock instead of $1,000 of a winner produced roughly 4.4% higher returns over the following year. This 4.4% included tax savings from harvesting capital losses early under the US tax code (United States, 1987–1993).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Odean T (1998). Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses? The Journal of Finance 53(5):1775-1798. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072>
- Notes: This rule addresses whether to sell; item 15 (avoid frequent stock trading) addresses not trading constantly—the two do not conflict. In the author's experience, setting clear rules in advance and sticking to them actually results in fewer trades, though the study did not measure this directly. Averaging down by buying on dips is mathematically sound—buying more at lower prices does lower your average cost. The mistake lies in treating it as a way to turn things around. Its real effect is simply concentrating more money into a single stock (see item 19: do not concentrate money in a single stock). The tax-loss benefit was calculated under the US tax code and cannot be copied directly to China, but the rest of the findings remain valid. This reflects US account data from the 1980s and 1990s; transaction costs and tax structures differ in China's A-share market. Does not constitute investment advice.

### 39. Buy insurance only for losses you cannot bear, and cover the rest with an emergency fund
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. All it takes is half an hour to list the major financial shocks your household could face and see which ones your savings cannot absorb.
- In plain terms: You will never get all your premiums back in claims; part of that money pays for the insurer's overhead and profit. This means insurance is only worth buying for rare catastrophes your household could never afford out of pocket—like a critical illness, severely injuring someone in a car crash, or losing the family's breadwinner. Smaller losses your savings can easily handle should be left to an emergency fund.
- Benefit: Insurers pay out only a portion of the premiums they collect as claims. The rest covers sales, operations, and company profit. Regulators require insurers selling individual short-term health insurance to publish their "comprehensive payout ratio" on their official websites every six months. This roughly reflects the percentage of premium income returned as claims, so you can check an insurer's website to see how much it actually pays out. On average, buying insurance for any given loss is a slight net financial loss over the long run. In return, it converts a potentially ruinous lump-sum expense into a predictable, manageable annual fee. The smaller the loss and the easier you can pay it out of pocket, the less sense the trade-off makes. By this standard, several policies discussed in this book are worth buying: for one-year medical insurance, see section 7, item 20; for drivers, see item 26 of this section (third-party liability insurance); for travelers abroad, see section 21, item 4 (overseas medical care); for those with dependents relying on their income, see item 40 of this section (term life insurance) (nationwide, from 2021).

### 40. If family members rely on your income to live, buy term life insurance for the breadwinner first, not for your child
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A few hundred to several thousand yuan a year, depending on age, coverage amount, and policy duration (author's rough estimate, no official figures). Answering health disclosure questions truthfully before buying takes about half an hour once.
- In plain terms: Term life insurance only covers a set number of years; if you pass away during that period, your family receives a payout. It is meant to protect loved ones who depend on your income, such as when you still have a mortgage to pay off or your children are still young. If no one depends on you, you do not need it. Children do not earn an income, and the law strictly caps how much payout can be claimed on a death policy bought for a child.
- Benefit: Insurance regulators define life insurance as personal insurance where the insured person's lifespan is the subject matter of the policy, including term life insurance, whole life insurance, and endowment insurance. Term life insurance pays out benefits upon the death of the insured person, and the coverage period is a fixed number of years. Whole life insurance covers the insured person for their entire life. Accidental injury insurance only pays out when an accident leads to death or disability; it does not pay out for death caused by illness, so it cannot replace life insurance. A contract that pays out upon death requires the insured person's consent and agreement to the coverage amount, or else the contract is void. Parents buying death insurance for minor children are exempt from this restriction, but the total death benefit payable upon a child's death cannot exceed limits set by regulatory authorities. The coverage amount for life insurance is typically in the hundreds of thousands of yuan, which is why the benefit rating is set to large. There is no official standard for how much coverage to buy. The author's formula is: the remaining mortgage balance, plus the cost of raising the child to adulthood, plus several years of household living expenses (national, 2015 amendment)

### 41. Regret buying a multi-year life or health insurance policy? Cancel it during the cooling-off period for a near-full refund
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

For policies bought at a bank, it lasts 15 days after you receive the policy paperwork, during which cancelling costs at most a 10-yuan processing fee. If you cancel after this window closes, you only get back the policy's surrender cash value, which is usually far less than what you paid. When the insurance company calls you for the verification check to ask if everything is clear, do not say yes if you still have doubts.
- Benefit: For personal insurance with a coverage period exceeding one year, regulators require the contract to clearly state how the cooling-off period is counted, how many days it lasts, and what rights the policyholder holds during this window. Long-term health insurance must have a cooling-off period of no less than 15 days. For insurance with a term exceeding one year purchased through banks, contracts uniformly mandate a 15-day cooling-off period, counted in calendar days starting the day you receive and sign for the policy in writing. The cover of such policies must display this prompt: "You have the right to a full refund (with a processing fee of no more than 10 yuan deducted) within 15 days of receiving the insurance contract. Cancelling after 15 days will result in financial loss." Even after the cooling-off period expires, you can still terminate the contract at any time; the insurer must refund the policy's cash value within 30 days as stipulated in the contract. Official risk warnings from financial regulators emphasize that when surrendering a long-term personal insurance policy after the cooling-off period, the cash value is often substantially lower than the total premiums paid. For new policies with a term over one year, insurers are required to conduct a follow-up verification call during the cooling-off period to confirm whether you understand the financial loss of cancellation and your rights under the cooling-off period (National, effective 2010, 2019).

### 42. Check Whether It Is a Deposit or Insurance Before Signing at the Bank Counter
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just take two extra minutes to check the contract cover before signing.
- In plain terms: Banks are forbidden from pitching insurance as a deposit. Policy covers must display "Insurance Contract" and the insurer's name in large print, and the words "deposit" or "savings" are strictly prohibited. If an insurance company is named on the cover, it is not a bank deposit, and canceling after the cooling-off period means losing money. Customers over 65, those over 60 buying regular installment policies, or anyone earning below the local average disposable income can generally only be sold products with guaranteed returns.
- Benefit: Regulations stipulate that banks must not mix insurance sales with savings deposits, mutual funds, or wealth management products. ### 47. Inspect a new car upon delivery, keep the invoice and warranty certificate; request an exchange or refund if major defects appear in the first 7 or 60 days
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No monetary cost. Spend half an hour upon delivery inspecting the exterior and interior with the salesperson and checking accompanying documents. - In plain terms: If major components like the engine or transmission fail and need replacement within the first 7 days, you can directly request a vehicle exchange or refund. Within the first 60 days or 3,000 km, you can also exchange or return the car if you experience brake or steering failure, body cracking, fuel leaks, or battery fires. During the statutory warranty period, if the same defect cannot be resolved after multiple repairs, you are also eligible for an exchange or return, though you must pay a usage compensation fee based on the distance driven.
- Benefit: These rules are known as the Domestic Automobile "Three Guarantees" (repair, replacement, and return), covering passenger vehicles such as sedans, SUVs, and pickups purchased for personal living consumption, excluding commercial operations. The statutory warranty period (Three Guarantees period) is at least 2 years or 50,000 km, and the basic repair warranty period is at least 3 years or 60,000 km, whichever comes first. The term begins on the invoice date, or on the vehicle delivery date if the delivery date differs from the invoice date. During the repair warranty period, repairs for quality defects are free, including labor and material costs. Within the first 7 days, if quality defects require replacing the engine, transmission, power battery, drive motor, or their major components, you can choose an exchange or return with the invoice and warranty certificate. Within the first 60 days or 3,000 km, you can likewise choose an exchange or return for steering failure, brake failure, body cracking, fuel leaks, or power battery fires caused by quality defects. Here, n is specified by the manufacturer on the warranty certificate and cannot exceed 0.5%. If a single repair exceeds 5 days, starting from day 6, the dealer must provide a courtesy car or compensate for transportation expenses. Upon an exchange or return, the dealer must also reimburse vehicle registration fees, aftermarket modification and accessory costs after deducting depreciation, and any service fees charged by the dealer. After receiving an exchange or return request, the dealer must reply within 10 working days, providing written reasons if declining. - In plain terms: If a dealership claims taking out an auto loan requires an extra "financial service fee," regulations prohibit dealers from charging this. ### 50. If a tour guide takes you to an uncontracted shop and you buy goods, demand the travel agency process the return and advance the refund within 30 days of returning
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Keep the travel contract and itinerary before leaving, request invoices and payment card receipts when buying goods, and leave the items unopened and unused. After returning, contact the travel agency and ship the items back. If negotiations fail and you have to file a complaint, the whole process may drag on for 1 to 2 months.
- In plain terms: It is illegal for a travel agency to take you shopping without prior agreement, or to lure you into shopping stops using absurdly cheap tour fees. Within 30 days of returning, you can require the travel agency to handle the return and advance the refund out of its own pocket first. If you signed a supplementary "voluntary shopping" agreement, you are considered to have agreed, making this kind of refund much harder to obtain.
- Benefit: Article 35 of the Tourism Law has three paragraphs. The first paragraph states that travel agencies shall not organize tours at unreasonably low prices to lure tourists and profit from shopping kickbacks. The second paragraph states that travel agencies shall not designate specific shopping venues; visits are only permitted if both parties reach a consensus through negotiation, or if tourists request it without affecting other travelers' schedules. Signing a supplementary "voluntary shopping" agreement generally counts as mutual consensus. The third paragraph states that if either of the first two paragraphs is violated, tourists have the right, within 30 days after the end of the trip, to demand that the travel agency process the return and advance the refund payment. For separate paid activities, tourists can also demand a refund. This clause does not require you to prove quality defects first. Article 9 of the Tourism Law also stipulates that tourists have the right to refuse forced transactions. In 2025, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism reported a case: a Chengdu travel agency took 15 tourists to Jiuzhaigou, charging only 6,015 yuan in total tour fees. In 2024, the Guangdong Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism published a complaint: two tourists joined a Hong Kong-Macau-Zhuhai tour costing 800 yuan per person and bought over 20,000 yuan worth of jewelry and pillows at a duty-free shop. ### 51. Look up any bank-recommended investment on the bank's official app or website before buying—if it is not listed, the employee may be selling an unauthorized rogue product
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. It takes just a few minutes to search for the product name on the bank's app or website before buying. The hard part is telling a familiar relationship manager: "Let me check it myself first."
- In plain terms: When bank employees secretly sell third-party products that the bank never issued or authorized, it is called a "flying order" (a rogue off-the-books sale). Genuine products distributed by a bank can only be sold through its branches, official website, and app, and they can always be looked up there; if you cannot find it, do not buy it. If you get scammed by a rogue sale, some courts order the bank to cover half the loss. In another brokerage case, the customer was awarded nothing in the first trial and only received partial compensation on appeal.
- Benefit: According to the Beijing No. 3 Intermediate People's Court, a wealth management "flying order" occurs when financial institution staff take advantage of their position to privately sell third-party products that their institution never issued or authorized for distribution. Under 2025 regulations from the National Financial Regulatory Administration, banks distributing third-party products may only sell them within dedicated zones across their own branches, official websites, and apps. Banks must provide a lookup function on their official channels that clearly identifies the product as third-party distributed, its category, and its issuer. Banks are prohibited from commingling third-party distributed products with deposits or their own proprietary products. The front page of marketing materials must state: "This product is issued and managed by [Partner Institution]; as a distributor, [Bank Name] does not assume investment or redemption liabilities." Banks must formally authorize which sales personnel can sell which products and publicly post this authorization in branches. In-branch sales must be audio- and video-recorded. Banks and staff are prohibited from "privately recommending or selling unapproved products under the pretext of representing their institution" (Nationwide, effective October 1, 2025). The Beijing No. 3 Intermediate People's Court reported a case in 2022: in 2013, a bank relationship manager told a client that a certain product was the bank's hit product and that the bank served as the custodian. The client signed a partnership agreement with a fund company and transferred 1.2 million yuan to the fund company's account. The principal was never returned, and the actual controller of the fund company was imprisoned. The court held that the employee's pitch during working hours and at the workplace constituted conduct within the scope of employment. However, because the client one-sidedly chased high yields, they were also at fault. The first-instance court ordered the bank and the client to each bear half the loss, which was affirmed on appeal. In October 2026, the Supreme People's Court, the People's Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, and the CSRC jointly released a model case: the head of a securities branch office privately sold private equity fund shares that the branch had not authorized. The client invested 1 million yuan and sued in August 2023; the claim was dismissed in the first trial. On appeal, the court found fault on both sides and ordered the branch to pay approximately 577,000 yuan (exact value: 576,716.97 yuan), under a 2024 docket number (Nationwide, 2022, 2026).
