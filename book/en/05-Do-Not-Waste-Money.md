# 5. Don't Waste Money

This section's unit of account is money: savings percentages, annualized return differences, and expected value — not converted into lifespan or time. Entries involving investments discuss only the algorithms and verified historical data; nothing here constitutes investment advice.
### 1. Turn off every auto-renewal and switch to renewing manually when it expires
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Going through the "automatic deductions" lists in Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android takes 10 to 20 minutes once. From then on, every renewal costs you one extra tap.
- In plain terms: What you save is the subscriptions you stopped using long ago that keep charging you every month. One pass through the auto-deduction lists of Alipay, WeChat, Apple, and Android takes well under 20 minutes. Under the rules, a merchant must clearly remind you before charging, and must also give you an entry point for cancelling at any time — one that is easy to find and easy to tap.
- Benefit: The money saved equals the sum of the subscriptions you no longer use but are still being charged for; it differs from person to person, and there is no single figure. Regulators require merchants to remind you prominently before a renewal. Merchants must also give you a "simple, cancel-anytime" option, and cancelling costs nothing extra (China, in force since 2021)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2021, 2025 修正). 网络交易监督管理办法（总局令第 37 号，第十八条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Notes: For services you genuinely use often, paying annually is usually cheaper than paying monthly, and keeping auto-renewal is actually the better deal. This unit targets the other kind: the one you forgot about long ago that is still charging you.

### 2. Do your annual individual income tax reconciliation between March and June, and fill in every special additional deduction you qualify for
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. You do it in the Individual Income Tax app, and a dozen or so minutes is enough.
- In plain terms: The people who usually get money back are those who worked only a few months of the year, changed jobs mid-year, or never filled in deductions like children's education, mortgage, rent, or supporting elderly parents. It takes a dozen or so minutes on your phone. If your annual comprehensive income is under 120,000 yuan, or the tax you owe is under 400 yuan, you can skip it.
- Benefit: Wages and salaries, labor remuneration, author's remuneration, and royalties are added together and settled in one pass. What you overpaid is refunded; what you underpaid you make up. Two situations let you skip it. One: annual comprehensive income not exceeding 120,000 yuan. Two: tax due not exceeding 400 yuan. Both were set by a 2023 announcement of the State Taxation Administration and run through December 31, 2027. The other way around, these people usually get money back: those with income in only a few months of the year, those who changed jobs mid-year, and those who left deductions like children's education, mortgage, rent, or supporting elderly parents unfilled
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家税务总局 (2025). 个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴管理办法（第六条）及其解读. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm>；财政部、税务总局 (2023). 关于延续实施个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴有关政策的公告. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Notes: If you owe tax you must still file; not filing is against the law. Special additional deductions come to seven items: children's education, continuing education, major medical expenses, housing loan interest, housing rent, supporting elderly parents, and care of infants under 3. Of these, housing loan interest and housing rent are either-or: you can fill in only one.

### 3. Use your housing provident fund for more than buying a home: rent, renovation, and property fees all qualify for withdrawal, and from September 20, 2026 the revised regulation applies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One withdrawal application is all it takes.
- In plain terms: The housing provident fund is not something you can touch only when buying a home. The revised regulation lists nine situations where you can withdraw, and renting, renovating the home you live in, and paying property fees are all among them. Once you file the application, the fund center must answer yes or no within 3 days.
- Benefit: The revised regulation lists nine permitted withdrawal situations, including paying rent, renovating an owner-occupied home, and paying property fees for an owner-occupied home. The fund management center must decide whether to approve the withdrawal within 3 days of the day it accepts your application
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2026). 国务院关于修改《住房公积金管理条例》的决定（国务院令第 844 号，2026 年 7 月 31 日国务院第 93 次常务会议通过，「自2026年9月20日起施行」）：修改后第二十四条「职工有下列情形之一的，可以提取职工住房公积金账户内的存储余额：（一）支付房租的；（二）购买、建造、翻建、大修自住住房的；（三）偿还购房贷款本息的；（四）装修自住住房的；（五）支付自住住房物业费的；（六）离休、退休的；（七）完全丧失劳动能力，并与单位终止劳动关系的；（八）出境定居的；（九）国务院批准的其他住房消费情形。」；第二十五条「住房公积金管理中心应当自受理申请之日起3日内作出准予提取或者不准提取的决定」；第十八条缴存比例「均不得低于职工上一年度月平均工资的5％」. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>
- Notes: The money in the account is your own wages plus the matching part your employer puts in. Left sitting, it earns interest only at a very low rate. The regulation also states that if a contributor dies or is declared dead, heirs and legatees may withdraw the account balance. Which documents to bring and the exact procedure are set by each local fund management center — check your local center's service guide before making the trip.

### 4. Recalculate your mobile and broadband plans once a year, downgrade the ones you do not use, and complain if the carrier will not let you switch
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Checking one bill takes a dozen or so minutes. The hard part is standing up to customer service when they refuse to downgrade you.
- In plain terms: The old plan you are on is often tens of yuan a month more expensive than what is on sale now. Tens a month is several hundred yuan a year thrown away for nothing. Under the rules, a carrier with no valid reason may not refuse to port your number, may not drag the process out, and may not set up a separate tariff just for people who port in. If it holds your number hostage, complain.
- Benefit: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology rules state that carriers may not "refuse, obstruct, or delay providing number portability services to users without a valid reason," may not "use technical means such as interception or restriction to affect the service quality of number-portability users," and may not "set special tariff plans and marketing schemes for number-portability users." Old plans are often tens of yuan more expensive than the ones currently on sale; never switching means throwing away several hundred yuan a year
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 工业和信息化部 (2019). 携号转网服务管理规定（工信部信管〔2019〕242 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Notes: First check in the carrier's app how much data and call time you actually used over the past six months, and pick a plan based on that usage. If customer service says "downgrades must be done at a store" or "you cannot change during a contract," demand written justification. If they cannot produce it, file a complaint by calling 12300.

### 5. Do not buy lottery tickets
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Not buying is the saving.
- In plain terms: Under the rules, the money lottery-number games must pay out as prizes is at least 50% of sales, and for the Double Color Ball it is 51%. So for every 1 yuan you bet, the long-run average return is 0.51 yuan — a steady loss of 49%. In 2024, 623.486 billion yuan of tickets were sold nationwide, of which 161.031 billion yuan went straight into the public welfare fund and never came back as prizes.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Finance sets the minimum share of sales paid out as prizes: 50% for lottery and number games, 65% for instant tickets, 73% for sports betting. The Double Color Ball accrues prizes at 51% of sales — for every 100 yuan sold, only 51 yuan goes back as prizes. In your terms: every 1 yuan bet returns 0.51 yuan on long-run average, a long-run average loss of 49%. In 2024, national lottery sales were 623.486 billion yuan, of which 161.031 billion yuan went into the public welfare fund and never reached the people who bought the tickets (China, 2024)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 财政部 (2015). 关于规范和加强彩票资金构成比例管理的通知（财综〔2015〕94 号）. <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm>；财政部 (2025). 财政部公告 2025 年第 8 号（2024 年彩票公益金筹集分配情况）. <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm>；中国福利彩票双色球游戏规则（第十四条）. <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Notes: Losing on long-run average is fixed by the system's design; it is not a question of luck. Buying anyway is fine, but only as a 2-yuan entertainment expense, never as investing.

### 6. Walk away the moment you hear "high yield", "guaranteed principal", or "sure profit"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. You only need to pause when you hear those words.
- In plain terms: In the regulator's own words: a yield above 6% deserves a question mark, above 8% is very dangerous, and above 10% you should be ready to lose your entire principal. Proper wealth-management products are no longer allowed to promise principal protection or guaranteed returns. Money lost in illegal fundraising is, by rule, borne by you alone; when it blows up, nobody covers for you.
- Benefit: The words of the then-chairman of the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission at the 2018 Lujiazui Forum, in the original: "yields above 6% deserve a question mark, above 8% are very dangerous, and above 10% you should be prepared to lose all your principal." The new asset-management rules require that financial institutions "must not promise principal protection or guaranteed returns," and that "when redemption difficulties arise, they must not advance funds to honor payments in any form." That is, proper wealth-management no longer comes with principal protection. Losses from taking part in illegal fundraising are "borne by the fundraising participants themselves." When it blows up, nobody covers for you (China, 2018 and 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 郭树清在第十届陆家嘴论坛的讲话（2018 年 6 月），天津市地方金融管理局转载. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html>；中国人民银行等 (2018). 关于规范金融机构资产管理业务的指导意见（银发〔2018〕106 号，第二条、第六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5323101.htm>；国务院 (2021). 防范和处置非法集资条例（国务院令第 737 号，第二十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>
- Notes: The 6%, 8%, and 10% lines are rules of thumb drawn by the regulator, not legal provisions. ### 7. Do not pay the credit card minimum, and do not take installments or consumer loans for spending
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. You need to keep enough cash before the statement due date to pay in full. The hard part is resisting the urge to spread a big expense out in installments.
- In plain terms: The minimum payment is not a perk; it is a loan with interest counted by the day, which works out to about 18% a year. Use it, and the interest-free period is voided on the spot: the unpaid part of your principal accrues interest from beginning to end. When you look at a rate, trust only the annualized figure. The central bank already requires card issuers to display the annualized rate prominently and forbids them from writing only a "daily rate."
- Benefit: Before 2020, credit card overdraft rates had both a cap and a floor: the cap was "a daily rate of 5 in 10,000," and the floor was 0.7 times the cap. Five in 10,000 a day works out to 18.25% a year, and that is simple interest — the interest does not roll back into the principal. From 2021 this changed to pricing negotiated between the issuer and the cardholder. After a minimum payment, interest accrues daily on the full unpaid principal — every day the money is earning interest against you — and the interest-free period lapses at the same time. The central bank already requires issuers to display the annualized rate, the rate worked out over a full year, in a prominent way, and "not to display only the daily rate or the daily repayment amount." Look at that annualized number before you decide (China, 2020 notice)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 中国人民银行 (2020). 关于推进信用卡透支利率市场化改革的通知（银发〔2020〕327 号）. <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Notes: The 18.25% comes from multiplying "5 in 10,000 × 365" straight out, with no compounding; compounded monthly it would be higher still. Some banks offer discounts — go by the annualized rate written on your own statement. When you genuinely cannot cover the bill, borrow first through a legitimate channel with a lower rate, instead of letting minimum payments roll the interest along.

### 8. Do not tip streamers, do not top up games, do not impulse-buy
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes for three things: turn off password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, and delete the payment methods linked in the platforms. The hard part is holding back from the tap in the moment.
- In plain terms: In 2024, domestic game revenue was 325.783 billion yuan from 674 million users — about 480 yuan per person. Money spent is basically unrecoverable. The law supports only one kind of refund: what a minor spent without a parent's consent. Adults have no such clause. The cheapest defense is to set up barriers in advance: turn off password-free payment, set per-transaction and daily limits, and unlink the payment methods.
- Benefit: This is a large block of spending. In 2024 the domestic game market's actual sales revenue was 325.783 billion yuan from 674 million users, about 480 yuan per person (this is the first two figures divided). Money spent is basically unrecoverable. There is one exception: for tipping and top-ups by persons with limited civil capacity — that is, minors — made without their guardian's consent, when the guardian demands the money back "the people's court shall support" the claim. For adults' own tipping and top-ups there is no such provision. On top of that, minors were already allowed online games only on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays, for the 1 hour from 8 to 9 p.m. each day (China, 2020 and 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家新闻出版署 (2024). 今年中国游戏市场收入超 3257 亿元创新高（中国音数协游戏工委《2024 年中国游戏产业报告》）. <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/ywdt/202412/t20241216_877445.html>；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于依法妥善审理涉新冠肺炎疫情民事案件若干问题的指导意见（二）（法发〔2020〕17 号，第 9 条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/230181.html>；国家新闻出版署 (2021). 关于进一步严格管理 切实防止未成年人沉迷网络游戏的通知（国新出发〔2021〕14 号）. <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/tzgs/202108/t20210830_666285.html>
- Notes: Graded B because nobody has directly measured "how much not tipping saves"; there are only the game industry's aggregate numbers. Turning off password-free payment and setting limits are rules of thumb, graded C on their own. The original text of Articles 19 and 145 of the Civil Code is quoted word for word in unit 9 of this chapter (children topping up and tipping on phones). Not investment advice.

### 9. When a child tops up or tips on a phone: for large sums spent over age eight without parental ratification, you can claim a refund
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. It does take time: gathering evidence and negotiating with the platform. Platforms usually refuse first and refund later. If talk fails, only a lawsuit is left, which starts at several months, and the lawyer fees come out of your pocket. So get the materials ready first — the platform and 12315 route is the better deal. The hard part is that the child will not want to tell you, and you have to ask patiently and get the story straight.
- In plain terms: Money spent by a child over eight is invalid if the amount is clearly out of proportion to the child's age and intelligence and the parents neither consented nor accept the charge — and you can demand it back. Children under eight cannot make spending decisions on their own in the first place. If the parents never take a position, the law counts that as refusing to accept the transaction.
- Benefit: Minors over eight years of age are persons with limited civil capacity, meaning they can do on their own only what suits their age and intelligence. A civil juristic act out of proportion to their age and intelligence takes the consent or ratification of their statutory agent to be valid — a parent's advance approval, or acceptance after the fact. Children under eight are persons without civil capacity and cannot decide anything themselves; their affairs are handled by the statutory agent on their behalf. If the statutory agent says nothing, this is deemed a refusal to ratify — the parents staying silent counts as not accepting the charge
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典. 最高人民检察院转载全文. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> 第十九条：「八周岁以上的未成年人为限制民事行为能力人，实施民事法律行为由其法定代理人代理或者经其法定代理人同意、追认；但是，可以独立实施纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力相适应的民事法律行为。」第二十条：「不满八周岁的未成年人为无民事行为能力人，由其法定代理人代理实施民事法律行为。」第一百四十五条：「限制民事行为能力人实施的纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力、精神健康状况相适应的民事法律行为有效；实施的其他民事法律行为经法定代理人同意或者追认后有效。」「法定代理人未作表示的，视为拒绝追认。」
- Notes: Everything hinges on evidence: you have to show it was the child operating the phone, not you. Useful materials: top-ups clustered during school hours or the hours parents are at work, the device's usage records, the child's chat and game records, and the child's own account of what happened. There is no uniform money line for "suited to age and intelligence"; a game skin costing a few dozen yuan and tipping worth tens of thousands are not the same thing. Blocking it in advance is cheaper: set a password-free payment cap on your payment methods, turn on youth mode, and never tell the child the payment password. For the side of this where you yourself tip and top up on impulse, see unit 8 of this chapter (do not tip streamers, do not top up games, do not impulse-buy).

### 10. Teach your child one sentence: anyone who tells you to take a parent's phone and operate it, or to read out a verification code, is a scammer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Walk your child through the six categories below once, and agree on a phrase: "if you get scammed, tell me first — I will not scold you." At the same time set a password-free payment cap, turn on youth mode, and keep the payment password from the child. The hard part is holding back your anger after a scam has actually happened.
- In plain terms: The six scam formats differ in every detail except the last step, which is always the same action: getting the child to transfer money on a parent's phone or read out a verification code. So one sentence is enough: whoever tells you to touch a parent's phone or read out a code is a scammer. Add "if you get scammed, tell me first — I will not scold you." Only if the child dares to say it the same day is there a chance of getting the money back.
- Benefit: The Quanzhou anti-fraud center, together with the Quanzhou education bureau, took apart six categories of scams aimed at teenagers. The setups differ, but the last step is the same: making the child transfer money on a parent's phone. Category one: cheap off-platform private trades of game accounts and gear. Xiao Wang privately transferred 260 yuan; the other side said "transfers by minors violate the rules and the account will be frozen," and using his father's phone he clicked a "verification link" — more than 30,000 yuan was drained from the account. Category two: "removing the anti-addiction limit." The scammer threatened "pay, or it is cancelled and your parents go to jail," and the child transferred 7,000 yuan on his mother's phone. The source states that the anti-addiction system is an official setting and that "there is no such thing as a 'private unlock' channel." Category three: tipping refunds. Posing as "official customer service," the scammer said minors' tips can be refunded and demanded "sending gifts in a designated livestream room from a parent's Alipay to process the refund"; 73,000 yuan was tipped away from the mother's account and she was then blocked. The source says: "if someone adds you and asks you to 'send gifts for a refund', it is 100% a scam." Category four: impersonating classmates or teachers. Having stolen an account, the scammer sends a voice message giving the name and asking to borrow money. Around the start of the school term they slip into class group chats posing as the teacher and demand "500 yuan in school fees," with the same avatar and nickname as the real teacher, and plant shills in the group saying "already paid." Category five: celebrity fandom. The child is pulled into a "star's fan group, claim an 888 yuan red packet," first "verified" with a 48.88 yuan transfer, then frightened: "a minor taking part is a violation, the platform will freeze 80,000 and your parents go to jail." The total taken was 16,000 yuan. The source says: "real police and lawyers will never contact a minor online for an investigation, let alone ask to operate a parent's phone." Category six: second-hand platforms, where a low-priced item lures the buyer off the platform into a private transfer. The same source lists three red lines the children themselves must not cross. One: do not lend, sell, or rent out bank cards or phone cards — if a card is used to move criminal proceeds, this can constitute the crime of assisting information network criminal activity, and a record affects graduate school and civil-service exams. Two: "phone-port" side gigs are a crime. Three: "high-paying jobs abroad" — a person can be held the moment they arrive overseas (nationwide, October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 泉州市反诈骗中心 (2025). 开学第一课｜反诈是青少年的必修课，这 6 大陷阱一定要避开！<http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202510/t20251028_7026836.htm>（福建省公安厅转载）
- Notes: Graded B because the authorities provide breakdowns of scam scripts and individual cases; there are no incidence rates and no statistics on how well prevention works. Keep this apart from unit 9 of this chapter (children topping up and tipping on phones). Money a child spent on top-ups and tips themselves can be claimed back under the Civil Code. Money a child was scammed into transferring does not fall under that rule: the only move is to report to the police for payment interception, as in unit 2 of chapter 8, and whether it can be recovered depends on whether the money is still in the account. The scammer's core line for frightening a child is "you broke the rules and your parents go to jail." Telling the child in advance that this very phrase is the mark of a scam works better than questioning afterwards. The promise "tell me first and I will not scold you" has to be actually kept; a child hiding the loss out of fear of being scolded is the common reason the losses in these cases kept growing. For the adult version of impersonating acquaintances, and for AI face-swapping, see units 3 and 4 of chapter 8 (hard anti-fraud rules; hang up first when a transfer is involved).

### 11. Decline the extended warranty on electronics
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The price is that if a repair is needed after the warranty runs out, you pay out of your own pocket — though the odds of that are small.
- In plain terms: Extended warranties typically sell for 10% to 50% of the product's original price. The seller's margin on them is more than ten times the margin on ordinary goods. In 2003 Best Buy in the United States earned more than half its profits from extended warranties, which made up only 3% to 4% of its revenue. That gap is the extra money you are paying.
- Benefit: Extended warranties are usually priced at 10% to 50% of the product's original price. Industry estimates cited in the research put the average gross margin at 50% to 60% — of every 100 yuan taken in, 50 to 60 are profit. That is roughly 18 times the gross margin on ordinary goods. In 2003, extended warranties made up only 3% to 4% of Best Buy's revenue yet contributed more than 50% of its profit (United States, 2000s retail data)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611-623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Notes: The gross margin figure is an industry estimate the paper cites from someone else (Business Week 2004), not something the paper measured itself. This unit is for ordinary people who can absorb one repair bill out of pocket. Someone who lives off a single device with no backup needs to calculate separately. Phone screen-crack insurance also needs its own arithmetic: first estimate how often you actually drop your phone, then check whether the premium pays off.

### 12. Prefer generics that passed consistency evaluation and drugs selected in centralized procurement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. When the doctor prescribes or you buy the drug, ask one extra question: "is there a centralized-procurement version, a generic-name version?"
- In plain terms: The second centralized-procurement round cut prices by 53% on average, with the biggest single cut at 93%. By April 2026, 11 rounds had been run, covering 490 drugs. ### 19. Do not stake your money on one stock, one platform, or one property
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is accepting one thing: you will not happen to hit the single biggest earner.
- In plain terms: As long as a few assets do not rise and fall in perfect lockstep, spreading the money across them leaves your average earnings unchanged while the account's swings get smaller. The other way around: money all on one stock, one platform, or one property — when it fails, all of you fails.
- Benefit: As long as several assets are not perfectly correlated, spreading the money across them leaves expected return unchanged and reduces volatility — meaning your average earnings stay the same and the account's swings get smaller. This is the basic conclusion of portfolio choice put forward by Markowitz in 1952, used ever since. The other way around: with all the money on one stock or one platform, its variance is all of your variance, and it moves you move. When it fails there is no hedge, nothing to fill the hole back up
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Notes: Graded B because the classic paper is pure mathematical derivation, with no actual figure for "how much diversification saves". Diversification only makes the account's swings smaller; it does not guarantee you will not lose money. Buying a broad-based index fund (see unit 17 of this chapter) is itself the least-effort form of diversification. Not investment advice.

### 22. Sign a written contract when paying in advance; when a merchant turns risky it must stop collecting — and before it runs off you have the right to recover the balance you have not spent
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

### 24. Do not stockpile over "strikethrough prices" and big promotions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The price is that some genuine discounts pass you by. You have to remember the everyday prices of the few things you buy regularly. The hard part is not stockpiling on promotion day.
- In plain terms: People judge whether something is expensive by the first number they see, and whatever adjusting they do afterwards is never enough. So "was 999, now 499" systematically makes you overestimate how cheap it is. And under the rules, the baseline a discount must be measured against is only the lowest transaction price in that store during the seven days before the promotion. The struck-through price was not necessarily ever charged.
- Benefit: Judgment anchors on the first number seen, and the adjustment afterwards usually falls short. So "was 999, now 499" systematically inflates your estimate of how much you are saving. The regulator rules that the basis for a discount should be the lowest transaction price of the same store within the seven days before the promotion. That is, the size of a discount is measured against the lowest price at which that store actually sold the item in the seven days before the promotion. In other words, the struck-through price was not necessarily ever really charged (China, in force since 2020)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Tversky A, Kahneman D (1974). Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases. ### 26. Buy enough third-party liability coverage: compulsory insurance limits are uniform nationwide and not high, and anything beyond comes out of your own household
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Commercial third-party liability insurance runs a few hundred to over a thousand yuan a year. The higher the coverage you buy, the cheaper the premium per unit of coverage turns out to be.
- In plain terms: When the fault is yours, compulsory insurance pays at most these three items: 180,000 yuan for death and disability, 18,000 yuan for medical expenses, 2,000 yuan for property damage. The three are counted separately and cannot be shifted into each other, and together they cap out at 200,000 yuan. Injure one person, and medical bills plus lost income can easily blow past the 18,000-yuan item. If someone really dies, the cost goes far beyond 180,000. The excess comes out of your own household, so buy enough commercial third-party liability coverage.
- Benefit: Compulsory insurance applies uniform liability limits nationwide. The limits are counted separately across four items: death and disability, medical expenses, property damage, and what is paid when you are not at fault. After the 2020 comprehensive auto insurance reform, the current total liability limit is 200,000 yuan. Within that 200,000, death and disability get 180,000, medical expenses 18,000, and property damage 2,000. When you are not at fault, the figures are 18,000 for death and disability, 1,800 for medical expenses, and 100 for property damage. Amounts beyond the limits are borne by the tortfeasor personally — that is, by the side that caused the injury. A vehicle without compulsory insurance is detained, and the fine is 2 times the premium due

### 28. Before prepaying your mortgage, run one comparison first; do not go by feel
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Half an hour of arithmetic. You have to honestly estimate the stable after-tax return you can actually get.
- In plain terms: Prepaying buys you a certain return, and its value equals your mortgage rate. So you only need to answer one question: is the after-tax return you can reliably earn over the long run higher than your mortgage rate? If yes, do not rush to prepay; if no, or you cannot say for sure, prepay. With equal installments, interest takes a large share of the early payments, so the earlier you repay the more you save. The bank app's "prepayment calculator" shows the numbers directly.
- Benefit: The arithmetic is this. First let the mortgage's annual rate be r, per your contract. On August 20, 2026, the 5-year-plus LPR was 3.5%, and the rate actually charged is the LPR plus or minus a margin. Then let g be the annualized after-tax return of the alternative investment you could pick instead — the rate this money would earn on average per year somewhere else. The certain return on prepayment is r: every sum you repay is r earned for certain. If g stays above r, the money is better off left in the investment. If g is below r, or g simply cannot be pinned down, then prepaying is the same as locking in a risk-free r, with no risk taken to get it. With equal installments, interest takes a large share of the early payments — in the first years, most of what you pay in is interest — so the earlier you repay, the more interest you save. See the numbers directly in the bank app's "prepayment calculator" (China, 2026 rates)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 全国银行间同业拆借中心受中国人民银行授权 (2026). 2026 年 8 月 20 日贷款市场报价利率（LPR）公告. <https://www.chinamoney.com.cn/chinese/rdgz/20260820/3399885.html>
- Notes: Only the arithmetic is given here, not a conclusion. Cases where investment returns reliably beat the mortgage rate are the minority. Whichever side you choose, first keep enough emergency cash on hand. Also check the contract for a prepayment penalty clause. Not investment advice.

### 31. Buy unsafe food and, beyond a refund, you can claim ten times the price — and if the added compensation is under 1,000 yuan, it is paid as 1,000
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

Go to the seller or the platform first, and call 12315 if the talk goes nowhere. If it really ends up in court, that takes months. The hard part is holding back from throwing the stuff out first.
- In plain terms: If you are sold food that is expired, spoiled, or has a foreign object in it, the food itself, the packaging, and the receipt are your entire evidence, so do not rush to throw them away. The law gives you compensation of "ten times the price or three times your loss," and where the added amount is under 1,000 yuan it is paid as 1,000. So for a pack of expired food that cost a few yuan, what you are really negotiating over is that 1,000 yuan. Whether to go after the seller or the manufacturer is your choice, and whoever you approach has to pay first and may not push you onto the other party.
- Benefit: If food that does not meet food safety standards has caused you harm, you can claim damages from the business that sold it and equally from the producer. First, three conditions must all hold. The buyer bought for personal or household consumption needs. The food does not meet food safety standards. For repeated purchases and repeated lawsuits, the total across all the purchases is assessed within that same single range. Third, courts do not support punitive damages for several kinds of defects, provided they do not give consumers a false impression about food safety. ### 33. Count beads, jade, luxury watches, and designer toys as money spent, not money saved
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just leave "it will appreciate" out of your reasons for buying it. The hard part is not raising the budget for the sake of "holding value".
- In plain terms: The moment you buy one of these things, a slice of tax has already been taken out of the price. Watches with a price of 10,000 yuan or more each, excluding VAT, are taxed 20% consumption tax as "high-end watches". Gold, silver, and platinum jewelry and diamonds are taxed 5%; other precious jewelry, gems, and jade 10%. VAT comes on top of that. None of this money comes back when you resell the piece to the next person. If you truly like something, buy it as consumption; do not book it as an investment.
- Benefit: Start with the buying side. The consumption tax items and rates table sets the rates like this. "High-end watches": 20%. Within "precious jewelry, gems, and jade": gold and silver jewelry, platinum jewelry, diamonds and diamond products 5%; other precious jewelry and gems and jade 10%. "High-end watches" is defined as "all watches with a selling price (excluding VAT) of 10,000 yuan (inclusive) or more each". Now the selling side, under the administrative measures for pawning. Article 36: a pawn period may not exceed 6 months. Article 37 covers the pawn-loan interest rate, that is, the interest on the money the pawnshop lends you. That interest is converted from the statutory 6-month loan rate published by the People's Bank of China over the length of the pawn. Interest may not be deducted in advance, that is, it cannot be skimmed off the money before it is handed to you. Article 38 covers pawn with a chattel pledge, that is, pledging a watch or a piece of jade. For that kind of pawn the monthly comprehensive fee rate may not exceed 42‰ of the loan; in other words, the comprehensive fee charged each month on top cannot exceed 4.2% of the amount borrowed. Real estate mortgages are capped at 27‰ and pledges of property rights at 24‰. Article 40: if within 5 days after the term ends you neither redeem nor renew, the pawn becomes a dead pawn, and the item will no longer wait for you to redeem it. Article 43: a dead-pawn item appraised at 30,000 yuan or more may be consigned to auction. Auction proceeds first cover the auction fees and the loan principal and interest; the remainder is returned to the pawner, and any shortfall is still pursued against the pawner (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2008). 中华人民共和国消费税暂行条例（国务院令第 539 号，附《消费税税目税率表》）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-11/14/content_1795.htm>；财政部、国家税务总局 (2006). 关于调整和完善消费税政策的通知（财税〔2006〕33 号）. <http://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/zhengcefabu/2006zcfb/200805/t20080524_34758.htm>；商务部、公安部 (2005). 典当管理办法（2005 年第 8 号令，第三十六、三十七、三十八、四十、四十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2005/content_93074.htm>
- Notes: The pawnshop route needs clear arithmetic: the monthly comprehensive fee is capped at 42‰ of the loan, which over a year comes to roughly half the loan amount, and the interest on the loan is charged separately. That "half in a year" figure is the monthly fee rate multiplied straight by 12, a rough number meant to show the scale, not a precise annual rate. The tax rates and the fee rates are numbers written in black and white. But whether "these things appreciate over the long run" has no official statistics to cite; this book makes no forecasts and does not rate any category. What this unit is trying to stop is only the part of the reasoning that dresses consumption up as investment: liking something and enjoying wearing it, buying it as consumption is fine; raising the budget because "it holds value" or "I can sell it for more later" is where the money gets lost. Gold has two halves too: investment bars traded by gram weight and jewelry sold by the piece are not the same thing. The price of a piece of jewelry includes design and workmanship fees, and buyback quotes usually do not count that part. Asking prices on second-hand platforms are not transaction prices; do not read them as the market.

### 34. For gems and jade accept only test reports with the CMA mark, and check the lab on the certifying authority's website

- Cost: Free. Before ordering, check the accreditation of the lab that issued the certificate once; a few minutes is enough.
- In plain terms: A certificate itself can be forged, so what really needs checking is the lab that issued it. Before a testing lab can issue reports to the public it must obtain qualification accreditation, and its reports must carry the CMA mark. The accrediting authority must publish the list of accredited labs and their certificate status on its official website, so you can check yourself whether the lab is on it. In the 2026 "counterfeit clean-up" cases, some of the "national inspection certificates" that came with the goods had been made by people the sellers hired, and even the signboards were fake.
- Benefit: The rules first, from the measures for the administration of qualification accreditation of inspection and testing institutions. Article 2: qualification accreditation is the review-and-license applied to inspection and testing institutions "that issue data and results with probative effect to society" — the lab is vetted first and only then given the qualification to issue certificates. Article 15: the qualification accreditation mark consists of the CMA emblem and the qualification accreditation certificate number. Article 21: an institution that issues data or results with probative effect to society must mark the qualification accreditation mark on its inspection and testing report. Article 22: the accreditation department must publish on its official website the information of institutions that have obtained accreditation, noting the status of each certificate. Article 34: issuing data or results with probative effect to society without accreditation as required by law draws a fine of 30,000 yuan. Three: a shop in Zhenping, Henan, made its own display board for use in livestreams, reading "GIA CMA Zhongyu Gem and Jade Appraisal Center, certified cooperation unit". - Sources: 国家质量监督检验检疫总局 (2015 公布，2021 修改). 检验检测机构资质认定管理办法（第二、十五、二十一、二十二、三十四、三十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2021-04/22/content_5723677.htm>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2026). 市场监管总局公布一批传统工艺市场「打假清源」典型案例. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/zj/art/2026/art_1529399cfe874b8780c4496eb0158020.html>；国家标准化管理委员会. ### 35. Treat blind boxes, card pulls, and other randomized sales as money that will not come back
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Just fix the budget before you buy. The hard part is stopping when the pull you wanted did not come.
- In plain terms: The rules give you the right to know, not the right to a second chance. Sellers must publish the draw rules, the item distribution, the draw probabilities, and the release quantities of limited items prominently, and they may not manipulate results from the back end or set up empty boxes. But "floor guarantees, spending caps, and pull caps" are only "encouraged" in the document, not mandatory. For blind boxes bought online, breaking the seal is the last moment you can change your mind.
- Benefit: The basis is the Guidelines for Regulating Blind Box Business Conduct (Trial). Article 9 lists the key information that must be published prominently so that consumers know it before buying: item name, category, styles, draw rules, item distribution, release quantities of limited items, draw probabilities, and the value range of the items. The same article sets three things that are forbidden. One: no covertly steering purchases by manipulating draw results from the back end or adjusting probabilities at will. Two: no refusing or deliberately delaying delivery of blind boxes by offering cash conversion, buybacks, or exchanges instead. Three: no empty boxes. Article 11 only "encourages" operators to build floor mechanisms, guiding rational consumption by setting caps on draw time, draw amount, and number of draws. Article 17 governs blind box items sold over the internet. Where the operator has fully informed and warned the consumer and the consumer confirmed at the time of that single purchase, such a blind box can be exempt from the seven-day no-reason return once it is unsealed. Operators may not replace the consumer's confirmation with a pre-ticked box. Article 21: no gambling, direct or disguised, in the name of blind boxes. Article 23: blind boxes may not be sold to minors under 8; selling to those 8 and older requires confirming, as the law demands, that the relevant guardian's consent has been obtained (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 市场监管总局 (2023). 盲盒经营行为规范指引（试行）（国市监稽发〔2023〕39 号，第九、十一、十七、二十一、二十三条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/zfjcs/art/2023/art_e2facd76a7fb4e90b03912cffb5e7d53.html>
- Notes: This document is a guideline: it governs how operators should behave and is not itself a basis for penalties. ### 36. If you care what is added to your food, buy prepackaged: bulk counters are not legally required to carry an ingredient list
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The same item prepackaged usually costs a little more than from the bulk bin. Reading an ingredient list takes seconds.
- In plain terms: The law requires an ingredient list only on prepackaged food, not on bulk. A bulk counter has to mark the name, the production date, the shelf life, and who made it, and that is all. So "no additives listed" does not mean "nothing added"; it only means the law does not require it to be written. If you care, buy the prepackaged version, where the ingredient list and the names of the additives are printed on the pack.
- Benefit: Article 67 of the Food Safety Law says the packaging of prepackaged food must carry a label. The label must state nine things. The second is "ingredients or the ingredient list", and the seventh is "the generic names, as given in the national standard, of the food additives used". Article 68 asks far less of bulk food. Its text reads: when selling bulk food, the seller must mark on the container or outer packaging "the name of the food, the production date or production batch number, the shelf life, and the name, address, and contact details of the producer or seller". That article mentions neither an ingredient list nor additives. Frozen aquatic products are where the gap shows most clearly. GB 2760—2024 allows phosphoric acid and phosphates in frozen aquatic products as water-retention agents, known in everyday speech as water-keeping agents. The maximum is 5.0 g/kg, counted as phosphate. Using them within that limit is legal. The trouble is overdosing and ice glazing to add weight. After CCTV's 3·15 gala in 2025 exposed shrimp that had been "illegally overdosed with water-retention agents (compound phosphates) and ice-glazed to add weight", market regulators required producers that "product labels must clearly state the water-retention agent (such as phosphate), the net content (excluding the weight of the ice glaze), and the ingredient list" (nationwide; third amendment to the Food Safety Law adopted in 2025, effective December 1, 2025)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2025 年第三次修正). 中华人民共和国食品安全法（第六十七、六十八条，2025 年 9 月 12 日公布，2025 年 12 月 1 日施行）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=7b5a76d0461745a08d3f964916b87ef3>；国家卫生健康委员会、国家市场监督管理总局 (2024). 食品安全国家标准 食品添加剂使用标准（GB 2760—2024，附录 A 表 A.1「磷酸及磷酸盐」项下食品分类号 09.02.01 冷冻水产品；A.3 规定表 A.1 列出的食品添加剂按照规定的使用范围和最大使用量使用）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202403/content_7001729.htm>；六安市市场监督管理局 (2025). 关于水产制品及使用保水剂的相关企业提醒告诫函（2025 年 3 月 24 日）. <https://scjgj.luan.gov.cn/zwzx/tzgg/5414606.html>
- Notes: "Not labeled" does not mean "not added"; it only means the law does not require a label. If you want to know the ingredients, buy the prepackaged version. For frozen shrimp and fish blocks, the net content on the prepack is the weight after the ice glaze is removed, and only a unit price computed on that weight is the real price. Bulk is sold at the marked price, and after thawing it can shrink by a large share. Water-retention agents within the limit are legal and not a safety problem, so do not treat them as poison. Overdosing and ice glazing to add weight are money problems: you paid seafood prices for water. The other way around, the four things a bulk counter must mark may be missing too: name, production date, shelf life, and the name, address, and phone of the producer or seller; if even one is missing, Article 68 is violated. ### 37. Sell losing stocks by a rule you fixed in advance; do not count on averaging down to get even
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is admitting this trade is already a loss, and then selling it.
- In plain terms: Human instinct is to take profits fast and cling to losers. In seven years of records from 10,000 American accounts, a stock that had gone up was more than 50% likelier to be sold than one that had gone down. The same people were also more willing to add money to the stocks that were under water. Yet the losing stocks they kept went on to trail the ones they sold by 3.4 percentage points over the following year on average.
- Benefit: The study looked at all trading records of 10,000 accounts at an American discount broker from 1987 to 1993. The proportion of winning positions that got sold (PGR) was 0.148. The proportion of losing positions that got sold (PLR) was 0.098. The gap is 0.050, with a t-statistic above 35. In other words, day by day, a stock that had risen was more than 50% likelier to be sold than one that had fallen. The same accounts also added to losing positions more eagerly: losing positions were bought again at a rate of 0.135 against 0.094 for winners, with a t-statistic of 19. The study also tested the idea that "the ones you keep will come back". The result ran the other way: winning stocks that were sold beat losing stocks that were kept by 3.4 percentage points of excess return over the following year on average. Excess return means the part above the market index. The authors also worked out an example on averages: selling 1,000 dollars of a losing stock rather than a winning one left you about 4.4% better off over the following year. That 4.4% includes the tax saved by realizing the loss early, computed under the American tax code (United States, 1987 to 1993)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Odean T (1998). Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses? The Journal of Finance 53(5):1775-1798. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072>
- Notes: This unit governs whether to sell; unit 15 of this chapter (do not trade stocks frequently) governs not fiddling with the portfolio too much, and the two do not conflict. Fixing the rule in advance and following it actually means fewer moves. Averaging down is not wrong as arithmetic: buy a bit more and the average cost is a bit lower. The mistake is treating it as the way back to even: what it really does is put more of your money on this one stock; see unit 19 of this chapter (do not stake your money on a single stock). The tax part is computed under the American system; China does not copy it, and the rest still holds. This is American account data from the 1980s and 1990s, where both trading costs and taxes differ from the A-share market. Not investment advice.

### 38. When the market is surging and crashing, trade less; do not scale up at the hottest moment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is not piling on while everyone around you is making money.
- In plain terms: Shanghai's exchange ran the numbers on 40 million accounts for the boom-and-bust round of 2014 to 2015. The 85% of households with the smallest accounts earned 250 billion yuan less than simply holding still would have brought in, purely through frequent buying and selling. That is roughly 30% of their starting stock value. In the two and a half calm years, the same gap was only one to three percent.
- Benefit: The study used day-by-day account data from the Shanghai Stock Exchange covering all of its roughly 40 million accounts. The sample ran from July 2014 to December 2015, 18 months in all. Over those 18 months the Shanghai Composite first rose more than 150%. It peaked at 5,166.35 on June 12, 2015, then fell 40% by the end of December. Household accounts were sorted into four tiers by starting market value. The smallest tier held 85% of all household accounts and the largest 0.5%. The two tiers started with almost the same total stock value: 880 billion yuan against 808 billion. The result: the smallest 85% lost 250 billion yuan to active trading, while the largest 0.5% gained 254 billion. "Loss" here is measured against holding still, that is, against what doing nothing through the same market move would have brought. Each figure is about 30% of that group's starting stock value, 28% for the former and 31% for the latter. As a comparison, the two and a half years before June 2014 were calm. Take any 18 months in that stretch and the same gap was only 1% to 3% of starting value. In this episode household accounts turned over their whole holding in about three weeks on average, nearly 18 times a year (Shanghai, China, 2014 to 2015; comparison period 2012 to 2014)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: An L, Lou D, Shi D (2022). Wealth redistribution in bubbles and crashes. Journal of Monetary Economics 126:134-153. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2022.01.001>；同文作者公开的工作论文稿（2021 年 10 月，本条数字按它逐字核对）. <https://personal.lse.ac.uk/loud/AnLouShi.pdf>
- Notes: This unit is not a warning against owning stocks. It says that within the same market move, the more actively the small-account end trades, the more money flows to the other side of the trade, and "holding still" is the baseline in this study. The money was not taken by institutions either, but by the individuals with the largest accounts: retail accounts made up about 90% of exchange turnover in this period. The circulating claim that "90% of retail investors lose money" has no checkable official statistic behind it, so this unit does not use that number. Turnover puts the two episodes side by side: household accounts in this rally turned over nearly 18 times a year, while the American households in unit 15 of this chapter (do not trade stocks frequently) replaced only three quarters of their holdings a year. Not investment advice.
