# 5. Don’t waste money

This section is all about money. We’ll look at three things: how much you can save, what the yearly return difference is, and how much you can expect to get back over the long run from any given sum. None of these figures are converted into years of life or time saved. For any items dealing with investments, we’ll only explain how the calculations work, using only verified historical data. None of this should be taken as investment advice.
### 1. Turn off all automatic renewals and switch to manual renewal
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Simply review the lists of automatic payments in Alipay, WeChat Pay, Apple, and Android — this takes only 10 to 20 minutes. From then on, you’ll need to manually trigger each renewal yourself.
- In plain terms: What you save is exactly the money spent on subscriptions you no longer use but are still being charged for each month. It takes less than 20 minutes to go through the automatic payment lists in Alipay, WeChat Pay, Apple, and Android. By regulation, merchants must give you a clear notice before charging you, plus an easy-to-find and readily accessible option to cancel at any time.
- Benefit: The amount saved equals the total cost of those subscriptions you no longer need yet continue paying for. This amount varies from person to person, so there is no single universal figure. Regulations also require merchants to provide a straightforward way for you to cancel at any moment — and this cancellation process itself costs nothing (China, effective since 2021).
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2021, 2025 修正). 网络交易监督管理办法（总局令第 37 号，第十八条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Notes: For services you actually use regularly, annual subscriptions are generally cheaper than monthly ones, so keeping automatic renewal on may be more cost-effective. This advice applies specifically to subscriptions you’ve forgotten about but are still being charged for.

### 2. Conduct your annual tax reconciliation from March to June; be sure to include any eligible special deductions.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no cost involved. You can complete the process in just a few minutes via the “Individual Income Tax” app.
- In plain terms: Generally, the following individuals can expect a tax refund: those who worked only part of the year, those who changed jobs mid-year, or those who failed to claim deductions for child education, mortgage payments, rent, or elderly support. The whole process takes only a few minutes on a smartphone. If your total annual income is under 120,000 yuan or the amount of tax you owe is less than 400 yuan, you may skip this step.
- Benefit: This process consolidates calculations for four types of income: wages, freelance earnings, royalties, and licensing fees. Any overpaid tax is refunded, while any shortfall must be paid. There are two exceptions: if your total annual income stays under 120,000 yuan, or if the tax you owe is less than 400 yuan. These thresholds were set by the State Taxation Administration in 2023 and remain valid through December 31, 2027. Conversely, the groups mentioned above are precisely those who are likely to receive a refund.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家税务总局 (2025). 个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴管理办法（第六条）及其解读. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm>；财政部、税务总局 (2023). 关于延续实施个人所得税综合所得汇算清缴有关政策的公告. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Notes: Even individuals who owe tax must still complete this reconciliation; failure to do so is illegal. There are seven categories of special deductions available: child education, continuing education, major medical expenses, mortgage interest, rent payments, elderly support, and childcare for infants under three. Note that you may claim either mortgage interest or rent deductions, but not both.

- In plain terms: The housing provident fund isn’t limited to home purchases. The updated regulations list nine eligible scenarios for withdrawals, including renting, renovating a personal residence, and paying associated property fees. Once you submit your application, the housing provident fund authority must respond within three days whether your request is approved or not.
- Benefit: The revised regulations expand eligible withdrawal scenarios to nine total, covering rent payments, home renovations, and property fee payments for personal residences. The housing provident fund authority must issue a decision on your application within three days of receipt.

### 4. Recalculate your mobile and broadband plans yearly: downgrade unused services, and file a complaint if the provider refuses
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No cost at all. It takes just a few minutes to review your bill. The real challenge is resisting the pressure from customer service reps who try to dissuade you from downgrading.

- In plain terms: Older plans you’re on often cost dozens of yuan more per month than newer plans currently offered. Those extra costs add up to hundreds of yuan each year — money essentially wasted. By regulation, providers have no legitimate reason to deny or delay such changes, nor may they impose special pricing structures solely for customers who switch plans. If they refuse without cause, you have grounds to file a complaint.

- Benefit: Ministry regulations explicitly state that providers must not “refuse, obstruct, or delay providing number portability services without valid reasons.” They also must not “use blocking or restrictive technical measures that degrade service quality for such users,” nor “create separate pricing or marketing plans exclusively for them.” Older plans typically cost significantly more than current offerings; sticking with them means losing hundreds of yuan annually.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 工业和信息化部 (2019). 携号转网服务管理规定（工信部信管〔2019〕242 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>

- Notes: First, use your carrier’s app to check your actual monthly data and call usage over the past six months, then select a plan matching those figures. If a rep claims “downgrades must be handled in person at a store” or “contract terms prohibit changes,” demand written justification. Should none be provided, contact the hotline at 12300 to file a formal complaint.

### 5. Not buying lottery tickets
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Not buying means saving money.
- In plain terms: According to regulations, the minimum proportion of sales revenue allocated to prizes for lotteries is 50% for regular and number-based types, and 51% for the Double Color Ball. This means that on average, for every 1 yuan spent, you can expect to receive back only 0.51 yuan over the long term — a net loss of 49%. In 2024, China sold 623.486 billion yuan worth of lottery tickets; of this amount, 161.031 billion yuan went directly into public welfare funds and was not distributed as prizes.
- Benefit: Government regulations set minimum prize payout ratios: 50% for regular and number-based lotteries, 65% for instant-win types, and 73% for sports betting variants. For the Double Color Ball, 51% of total sales is earmarked for prizes, meaning only 51 yuan out of every 100 yuan sold is returned to players. In practical terms, each yuan spent yields an average return of 0.51 yuan over time, resulting in a consistent 49% loss rate. The 2024 figures show that 161.031 billion yuan of the total 623.486 billion yuan in lottery sales was diverted to public welfare purposes rather than being paid out as winnings (China, 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 财政部 (2015). 关于规范和加强彩票资金构成比例管理的通知（财综〔2015〕94 号）. <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm>；财政部 (2025). 财政部公告 2025 年第 8 号（2024 年彩票公益金筹集分配情况）. <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm>；中国福利彩票双色球游戏规则（第十四条）. <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Notes: Statistically, buying lottery tickets guarantees a net loss over time; this outcome is dictated by the system itself, not by luck or chance. Purchasing tickets is permissible if viewed solely as a form of entertainment costing 2 yuan per attempt — it should never be considered a viable financial investment strategy.

- In plain terms: Regulatory authorities themselves state that any return rate above 6% raises red flags, anything over 8% is highly risky, and returns above 10% mean you should expect to lose all your principal. Legitimate financial products are no longer allowed to promise guaranteed returns or principal protection. Any losses incurred from participating in illegal fundraising are your own responsibility per regulations; no one will cover those losses if things go wrong.
- Benefit: The former chairperson of the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission stated at the Lujiazui Forum in 2018: “Any return rate above 6% raises red flags, anything over 8% is very risky, and returns above 10% mean you should prepare to lose all your principal.” New asset management regulations require financial institutions to “not make any promises of guaranteed returns or principal protection,” and also stipulate that “when facing difficulties in fulfilling payment obligations, financial institutions must not provide any form of financial support to honor payouts.” This means legitimate financial products no longer offer principal protection. Any losses from illegal fundraising are borne solely by the participants. No one will step in to cover your losses if such issues occur (China, 2018, 2021).

### 7. Avoid making minimum payments or using installment plans or consumer loans
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. You simply need to have enough cash on hand to pay the full balance by the due date. The real challenge is resisting the urge to spread out large purchases via installment plans.
- In plain terms: Making minimum payments is not a discount — it is essentially a loan that accrues interest daily, which translates to roughly 18% per year. Once you opt for minimum payments, the interest-free period ends immediately, and all remaining unpaid principal is charged interest from that point onward. When evaluating interest rates, always look at the annual percentage rate. Regulators now require card issuers to clearly display this annual rate instead of only listing the daily rate.
- Benefit: Prior to 2020, credit card interest rates had set upper and lower limits: the maximum daily rate was 0.05%, and the minimum was 0.7 times that cap. At 0.05% daily, the annual simple interest rate comes to 18.25% — meaning interest is calculated solely on the original principal without compounding. Starting in 2021, rates are now negotiated between card issuers and cardholders. Opting for minimum payments means all unpaid principal is charged interest daily, while the interest-free period is lost entirely. Regulators now require issuers to prominently display the annual percentage rate, rather than only the daily rate or daily payment amount. Use this annual figure to make informed decisions. (China, 2020 regulatory notice)
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 中国人民银行 (2020). 关于推进信用卡透支利率市场化改革的通知（银发〔2020〕327 号）. <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Notes: The 18.25% figure is calculated by multiplying 0.05% by 365, without accounting for compound interest; actual rates would be higher if compounded monthly. Some banks offer discounts, but you should always refer to the annual rate stated on your own account statements. If you truly cannot afford payments, seek out legitimate lenders offering lower rates rather than relying on minimum payments that keep accruing interest.

### 8. Don’t give streamers tips, don’t make in‑game purchases, and don’t place impulsive orders

- Cost: It costs nothing. Spend a few minutes doing three things: turn off automatic payment settings, set daily and per‑transaction limits, and remove any linked payment methods from the platforms. The hard part is resisting the urge to click at the moment.

- In plain terms: In 2024, China’s gaming industry generated 325.78 billion yuan in revenue, serving 674 million users — that’s roughly 480 yuan per person on average. The total amount spent that year was 325,783,000,000 yuan, involving 674 million users and 325,783,000,000 people overall. Once that money is spent, it’s almost impossible to get back. The law only guarantees refunds for spending done by minors without parental consent; adults have no such protection. The easiest way to avoid losses is to act in advance: disable automatic payments, set limits, and unlink payment options.

- Benefit: This represents a massive amount of spending. In 2024, China’s gaming market brought in 325.78 billion yuan, with 674 million users spending an average of about 480 yuan each (calculated by dividing the total revenue by the user count). That money is largely unrecoverable. One exception exists: if a minor — a person with limited civil capacity — makes purchases or gives tips without a guardian’s permission, a court must support the guardian’s request for a refund. No such rule applies to adult gamers. Additionally, minors are legally restricted to playing online games only on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays, and only between 20:00 and 21:00 (China, 2020‑2021).

### 9. When kids make in-app purchases or send tips using a phone, parents can request refunds for large amounts spent by kids over eight years old if they never gave consent.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: There’s no direct monetary cost, but it does take time to gather evidence and negotiate with platforms. Platforms usually reject requests at first, then sometimes issue refunds. If talks fail, legal action becomes necessary — a process that can take months and requires paying attorney fees. Therefore, it’s far more efficient to compile all relevant documents and contact the platform or file a complaint via 12315 first. The real challenge is getting the child to cooperate and share details.

- In plain terms: Money spent by kids over eight years old is generally considered invalid if the amount clearly exceeds what’s appropriate for their age and level of understanding, and if parents neither approved nor acknowledged it. Parents can therefore demand a refund. Kids under eight, by law, cannot make independent spending decisions at all. Even if parents stay silent, the law treats this as a refusal to acknowledge the transaction.

- Benefit: Minors aged eight and older are classified as having limited legal capacity — meaning they may only engage in activities fitting their age and maturity level. Any financial transaction beyond that scope must be approved or ratified by a legal guardian, i.e., parents must either give prior consent or later accept responsibility. Children under eight lack any legal capacity; all actions must be handled by guardians. If guardians remain silent, it’s legally interpreted as a refusal to ratify those transactions.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典. 最高人民检察院转载全文. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> 第十九条：「八周岁以上的未成年人为限制民事行为能力人，实施民事法律行为由其法定代理人代理或者经其法定代理人同意、追认；但是，可以独立实施纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力相适应的民事法律行为。」第二十条：「不满八周岁的未成年人为无民事行为能力人，由其法定代理人代理实施民事法律行为。」第一百四十五条：「限制民事行为能力人实施的纯获利益的民事法律行为或者与其年龄、智力、精神健康状况相适应的民事法律行为有效；实施的其他民事法律行为经法定代理人同意或者追认后有效。」「法定代理人未作表示的，视为拒绝追认。」

- Notes: Success hinges on proper evidence — you must prove that it was indeed the child, not you, who operated the phone. Useful evidence includes: transaction times falling during school hours or while parents are at work, device usage logs, the child’s chat and gaming histories, and a written statement from the child himself. There’s no fixed monetary threshold defining “age-appropriate” spending; a few dozen yuan spent on game skins differs greatly from tens of thousands spent on tips. Prevention is easier: set spending limits without requiring a password, enable youth-friendly modes, and never share payment passwords with kids. For parents who impulsively spend money on tips or in-game purchases themselves, see the section titled “Avoid tipping streamers, avoid in-game spending, and avoid impulsive purchases.”

### 10. Teaching kids one key phrase: anyone who asks them to use a parent’s phone to enter verification codes is a scammer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There’s no cost involved. Simply explain these six types of scams to your child, then agree on the phrase “If I get scammed, I’ll tell you right away and you won’t scold me.” Also set spending limits for contactless payments, enable teen mode on devices, and never share payment passwords with kids. The hardest part is staying calm and not losing your temper if they actually get scammed.
- In plain terms: These six scam types use different tactics, but they all end the same way: they get kids to use a parent’s phone to make transfers or share verification codes. That’s why teaching just one phrase is enough: anyone who asks you to touch your parent’s phone or share verification codes is a scammer. Add “If I get scammed, I’ll tell you right away and you won’t scold me” to this phrase. Only if kids feel safe speaking up immediately can stolen money possibly be recovered.
- Benefit: The Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Center and local education authorities identified six scam types that specifically target teens. Despite their different methods, all end with the same step: getting kids to use a parent’s phone for transfers. Type 1 involves buying game accounts or gear at low prices privately. One boy transferred 260 yuan, then the scammer told him “Minor transactions are against the rules and your account will be frozen,” so he clicked a “verification link” on his dad’s phone — losing over 30,000 yuan. Type 2 is “removing anti-addiction restrictions.” Scammers threaten “If you don’t transfer money, your parents will go to jail,” so a child transferred 7,000 yuan from his mom’s phone. The official rules state there are no “private unlock” options for anti-addiction settings. Type 3 is refund requests for online gifts. Scammers pose as “official staff” and claim minors can get refunds if parents send gifts to a specific livestream — a mom ended up sending 73,000 yuan, then was blocked by the scammers. Official sources confirm “Anyone asking you to ‘send gifts to get a refund’ is 100% a scammer.” Type 4 involves impersonating classmates or teachers. Scammers hack accounts and send voice messages calling kids by name to ask for money. At the start of the school year, they also join class groups posing as teachers, posting “Pay 500 yuan for school fees” with fake profiles and fake classmates confirming payment. Type 5 targets fans of celebrities. Scammers invite kids into “fan groups” offering 888 yuan in cash prizes, first asking them to send 48.88 yuan as a “verification fee,” then threatening “Since you’re a minor, the platform will freeze 80,000 yuan and your parents will go to jail” — stealing 16,000 yuan total. Official sources also note “Real police or lawyers never contact minors online for investigations, nor ask parents to operate phones.” Type 6 involves luring people to private transfers via low-priced items on second-hand platforms. The same materials also list three red lines kids must avoid: 1) Never lend, sell, or rent bank cards or SIM cards — using them for illegal fund transfers can lead to criminal charges, leaving a permanent record that affects college admissions and civil service exams; 2) “Mobile voice acting” jobs are illegal; 3) Overseas “high-paying jobs” often trap victims once they arrive (Quanzhou, October 2025).

### 11. Skipping extended warranties for electronics
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There’s no upfront cost. The trade‑off is that after the warranty expires you’d have to pay for any repairs yourself, though that situation rarely occurs.
- In plain terms: Extended warranties typically cost 10% to 50% of the item’s original price. The profit margin on these warranties is roughly ten times higher than on regular merchandise. In 2003, Best Buy in the U.S. earned over half of its total profits from extended warranties, even though they accounted for only 3% to 4% of its overall revenue. That price gap represents exactly what you’re paying extra for the warranty.
- Benefit: Extended warranties are priced at 10% to 50% of the product’s original cost. Industry estimates cited in studies show an average gross margin of 50% to 60% on these warranties — meaning 50 to 60 out of every 100 dollars spent translates into profit. This is about 18 times higher than the typical gross margin on ordinary goods. In 2003, extended warranties made up just 3% to 4% of Best Buy’s revenue yet generated more than 50% of its profits (U.S. retail data from the early 2000s).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611-623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Notes: The reported gross margin figure comes from an industry estimate referenced in a paper (Business Week, 2004) rather than being measured directly by the authors. This advice applies to ordinary consumers who can afford a single repair bill if needed. For people whose livelihood depends on a single device and who lack a backup, the calculation changes. Separate considerations also apply to screen‑protection plans for phones: you should first estimate how often you drop your device and then decide whether the premium is worthwhile.

### 16. Don’t borrow money to invest, don’t use leverage, and don’t buy things you don’t understand

### 17. Replacing actively managed funds with broad market index funds as a long-term core holding (the portion of money you keep invested for years)

### 19. Don’t put all your money into one stock, one platform, or one property
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. The hard part is accepting that you simply won’t be able to pick the single most profitable option every time.
- In plain terms: As long as several assets don’t all rise or fall together, spreading your money across them keeps your average expected returns the same while reducing the ups and downs of your account balance. Conversely, if you invest everything in one stock, platform, or property, any trouble there directly affects your entire portfolio.
- Benefit: When multiple assets don’t move in perfect lockstep, diversifying keeps expected returns unchanged while lowering overall volatility. In other words, you still earn roughly the same average profit, but your account balance swings far less. This is the core finding of Markowitz’s 1952 portfolio theory, which remains the foundation of modern investing. On the flip side, putting all funds into a single stock or platform means its variance becomes your total variance — any rise or fall in that asset directly determines your own results, with no hedge or backup to fall back on when problems arise.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Notes: This is graded B because the original paper relies purely on mathematical reasoning and does not provide concrete figures on exactly how much diversification reduces losses. Diversification merely dampens the volatility of your portfolio; it does not guarantee you won’t lose money. Buying broad‑based index funds (see item 17 in this section) is the easiest way to achieve diversification. This information is not intended as investment advice.

### 24. Don’t stock up based on “strikethrough prices” or big sales
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: Zero monetary cost. The trade‑off is that you might miss out on some real discounts. You’ll need to keep track of the regular prices of the items you buy most often. The hard part is resisting the urge to stock up on sale day.

- In plain terms: When judging whether something is cheap, people rely on the first price they see, and later adjustments rarely make up for that bias. Thus, “original price 999, now 499” makes us systematically overestimate how much we’re saving. By regulation, the baseline for a discount should be the lowest price the same store charged in the seven days prior to the sale. The crossed‑out price isn’t necessarily one that was actually sold at.

- Benefit: Human judgment gets anchored to the first price encountered, and subsequent corrections usually fall short. Consequently, “original price 999, now 499” leads us to overestimate the true discount. Rules require that the reference price be the minimum transaction price recorded by that retailer in the seven days before the promotion. In other words, the discount percentage must be calculated using that actual minimum price — not a price that may never have been offered. (China, effective since 2020)

### 28. Run a quick comparison before paying off your mortgage early — don’t rely on gut feeling
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost:  
  There’s no cost at all. It only takes about half an hour to do the math. You’ll need to honestly estimate the stable after‑tax return you can expect over the long run.

- In plain terms:  
  The guaranteed return you get from paying off your mortgage early equals your mortgage interest rate. So the only question you need to answer is: is the long‑term after‑tax return you can reliably earn higher than that rate? If the answer is yes, it’s better to keep the money invested; if it’s no, or you’re unsure, then paying off the mortgage makes sense. With an equal principal‑and‑interest loan, a large portion of each payment goes toward interest early on, so the earlier you pay, the more you save. Most bank apps have a “prepayment calculator” that shows these numbers right away.

- Benefit:  
  The calculation works like this: let r be the annual mortgage interest rate as specified in your contract. As of 20 August 2026, the 5‑year LPR in China is 3.5 %; the actual rate you pay is either higher or lower than this figure. Next, let g be the after‑tax annual return you can expect from an alternative investment — the average percentage you’d earn each year by putting that money elsewhere. The guaranteed return from early repayment is exactly r; each dollar you pay off effectively earns you that rate without any risk. If g stays consistently above r, keeping the money invested is the better choice. If g is lower than r, or if you can’t reliably predict it, then paying off the mortgage locks in a risk‑free return of r. Because early payments on an equal principal‑and‑interest loan consist mostly of interest, the earlier you pay, the more interest you avoid. This effect is clearly visible in the figures produced by your bank’s “prepayment calculator” (China, 2026 interest rates).

### 31. Purchasing unsafe food: you can demand ten times the purchase price as compensation, with any shortfall under 1,000 yuan made up to 1,000 yuan

- In plain terms: If you eat food that is expired, spoiled, or contains foreign objects, the food itself, its packaging, and the receipt constitute all your evidence — so do not toss them out hastily. The law mandates compensation equal to “ten times the purchase price or three times the actual loss,” and if that amount falls short of 1,000 yuan, the shortfall is made up to exactly 1,000 yuan. Thus, even for a few‑dollar pack of expired food, you are essentially entitled to that 1,000‑yuan sum. You may choose whether to seek payment from the seller or the manufacturer; the party you contact must pay first and cannot shift responsibility to the other.

- Benefit: Consumers harmed by food that fails to meet safety standards may demand compensation from either the seller or the producer. ### 33. Bracelets, jade, luxury watches, and collectibles should be judged by “money spent” rather than “money saved”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There’s no upfront cost — just don’t justify buying them on the grounds that they’ll appreciate in value later. The real challenge is resisting the urge to allocate extra budget for “preservation of value.”
- In plain terms: The moment you buy such items, a portion of their price functions like a tax. For watches priced at 10,000 yuan or more (excluding VAT), a 20% consumption tax applies as “luxury watches.” Gold, platinum jewelry and diamonds incur a 5% tax, while other precious jewelry and jade products are taxed at 10%. VAT applies on top of that. None of this amount can be recovered when you later sell the item to someone else. If you genuinely like it, treat it as a discretionary purchase rather than an investment.
- Benefit: On the purchase side, the Consumption Tax Schedule sets these rates: 20% for “luxury watches” defined as any watch whose selling price — excluding VAT — is 10,000 yuan or higher. For “precious jewelry and jade,” gold, platinum jewelry and diamonds are taxed at 5%, while other precious jewelry and jade items face a 10% rate. On the resale side, the Pawnshop Management Regulations govern the process. Article 36 limits pawn terms to a maximum of six months; Article 37 specifies that pawn interest must be calculated using the six‑month benchmark lending rate published by the People’s Bank of China and cannot be deducted in advance from the loan amount. Article 38 caps the monthly overall fee for pawning movable assets such as watches and jade at 42‰ of the loan amount — that is, no more than 4.2% of the borrowed sum each month. By comparison, real‑estate mortgages incur a 27‰ fee and pledges of property rights a 24‰ fee. If the pawn term expires and the borrower fails to redeem or renew the pawn within five days, the item becomes “forfeit” and is no longer held for redemption. Article 43 permits auctioning forfeit items valued at 30,000 yuan or more; auction proceeds must first cover auction fees and the principal plus interest owed, with any shortfall still recoverable from the borrower (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2008). 中华人民共和国消费税暂行条例（国务院令第 539 号，附《消费税税目税率表》）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-11/14/content_1795.htm>；财政部、国家税务总局 (2006). 关于调整和完善消费税政策的通知（财税〔2006〕33 号）. <http://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/zhengcefabu/2006zcfb/200805/t20080524_34758.htm>；商务部、公安部 (2005). 典当管理办法（2005 年第 8 号令，第三十六、三十七、三十八、四十、四十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2005/content_93074.htm>
- Notes: Be sure to factor in the costs of pawning: the maximum monthly fee is 42‰ of the loan amount, which translates to roughly 50% of that amount over a year — interest on the loan is extra. This “50% per year” figure is a rough approximation derived by multiplying the monthly rate by 12; it serves only to illustrate scale, not as an exact annual rate. Both tax and fee rates are clearly stipulated by law. However, there are no official statistics confirming whether these items appreciate over time, so this guide makes no predictions or endorsements for any particular category. The aim here is to discourage treating such purchases as investments: if you enjoy wearing them, buying them as personal indulgences is fine; adding budget on the premise that they’ll retain or increase in value is where losses arise. Gold also falls into two categories: investment bars traded by weight and jewelry sold as individual pieces. Jewelry includes design and craftsmanship costs that are generally not recovered upon resale. Listed prices on second‑hand platforms are not necessarily actual transaction values, so they shouldn’t be taken as reliable market indicators.

### 34. Jewelry and gemstones require test reports bearing the CMA mark; verify the issuing agency on its official website.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Checking an agency’s credentials takes just a few minutes before placing an order.
- In plain terms: Certificates themselves can be forged, so the real task is to verify the agency that issues them. Any testing agency must first obtain official accreditation to issue reports, which must feature the CMA mark. The accrediting authority publishes a list of approved agencies and their certificate statuses online, allowing anyone to confirm whether a given agency qualifies. In 2026, several “anti-counterfeiting” cases revealed that some “national inspection certificates” accompanying goods were fabricated by the sellers themselves, and even fake agency logos were used.
- Benefit: The relevant regulations are outlined in the *Administrative Measures for Accreditation of Inspection and Testing Agencies*. Article 2 states that accreditation is a formal approval process for agencies that issue data or results with legal evidentiary value to the public; this means agencies must undergo review prior to being granted permission to issue certificates. Article 15 specifies that the CMA mark comprises both the CMA logo and the agency’s accreditation number. Article 21 requires all agencies issuing legally valid test results to display this CMA mark on their reports. Article 22 mandates that accreditation authorities publish lists of approved agencies and their certificate statuses on official websites. Article 34 imposes a fine of up to 30,000 RMB on any agency issuing test results without proper accreditation. Article 37 levies similar penalties for forging, altering, or misusing accreditation certificates or marks. Real-world examples from these 2026 cases include: First, a livestream in Donghai, Jiangsu, advertised “natural, unacid-washed, unprocessed Burmese A-grade jade” and displayed a “national inspection certificate.” Lab tests later revealed the material was dyed quartzite, treated jade, glass, and plastic — all certified by a certificate fabricated by the seller. Second, another case in the same county uncovered 469 suspected counterfeit crystal products; 210 were natural crystals while 259 were synthetic. Third, a shop in Zhenping, Henan, created its own promotional board claiming to be an “authorized partner of the GIA CMA China Gemstone Testing Center” and sold 181 counterfeit “National Gemstone Quality Inspection Center” certificates alongside bracelets nationwide.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家质量监督检验检疫总局 (2015 公布，2021 修改). 检验检测机构资质认定管理办法（第二、十五、二十一、二十二、三十四、三十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2021-04/22/content_5723677.htm>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2026). 市场监管总局公布一批传统工艺市场「打假清源」典型案例. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/zj/art/2026/art_1529399cfe874b8780c4496eb0158020.html>；国家标准化管理委员会. GB/T 16552—2017《珠宝玉石 名称》（现行，2018 年 5 月 1 日实施）. <https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/gb/newGbInfo?hcno=18FFB042811998A0309E05CF452B2438>
- Notes: National standards define terminology for jewelry and gemstones. Test results are often phrased as “quartzite (dyed)” or “jade (treated),” where the text in parentheses indicates the price difference between natural and processed versions. ### 35. Random sales such as blind boxes and gacha games are considered “money that won’t come back”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: There is no direct cost — you simply set a budget before buying. The hard part is resisting the urge to keep spending when you don’t get the item you wanted.

- In plain terms: The rules give consumers the right to be informed, but not the right to change their mind after a purchase. Merchants must clearly display all relevant details: product names, types, designs, draw rules, distribution of items, quantities of limited editions, and actual draw probabilities. They are also prohibited from manipulating results behind the scenes or using empty boxes. However, “guaranteed minimum rewards, spending caps, and limits on draw attempts” are only encouraged, not mandatory. For online blind box purchases, the moment you open the box is the final chance to cancel the transaction.

- Benefit: This guidance is based on the “Pilot Guidelines for Blind Box Business Practices.” Article 9 requires merchants to prominently display a list of key information so buyers can know what they’re getting beforehand. This includes product name, type, design, draw rules, item distribution, quantities of limited editions, draw probabilities, and estimated value range. The same article also bans three practices: altering draw results or probabilities behind the scenes to push more spending; refusing or delaying delivery of blind boxes through cashbacks, buybacks, or exchanges; and using empty boxes. Article 11 only “encourages” merchants to set minimum guarantees, spending limits, and draw caps to promote responsible spending. Article 17 specifically applies to blind boxes sold online. If merchants fully inform buyers and those buyers explicitly agree during a single purchase, then opening the box may exempt the transaction from the seven-day no‑reason return policy. Merchants must not use pre‑checked boxes to replace explicit consent. Article 21 forbids using blind boxes as a guise for gambling. Article 23 bans sales to children under eight; for those aged eight and older, merchants must verify that parental consent has been obtained (nationwide).

- Notes: These guidelines are meant to steer merchants’ behavior; they are not themselves a legal penalty tool. ### 36. If you care about what’s in your food, buy pre-packaged items: loose goods at retail counters aren’t legally required to list ingredients  
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Zero expense. Pre-packaged versions of the same products are usually a bit pricier than loose ones. Checking the ingredient list takes just a few seconds.  
- In plain terms: The law only mandates ingredient lists for pre-packaged foods; no such requirement exists for loose goods. Retail counters selling loose items only need to display the product name, production date, shelf life, and manufacturer details. So, the absence of listed additives does not mean they are not added — it simply means the law does not require them to be disclosed. If you care about what’s in your food, opt for the pre-packaged version, which clearly lists all ingredients and additives.  
- Benefit: Article 67 of China’s Food Safety Law stipulates that pre-packaged foods must carry a label with nine mandatory pieces of information. The second item on this list is the “list of ingredients or components,” and the seventh is “the generic names of food additives used, as defined by national standards.” Article 68 contains far fewer requirements for loose foods: it only mandates that retailers display the product name, production date or batch number, shelf life, and manufacturer contact details on containers or outer packaging. There is no mention of ingredient lists or additives at all. This distinction is most evident in frozen seafood products. GB 2760—2024 permits the use of phosphoric acid and phosphates as moisture retention agents — commonly known as water-retaining agents — in frozen seafood, with a maximum permitted usage level of 5.0 g/kg calculated as phosphate. Use within this limit is fully legal. The main issues arise from excessive usage and adding ice to artificially increase product weight. Following the 2025 CCTV 3·15 Gala exposé on illegal overuse of water-retaining agents (including compound phosphates) and ice addition in shrimp processing, regulatory authorities mandated that all relevant products must clearly list water-retaining agents such as phosphates, net weight (excluding ice coating), and full ingredient lists on packaging (effective 1 December 2025, per the third 2025 amendment to China’s Food Safety Law).

### 37. Stocks that have lost value should also be sold according to pre-set rules — rather than buying more to average down the cost
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No monetary cost involved. The hard part is admitting that a stock has already lost value and then selling it.

- In plain terms: Human nature drives us to sell stocks that have gone up and hold on to those that have dropped. Across 10,000 U.S. accounts over seven years, stocks that rose were sold at a rate more than 50% higher than those that fell. The same investors also tended to add more money to losing positions. Yet, over the following year, those stocks they kept underperformed the ones they sold by an average of 3.4 percentage points.

- Benefit: This study analyzed all trades from 10,000 accounts at a U.S. discount brokerage between 1987 and 1993. The proportion of profitable stocks sold (PGR) was 0.148, while the proportion of losing stocks sold (PLR) was only 0.098 — a difference of 0.050, with a t-value exceeding 35. In other words, on any given day, the odds of selling a rising stock are more than 50% higher than selling a falling one. These same investors also bought more of their losing positions: the rate of re-purchasing falling stocks was 0.135, compared to 0.094 for rising stocks, with a t-value of 19. The research also tested the idea that “stocks you hold will eventually rebound.” The result ran contrary to that belief: over the next year, stocks sold after posting gains outperformed the losing stocks kept in portfolios by an average of 3.4 percentage points. This excess return refers to the amount above the general market index. The authors also ran a sample calculation based on averages: selling $1,000 worth of a losing stock instead of a profitable one would yield roughly 4.4% more return over the following year. This 4.4% includes tax savings from selling losing positions early, calculated under U.S. tax rules (U.S., 1987–1993).

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Odean T (1998). Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses? The Journal of Finance 53(5):1775-1798. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00072>

- Notes: This point addresses the decision of “whether to sell or not,” while point 15 (“Avoid frequent trading”) deals with “how often to trade” — the two are not contradictory. Following pre-set rules actually leads to fewer trades overall. Mathematically, buying more shares to lower the average cost is sound, but treating this as a way to recover losses is flawed: it simply increases the amount of capital tied up in a single stock, as explained in point 19 (“Don’t put all your money into one stock”). The tax calculations apply specifically to U.S. tax laws and cannot be directly applied to China; the rest of the findings remain valid. These data come from U.S. accounts in the 1980s and 1990s; trading costs and tax rules in China’s A-share market differ. This information does not constitute investment advice.

### 38. Avoid trading during periods of wild market swings — don’t ramp up buying or selling at the peak
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No direct cost. The real challenge is resisting the urge to increase positions when everyone else seems to be making money.

- In plain terms: Researchers analyzed trading patterns of 40 million accounts on the Shanghai Stock Exchange between July 2014 and December 2015. The bottom 85% of accounts, which traded frequently, ended up earning roughly 250 billion yuan less than those who simply held onto their shares. That figure equals about 30% of the initial market value of those accounts. During a calmer two-and-a-half-year stretch prior to this period, the gap between active and passive investors was only 1% to 3%. On average, these smaller accounts swapped their holdings roughly every three weeks — about 18 times per year. In contrast, American households studied in another study traded only three-quarters as often.

- Benefit: This research draws on daily transaction data from all 40 million accounts on the Shanghai Stock Exchange. The study period covers 18 months, during which the Shanghai Composite Index rose over 150% before peaking at 5,166.35 points on June 12, 2015, and then dropping 40% by year’s end. Accounts were grouped by initial market value; the smallest 85% held roughly 880 billion yuan in shares, while the top 0.5% controlled about 808 billion yuan. The former group lost 250 billion yuan due to active trading, while the latter earned 254 billion yuan — both figures represent roughly 30% of their respective starting portfolios. By comparison, during a calmer market phase from mid-2012 to mid-2014, the same kind of performance gap narrowed to just 1%–3% of initial portfolio value.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: An L, Lou D, Shi D (2022). Wealth redistribution in bubbles and crashes. Journal of Monetary Economics 126:134-153. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2022.01.001>；同文作者公开的工作论文稿（2021 年 10 月，本条数字按它逐字核对）. <https://personal.lse.ac.uk/loud/AnLouShi.pdf>

- Notes: This finding does not imply that investors should avoid buying stocks altogether. Rather, it shows that within any given market cycle, smaller investors who trade more frequently tend to lose money relative to those who stay invested. The bulk of profits went to the largest individual investors, who accounted for about 90% of total trading volume during this period. There is no official statistical basis for the commonly cited claim that “90% of retail investors lose money”; this study does not rely on that figure. The turnover rate observed here — nearly 18 trades per year — far exceeds that seen among American households, who traded only three-quarters as often.
