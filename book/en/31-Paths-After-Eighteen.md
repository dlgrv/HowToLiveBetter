# 31. What options are available after turning 18?

This section deals with money and time, while the option of avoiding military service also affects personal freedom. It serves as a roadmap of possible paths, not a step‑by‑step guide for any single one. First, we list the legal requirements for each path. Then we describe the details that become relevant only after you decide to pursue them: what benefits military service provides, other ways to enter the civil service besides the civil service exam, which college majors directly lead to government positions, how to later upgrade your education, what kind of employers to seek for overseas work, how to handle social insurance without a formal job, and whether there are government loans that cover part of the interest for those lacking startup capital.

Let us be clear: after turning 18 there are many more options than those covered here. We only include paths for which nationwide official documents specify eligibility criteria, requirements, and benefits. Paths such as joining state‑owned enterprises, working in factories, becoming a seafarer, pursuing freelance work, or continuing high school or university lack such uniform documentation. They are certainly possible, but without verifiable legal references we cannot describe their conditions in this book.

Choosing a path is entirely up to you. This section provides two things only: the official documents that set the eligibility rules, and the deadlines and qualifications that expire once you start down a particular route. How to weigh the decision between studying and working, and how to pick relevant skills, is explained in Section 23. Overtime pay, workplace injuries, and severance after joining a job are covered in Section 19. Starting or closing a business is discussed in Section 12. Studying abroad is the topic of Section 32, while safety nets for the unemployed are outlined in Section 7. None of those topics are repeated here.
### 1. Check the hard thresholds first: age and education requirements for most of the twelve paths are written in black and white
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. Spend ten minutes checking the list to see what you actually qualify for.
- In plain terms: The basic requirements for most of these twelve paths are set by official rules. To enter regular work, you must be at least 16 years old. To enlist in the military, men must be at least 18, extended up to 24 for college graduates. The civil service exam generally requires ages 18 to 35 and an associate degree or higher. Higher education self-study exams have no age or education limits, but full-time enrolled students cannot register. Registering as a self-employed business only requires "the capacity to do business."
- Benefit: For military enlistment, the Military Service Law states: "Male citizens who have reached the age of 18 shall be drafted for active service; those not drafted that year may still be drafted before reaching the age of 22. The draft age for graduates of regular higher education institutions may be relaxed to 24, and for graduate students to 26." For the civil service exam, Article 18 of the Civil Service Recruitment Provisions lists qualifications including "between 18 and 35 years of age" and "having a college diploma (associate degree) or higher"; both conditions may be adjusted with approval from civil service authorities at or above the provincial level. Article 19 lists five categories of barred applicants, such as those with criminal penalties or dismissed from public office. For firefighters, Article 5 of the National Comprehensive Fire and Rescue Team Firefighter Recruitment Measures requires "ages 18 to 22" and "a high school diploma or higher"; Article 6 relaxes the age limit to 24 for applicants with an associate degree or higher, as well as veterans of the PLA and People's Armed Police. For PLA civilian personnel, Article 9 of the Regulations on Civilian Personnel of the Chinese People's Liberation Army sets the baseline age at 18 or older; Article 19 sets the maximum age for first-time recruitment into entry-level posts at 35. For public institutions (shiye danwei), Article 8 of the Regulations on the Personnel Management of Public Institutions requires open recruitment from the public. For self-employed individuals, the Regulations on Promoting the Development of Individual Industrial and Commercial Households apply to "citizens capable of doing business who register according to law." For self-study exams, Article 3 of the Interim Regulations on Higher Education Self-Study Examinations states that all citizens may participate regardless of gender, age, ethnicity, race, or previous education level; Article 20 prohibits currently enrolled full-time students at all school levels from registering. For government-funded teacher-training students, Article 2 of the Implementation Measures for Tuition-Free Education of Teacher-Training Students Linking Undergraduate and Master's Degrees at Normal Universities Directly Under the Ministry of Education restricts programs to six universities: Beijing Normal, East China Normal, Northeast Normal, Central China Normal, Shaanxi Normal, and Southwest University. Programs integrate undergraduate and graduate study with stage assessments; the central government covers tuition and accommodation, plus living stipends; Article 5 specifies early-batch admission. For targeted medical students, the Notice on Job Placement and Contract Management for Order-Oriented Free Training of Rural Medical Students requires signing agreements with the training school and county health and human resources departments after admission but before receiving the formal acceptance letter. For overseas employment, Article 5 of the Regulations on the Administration of Overseas Labor Cooperation requires a qualification license from commerce authorities; Article 7(4) makes it unlawful to organize labor export without this qualification certificate; Article 8(2) prohibits organizing workers abroad under the pretext of business, tourism, or study. Gig work and flexible employment have no statutory education requirements (see items 11 and 12 of this chapter for insurance and occupational injury coverage). For general labor, the Compulsory Education Law prohibits employers from hiring school-age children and adolescents; Article 2 of the Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labor bans hiring anyone under 16 (for penalties, see Chapter 23, item 1).

Gig platforms have no statutory diploma requirement. Civil service age and degree rules can be adjusted with provincial-level approval. ### 2. In the year you turn 18, military service registration has to be done before October (10) 31; conscript active duty is 2 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Registration is filled in online once, and it takes a few minutes. Actually going means 2 years full time, and during those 2 years you cannot do other work at the same time.
- In plain terms: Military service registration is a legal duty. It is not an application to enlist, and registering does not mean you have to go. Men who turn 18 that year must finish registering online before October (10) 31. If you do not register, the county orders you to correct it by a deadline. If you still do not, it forces you to perform the military service duty and fines you. If you do enlist, conscript active duty is 2 years.
- Benefit: Registration is in the Provisions on Military Service Registration. They say: "The military service organ of the people's government of a county, autonomous county, city without districts, or municipal district shall, before October (10) 31 each year, organize initial military service registration for male citizens in its area who reach 18 before December (12) 31 of that year." The same provisions also say: "Initial military service registration is mainly done online, completed by the citizen on the military service registration network platform." What happens if you do not register is in Article 57, paragraph 1, of the Military Service Law. That paragraph covers "refusing or evading military service registration." The penalty is: "the county people's government orders correction by a deadline; if it is not corrected in time, the county people's government forces performance of the military service duty and imposes a fine." Article 15 of the Military Service Law says the people subject to initial military service registration are "male citizens who reach 18 before December (12) 31 each year." Article 20, paragraph 2, says: "According to the needs of the armed forces, female citizens may be called up for active service under the preceding paragraph." Article 15 of the Provisions on Military Service Registration says: "According to the needs of the armed forces... female citizens who meet the conditions may be organized for reserve registration." The Military Service Law also says: "The term of active service for a conscript is 2 years." "According to the needs of the armed forces and the person's own wish, citizens who have reached 17 but are not yet 18 may be called up for active service." A non-commissioned officer "who has served 12 years, or who meets other conditions set by the state, is assigned a job by the local people's government at county level or above in the place of placement." A non-commissioned officer "who has served 30 years, or who has reached 55, or who meets other conditions set by the state, is placed in retirement." Assigning a job means the government gives you a job. Retirement placement means you step down under retirement rules. The Regulations on Conscription say: "Priority goes to ensuring the call-up of graduates of ordinary higher-education institutions and of recruits with special requirements for political quality, physical condition, or professional skill." Those regulations also say: "Where the person to be called up is a full-time student or a graduating student of an ordinary higher-education institution, the call-up may be at the place of household registration before enrollment, or at the place of the school" (nationwide).

- Notes: Registration and call-up are different things, and the price differs by a long way. The string of penalties that includes a ban on leaving the country for 2 years punishes only people who back out after being called up. See item 3 (refusing military service). The people the law names for initial military service registration are male only. Women are called up according to the needs of the armed forces, and they may also be registered for the reserve according to those needs. Admission of soldiers to military academies, and turning a soldier with a strong record into an officer, are written in internal military rules and cannot be looked up from outside. The Military Service Law says only 2 things: active-duty officers are selected from "active-duty soldiers with an excellent record" and similar personnel, and military academies recruiting cadets are not limited by the call-up age. This book does not give score lines or quotas.

### 3. Refusing military service after being drafted: barred from leaving the country or resuming studies for two years, and blocked from civil service and state-owned enterprises
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No monetary cost. The real price is thinking it through carefully before signing up.
- In plain terms: If you sign up and then refuse to serve, and still refuse after being ordered to rectify it, a whole chain of consequences follows. You cannot be hired as a civil servant, nor can state-owned enterprises or public institutions hire you. For two years, you are barred from leaving the country and from entering or resuming higher education, and you will be placed on the serious dishonesty blacklist for national defense obligations. So do not sign up thinking, "I'll register first and drop out later if it doesn't suit me."
- Benefit: Under Article 57, Paragraph 1, Item 2 of the Military Service Law, for "draft-eligible citizens who refuse or evade conscription for active duty," the penalty is likewise: "the county-level people's government shall order them to rectify within a specified time limit; if they fail to rectify within the time limit, the county-level people's government shall compel them to perform military service obligations and impose a fine." Paragraph 2 of the same article specifies: "Anyone committing the act under Item 2 of the preceding paragraph who refuses to rectify shall not be recruited as a civil servant or personnel managed with reference to the Civil Servant Law of the People's Republic of China, shall not be recruited or hired as an employee of a state-owned enterprise or public institution, shall not be permitted to leave the country or enter or resume higher education for two years, and shall be included in the list of entities with serious dishonesty in performing national defense obligations to implement joint disciplinary sanctions." Joint disciplinary sanctions mean multiple government departments enforce these restrictions together based on the same list. Article 58 applies the same set of sanctions to those who have already enlisted. If service members refuse to perform duties or desert the military to evade service, the consequences depend on the administrative outcome: those expelled from the military, stripped of military status, or held criminally liable according to law are penalized in accordance with Article 57, Paragraph 2. Among them, those expelled are also fined (nationwide, effective October 1, 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2021). 兵役法（2021 年修订，第五十七、五十八条）. <http://www.mod.gov.cn/regulatory/2021-08/20/content_4892491.htm>（国防部刊登）
- Notes: What is at stake here is personal liberty and employment eligibility, not money. This chain of sanctions applies to only two groups: draft-eligible citizens who refuse or evade conscription for active duty, and service members who are expelled, stripped of military status, or criminally prosecuted after enlisting. Merely missing military service registration is not included here; see item 2 for registration. The prerequisite for punishment is "refusal to rectify," determined and penalized by the county-level people's government. Fine amounts are decided locally and are not listed in this book. Whether to go is up to you, but make up your mind before signing up.

### 4. Enlist in the military as a college student: get up to 20,000 yuan a year in tuition aid, keep your student status for two years after discharge, and access dedicated graduate school quotas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Submit application materials as required by your university after discharge, usually taking one or two trips back and forth.
- In plain terms: If you enlist as an enrolled student, fresh graduate, or admitted freshman, the government covers your tuition for your years of study. Undergraduates and vocational students receive up to 20,000 yuan per year, and graduate students receive up to 25,000 yuan per year, matching whichever is higher between actual tuition and student loans. Your academic enrollment status is preserved while on active duty, and you can resume your studies within two years after discharge. For master's degree exams, there is also a separate admissions quota reserved exclusively for discharged student soldiers.
- Benefit: A 2024 notice from the Ministry of Finance and three other departments specifies three forms of support for college students entering military service: tuition compensation, national student loan repayment, and tuition waivers. Loan repayment means the government pays off your student loan for you. The maximum standards were "adjusted accordingly to not exceed 20,000 yuan per person per year for undergraduate and junior college students, and 25,000 yuan per person per year for graduate students." These new standards took effect in the autumn semester of 2024. The exact subsidy amount is determined by this rule: "calculated according to whichever is higher between the actual tuition paid by the student or the national student loan obtained (including principal and all interest accrued prior to full repayment)." It also adds that "amounts exceeding the ceiling will not be compensated, repaid, or waived." Therefore, the annual payment takes the higher of actual tuition or student loans, then takes the smaller figure when compared against the ceiling. For instance, if annual tuition is 5,000 yuan, you receive 5,000 yuan. The funded duration is based on years of academic study, not years of military service. The 2019 student financial aid management measures state: "The prescribed schooling duration completed prior to enlistment serves as the period for tuition compensation or national student loan repayment; the remaining prescribed schooling duration completed upon resuming studies after discharge serves as the period for tuition waiver." For fresh graduates who enlist, tuition for their entire degree program is reimbursed. Regarding enrollment status, the Veterans Support Law states: "Active-duty service members who were admitted to or enrolled in regular institutions of higher education prior to enlistment shall have their admission qualifications or student status reserved during active service, shall be permitted to enter or resume studies within two years after discharge, and may transfer to other majors at the institution in accordance with relevant state regulations." The law also notes that higher education institutions may recruit veterans through separate quotas and independent admissions. For graduate school, the General Office of the Ministry of Education's 2026 notice outlines the "Discharged College Student Soldier Plan." It specifies: "Single-listed and specifically dedicated within the total national master's degree enrollment plan; it must not be adjusted into regular plans or other special plans. Admissions institutions independently determine... preliminary examination score requirements (generally not higher than the cutoff for regular plan candidates in the corresponding discipline/major)... candidates with longer active service are prioritized under equal conditions." "Single-listed and specifically dedicated" means these spots are walled off exclusively for discharged student soldiers. In 2026, 532 regular higher education institutions nationwide participate in this plan.

### 5. Only six scenarios allow the government to assign jobs after discharge; those opting for self-employment must register within 30 days
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost involved. Simply bring your discharge certificate and visit the local Civil Affairs Bureau for veterans.
- In plain terms: The notion that “the military guarantees job placement” no longer applies broadly. Currently, only six specific scenarios qualify for government job assignments. Most conscripts who serve two years do not fall into these categories. Those not eligible for government placement are considered self-employed, and they must register within 30 days of discharge. Failing to do so without valid reasons means they forfeit any entitlement to government assistance.
- Benefit: Article 25 of the Regulations on Placement of Veterans specifies six circumstances under which local governments must assign jobs. These include: (1) non-commissioned officers with at least 12 years of service; (2) personnel awarded medals or honorary titles during service; (3) those receiving third-class combat merits or higher; (4) individuals receiving first-class commendations; (5) those disabled at levels 5–8 due to combat; and (6) children of martyrs. The same regulations state that local governments must complete job placement within six months of receiving discharged personnel. Employers must sign medium- or long-term contracts lasting at least three years; for non-commissioned officers with ten-plus years of service, employers must offer open-ended contracts. During the placement period, veterans receive monthly living allowances equal to the local minimum wage. If placement fails due to reasons unrelated to the veteran, they receive 80% of the average wage of comparable workers until employment is secured. Exceptions include individuals expelled from the Communist Party of China or convicted of crimes. Article 51 clarifies that self-employed veterans must register within 30 days of discharge; otherwise, they lose eligibility for government support. (Nationwide, effective 1 September 2024.)

The 30-day registration rule applies solely to self-employed veterans; others must register “within the prescribed timeframe.” Upon registration, veterans receive a preferential treatment certificate, which serves as a prerequisite for subsequent benefits such as pensions, employment support, and educational grants.

### 6. Three Post-Discharge Benefits: One-Time Discharge Allowance, Service Years Counted as Work Tenure, and 20,000 Yuan Annual Tax Deduction for Self-Employment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Upon discharge, the military transfers out your social insurance relations and funds. You then visit the social insurance administration office (the service window) at your resettlement location to handle the transfer and continuation.
- In plain terms: After leaving the military, three key financial benefits apply. First is the one-time discharge allowance for self-directed employment, paid according to your years of active service, with bonus percentages for commendations. Second, military service counts toward your overall work tenure, pooling contribution years from before enlistment and after discharge. Third is tax relief: if you start your own business or shop, you can reduce your taxes by up to 20,000 yuan per year for 3 years, with the ceiling eligible to rise by up to 20%.
- Benefit: For the first benefit, see the *Regulations on the Resettlement of Demobilized Military Personnel*. It states: "For demobilized NCOs and conscripts choosing self-directed employment, a one-time discharge allowance shall be issued based on their active service years." Who pays: "Funded by a dedicated allocation from the central government, with specific standards determined and adjusted periodically by the State Council department in charge of veteran affairs and the Political Work Department of the Central Military Commission, in consultation with the finance department of the State Council..." Bonus percentages have five tiers: receiving a medal or honorary title adds 25%; first-class wartime merit or first-level commendation adds 20%; second-class wartime merit, first-class merit, or second-level commendation adds 15%; third-class wartime merit or second-class merit adds 10%; fourth-class wartime merit or third-class merit adds 5%. The regulation also states: "Veterans who are not immediately employed may apply for unemployment insurance benefits in accordance with the law from the human resources and social security department at their hukou registration location; years of active service are deemed equivalent to contribution payment years, except for veterans resettled via retirement or state care." ### 10. Upgrade your education later: self-taught exams have no age or schooling limits, adult exams and open universities offer real options, only CHSI-verifiable credentials count, and avoid classes promising a "guaranteed pass without exams"

- Cost: Registration and textbook fees cost several hundred yuan per year. The real cost is long-term self-directed study or attending classes in your spare time; part-time upgrades from an associate to a bachelor's degree take at least two and a half years, and the true difficulty lies in staying committed over the long haul.
- In plain terms: If you missed out on higher education at age eighteen, three legitimate pathways remain open: self-taught higher education examinations (Zikao), adult college entrance examinations, and the Open University. Self-taught exams have no age or prior schooling limits, though currently enrolled full-time students cannot register. A diploma only counts if it can be verified on CHSI (the national student credential database); once registered there, the school cannot alter it. Never hand over money to anyone promising "no studying needed" or a "guaranteed pass without taking exams."
- Benefit: For self-taught examinations, see the *Interim Regulations on Higher Education Self-Taught Examinations*. Article 3: "Citizens of the People's Republic of China, regardless of gender, age, ethnicity, race, and received education level, may participate in higher education self-taught examinations in accordance with the provisions of these Regulations." Article 25 outlines three conditions for obtaining a diploma: passing all required courses, completing the graduation thesis or practical tasks, and passing an ideological and moral evaluation. Article 32 addresses compensation: "Upon employment, non-employed individuals shall receive treatment equal to that of graduates from ordinary institutions of higher learning in the same category." Those who pass the examinations shall be issued corresponding academic qualification certificates or other academic certificates."

Eligible candidates are "employed personnel and other members of society other than currently enrolled students of various higher and secondary schools recognized by the state." "Applicants for high-school-to-bachelor or high-school-to-associate tracks must have graduated from senior secondary schools or hold an equivalent academic background. Applicants upgrading from associate to bachelor's degree must hold... an associate graduation certificate, undergraduate certificate of completion, or higher." ### 11. Enroll in pension and medical insurance yourself if flexibly employed: residency curbs are lifting where you work, phased in across megacities
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: You pay the full premium for pension and medical insurance yourself, calculated from the local contribution base, running over 1,000 yuan a month. - Benefit: The 2027 national civil service recruitment announcement says that, for central organs, "organs directly under them at city (prefecture) level and below mainly recruit fresh college graduates." Question 3 of that year's application guide answers whether a past graduate counts as a fresh graduate. The original text is: "A graduate of an ordinary higher-education institution under the unified national admissions plan who, on leaving school and during the job-seeking period (the state sets the job-seeking period at 2 years), has not been placed in a work unit, and whose hukou, file, and organizational affiliation are still kept at the original school, or kept at graduate-employment authorities at each level (graduate employment guidance and service centers), talent-exchange service agencies at each level, and public employment service agencies at each level, may be treated as a fresh college graduate." The job-seeking period is the 2 years after graduation spent looking for work. So the national exam requires both things at once: no work unit placed during the 2 years, and the file, hukou, and organizational affiliation not transferred to an employer. Each province sets its own exam. Fujian's 2023 notice says: "For that year's fresh graduates, signing an employment agreement or a labor contract, or paying social insurance, does not affect recognition as a fresh graduate; a graduate of the previous year who has work experience and a social insurance payment record may apply for a fresh-graduate post." This covers Fujian's civil service, public institutions, state-owned enterprises, and central-enterprise offices in Fujian. Chongqing's 2025 notice writes the exams separately. For Chongqing's civil service exam, it copies the national-exam sentence above. For Chongqing's public institutions and state-owned enterprises, as long as within 2 years you have not been hired into a staffed post, "signing an employment contract (agreement), work experience, and payment of social insurance do not affect the application" (nationwide, and each province sets its own).

- Notes: The national-exam guide does not say whether signing a labor contract or paying social insurance counts as "being placed in a work unit." If you are not sure, call the consulting phone of the recruiting organ printed on the guide. Question 21 of the guide tells candidates to go straight to the recruiting organ. What is often said online, that paying social insurance means you no longer count as a fresh graduate, is the rule of some exams, not a rule for the whole country. The Fujian and Chongqing notices cited above are only examples. Provinces may change them every year. Follow the announcement of the year you take the exam. People in a grassroots service project such as «三支一扶» can also apply for fresh-graduate posts within 2 years after the term ends. See item 7 of this section (grassroots service projects). The same application guide also states that this exam does not designate a prep book, and does not run or entrust any institution or person to run a prep class. Article 4 of the Regulations on the Recruitment of Civil Servants states that civil servants are recruited by "open examination, strict assessment, equal competition, and admission of the better candidates." So if someone takes money and promises an "internal slot" or "a guaranteed staff post," treat it as a scam and report it. See section 8, item 3 (hard rules on fraud). The national-exam wording here follows the 2027 announcement as of October (10) 2026. In later years, also read that year's announcement.
