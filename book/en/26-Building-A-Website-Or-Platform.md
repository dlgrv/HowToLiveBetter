# 26. Building a website or platform: licenses, filing, and servers

You want to build a website or an app. This section answers 4 things: what kind of business it counts as in law, which permit you need, where the servers sit, and who is responsible for what users post and for the money that comes in. The measure is personal liberty and money. If you are a hired technician, the red lines are in section 11. How to register a company and how to file taxes are in section 12, and this section does not repeat them. A comparison table of the permits, and a decision table for choosing a server, are in [docs/做平台要办哪些证.md](../../docs/research/做平台要办哪些证.md).
### 1. Platforms must never collect payments themselves and then transfer them to sellers; funds must be settled directly via a licensed payment institution
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No monetary cost is involved. However, it requires some time to integrate a product from a licensed payment institution — that is, a company holding a payment license issued by the People’s Bank of China. By using such institution’s split-payment or escrow features, buyer payments are transferred straight to sellers without ever entering the platform’s own accounts.
- In plain terms: If the buyer's money goes into your account first and you then pass it to the seller, you are running a payment business. That business needs approval from the People's Bank of China, and you have to actually put up 100000000 yuan of capital. Doing it without approval gets you shut down, the money you earned taken, and a fine on top. The boss and the person who handled it are fined separately, and a serious case can also mean a criminal sentence. There is only 1 safe way: the money does not enter your account.
- Benefit: According to regulations, a payment business is defined as “transferring monetary funds based on electronic payment instructions submitted by payers or payees.” In other words, it involves moving money on behalf of others per their instructions. To operate such a business, one must obtain a payment license from the People’s Bank of China, and the minimum registered capital must be 100000000 yuan, and that 100000000 yuan must actually be paid into the company account. Unauthorized operation results in immediate closure and seizure of all profits. If illegal gains are 500000 yuan or more, an additional fine of 1 to 5 times that amount is imposed. If there are no illegal gains or they are under 500000 yuan, the fine is 500000 to 2000000 yuan; additionally, a further fine of 100,000 to 500,000 RMB applies to legal representatives and responsible personnel. Criminal law also classifies “illegally conducting fund payment and settlement services” as illegal business activity; serious violations may lead to imprisonment of up to five years, while especially grave cases incur sentences exceeding five years. As of May (5) 2024, the threshold for “serious” violations is defined as total illegal transaction volume exceeding 5 million RMB or illegal profits surpassing 100,000 RMB nationwide.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2023). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例（国务院令第 768 号，第二、六、八、二十七、四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; 中国人民银行 (2024). 非银行支付机构监督管理条例实施细则（中国人民银行令〔2024〕第 4 号，第六十八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; 全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第二百二十五条第三项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; 最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2019). 关于办理非法从事资金支付结算业务、非法买卖外汇刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第一、三、四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Notes: Designing the payment flow so that funds never enter the platform’s account is the sole safe method. In industry parlance, this practice is sometimes called “second clearing,” though this term does not appear in official documents; instead, regulations refer to it as “indirectly engaging in payment services.” The Supreme People’s Court and Supreme People’s Procuratorate explicitly list only three scenarios as illegal: fabricating transactions for cash-out, using corporate accounts for cash-out, and using cheques for cash-out. Platform-based collection and redistribution of payments are not explicitly named, so this description strictly follows statutory wording without asserting that it automatically constitutes a crime.

### 2. Most websites that charge need a license; selling only your own goods, without letting others open a shop, does not; matching a trade is another license; a free site only needs filing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing costs nothing. A value-added telecommunications business license costs money. It governs whether a website that charges users may open. You need a company before you can apply for it. For business only inside a single province, the registered capital is at least 1000000 yuan. For business across provinces, it is at least 10000000 yuan. The review period is 60 days, and the license is valid for 5 years.
- In plain terms: A website that charges users mostly has to get a value-added telecommunications business license first, and an individual cannot apply for it. The Guangdong Communications Administration answered: if you only sell goods on your own website or app, and you do not let others open a shop there, you do not need it. If you only run a mini program or an online shop on WeChat or Alipay, you do not need it either. If you open a paid site without the license you should have, the income is confiscated and a fine is added, and a serious case means the site is shut.
- Benefit: The regulation says: "The state implements a licensing system for commercial internet information services, and a filing system for non-commercial internet information services. Without a license, or without completing the filing, internet information services may not be provided." A site that charges has to get the license first. A site that does not charge has to file first. Without either of the 2, the site cannot open. What counts as commercial? The regulation says it is "providing information, web-page production, or similar services to internet users for a fee through the internet." In short, charging the user. The kind that matches a trade is called online data processing and transaction processing. In the trade it is shortened to EDI. The regulation defines it as "using data and transaction or business-processing application platforms connected to the public communications network or the internet, to provide users with online data processing and transaction or business processing through the public communications network or the internet." It also "includes transaction-processing services, electronic data interchange services, and network or electronic-device data-processing services." That means the website processes data online for the user, or matches both sides of a sale and processes the transaction. Operating without the license, or operating beyond its scope, means the illegal gains are confiscated, and a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains is added. Operating beyond the scope means you have the license, but what you do goes past what the license writes. If there are no illegal gains, or the illegal gains are under 50000 yuan, the fine is 100000 to 1000000 yuan. In a serious case, the website is ordered closed (nationwide). Charging money does not always mean you must get the license. A question-and-answer issued by the Guangdong Communications Administration in October (10) 2025 says that, under a requirement of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the 3 cases below do not need this license. Case 1: a company, on its own website or app, "directly sells its own goods or services, or those of another company, in a self-operated way," and no other unit or individual opens a shop there. Case 2: a company posts only its own information on its own website or app, and does not provide other people with a platform for posting information. Case 3: a company does business only through mini programs, official accounts, video accounts, or online shops on platforms such as WeChat and Alipay, "and has no other independently operated platform." The other way around: as soon as you let others open a shop, or let users post information here, you are outside these 3 cases.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:广东省通信管理局 (2025). 关于办理增值电信业务经营许可证的常见问题解答（2025 年 10 月 17 日，第一项）. <https://gdca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/jyxk/tzgg/art/2025/art_6bcbfa4f041247868c0de08e765b33e9.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（国务院令第 292 号，2024 年 12 月 6 日第二次修订，第三、四、七、十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; 国务院 (2024). 关于修改和废止部分行政法规的决定（国务院令第 797 号，第十八项）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; 国务院 (2000). 电信条例（2016 年第二次修订，第七、九、十三、六十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第六、九、十一、十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Notes: Whether what you want to do is the EDI kind, you match it yourself against the definition in the Classification Catalogue of Telecommunications Services. No official sentence states outright that "an e-commerce platform with matching and fund handling must get an EDI license." The Ministry's guide for applicants only says "apply for the corresponding telecommunications business license according to how the service is defined." So whether to apply can only be judged from the definition. Another point: an individual cannot get this license. The applicant must be a company established according to law. The 3 cases where self-operated business does not need the license come from the Guangdong Communications Administration's question-and-answer. It says this follows a requirement of the Ministry, but the same public wording was not found in other provinces. If you are not sure, call the communications administration where you are first. That question-and-answer also reminds you that you do not need an agent to apply. You submit the materials only on the Ministry's "comprehensive management information system for the telecommunications-services market," and the authority does not charge a fee.

### 3. Get an online cultural business license to run live streams; a private company basically cannot get the permit for audiovisual programs
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The online cultural business license, commonly called the network-culture license (网文证), governs whether you may make money from online performances such as live streams and game commentary. A provincial culture department approves it. You get a result within 20 days after the application is accepted. It is valid for 3 years, and you go to renew it in the 30 days before it expires. The audiovisual-program permit governs whether you may put programs online. It requires the applicant to be a wholly state-owned unit or a state-controlled unit.
- In plain terms: To run live streams or game commentary, you need an online cultural business license issued by the provincial culture department, and the business scope has to state online performance. You get a result within 20 days after acceptance, and the license covers 3 years. You have to keep videos you have already streamed for a full 60 days. If you open without the license, you are told to stop and given a warning, and then fined up to 30000 yuan. If you do not stop, you go on a blacklist.
- Benefit: A unit that does online performance has to get the license first. The regulation says it "shall, under the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture, apply to the provincial culture department for an Online Cultural Business License, and the business scope of the license shall expressly include online performance." Playing a game for people to watch, or explaining it to them, is handled under the same set of rules. The regulation says it is "administered by reference to these measures." Video records of all online performances "shall be kept for no less than 60 days." You keep videos you have streamed for a full 60 days yourself. Doing commercial internet cultural activities without approval brings an order to stop, a warning, and a fine of up to 30000 yuan. If you are told to stop and you still do not, you are put on the cultural-market blacklist. Putting programs online is a different permit. You "shall obtain a Permit for Disseminating Audiovisual Programs over Information Networks." The applicant must "have the status of a legal person, and be a wholly state-owned or state-controlled unit." It has to be a unit wholly owned by the state or controlled by the state. A private company basically cannot get it. Doing it without this permit brings a warning and an order to correct, and a fine of up to 30000 yuan may be added. In a serious case, the penalty follows the regulations on radio and television administration (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:文化部 (2016). 网络表演经营活动管理办法（文市发〔2016〕33 号，第二、四、六、十三、二十条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; 文化部 (2011). 互联网文化管理暂行规定（文化部令第 51 号，2017 年经文化部令第 57 号修订，第七、八、九、十六、二十一、二十八条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; 广电总局、信息产业部 (2007). 互联网视听节目服务管理规定（第 56 号令，2015 年修订，第二、七、八、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 关于加强网络直播规范管理工作的指导意见（国信办发文〔2021〕3 号，第 9 项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Notes: Which business goes with which permit is clearest in the document from the 7 departments. A platform that makes money from live streams needs the online cultural business license plus ICP filing. ICP filing is the step of registering the website with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. Putting programs online needs the audiovisual permit, or completed registration. Publishing news needs an internet news information service license. Also, the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture reposted on the Chinese government website in 2025 is the unrevised text from 2011. A citation should follow the current text of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

### 4. File if the server sits inside China; file an app and a mini program before they are listed; the access provider must itself hold a value-added telecommunications license
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Filing costs nothing. You submit it together with the server, at the service provider, and you usually wait a few days to a few weeks. Filing for an app or a mini program is submitted through the service provider or the app store, and it is finished within 20 working days once the materials are complete. Checking once whether the service provider has the qualification takes 10 minutes.
- In plain terms: If the server sits inside China, you have to file before the site opens. It costs nothing, and it usually takes a few days to a few weeks. Without filing you are first ordered to correct and fined 10000 yuan, and if you do not correct, the site is shut. An app and a mini program also have to be filed first. Without filing, the app store and the mini-program platform are not allowed to list you.
- Benefit: The regulation says: "Without filing, non-commercial internet information services may not be provided inside the People's Republic of China." A website that does not charge users still has to file first, as long as it is placed inside China. Without filing, you are ordered to correct within a time limit, and fined 10000 yuan. If you refuse to correct, the website is closed. The provider that connects your server to the network is also regulated. It "may not provide internet access services for an organization or an individual that has not filed and that engages in non-commercial internet information services." A provider that collects money for you is the same. The regulation says this kind of provider "may not provide access, collection of fees on behalf of others, or similar services to a unit or an individual that has not lawfully obtained an operating license or completed the filing for non-commercial internet information services." That means a regular provider is not allowed to connect a site that has neither a license nor a filing. Also, colocation and a cloud server are called internet data center services in the regulation, and broadband access is called an internet access service. These 2 themselves require a license. The provider has to hold the license first. If the website homepage does not show the filing number, you are ordered to correct, and fined 5000 to 50000 yuan. From September (9) 2023, apps and mini programs also have to be filed. A notice from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology says that the sponsor of an app providing services inside China "may not engage in app internet information services if the filing has not been completed." Distribution platforms include mini-program platforms. The notice requires app stores, mini-program platforms, and phone makers "not to provide network access, distribution, pre-installation, or similar services for an app that has not completed the filing." Once a provincial communications administration receives complete and accurate materials, it "shall complete the filing within 20 working days." The filing number has to be shown in a prominent place in the app. The Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law also states that setting up an app "shall go through licensing or filing with the telecommunications authority in accordance with the relevant state provisions" (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:信息产业部 (2005). 非经营性互联网信息服务备案管理办法（信产部令第 33 号，2024 年经工信部令第 68 号修订，第五、十三、十八、二十二条）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; 工业和信息化部 (2017). 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第二十、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; 工业和信息化部 (2015). 电信业务分类目录（2015 年版，B11、B14、B21、B25）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html> ; 工业和信息化部 (2023). 关于开展移动互联网应用程序备案工作的通知（工信部信管〔2023〕105 号，第二部分第一、七、八、九项，第三部分第二项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6897341.htm> ; 全国人大常委会 (2022). 反电信网络诈骗法（第二十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>
- Notes: Before you talk price, check whether the service provider has the license. Look it up in the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology's comprehensive management information system for the telecommunications-services market, at tsm.miit.gov.cn, by the company's full name or by the license number. Look up a website filing at beian.miit.gov.cn. A "machine room" without the matching license leaves you, on the day something goes wrong, not even knowing whom to appeal to. If the website is already filed, the app filing only adds the app's information. You do not fill in the sponsor's identity again. Putting the website's machines abroad does get around website filing, but an app you want listed in a domestic app store cannot get around it. If the users are inside China and the money is inside China, not 1 of the duties written in this section, item 5 through this section, item 10 drops away, and a further cost of sending data abroad is added on top.

### 5. Requiring sellers to join the platform means the platform must verify identities, register data, report information, and retain records for three years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No direct monetary cost. However, the platform must perform background checks on sellers’ qualifications and maintain a registration database. Tax-related information must be reported quarterly, while transaction data must be kept for at least three years. The main challenge is that this is an ongoing obligation rather than a one-time task.
- In plain terms: If you let other people sell on your platform, you first check the seller's identity, address, contact details, and license, then register them and open a file. Once the file is open, you go back and check again at least every 6 months. Who the seller is has to be reported to market supervision, the tax situation is reported to the tax authority every quarter, and the goods and transaction records are kept for 3 years. Miss 1 of these and you are first ordered to correct. If you do not correct, the fine is 20000 to 100000 yuan.
- Benefit: Legal regulations set specific obligations for platforms. The official wording states that platforms must require sellers to provide authentic identity, address, contact, and licensing information, then verify and register this data while maintaining updated records. This means sellers must submit their basic details and required permits before joining, which you must then confirm and log into a database. Periodic re-verification is also mandatory. The exact interval for re-verification is defined as “at least every six months” under relevant administrative rules. Additionally, you must submit seller identity data to market regulators and tax authorities, as required by tax administration laws. All product, service, and transaction data must be retained for no less than three years from the date each transaction concludes. Non-compliance triggers a rectification order; persistent violations incur fines of 20,000–100,000 yuan, while severe cases may lead to temporary suspension of operations and fines of 100,000–500,000 yuan. Tax reports must be submitted within the month following each quarter’s end, with identical penalties for late submissions nationwide.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第十、二十七、二十八、三十一、八十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 市场监管总局 (2021). 网络交易监督管理办法（市场监管总局令第 37 号，第八、二十四、二十五、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; 国务院 (2025). 互联网平台企业涉税信息报送规定（国务院令第 810 号，第二、三、四、十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; 国家税务总局 (2025). 关于互联网平台企业报送涉税信息有关事项的公告（税务总局公告 2025 年第 15 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Notes: Sellers themselves must also register as market entities. Certain exceptions apply: individuals selling self-produced agricultural or handmade goods, or providing small-scale services that do not require permits, are exempt. The threshold for “small-scale” activities is set at an annual transaction volume of 100,000 yuan under current online trade regulations. Platforms also bear two additional responsibilities: clearly marking registered versus unregistered sellers, and prohibiting the removal of consumer reviews.

### 6. Manage what users post: keep a review system and a report entry, and when you find something illegal, stop it at once and report it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: You have to spend money hiring people to review. This is the platform's largest long-running expense. The report entry, and a record of every action you take, have to leave a trace. The hard part is that someone has to watch it every day. Leave it for 1 day and something can go wrong.
- In plain terms: What users post, the platform has to manage. Registration, review of posts, review of comments, a daily patrol, and what to do when something happens all need a written system. Put a report entry where it is easy to see on the page. When you see illegal information, stop it from spreading at once, keep the record, and report it to the authority in charge. If you do not, the fine can be 50000 to 500000 yuan, and a serious case is more.
- Benefit: The regulation says the platform "shall fulfill the primary responsibility for managing information content." What users post is for the platform itself to manage. In particular it must "improve the systems for user registration, account management, review of published information, review of comments and replies, ecological management of pages, real-time patrol, emergency handling, and handling of online rumors and of information from black-market chains." From registration, posts, and comments, through the daily patrol and handling when something happens, every link needs a rule written down. It also "shall set a convenient complaint and report entry in a prominent position." The report button has to sit where a user can see it at a glance and can tap it. On finding illegal information, it "shall immediately take handling measures according to law, keep the relevant records, and report to the relevant authority." The Cybersecurity Law is more specific. It says: "On finding information whose publication or transmission is prohibited by laws or administrative regulations, it shall immediately stop transmission of that information, take handling measures such as removal, prevent the information from spreading, keep the relevant records, and report to the relevant authority." If this is not done, you are ordered to correct, given a warning, and given a circulated notice, and a fine of 50000 to 500000 yuan may be added. If you refuse to correct, or the case is serious, the fine is 500000 to 2000000 yuan. You may also be ordered to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the license or the business license revoked. The person directly in charge is fined another 50000 to 200000 yuan (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家互联网信息办公室 (2019). 网络信息内容生态治理规定（网信办令第 5 号，第八、九、十、十六、三十七条）. <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第四十九、六十九条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国务院 (2000). 互联网信息服务管理办法（第十五、十六、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Notes: You do not have to invent the review rules. Copy them. Which 9 kinds of content may not be posted is listed clearly in Article 15 of the Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services. Live streaming adds 3 more things. You must "set up a live-stream content review platform." You must be able to cut off a stream that is on the air at any moment. The article calls this "instant blocking." You also keep the live-stream content and the logs for 60 days. Video records of online performances are likewise no less than 60 days.

### 7. If you offer posting or instant messaging, require the user to provide real identity information
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: You have to connect a real-name check channel, and this kind of channel charges per use. On the product, design it as "real name in the back, voluntary on the front": the back end stores the real name, and the page shows a nickname.
- In plain terms: If your site can take posts, or can chat, you have to ask for the user's real identity information when the user opens the service. If the user does not give it, you cannot let them use it. The live-stream rule is that the back end stores the real name, and the nickname shown on the page is up to the user. You can check with a mobile number. If you do not do real-name checks, you are first ordered to correct. If you do not correct, the fine is 50000 to 500000 yuan, and the site may also be shut.
- Benefit: The law says: "Where a network operator handles network access or domain-name registration for a user, handles network-entry procedures for a fixed telephone, a mobile telephone, or the like, or provides the user with services such as information publishing or instant messaging, it shall, when signing an agreement with the user or confirming that the service will be provided, require the user to provide real identity information. If the user does not provide real identity information, the network operator may not provide the relevant service." If you did not ask for a real name, or the user did not give a real identity and you still let them use it, you are ordered to correct. If you refuse to correct, or the case is serious, the fine is 50000 to 500000 yuan. You may also be ordered to suspend the relevant business, to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the license or the business license revoked. The person directly in charge is fined another 10000 to 100000 yuan. Live-stream services follow the principle of "real name in the back, voluntary on the front," and do "authentication of real identity information based on a mobile telephone number or a similar method." That means the platform's back end stores the user's real name, the nickname shown on the page is chosen by the user, and the check uses something like a mobile number. Live-stream selling is stricter. It is "authentication of real identity information based on identity-document information, the unified social credit code, and the like." The unified social credit code is the string of numbers on the business license (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十六、六十四条；2016 年文本为第二十四、六十一条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2016). 互联网直播服务管理规定（第十二、十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; 国家网信办等七部门 (2021). 网络直播营销管理办法（试行）（国信办发文〔2021〕5 号，第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Notes: The article numbers have moved. The Cybersecurity Law was amended in 2025, and it takes effect on January (1) 1, 2026. The real-name article moved from Article 24 to Article 26. The penalty moved from Article 61 to Article 64. The wording of the articles did not change. If the materials you have still cite the old article numbers, change them.

### 8. No live streaming accounts may be created for anyone under 16; tipping limits are set by age group
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: There are no direct costs. The process simply requires adding two extra steps during registration and payment: first verify the user’s age, then confirm that a guardian has given consent.

- In plain terms: People younger than 16 cannot sign up for a live streaming account on this platform. Those aged 16 or older must have their identity verified and must also obtain permission from a parent or other guardian. Tipping is restricted based on age: children under 8 are not allowed to tip at all; those aged 8–16 may tip only with guardian consent; and anyone over 16 may tip only with guardian consent or proof of income. If a minor spends money without a guardian’s approval, the guardian can request a refund, and a court is required to grant it. A total of 2020 such cases have been reported to date.

- Benefit: The law clearly states that “online live streaming service providers must not offer account registration services to minors under 16; when providing such services to minors aged 16 or older, they must verify their identity and obtain consent from a parent or other guardian.” It also mandates “a dynamic verification mechanism for the real identity information of live streamers,” meaning platforms must be able to recheck a streamer’s identity at any time. Additionally, platforms must “reasonably limit the maximum amount a minor may spend per transaction and per day,” establishing spending caps based on age. Tipping rules are similarly tiered: “Tipping services must not be offered to minors under 8; for those aged 8–16, consent from a guardian is required; for those over 16, consent or proof of income is required.” Finally, if a minor lacking full legal capacity spends money without guardian approval, a court must return those funds to the guardian. Minors lacking full legal capacity are defined as those who cannot make independent decisions under the law.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2020). 未成年人保护法（第七十四、七十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; 国务院 (2023). 未成年人网络保护条例（国务院令第 766 号，第三十一、四十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; 中央网信办秘书局 (2026). 关于加强网络直播打赏规范管理的通知（第 7 项）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; 最高人民法院 (2020). 关于依法妥善审理涉新冠肺炎疫情民事案件若干问题的指导意见（二）（法发〔2020〕17 号，第九条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>

- Notes: In any dispute involving refunds, rulings must favor the minor. The wording of this policy has changed over time. In 2022, four government agencies and the National Radio and Television Administration both stated that minors were completely prohibited from tipping. However, a new notice issued in April 2026 introduced age-based limits at 8 and 16 years old, without explicitly revoking earlier rules or explaining how they relate. Therefore, the claim that “minors are entirely barred from tipping” is no longer accurate.

### 9. Act on an infringement notice in time, and restore the content if there is no follow-up within 15 days after you forward the statement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. You need a fixed process: receive the notice, forward it to the seller, receive the seller's objection, and make the result public. Keep a record of every step.
- In plain terms: If someone sends an infringement notice and attaches preliminary evidence, you delete the thing, block it, or cut the link in time, and you forward the notice to the seller at the same time. If you drag and do not handle it, you and the seller pay together for the extra part of the loss. After the seller objects, you forward the objection to the other side and wait 15 days. If the other side has neither complained nor sued, you put the thing back.
- Benefit: The rights holder "has the right to notify the e-commerce platform operator to take necessary measures such as deletion, blocking, disconnecting a link, and ending the transaction and the service. The notice shall include preliminary evidence that infringement is made out." The rights holder is the owner of a right such as a trademark, a copyright, or a patent. Sending 1 sentence does not count. Preliminary evidence has to be attached. After the platform receives the notice, it "shall take the necessary measures in time, and forward that notice to the operator on the platform. If it does not take the necessary measures in time, it bears joint and several liability with the operator on the platform for the enlarged part of the harm." If you drag and do not handle it, you and the seller pay together for the extra part of the loss. After the seller objects, the platform "shall promptly end the measures it took if, within 15 days after the forwarded statement reaches the intellectual-property rights holder, it has not received notice that the rights holder has complained or sued." That means you forward the seller's objection to the rights holder and wait 15 days. If the other side has neither complained nor sued, you restore what was deleted or taken down. If the platform knows, or ought to know, of infringement and does not handle it, it bears joint and several liability with the infringer. The intellectual-property department can also order you to correct within a time limit. If you do not correct in time, the fine is 50000 to 500000 yuan, and in a serious case 500000 to 2000000 yuan. The other way around: "A person who maliciously sends a wrong notice and causes loss to an operator on the platform bears double the liability for compensation" (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2018). 电子商务法（第四十二、四十三、四十四、四十五、八十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百九十五、一千一百九十六、一千一百九十七条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: If you are not an e-commerce platform and you are only an ordinary website, follow the notice-and-takedown rule in the Civil Code. The difference is how long you wait after the other side objects. The E-commerce Law gives a clear 15 days. The Civil Code only writes "a reasonable period." Also, making public the notice you received, the seller's objection, and the result of what you did is an express requirement of Article 44 of the E-commerce Law. If the platform itself organizes uploads of pirated copies, or knows of infringement and still leaves it up, the rule of "delete it when the notice arrives" does not protect it. See section 9, item 24 (fan-translated comics, and reposted films and TV shows).

### 10. Personal information must not be casually transferred overseas; legal conditions and thresholds apply
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No cost involved. The simplest solution is to keep user data within China. If transfer overseas is unavoidable, follow standard contracts or obtain proper certification.

- In plain terms: Sending information about users inside China to a machine abroad is called an export, and putting the server abroad counts the same. In general you have to pass a security assessment by the cyberspace authority, or get a certification, or sign a standard contract. If the running total for the year is under 100000 people and does not include sensitive information, all 3 of those are waived. You also have to tell the user who abroad receives it and what they will use it for, and get that person's separate consent.

- Benefit: To lawfully provide personal information to overseas entities — whether to foreign companies or foreign servers — at least one of four conditions must be met: passing a security assessment by the Cyberspace Administration; obtaining personal information protection certification; signing a standard contract drafted by the Cyberspace Administration; or meeting other requirements set by laws or administrative regulations. Additionally, you must inform users of the overseas recipient’s name, contact details, processing purpose, methods, and types of data involved, and obtain their separate consent. Threshold limits apply based on cumulative user counts. For most businesses not classified as critical information infrastructure operators, the exemption threshold is “fewer than 100,000 personal records (excluding sensitive information) transferred overseas in a calendar year.” Below this threshold, no assessment, contract, or certification is required. Between 100,000 and 1,000,000 records, a standard contract or certification is mandatory. For over 1,000,000 records, or when sensitive information of more than 10,000 individuals is involved, a formal security assessment is required (effective nationwide since March 2024).

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第三十八、三十九、四十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2024). 促进和规范数据跨境流动规定（网信办令第 16 号，第三、四、五、七、八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2022). 数据出境安全评估办法（网信办令第 11 号，第四条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>

- Notes: Hosting servers overseas does not equate to “data never leaving China.” If a user registers within China and their data is sent to foreign servers, this is still a cross-border transfer. Certain scenarios are exempt from assessment, certification, or standard contracts: cross-border shopping, shipping, or booking flights/hotels, provided these actions are necessary to fulfill a contract involving the user. Note also that user counts are calculated on a cumulative basis starting from January (1) 1 of each year, not as a rolling 12-month period.

### 11. Choose a server by whether you can stand the downtime first, then by whether anyone will run it, and compare prices last
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A cloud server from a mainstream cloud vendor is several hundred to several thousand yuan a month. A VPS from a small provider starts at a few tens of yuan a month. A VPS is a small virtual server. Colocation of a dedicated server starts at several thousand yuan a year, and you buy the hardware yourself.
- In plain terms: The difference among the 3 tiers is not the price. It is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. A large vendor has a filing channel, the qualifications are public, a failure comes with a notice and a compensation clause, and you can find a person. A small provider is half the price, but when it oversells, disappears, or gets cut off by the upstream, you do not even know whom to look for. A static site with no user data is enough on a VPS of a few tens of yuan.
- Benefit: The difference among the 3 tiers is not the price. It is what you have in hand on the day something goes wrong. A mainstream cloud vendor has a filing channel, the qualifications are public, a failure comes with a notice, the contract has a compensation clause, and when something goes wrong you can find a person. A small provider's price can be half as much, but when it oversells, disappears, or gets cut off by the upstream, you have nobody to appeal to. So before you talk price, check in the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology system whether it holds a value-added telecommunications business license. Colocation of a dedicated server pays only when 2 conditions both hold: the bandwidth or the hardware is clearly cheaper, and you have your own people to run it. How to choose: a static site with no user data is enough on the cheapest VPS. If there is user registration and there are transactions, choose a cloud server that can scale and can take snapshot backups, and keep the backups in at least 2 places.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:作者经验，无直接文献；服务商资质与备案的法定要求见本节第 4 条，日志留存 6 个月和等级保护义务见第 11 节第 16 条
- Notes: The above is a judgment from experience. There is no literature behind it. The only thing you can actually check is the service provider's license. If you want to save money, save it on the configuration and the bandwidth. Do not save it on the qualification.

### 12. If user information on the website or app leaks, remedy it at once, notify the users and the authority, and report an attack to the police
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Write a plan in advance for the day something happens: who decides, which hole to plug first, and how the notice to users is worded. The hard part is that the instinct in that moment is to cover it up and not let the users know.
- In plain terms: If user information leaks, or may leak, you remedy it at once and notify the authority in charge and the users who are affected. If you cover it up and do not report, you are first ordered to correct. If you do not correct, the fine is up to 1000000 yuan, and a serious case is a fine of up to 50000000 yuan. If a user is harmed by it, you pay unless you can prove you were not at fault. If you had protections in place, handled it under the plan, and reported in time, the case can be treated more lightly, or not pursued, depending on the circumstances.
- Benefit: The Personal Information Protection Law says: "Where a leak, alteration, or loss of personal information occurs or may occur, the personal-information handler shall immediately take remedial measures, and notify the department performing personal-information protection duties and the individuals." The notice has to write 3 things: which kind of information leaked, the cause, and the harm that may follow; what remedy you took, and what the user can do to reduce the harm; and your contact details. If your remedy really can avoid the harm, you may skip notifying the user. But if the authority believes harm may result, it has the right to require you to notify. The Regulations on the Administration of Network Data Security say the notice may use "a telephone call, a text message, an instant-messaging tool, email, or a public announcement." If while handling it you find a lead that a crime may be involved, report it to the public security organ and the state security organ. If you do not perform these duties, you are ordered to correct, warned, and the illegal gains are confiscated, and an app that broke the rule can be ordered to suspend or end the service. If you refuse to correct, the fine is up to 1000000 yuan, and the person directly in charge is fined 10000 to 100000 yuan. In a serious case, the fine is up to 50000000 yuan or up to 5% of the previous year's turnover, and the person in charge is fined 100000 to 1000000 yuan. On the civil side, if a user is harmed by this, you "shall bear tort liability such as damages if you cannot prove that you were not at fault." A leak on a large scale also has to be reported on time. The measures of the Cyberspace Administration of China provide that a leak of personal information of 1000000 or more people counts as a "fairly large cybersecurity incident." For this kind of incident, an ordinary website or app reports to the cyberspace department of the province where it is, "no later than 4 hours," and a channel for it is the 12387 hotline. Within 30 days after the handling ends, you also submit a summary report. Late reporting, omitted reporting, false reporting, or concealed reporting that causes a serious harmful result is punished more heavily. The other way around: if reasonable and necessary protection was already in place, the case was handled under the plan, and it was reported in time, "it may be treated more lightly, or not pursued, depending on the circumstances" (nationwide, the reporting measures from November (11) 2025).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法（第五十七、六十六、六十九条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; 全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十七、四十四、七十一条；2016 年文本为第二十五、四十二条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; 国务院 (2024). 网络数据安全管理条例（国务院令第 790 号，第十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11646/202410/content_6980863.html> ; 国家互联网信息办公室 (2025). 国家网络安全事件报告管理办法（第四、八、九、十、十一条，附件《网络安全事件分级指南》）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-09/15/c_1759583017717009.htm>
- Notes: A small site that leaks several hundred people, or several thousand, still has to remedy and notify under the Personal Information Protection Law. It just does not reach the 4-hour reporting line. When you notify users, write the things they can do in concrete terms: which password to change, and to watch for a phone call from someone pretending to be customer service. Keep logs for a full 6 months, so that when something happens you can see where it leaked. See section 11, item 16 (keep logs as graded protection requires). How users protect themselves after a leak is in section 14.

### 13. If you sell an auto-renewing membership, remind the user prominently before signup and before each charge, and make the cancel entry easy to find
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. On the signup page, state the auto-renewal plainly, send 1 separate reminder before each charge, and put 1 button in the account center that can cancel at any time. The hard part is that some users will cancel as soon as the reminder arrives.
- In plain terms: If you sell an auto-renewing membership, remind the user in a way that stands out, both before the user turns it on and before each charge. During the membership, give 1 entry that can cancel or change it at any time. It has to be easy to find and easy to tap, and you may not charge an unreasonable extra fee. If you take the money without the reminder, the fine can be 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. If there are no illegal gains, the fine is up to 300000 yuan.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Transactions say that, where a service is provided by automatic extension, automatic renewal, or a similar method, you "shall, before the consumer accepts the service and before the date of the automatic extension, automatic renewal, or similar step, draw the consumer's attention in a prominent way, and leave the choice to the consumer." During the service you must also "provide the consumer with a prominent and simple option to cancel or change at any time, and must not charge an unreasonable fee." The regulations implementing the Law on the Protection of Consumer Rights and Interests have the same reminder requirement. If the reminder is not given, the penalty follows those implementing regulations: an order to correct, and a warning, confiscation of the illegal gains, and a fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains are allowed. If there are no illegal gains, the fine is up to 300000 yuan. In a serious case, you are ordered to suspend business for rectification, and the business license is revoked. If you do not give a convenient cancel entry, you are ordered to correct within a time limit, and a fine of up to 30000 yuan is allowed (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家市场监督管理总局 (2021, 2025 修正). 网络交易监督管理办法（总局令第 37 号，第十八、四十一条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html> ; 国务院 (2024). 消费者权益保护法实施条例（国务院令第 778 号，第十条第二款、第五十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202403/content_6940159.htm>
- Notes: The rules do not say what a "prominent way" looks like in detail. The safe practice is to send 1 separate reminder before each charge, and keep the record of sending it, so you can produce the evidence if someone complains. How to turn auto-renewal off from the user's side is in section 5, item 1 (turn off every auto-renewal).
