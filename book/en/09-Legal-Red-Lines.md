# 9. Legal red lines ordinary people often cross

This section runs two ledgers: your personal liberty, and your money. The acts below are ones ordinary people often treat as “no big deal,” yet they can bring detention, fines, and in serious cases prison. Each item gives the concrete figures in the statutes, plus cases published through official channels. The Public Security Administration Punishment Law cited here is the 2025 revision, in force from 1 January 2026; article numbers differ from the old law. The Criminal Law citations follow a consolidated text carried by the Beijing Public Security Traffic Management Bureau, marked as amended under Amendment (XI); the body notes the matching amendment where needed.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Posting and forwarding online**: Do not forward unverified disaster, epidemic, or police news (item 1), do not forward content that insults heroes and martyrs (item 2), close foreign-site material after viewing (item 3), do not send pornographic videos into groups (item 4).

**Helping others take money, loan fraud, and insurance fraud**: Part-time work that uses your card to receive money (item 5), packaging materials to cheat loans (item 6), do not withdraw from a found bank card (item 7), claim discounts under your real identity (item 8), do not lend your ID card (item 16), fabricating accidents to cheat claims (item 21).

**Small everyday matters**: Do not throw anything from balconies or windows (item 9), do not buy imitation guns online (item 10), register drones under a real name first (item 11), card games without a rake or organizing for pay (item 13), do not eat wild game (item 14), never electrofish at any time (item 27), do not light fires in forest or grassland when visiting graves or cooking outdoors (item 28).

**Conflicts and the police**: Do not detain people to collect debts (item 15), cooperate when police stop you (item 17), count the cost before hitting someone (item 19), do not carry telescopic batons or stun devices for self-defense (item 26).

**Sex, children, and the body**: Do not secretly film others (item 12), no sexual relations with anyone under 14 (item 18), if you cannot raise a child use civil-affairs registration (item 20), do not sell your own organs (item 22), do not assume solicitation ends in a fine alone—default is detention (item 23).

**Copyright and derivative works**: Fan translation, reposting, and pirated e-books (item 24), get permission before selling fan works for money (item 25).

**Exams**: Do not take a test for someone else, hire a proxy, or buy or sell answers (item 29).
### 1. Do not share unverified messages about disasters, epidemics, or police incidents in groups; do not photoshop images or use AI to generate fake scene pictures
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No cost involved. Simply check whether the message is an official announcement before sharing. If you’re unsure, don’t share it, and avoid editing images or adding phrases like “allegedly.” The real challenge is resisting the urge to be the first person to spread the news.
- In plain terms: A message about a disaster, epidemic, or police incident that you know is false and still forward is punished as heavily as if you invented it. Even if you did not invent it, the same applies: detention for 5 to 10 days, and a further fine of up to 1000 yuan. If you seriously disrupt social order, the sentence is up to 3 years; if serious consequences follow, 3 to 7 years. Photoshopped or AI-made fake scene pictures all count as inventing it yourself.
- Benefit: Those who deliberately spread rumors or make false reports about disasters, epidemics, or police incidents to disrupt public order can be detained for 5 to 10 days and fined up to 1,000 yuan. For less serious cases, the penalty is detention for under 5 days or a fine under 1,000 yuan. Creating and posting fake reports about such incidents online also carries a maximum 3-year prison term, even if you know the content is untrue. This applies only when the actions cause significant disruption to social order, as defined by law. If serious consequences arise, the sentence rises to 3 to 7 years nationwide.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，第二十九条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2015). 刑法修正案（九）（第三十二条，即刑法第二百九十一条之一第二款）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180205_364562.shtml>；全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百九十一条之一）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>（北京市公安交通管理局转载）
- Notes: Sharing known false content carries the same legal penalties as creating it yourself; claiming “I just shared it” offers no exemption. Photoshopped images and AI-generated fake scene pictures are also considered self-created content. In 2025, China’s Ministry of Public Security released multiple batches of typical cases involving online rumors about disasters and emergencies. At the time of writing, the ministry’s website was inaccessible, so no specific examples were cited.

### 2. Never post or forward memes, images, or comments that insult heroes and martyrs, and never joke about the national flag or anthem
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. When it comes to heroes, martyrs, the national flag, national emblem, or national anthem, do not comment, do not edit images, and do not forward. The hard part is holding your tongue when someone else starts joking in a group chat.
- In plain terms: Insulting heroes and martyrs or denying their deeds brings 5 to 10 days of detention or a fine of 1,000 to 3,000 yuan for minor offenses, and 10 to 15 days of detention for heavier ones. Desecrating the national flag in public or mocking the national anthem brings up to 15 days of detention. If circumstances are deemed serious, both carry prison sentences of up to 3 years. In one case, posting just 2 messages defaming agronomist Yuan Longping in a 499-person WeChat group resulted in 6 months of public surveillance—not jail time, but strictly restricted freedom.
- Benefit: Using insults or fabrication to infringe upon the name, portrait, reputation, or honor of heroes and martyrs in a way that harms public interest triggers administrative penalties: 5 to 10 days of detention, or a fine of 1,000 to 3,000 yuan. Blaspheming or denying their deeds and spirit, or producing and spreading content that glorifies wars of aggression to disrupt public order, falls into this same tier. For relatively serious circumstances, penalties rise to 10 to 15 days of detention, plus an optional fine of up to 5,000 yuan. When circumstances are classified as "serious", it becomes a criminal offense punishable by fixed-term imprisonment of up to 3 years. Desecrating the national flag or emblem in public, or altering the lyrics and music of the national anthem or singing it in a distorted, derogatory manner, also carries up to 3 years in prison for serious cases. If an incident involving the national flag or anthem does not meet the criminal threshold, it is handled under the Law on the National Flag and the Law on the National Anthem: minor flag violations bring administrative detention of up to 15 days; anthem violations bring a warning or detention of up to 15 days. Official cases: a person surnamed Xiao posted 2 messages defaming Yuan Longping in a 499-person village WeChat group and was sentenced to 6 months of public surveillance (a criminal penalty that restricts personal movement without imprisonment). Another person surnamed Qiu posted 2 messages distorting the deeds of border defense soldiers on a Weibo account with more than 2.5 million followers, receiving an 8-month prison sentence and a court order to issue a public apology in national media.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，第三十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2018). 英雄烈士保护法（第二十二、二十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/gyssshmhsh/201912/t20191202_440079.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2020). 刑法修正案（十一）（第三十五条，即刑法第二百九十九条之一）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202012/t20201227_503682.shtml>；全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第二百九十九条、第二百九十九条之一）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院 (2022). 涉英烈权益保护十大典型案例（案例二、案例三）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/382301.html>；全国人大常委会 (2020 修正). 国旗法（第二十三条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80808175265dd40175e4c1504b0f2e>（国家法律法规数据库）；全国人大常委会 (2017). 国歌法（第十五条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf85f470a2d>（国家法律法规数据库）
- Notes: This provision in the Public Security Administration Punishments Law was added during its 2025 revision. Prior to that, such conduct was pursued either through the Criminal Law or via civil public interest litigation filed by the procuratorate. In the eyes of the court, a WeChat group with several hundred members qualifies as a public venue.

### 3. After viewing material on foreign websites, close the page — do not screenshot, forward, move it into groups, or chime in in the comments
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Closing the page ends it. Do not archive, forward, or move it onto domestic platforms. Do not use an account tied to your identity to post political content on foreign platforms over a long period. Do not take manuscript fees or funding from foreign institutions, organizations, or individuals. The hard part is not screenshotting or sharing when something jumps out at you.
- In plain terms: These statutes punish spreading outward, not looking. Forwarding, moving into a group, or chiming in in the comments all count as dissemination when a case is built. If it reaches inciting subversion or secession, the sentence is up to 5 years; ringleaders get 5 years or more. Books and audiovisual material on terrorism or extremism are the exception: knowingly keeping them, when circumstances are serious, brings up to 3 years.
- Benefit: Using rumor, defamation, or other means to incite others to subvert state power or overthrow the socialist system brings up to 5 years. Ringleaders (the statute’s term) or those whose crimes are major get 5 years or more; fixed-term imprisonment tops out at 15 years. Inciting secession or undermining national unity carries the same terms. Doing either in collusion with foreign institutions, organizations, or individuals is punished more heavily. Institutions, organizations, or individuals at home or abroad who fund others to do so: those directly responsible get up to 5 years; when circumstances are serious, 5 years or more. Stealing, spying out, buying, or illegally providing state secrets or intelligence for foreign institutions, organizations, or persons brings 5 to 10 years. Within that item, especially serious circumstances bring 10 years or more or life imprisonment; lighter circumstances bring up to 5 years. Crimes endangering national security always add deprivation of political rights for 1 to 5 years, during which voting rights and eligibility for public office are cancelled. Confiscation of property may also be imposed, taking personal property for the state. Administrative rules also apply. No individual or organization may use the network to engage in activities that endanger national security, honor, or interests. Nor may they incite subversion of state power or overthrow of the socialist system. Nor may they incite secession or undermine national unity. Publishing or transmitting such information is punished under the relevant laws and administrative regulations (nationwide). Another class does not need dissemination to be complete. Knowingly and illegally holding material that promotes terrorism or extremism, when circumstances are serious, is enough for a conviction. That material includes books, audio-video materials, or other items. The term is up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention (short custody), or public surveillance (not locked up but movement restricted). A fine is imposed together with or instead of imprisonment — sentenced and fined, or only fined. The basis is Criminal Law Article 120-6, added by Amendment IX.

- Sources:全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第四十五、五十五、五十六、一百零三、一百零五、一百零六、一百零七、一百一十一、一百一十三条，这几条未经修正案修改）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第十三条第二款、第七十一条第一项）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2015). 刑法修正案（九）（新增第一百二十条之六）. 国家信访局转载全文 <https://www.gjxfj.gov.cn/gjxfj/fgwj/flfg/webinfo/2017/03/1601761496614423.htm>
- Notes: The laws cited here punish inciting others, spreading outward, and providing to others; mere viewing is not among them. Material on terrorism or extremism is the statute’s stated exception: merely keeping it can convict (Article 120-6). So that class must never be archived. Once you have seen it, stop there: no screenshot archive, no forward, no comment, no pulling others to forward. Forwarding and chiming in in comments both count as dissemination when a case is built; “I only forwarded it” does not excuse you. Doing it in collusion with foreign institutions, organizations, or individuals is punished more heavily. When you are unsure whether something is true, still more reason not to join the forward — see item 1 in this section (do not forward unverified messages). How tools for bypassing the firewall are punished is in Section 11, item 11. Spreading this kind of content brings no real benefit and costs prison time, deprivation of political rights, and confiscation of property. This book has not obtained judgment documents checkable through official channels, so it follows the statutes only and attaches no cases.

### 4. Watch adult videos privately if at all (except anything involving minors); never post them to group chats, sell "resources," or create sharing groups
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. Do not forward them, do not upload them to group files, and never serve as the owner or administrator of such groups.
- In plain terms: Private viewing is not covered by penalty clauses, but anything involving minors is illegal even to possess. Posting to a group chat counts as dissemination—the law specifies no minimum count—risking 10 to 15 days of administrative detention and a fine of up to 5,000 yuan. Serious circumstances carry up to 2 years in prison, while selling files for profit can lead to life imprisonment. Setting up a sharing group with 30 or more members makes the creator, administrators, and primary contributors subject to criminal prosecution.
- Benefit: Disseminating obscene information online carries 10 to 15 days of administrative detention, plus a possible fine of up to 5,000 yuan. This provision does not specify a minimum number of offenses; by contrast, the clause penalizing message harassment in the same law explicitly requires "sending repeatedly." In minor cases, the penalty is up to 5 days of detention or a fine of 1,000 to 3,000 yuan. Heavier penalties apply if the content involves minors. If dissemination of obscene materials reaches the threshold of "serious circumstances," it carries a prison term of up to 2 years. Selling or distributing for profit carries a prison term of up to 3 years plus a fine; serious circumstances carry 3 to 10 years, and especially serious circumstances carry 10 or more years or life imprisonment. Establishing a group chat primarily used to disseminate obscene electronic information with 30 or more members is handled as a crime, as is causing severe consequences. The group creator, administrators, and primary contributors are all convicted of the crime of disseminating obscene materials (nationwide). Content involving minors is governed by another law that extends to possession: Article 52 of the Law on the Protection of Minors specifies that "it is prohibited to produce, reproduce, publish, disseminate, or possess obscene and pornographic articles and online information concerning minors." This law does not specify a separate penalty clause for this article.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，第五十条第一款第五项、第八十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；全国人大 (2020). 刑法（根据刑法修正案（十一）修正，第三百六十三、三百六十四条）. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>；最高人民法院、最高人民检察院 (2010). 关于办理利用互联网、移动通讯终端、声讯台制作、复制、出版、贩卖、传播淫秽电子信息刑事案件具体应用法律若干问题的解释（二）（第三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201002/t20100204_25001.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2024 修正). 未成年人保护法（第五十二条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081818f197cf001905e567af635a0>（国家法律法规数据库）
- Notes: Both laws penalize "dissemination"; private viewing on one's own is not covered by the statutory articles. Content involving minors is an exception, as the Law on the Protection of Minors prohibits even possession. Posting to a group chat constitutes online dissemination. Because the statutory text does not specify a frequency or count, sending even once falls within the scope of the article, which follows directly from the wording. The exact threshold for "serious circumstances" is defined by judicial interpretations and has not been verified for specific figures in this section.

### 5. Never take side gigs that use your bank card to receive, withdraw, or transfer money, no matter the payout
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. Walk away from gigs promising "a few hundred a day, just bring your card to the bank."
- In plain terms: Gigs like this pay a few hundred yuan, but the fallout ranges from detention and fines to prison time and a criminal record. In one case, a person brought 1 bank card to assist with transfers and withdrawals, making just over 500 yuan; prosecutors did not indict him, yet police still gave him 10 days of detention and a 1,000 yuan fine. In another case, someone handed their own and others' bank cards and SIM cards to a crime ring to collect and withdraw cash, resulting in a prison sentence of 2 years and 3 months and a fine of 22,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Knowingly assisting in transferring, purchasing, selling, or otherwise concealing or disguising money or property obtained through crime is punishable by fixed-term imprisonment of up to 3 years, and/or a concurrent or independent fine. Official cases: Following instructions, Wang brought an ID card and 1 bank card to a designated location to assist with transfers and cash withdrawals, earning over 500 yuan. While the procuratorate did not prosecute him, the public security bureau imposed 10 days of administrative detention and a 1,000 yuan fine under the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law. Man handed over personal and third-party bank cards and SIM cards to a gang to collect, transfer, and withdraw cash, receiving 2 years and 3 months in prison and a 22,000 yuan fine (national). Laundering funds through cryptocurrency carries the same consequences: acting on instructions from a telecom fraud ring, An and two others used their bank cards to receive funds, bought cryptocurrency on trading platforms, transferred it to designated accounts, and took a percentage cut. Investigators confirmed over 500,000 yuan in defrauded victim funds passed through them. All three were convicted of concealing and disguising criminal proceeds, receiving sentences ranging from 2 years and 8 months to 3 years and 3 months, alongside fines of 30,000 to 40,000 yuan each (Tongzhou, Beijing).

Even in the case above where prosecutors declined to indict, the individual still served 10 days of administrative detention.

### 6. Some people try to get you to “package loan applicants” for them and offer a commission based on the loan amount — don’t fall for it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: There’s no cost at all. Just say no. Never lend your ID card or credit report to anyone, sign any loan contracts you haven’t read, or go to the bank to sign on someone else’s behalf.

- In plain terms: Helping others “package materials” for a loan and taking a cut is treated as fraud. A 126-person gang dressed unemployed people as “prime customers” and cheated more than 30000000 yuan (3000多万) in loans across over 80 bank outlets. Those who signed in person took a 30% kickback on the loan amount; of the 80 already sentenced, 76 got from 1 year 4 months to 6 years 6 months. Whoever’s name the loan is under owns the debt.

- Benefit: Committing fraud to obtain loans, acceptance letters, letters of credit, or guarantees from banks or other financial institutions is a serious crime. If such actions cause substantial financial harm, offenders face up to 3 years in prison plus a fine; if the damage is especially severe, sentences range from 3 to 7 years, also with fines. The threshold for police investigation is a direct financial loss exceeding 500,000 yuan. Those who fabricate investment projects, fake contracts, or forged documents to secure loans commit loan fraud — this crime is punishable by up to 5 years in prison and fines ranging from 20,000 to 200,000 yuan if the amount obtained is 50,000 yuan or more. Larger sums or more serious circumstances lead to sentences from 5 to 10 years, or even life imprisonment. Forging, altering, or selling official government documents or seals carries penalties of up to 3 years; more serious cases result in 3–10 years in prison. A notable case involved 126 criminals who created fake “prime customers” in 16 provinces and tricked over 80 banks out of more than 30 million yuan; 105 “front” applicants were prosecuted. In 2026, 76 of them received sentences of 1 year and 4 months to 6 years and 6 months for loan fraud, while four others were additionally convicted of money laundering and received sentences from 2 years and 4 months to 7 years and 6 months. Under the 2021 amendment to the Criminal Law, anyone who signs a loan contract becomes legally liable for repayment — even if they never intended to pay, the debt and negative credit record remain theirs. Attempts to flee abroad won’t help; fugitives can still be extradited years later. Those serving sentences, under investigation, or involved in civil disputes may be barred from leaving the country. For details on how to spot unauthorized loans taken out in your name, see Section 8, Item 9.

### 7. Do not withdraw from a found bank card; for a found phone or wallet, contact the owner or hand it to police — do not pocket it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money; a few minutes. Call to find the owner, or take the item to a police station.
- In plain terms: Keeping found property for yourself, when the amount reaches “relatively large” and you refuse to hand it over, brings up to 2 years; when the amount is huge, 2 to 5 years. Taking a found bank card to an ATM is heavier: it counts as fraudulently using another’s credit card; a relatively large amount brings up to 5 years plus a fine of 20000 to 200000 yuan (2万 to 20万). For withdrawals, police file the case directly; the owner need not sue first.
- Benefit: Found property should be returned to the owner: notify them promptly to collect it, or deliver it to public security or another department. Keeping someone else’s left property for yourself, when the amount reaches “relatively large” and you refuse to hand it over, is the crime of embezzlement, punishable by up to 2 years. When the amount is huge, 2 to 5 years. Taking someone else’s found credit card to an ATM counts as “fraudulently using another’s credit card.” “Credit card” in the Criminal Law, per the NPC Standing Committee’s 2004 interpretation, means electronic payment cards issued by banks and other financial institutions. Having all or some of the functions of consumer payment, credit lending, transfer and settlement, and depositing and withdrawing cash is enough. So ordinary bank cards that can deposit and withdraw cash are included. When it reaches a crime, it is handled as credit-card fraud: a relatively large amount brings up to 5 years plus a fine of 20000 to 200000 yuan (2万 to 20万). A huge amount or other serious circumstances bring 5 to 10 years plus a fine of 50000 to 500000 yuan (5万 to 50万) (nationwide)

A repairer who agrees with you to fake it is treated as a joint crime. In a Xuancheng, Anhui case, 8 people got 2 to 4 years and each was fined 10000 yuan (1万).

### 25. Get permission or change borrowed character names and relationships before publishing or selling fanfiction

- Cost: Free. Before publishing or selling for profit, seek licensing from the copyright holder of the original work. - Benefit: Jiang Nan's novel *There They Were* used character names from Jin Yong's martial arts novels, including Guo Jing, Huang Rong, Qiao Feng, and Linghu Chong, depicting them as students on a modern college campus. When published in 2002, it carried the subtitle "The College Life of the Condor Heroes." By the time litigation began, the book had gone through multiple editions with over a million copies in circulation. In 2016, Jin Yong sued Jiang Nan and the publishers, seeking 5 million yuan in damages. The court of first instance found no copyright infringement, reasoning that character names, relationships, and personalities divorced from specific plotlines rarely constitute protectable expression under copyright law; however, it ruled that exploiting the original work's fame constituted unfair competition, awarding 1.68 million yuan in damages (calculated as 30% of Jiang Nan's cumulative royalties of 5.6 million yuan) plus 200,000 yuan in legal fees. On appeal, the Guangzhou Intellectual Property Court reversed the copyright finding, ruling that while the plots were not substantially similar, the ensemble cast of over 60 characters—whose names, personalities, interpersonal relationships, and backgrounds reflected Jin Yong's creative selection and arrangement—constituted protectable expression under copyright law, meaning Jiang Nan infringed on four of Jin Yong's novels. The court also held that linking the subtitle to *The Legend of the Condor Heroes* constituted unfair competition. It ordered Jiang Nan to cease unfair competition, publish a formal apology in newspapers, and pay 1.68 million yuan in damages and 200,000 yuan in reasonable expenses, with two publishers jointly liable for 330,000 yuan. The court did not enjoin future publication outright, but ordered that 30% of royalties from any future reprints be paid to the copyright owner. Both parties subsequently petitioned the Guangdong High People's Court for retrial. On September 19, 2025, under court mediation, the parties reached a settlement: they agreed not to characterize the work as "plagiarism"; Jiang Nan acknowledged a lack of copyright legal knowledge, admitted failing to obtain adaptation permission, and conceded this caused harm; he agreed not to reprint the novel in its original form and to remove Jin Yong's original character names and related elements in future editions; both sides confirmed the subtitle constituted unfair competition; Jiang Nan had already paid the 1.68 million yuan damages awarded on appeal; and signing the mediation agreement formally nullified the legal effect of both prior judgments. In official case 1, the person who made fake IDs for gunners got 3 years for forging identity documents. Document issues are in item 16 of this section (do not lend your ID). Paying an agency to get a professional title assessed is in Section 23, item 22 (do not use an agency for title assessment).
