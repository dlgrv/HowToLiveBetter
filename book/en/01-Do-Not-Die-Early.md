# 1. Do Not Die Early

This chapter covers three things: deaths from accidents and injuries such as road crashes, fires, and poisonings; the few vaccines and screenings backed by the strongest evidence; and warning signs in your body that mean you should get checked. The numbers in the Benefit lines come from randomized trials, long-term observational studies tracking large groups of people for years, or official statistics. The figures in the Cost lines are rough estimates based on prevailing market prices, meant only for comparison—do not cite them as exact figures.

The items in this chapter are grouped by topic below, with item numbers in parentheses.

**On the road**: Fasten your seatbelt (Item 1), wear a helmet (Item 2), do not speed or drive drunk (Item 9), use child safety seats (Item 10).

**Fire, gas, and electricity at home**: Install smoke alarms (Item 3), replace gas hoses before they expire (Item 4), do not bring electric bicycles into stairwells or hallways (Item 6), keep fire extinguishers and a first-aid kit ready (Item 26), install the safety guard before using an angle grinder (Item 42), ventilate, test, and ensure protective gear and supervision before entering a septic tank (Item 46).

**Accidents involving children, older adults, and the outdoors**: Install window restrictors (Item 11), keep children in sight when near water (Item 12), train balance if you are 60 or older (Item 13), do not eat wild mushrooms (Item 5), do not pick up discarded metal parts of unknown origin (Item 36), measure ice thickness before stepping onto natural ice (Item 41), eat soaked wood ear mushrooms in the same meal (Item 48).

### 1. Buckle up in both the front and back seats
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Takes an extra 2 seconds every time you get in.
- In plain terms: Wearing a seatbelt in the front seat of a car cuts the chance of dying in a crash by 45%, and by 60% in a light truck. Among people who died in vehicles in the US in 2022 where seatbelt use was known, half were not buckled up.
- Benefit: The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) estimates that wearing a seatbelt reduces the risk of fatal injury to front-seat passenger car occupants by 45%, and by 60% for front-seat occupants of light trucks. Among people who died in passenger vehicles in the US in 2022 whose restraint use was known, 50% were unrestrained. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), seatbelts reduce the risk of death among vehicle occupants by up to 50%. The WHO estimates that 248,099 people died in road traffic crashes in China in 2021, equivalent to 17.4 deaths per 100,000 people. In China's National Bureau of Statistics yearbook, road traffic crash deaths in 2021 were reported as 62,218.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573)：「Among killed passenger vehicle occupants with known restraint use, 48 percent of those in the front row and 60 percent of those in the second row were unrestrained.」<https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; WHO GHO RS_196/RS_198 (China, 2021). <https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/RS_196?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20%27CHN%27> ; 国家统计局 (2022). 中国统计年鉴 2022，表 24-5 道路交通事故情况（2021 年）：死亡人数 62218 人. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/ndsj/2022/html/C24-05.jpg>
- Notes: Buckle up in the back seat as well. In the same report, among second-row fatalities with known belt use, 60% were unrestrained. The figures of 45% and 60% are estimates calculated by NHTSA from crash databases (citing Kahane 2015); they are not from randomized controlled trials. The WHO Chinese fatality figure is an estimate, roughly four times the 62,218 deaths reported in China's statistical yearbook. Because the statistical scopes differ, only the WHO estimate is used here.

### 2. Wear a helmet and fasten the strap when riding a motorcycle or electric bicycle
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 100 to 300 yuan to buy one. Takes 5 seconds to put on each time.
- In plain terms: When motorcycle riders crash, those wearing a helmet with the strap properly fastened suffer significantly fewer deaths and head injuries. The strap must be pulled tight; loosely perching the helmet on your head does not count as wearing it.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review pooled multiple studies together. Motorcycle riders who wear helmets suffer significantly fewer deaths and head injuries. The odds ratio for death is 0.58 (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.68). The odds ratio for head injury is 0.31 (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.38).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Liu BC 等 (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3>
- Notes: The helmet strap must be buckled; hanging it on the handlebars does not count as wearing it. The data above comes from motorcycles. Direct research on electric bicycles is lacking, but findings are extrapolated because crash-related head injury mechanisms are the same. For wearing helmets while skiing, snowboarding, or bicycling, see Section 22, Item 12 (Wear a helmet while skiing or mountain biking). For legal age thresholds for children riding on the road, see Section 30, Item 20 (Riding an electric bicycle on the road under age 16).

### 3. Install smoke alarms; add a carbon monoxide alarm if burning coal or using gas indoors for winter heating
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 30 to 100 yuan per smoke alarm. 50 to 150 yuan per carbon monoxide alarm. Replace the battery once a year after installation.
- In plain terms: Having a working smoke alarm at home clearly reduces deaths in residential fires. In fatal house fires in the US, four in ten started while people were asleep. If you burn coal or use gas indoors during winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm. In China, 11,523 people died of carbon monoxide poisoning in 2018, with about seven in ten deaths during the three winter months happening at home.
- Benefit: A study in North Carolina examined residential fires by comparing affected homes with unaffected ones. Having a working smoke alarm in the home significantly reduced fire deaths, with an odds ratio for death of 0.39 (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.83). In the US from 2018 to 2020, among fatal fires in occupied homes, 24% had no smoke alarm, and 41% started while people were sleeping. In 2018, China reported 11,523 carbon monoxide poisoning deaths. Of these deaths, 72.59% in December, 67.42% in January, and 66.48% in February occurred at home.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Marshall SW 等 (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018-2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf> ; You J 等 (2020). Number of Deaths due to Carbon Monoxide Poisoning by Month and by Place of Death — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2020.008>
- Notes: Reason for grade B: There is only one study on smoke alarms, which compared households that had fires with those that did not, rather than being a randomized controlled trial. For carbon monoxide alarms, there are no direct studies showing reduced mortality; the benefit here is inferred from poisoning deaths being concentrated in winter and at home. 〔11500〕

### 4. Replace gas hoses and stoves when expired, never modify pipes yourself, and decline doorstep sales pitches from the gas company
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A compliant gas hose costs a few tens of yuan. Stoves are replaced once every few years. When replacing them, take a quick look to see if they have expired.
- In plain terms: Replace hoses and stoves when they reach their expiration date—this is an explicit user obligation under China's Regulations on the Administration of Urban Gas. Modifying gas pipes or removing gas meters on your own is strictly prohibited by the regulations. The same regulations also state that gas companies cannot force you to buy their designated products or services. If someone shows up at your door claiming you "must replace" something with their specific product, you can turn them down outright.
- Benefit: The regulations define "timely replacement of gas-burning appliances and connecting hoses that have been phased out by state orders or have reached the end of their service life" as a user obligation: once stoves and hoses reach their lifespan or are officially phased out, you must replace them. The regulations also classify "installing, modifying, or dismantling indoor gas facilities and gas metering devices without authorization" as prohibited acts, which includes DIY pipe alterations and gas meter removals. At the same time, the regulations specify that gas operators may not require users to purchase designated products or accept services they provide.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:国务院 (2010). 城镇燃气管理条例（国务院令第 583 号）第二十七条：「燃气用户应当遵守安全用气规则，使用合格的燃气燃烧器具和气瓶，及时更换国家明令淘汰或者使用年限已届满的燃气燃烧器具、连接管等」；第二十八条禁止行为含「（一）擅自操作公用燃气阀门」「（二）将燃气管道作为负重支架或者接地引线」「（四）擅自安装、改装、拆除户内燃气设施和燃气计量装置」；第二十条燃气经营者不得「（六）要求燃气用户购买其指定的产品或者接受其提供的服务」；第二十九条用户可就收费、服务向燃气管理等部门投诉，「有关部门应当自收到投诉之日起15个工作日内予以处理」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1758214.htm>；深圳市住房和建设局 (2024). 安全用气小知识（二）：「立即关闭家里的燃气总阀门」「迅速开门、开窗进行通风」「一定不要开、关家里的电灯、抽油烟机等电器」「人员应该迅速撤离，转移到安全的地方，并且及时拨打燃气公司的报警电话」. <https://zjj.sz.gov.cn/zcjzts/content/post_11882410.html>；张家港市应急管理局 (2024). 燃气安全使用知识：「不要情急之下打开排气扇进行通风」「应远离现场之后才能打电话求助」. <https://www.zjg.gov.cn/zjgszwz/yjaqzs/202411/72b4fad7399c48378302460c6ce2d55b.shtml>；市场监管总局 (2023). 燃气器具产品质量安全风险提示（鄂州市城建委转载）：「鼓励用户安装使用燃气智能表、燃气泄漏安全保护装置、自闭阀、独立式感烟火灾探测器、可燃气体探测报警器等保护装置」. <https://cjw.ezhou.gov.cn/zxzx/gzdt/202308/t20230821_569108.html>
- Notes: The text of the regulations can be verified word for word, but it only states that replacing expired hoses is your legal obligation; it does not show how many accidents are prevented by doing so. Because no official statistical data was found showing by how much replacing hoses on time reduces gas accidents, this receives a C grade. If a door-to-door inspector insists that you buy a specific self-closing valve or alarm, you can refuse or file a complaint, because Article 20, Item 6 of the regulations forbids gas companies from making such demands. Self-closing valves and alarms are encouraged by the State Administration for Market Regulation; if you want to install them, choose certified, compliant products yourself. If you smell gas, first shut off the main gas valve (or the cylinder valve for bottled LPG). Then open doors and windows for ventilation—do not turn on exhaust fans. Do not switch any electrical appliances on or off, including lights and range hoods, and do not strike a lighter. Evacuate immediately, and call the gas company's emergency hotline only after leaving the premises. Carbon monoxide poisoning is a separate issue; see Section 13.

### 5. Never pick, buy, or eat wild mushrooms: no folk identification method works
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is giving up a seasonal wild delicacy.
- In plain terms: In 2025 alone, the Chinese CDC investigated 828 wild mushroom poisoning incidents involving 2,165 people, with 13 deaths. That year, 34 mushroom species were identified as poisonous to humans for the first time. Testing with a silver needle, assuming mushrooms nibbled by insects are safe, or believing only brightly colored ones are toxic—none of these folk rules work.
- Benefit: In 2025, the Chinese CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents involving 2,165 individuals and 13 deaths. The case fatality rate was 0.6%, meaning 0.6% of poisoned individuals died. From 2019 to 2024, there were 276 to 676 incidents per year, with case fatality rates ranging between 0.87% and 2.86%. In 2025 alone, 138 toxic mushroom species were identified, 34 of which were documented in China as causing human poisoning for the first time. Chinese CDC public education systematically debunks folk identification methods. Myth: Only brightly colored mushrooms are poisonous (False: toxicity has no relationship with color). Myth: Mushrooms eaten by insects or maggots are safe (False: many lethally toxic mushrooms are eaten by insects once mature). Myth: Mushrooms that discolor when boiled with silver utensils or garlic are poisonous (False: mushroom toxins do not contain sulfur, which is what tarnishes silver). Myth: High-temperature frying neutralizes toxins (False: also unreliable). Its conclusion is that "there is currently no simple, quick, or effective method to identify whether a mushroom is toxic." A 2026 alert from the Beijing municipal market supervision administration advises that if anyone feels unwell after eating wild mushrooms, they should "seek medical care immediately and never attempt home remedies," while saving leftover mushrooms and food scraps to help physicians identify the toxin.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Mushroom Poisoning Outbreaks — China, 2025. China CDC Weekly (2026)：「In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents across 27 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs), affecting 2,165 individuals and causing 13 deaths - a case fatality rate of 0.6%, the lowest of the past six years. In total, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, including 34 newly recorded in poisoning incidents in China.」「From 2019 to 2024, the annual number of incidents ranged from 276 to 676, and the case fatality rate ranged from 0.87% to 2.86%.」<https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2026.120>；同刊 2024 年度报告 <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2025.106>；中国疾病预防控制中心职业卫生所 尹萸、李海蛟 (2019). 防控蘑菇中毒，任重而道远：「方法4：虫食或生蛆的蘑菇无毒。错！」「方法6：与银器、大葱、大蒜、大米同煮可使其变色的蘑菇有毒。错！」「因此，目前没有简单易行、快速有效的识别蘑菇是否有毒的方法」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkkp/zyjk/zdzs/201908/t20190802_301109.html>；北京市疾病预防控制中心 (2023). 远离毒蘑菇，不做「菇勇者」：「民间流传辨别毒蘑菇的方法，如看颜色和形状、看生长环境、用银器辨毒、看分泌物、看有无生蛆生虫等均不可靠」. <https://www.bjcdc.org/cdcmodule/jkfw/yyyspjk/jkyf/swzdyf/2023/35518.shtml>；北京市市场监督管理局 (2026). 关于防范误食野生蘑菇中毒的风险提示：「请立即就医，切勿自行处理，以免延误诊疗时机。妥善保存剩余野生蘑菇、食物残渣等样本」. <https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/zwxx/scjgdt/202606/t20260625_4715961.html>
- Notes: If you suspect you ate poisonous mushrooms, go to the hospital immediately instead of attempting home remedies. Bring any leftover mushrooms, food scraps, or photographs. Mushroom poisonings fall into several distinct clinical types, and identifying the exact species helps doctors provide targeted treatment. New poisonous species are documented every year, which disproves the common assumption that "locals know how to tell them apart." Testing with a silver needle, cooking with garlic, trusting insect-chewed caps, or looking for bright colors are all debunked myths. Soaking wood ear or snow fungus for too long and leaving wet rice noodles out involve a completely different bacterial toxin; see Item 48 (Soaked wood ear mushrooms).

### 6. Keep e-bikes out of hallways and elevators, and never charge them at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is parking at the outdoor central charging station each time and walking an extra few dozen meters.
- In plain terms: Parking or charging an e-bike in high-rise lobbies, hallways, or stairwells is explicitly prohibited by Ministry of Emergency Management regulations. Anyone who refuses to correct the violation faces a fine of 500 to 1,000 yuan, or 2,000 to 10,000 yuan for businesses. The fine is the minor issue; what matters most is that these exact areas are your escape routes during a fire.
- Benefit: Regulations from the Ministry of Emergency Management state explicitly: "It is prohibited to park electric bicycles or charge electric bicycles in public lobbies, evacuation walkways, stairwells, and emergency exits of high-rise civil buildings." In lobbies, walkways, stairwells, and emergency exits, parking and charging are both banned. Those who refuse to comply face fines of 500 to 1,000 yuan for non-commercial entities and individuals, and 2,000 to 10,000 yuan for businesses. This regulation does not cover elevators or private apartments. In a response to NPC deputies, the Ministry of Emergency Management noted that illegally parking or charging e-bikes and batteries indoors has been targeted as a major fire safety hazard, warning the public against "bringing vehicles indoors, running cables outside windows, and unauthorized modifications." Elevators fall under local regulations: Beijing will prohibit bringing e-bikes and charging batteries into elevator cabs starting May 1, 2025, with fines of 500 to 1,000 yuan for individuals who refuse to comply.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:应急管理部 (2021). 高层民用建筑消防安全管理规定（应急管理部令第 5 号，第三十七条、第四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/01/content_5628815.htm>；应急管理部 (2023). 关于政协第十三届全国委员会第五次会议第00969号提案答复的函：「2021年，全国共接报电动自行车火灾1.8万起，造成65人死亡、222人受伤，直接财产损失达2.21亿元，其中发生在建筑物内3782起，造成60人死亡、175人受伤」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/jytabljggk/zxwytadfzy/2022zx/202301/t20230119_440918.shtml>；应急管理部 (2025). 2025年1月15日新闻发布会：「每百万辆电动自行车火灾数量由2023年的5.6起下降至3起，火灾起数同比下降42.2%，伤亡人数下降超过90%」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/xw/xwfbh/2025n01y15xwfbh_6337/>；应急管理部 (2024). 对十四届全国人大一次会议第7187号建议的答复：「将电动车（蓄电池）违规在室内停放或充电、未按要求设置防火分区等问题作为消防安全重大风险隐患专项排查整治2023行动整治重点」「向社会宣传提示"车入楼、线出户、自拆改"的火灾危险危害」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/jytabljggk/rddbjydfzy/2023_5984/202401/t20240130_476731.shtml>；北京市东城区人民政府转载北京消防 (2025). 北京消防条例：电动自行车、电池进电梯，个人最高可罚1000元：「禁止携带电动自行车、充电电池进入电梯轿厢。违规拒不改正的，可对单位处2000元以上1万元以下罚款，对个人处500元以上1000元以下罚款」. <https://www.bjdch.gov.cn/ztzl/aqsczbgjsnxd/zdgz/202504/t20250410_4061604.html>
- Notes: Do not buy modified batteries, do not use off-brand chargers, and never leave batteries charging unattended overnight. Once a lithium battery catches fire, thermal runaway spreads across the entire pack within dozens of seconds while releasing highly toxic smoke. If an e-bike catches fire parked in a hallway, it directly blocks your escape route downstairs. According to Ministry of Emergency Management figures, in 2021 there were 18,000 e-bike fires nationwide, causing 65 deaths. Of those, 3,782 fires occurred inside buildings, resulting in 60 deaths—almost all fatalities occurred indoors. Following targeted crackdowns in 2024, the number of fires dropped by 42.2% compared to the previous year, and casualties fell by over 90%. Fires per million e-bikes dropped from 5.6 to 3. The press conference did not disclose absolute totals for the full year.

### 7. Check blood pressure, and take medication to reach target if it is high
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: An electronic blood pressure monitor costs 100 to 200 RMB. One measurement takes 1 minute. Most blood pressure medications cost a few to tens of RMB per month. The hard part is continuing to take them even when you have no symptoms.
- In plain terms: For every 10 mmHg drop in top blood pressure (systolic), the risk of major cardiovascular events falls by 20%, stroke falls by 27%, heart failure falls by 28%, and the risk of death falls by 13%. Among people aged 35 to 75 in China, 44.7% have hypertension, but only 7.2% actually have it under control.
- Benefit: Pooling 123 trials with over 610,000 participants: for every 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure, major cardiovascular events dropped by about 20% (RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.83). Systolic pressure is the higher of the two blood pressure numbers. Stroke dropped by about 27% (RR 0.73). Heart failure dropped by about 28% (RR 0.72). All-cause mortality dropped by 13% (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84 to 0.91). A screening of 1.7 million people aged 35 to 75 in China found that 44.7% had hypertension. Among those with hypertension, 44.7% were aware of their condition, 30.1% were receiving treatment, and only 7.2% had it controlled.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Lu J 等 (2017). Prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension in China (China PEACE Million Persons Project). Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32478-9>
- Notes: Start by getting your blood pressure checked once. Fewer than 1 in 10 people with hypertension in China have it under control, making this single measurement one of the most cost-effective actions in this book. Exactly what target counts as controlled (130 or 140) is still debated, but knowing you have high blood pressure and bringing it down is beyond dispute.

### 8. After age 35, get your fasting blood sugar checked if you are overweight, and repeat every three years even if normal
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=35岁 -->
- Cost: Fasting blood glucose costs around 10 to 20 yuan; glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) costs a few dozen yuan. Just a single blood draw at the hospital.
- In plain terms: Spending a few dozen yuan on a single blood draw can detect prediabetes long before any symptoms appear. Official US screening guidelines recommend testing for adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese. If results are normal, repeat the test every 3 years. The US defines overweight as a body mass index (BMI) of 25—lowered to 23 for Asians—while China's standard considers a BMI of 24 as overweight.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) is the official US body providing screening guidance. Its current recommendation is to screen for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in asymptomatic adults aged 35 to 70 who are overweight or obese. Overweight is defined as a body mass index (BMI) ≥25, and obesity as ≥30. For Asian Americans, it recommends lowering the threshold to a BMI ≥23, because Asians face higher diabetes risk at the same BMI. China's industry standard classifies a BMI between 24.0 and 28.0 as overweight, and ≥28.0 as obese. This recommendation carries a Grade B rating. It also notes: "Screening every 3 years is a reasonable approach for adults with normal blood glucose levels."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:US Preventive Services Task Force (2021). Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes>：「at a lower BMI (≥23) if the patient is Asian American」；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 居民体重管理核心知识（2024 年版）释义：「根据《成年人体重判定》（WS/T 428-2013）行业标准……24.0≤BMI＜28.0 为超重，BMI≥28.0 为肥胖」. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202407/P020240703481519509521.pdf>；Wang L 等 (2021). Prevalence and Treatment of Diabetes in China, 2013-2018. JAMA：「In 2018, among adults with diabetes, 36.7% (95% CI, 34.7%-38.6%) reported being aware of their condition」「These rates did not change significantly from 2013.」<https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2021.22208>
- Notes: Being diagnosed with prediabetes does not mean you must immediately start taking medication. Eating less, being more active, and losing a little weight can reduce the risk of developing diabetes by 30% to 60%. In a 2018 national survey, only 36.7% of Chinese adults with diabetes were aware of their condition, showing no significant improvement compared to 2013. For details on how much risk can be reduced, what lifestyle adjustments to make, and how often to follow up after diagnosis, see Section 2, Item 45 (Diagnosed with Prediabetes).

### 9. Avoid speeding, drunk driving, and using your phone, and pull over to rest when drowsy
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Driving slightly slower adds only a few minutes to a trip. The hard part is drinking less at dinners and resisting the urge to check incoming messages. On long drives, stop and rest for at least 20 minutes every 4 hours.
- In plain terms: For every 1% increase in average driving speed, the risk of a fatal crash rises by about 4%. With alcohol, there is no safe cutoff where "just a little won't hurt." Fiddling with a phone while driving raises crash risk to about 3.6 times that of focused driving, and dialing raises it to about 12.2 times. Looking at specific tasks: dialing had an OR of 12.2, texting had an OR of 6.1, reaching for a phone had an OR of 4.8, browsing had an OR of 2.7, and handheld talking had an OR of 2.2. When obvious drowsiness was visible on video, the odds ratio was 3.4 (OR 3.4).
- Evidence grade: B

When drowsy, pull into a service area to rest. Cold and allergy medications containing chlorpheniramine or diphenhydramine can also cause drowsiness; see Section 34, Item 12 (chlorpheniramine). - Cost: 300 to 2,000 yuan each, lasting several years. Takes about 1 extra minute each trip to buckle the child in.
- In plain terms: For infants under 1 year old, riding in a car seat lowers the risk of fatal crash injuries by about 71%, and for children aged 1 to 4, it lowers that risk by 54%. Holding them in your arms does not protect them.

### 12. Keep children in sight near water; wear a life jacket when boating or swimming outdoors

### 13. Train balance and leg strength and adapt home bathrooms and stairs after age 60

### 14. Test for hepatitis B markers and check vaccination records before getting booster shots
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Testing costs a few dozen yuan. For adults who need the vaccine, the complete 3-dose hepatitis B series costs around 100 to 300 yuan in total, depending on local clinic pricing. It is typically administered on a 0-, 1-, and 6-month schedule.
- In plain terms: In the landmark Qidong study, receiving the hepatitis B vaccine at birth lowered the later risk of liver cancer by 84%. Adults cannot simply expect this same reduction, nor should they rush to get three booster shots just because their antibody levels are low. Healthy people who completed the full primary series and once developed protective antibodies generally do not need a booster. If you are unsure whether you were ever vaccinated, take your lab results to an immunization clinic for an evaluation.
- Benefit: The Qidong trial was randomized by cluster rather than by individual and followed participants for 30 years. Vaccinating newborns against hepatitis B reduced primary liver cancer incidence by 84% (95% CI: 23% to 97%). The prevalence of hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)—the proportion of people carrying the virus—fell by 72% (95% CI: 68% to 75%). In the control group, 67.8% received catch-up vaccination between ages 10 and 14. Catch-up vaccination showed an efficacy of only 21% (95% CI: 10% to 30%) against HBsAg positivity in early adulthood, far weaker than vaccination at birth. Across China's general population, HBsAg prevalence dropped by 52% between 1992 and 2014, and by 97% among children under age 5. These figures cannot be directly applied to adult revaccination. Individuals with normal immune function who completed the full series and once generated protective antibodies retain immune memory even after antibody titers decline, and they generally do not require booster doses. Immune memory means the body can rapidly mobilize defenses when re-exposed to the virus. Deciding whether to revaccinate requires reviewing immunization records alongside tests for surface antigen, surface antibody, and core antibody. If your vaccination history is unclear, consult an immunization clinic.
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: Adults who were never vaccinated, did not finish the series, or have an unclear vaccination history should bring their immunization records and lab test results to a vaccination clinic. ### 15. Treat nail punctures, splinters, or dirty wounds the same day, and ask if you need a tetanus shot
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Tens of yuan for registration and wound care. If the doctor determines you need an injection, the vaccine or immunoglobulin costs tens to a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: In the US, about 1 in 10 people who develop tetanus die. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil carry the highest risk. If a wound is dirty and it has been at least 5 years since your last shot, you need 1 booster. Getting it treated the same day costs only tens of yuan—do not delay.
- Benefit: In US tetanus cases, about 1 in 10 patients dies. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil carry higher risk. China's 2024 clinical guidelines divide wounds into two risk categories. Superficial wounds treated within 6 hours that have not contacted dirt, feces, or animal saliva are considered low-risk. Wounds treated after 6 hours, contaminated with these substances, or resulting from punctures, avulsions, crush injuries, burns, frostbite, or containing foreign bodies are classified as high-risk. People who received 3 or more doses with the last dose less than 5 years ago need no injections. Between 5 and 10 years, high-risk wounds require 1 booster dose. At 10 years or more, 1 booster dose is required regardless of wound category. Those who have not completed 3 doses or cannot remember must complete the full vaccine series. High-risk wounds also require passive immunization at the same time (injections that deliver antibodies directly, such as immunoglobulin or antitoxin).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:美国疾病控制与预防中心. 破伤风：「Tetanus bacteria can get into someone's body through broken skin, usually through injuries.」「Tetanus can lead to death (1 in 10 cases in the United States are fatal).」「People who didn't complete the primary series or who aren't up to date with their 10-year tetanus booster shots are also at increased risk.」「Vaccination also helps prevent tetanus in people with wounds, depending on their tetanus vaccination history.」<https://www.cdc.gov/tetanus/about/index.html>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 关于印发非新生儿破伤风诊疗规范（2024 年版）的通知（国卫办医急函〔2024〕381 号，风险分级见附件第 16 页，表 4 见第 20 页）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/content_6982262.htm>；附件 PDF（扫描件）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/P020241023483425528555.pdf>
- Notes: Let the doctor decide at the hospital; do not make assumptions yourself or avoid asking out of inconvenience. Not all wounds require a shot. ### 16. Vaccinate females against HPV, the earlier the better

### 17. Screen for breast cancer starting at age 40: mammogram every two years

### 18. Screen for cervical cancer after age 30, prioritizing HPV testing

### 19. Start colorectal cancer screening at age 45 to 50 with a stool blood test or colonoscopy
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=35岁 -->
- Cost: Stool tests cost a few dozen yuan and are done every 1 to 2 years. A colonoscopy costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan, and if the result is negative, it only needs to be repeated once every 10 years. You need to set aside a day beforehand for bowel prep.
- In plain terms: Doing an inexpensive stool test for hidden blood every one to two years lowers the chance of dying from colorectal cancer by about 16%, and by 25% for people who complete at least one round. Among those invited for a screening colonoscopy, the chance of developing colorectal cancer over the next ten years drops from 1.20% to 0.98%.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review pooled multiple studies and found that people screened for fecal occult blood had about a 16% lower risk of dying from colorectal cancer (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.90). For those who completed at least one round of screening, the risk was about 25% lower (RR 0.75). In the NordICC randomized trial, inviting people for a screening colonoscopy reduced the 10-year incidence of colorectal cancer from 1.20% to 0.98% (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.93).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Hewitson P 等 (2007). Screening for colorectal cancer using the faecal occult blood test, Hemoccult. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001216.pub2> ; Bretthauer M 等 (2022). Effect of Colonoscopy Screening on Risks of Colorectal Cancer and Related Death. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2208375>
- Notes: Controversy. NordICC evaluated people based on who was invited to have a colonoscopy. Colorectal cancer mortality was 0.28% versus 0.31% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.16), a difference within the margin of error. This suggests the mortality benefit of colonoscopy may have been previously overestimated; stool testing actually has firmer evidence for reducing deaths. Guidelines across countries generally recommend starting screening between ages 45 and 50. Those with a family history should begin earlier. For exercise after surgery and chemotherapy in patients diagnosed with colon cancer, see Section 16, Item 14 (Exercise after colon cancer surgery and chemotherapy).

### 20. Get an annual flu vaccine if you have heart disease or are an older adult

- In plain terms: In a trial where patients got a flu shot after a heart attack, the proportion dying within a year dropped from 4.9% to 2.9%. ### 21. Get the shingles vaccine after age 50

- Cost: The recombinant shingles vaccine requires two doses, totaling about 3,000 to 4,000 yuan, paid entirely out of pocket.
- In plain terms: In phase 3 trials, this vaccine prevented 97% of shingles cases, with similar protection across all age groups. The downside is having to pay three to four thousand yuan for the two doses entirely out of pocket.
- Benefit: A phase 3 trial across 18 countries enrolled 15,400 people. Participants were randomly assigned to two groups, one receiving the vaccine and the other a control shot, with an average follow-up of 3.2 years. Shingles developed in 6 people in the vaccine group versus 210 in the placebo group. Converted to person-years, this was 0.3 versus 9.1 per 1,000 person-years (the number of cases observed per 1,000 people over one year). The original paper noted that "overall vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster was 97.2% (95% CI 93.7–99.0, P<0.001)." Efficacy across age groups ranged from 96.6% to 97.9%. Within 7 days after injection, local and systemic reactions were more common in the vaccine group. Grade 3 symptoms (the most severe tier) were reported by 17.0% in the vaccine group and 3.2% in the placebo group. Rates of serious adverse events and deaths were similar between the two groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lal H, Cunningham AL, Godeaux O, et al. (2015). Efficacy of an adjuvanted herpes zoster subunit vaccine in older adults. - Notes: Its cost-effectiveness is not particularly high, mainly because it is expensive. Shingles itself is rarely fatal; the real burden is postherpetic neuralgia, which can cause pain lasting months to years. Within a week after vaccination, both injection-site and whole-body reactions were more frequent than with placebo. ### 22. Get the pneumococcal vaccine if you are 65 or older
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=老人,60岁 -->
- Cost: A few hundred yuan; free for older adults in some areas.
- In plain terms: In a trial of 84,500 older adults, the 13-valent pneumococcal vaccine prevented about 46% of pneumonia cases caused by the strains it covers, and cut the most dangerous invasive infections by 75%. But it showed no effect against pneumonia from all causes combined, so do not expect it to protect against every type of pneumonia.
- Benefit: A randomized trial in the Netherlands followed 84,500 adults aged 65 and older, comparing two groups using the 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13). Its efficacy against vaccine-type pneumonia was 45.6% (about 46%; 95.2% CI, 21.8–62.5), meaning pneumonia caused by the specific strains covered by the shot. Its efficacy against invasive pneumococcal disease, the most severe category of infection, was 75.0% (75%; 95% CI, 41.4–90.8). It showed no effect against pneumonia from all causes combined.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Bonten MJ, Huijts SM, Bolkenbaas M, et al. (2015). Polysaccharide conjugate vaccine against pneumococcal pneumonia in adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(12), 1114-1125. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1408544>
- Notes: It only protects against the specific strains covered by the vaccine; getting the shot does not mean you cannot get pneumonia. Older adults in China commonly receive the 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV23), which is not the same as the 13-valent conjugate vaccine used in this trial, so the efficacy figures above cannot be directly applied to it.

### 23. Test for Helicobacter pylori and eradicate if positive
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A carbon-13 or carbon-14 urea breath test costs 100 to 200 RMB. Eradication requires taking four medications at the same time, costing 200 to 500 RMB for two weeks. The hard part is taking all doses strictly on schedule for the full two weeks.
- In plain terms: In the 22-year follow-up study in Linqu, Shandong, people who cleared *Helicobacter pylori* had about half the risk of developing stomach cancer, and their risk of dying from stomach cancer was about 38% lower. A test costs one to two hundred yuan, and eradication takes two weeks of pills.
- Benefit: The randomized trial in Linqu, Shandong followed participants for 22 years. Among those who received *Helicobacter pylori* eradication treatment, the odds ratio for developing stomach cancer was 0.48 (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.71). Because stomach cancer is a rare outcome, the odds ratio is close to the relative risk, meaning the risk of developing stomach cancer was roughly half. The risk of dying from stomach cancer was about 38% lower (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.99).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Li WQ 等 (2019). Effects of Helicobacter pylori treatment and vitamin and garlic supplementation on gastric cancer incidence and mortality: follow-up of a randomized intervention trial. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l5016>
- Notes: Re-infection is still possible after eradication, so family members should be tested and treated together. The population in this trial lived in an area with a high incidence of stomach cancer; for people living in low-incidence areas, the actual benefit will be much smaller. Eradication does not replace endoscopy; for high-risk individuals aged 45 and older, see Item 47 (get screened with an endoscopy).

### 24. Screen heavy smokers annually with low-dose chest CT
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=35岁 -->
- Cost: 200 to 400 RMB per scan, taking 10 minutes. Factor in follow-up tests after false alarms and the worry during that stretch of waiting.
- In plain terms: For eligible heavy smokers, an annual low-dose CT (computed tomography) scan makes the chance of dying from lung cancer 20% lower than with chest X-rays alone, and makes the overall chance of dying 6.7% lower. The trade-off is follow-up checks after suspicious findings and living with anxiety during that waiting period.
- Benefit: The US National Lung Screening Trial (NLST) enrolled 53,454 participants aged 55 to 74 with a smoking history of at least 30 pack-years who had quit no more than 15 years earlier. A pack-year measures smoking exposure: smoking one pack a day for one year equals one pack-year. Low-dose CT screening reduced lung cancer mortality by a relative 20.0% compared with chest X-rays (95% CI 6.8 to 26.7). All-cause mortality fell by a relative 6.7% (95% CI 1.2 to 13.6). Over three annual screening rounds combined, 24.2% of low-dose CT scans produced suspicious findings. Of those suspicious findings, 96.4% were false alarms (false positives).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:National Lung Screening Trial Research Team (2011). Reduced Lung-Cancer Mortality with Low-Dose Computed Tomographic Screening. New England Journal of Medicine：「The rate of positive screening tests was 24.2% with low-dose CT and 6.9% with radiography over all three rounds. A total of 96.4% of the positive screening results in the low-dose CT group and 94.5% in the radiography group were false positive results.」<https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873>
- Notes: Applies only to high-risk smokers. In the same trial, across three rounds of low-dose CT, 24.2% of screening results were suspicious, and 96.4% of those were false alarms (false positives). Non-smokers were not included, so this trial cannot determine whether screening offers them any benefit.

### 25. Call 12356 if feeling depressed or having suicidal thoughts, and do not hoard sleeping pills or pesticides at home

That is why you should not hoard sleeping pills or pesticides at home. If you feel overwhelmed, call 12356 first—it works nationwide and is open at least 18 hours a day.

### 27. Get checked if you see blood in your urine, even if it does not hurt and clears up the next day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A routine urinalysis costs a few tens of yuan, and a urinary tract ultrasound is one to two hundred yuan. If the doctor thinks it is necessary, an added cystoscopy runs around a thousand yuan.
- In plain terms: In UK outpatient records, roughly 2.8% of people aged 60 and older who noticed visible blood in their urine were ultimately diagnosed with bladder cancer, as were about 1.2% of those aged 40 to 59. While those percentages sound small, they are easily high enough to warrant an immediate checkup. Even if it only happened once, was very faint, or did not hurt at all, you still need to see a doctor.
- Benefit: A UK study using electronic medical records from general practice (primary care) compared 4,915 cases of bladder cancer with 21,718 controls. Among patients with visible blood in their urine (gross hematuria), the proportion ultimately diagnosed with bladder cancer (the positive predictive value) was 2.8% for those aged 60 and older (95% CI 2.5 to 3.1) and 1.2% for those aged 40 to 59 (95% CI 0.6 to 2.3). Microscopic hematuria (blood visible only under a microscope) was also significant, with an odds ratio of 20 (95% CI 12 to 33) and a positive predictive value of 1.6% in people aged 60 and older (95% CI 1.2 to 2.1). The UK National Health Service (NHS) emphasizes that blood in the urine can be a sign of cancer and must be evaluated. Even if there are no other symptoms, it happened just once, or the amount was tiny, book an appointment with a doctor promptly.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Price SJ, Shephard EA, Stapley SA, Barraclough K, Hamilton WT (2014). Non-visible versus visible haematuria and bladder cancer risk: a study of electronic records in primary care. British Journal of General Practice, 64(626), e584-e589. <https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp14X681409>；NHS. Blood in urine：「Blood in your pee must be checked out because it can be a sign of cancer.」「But do not self-diagnose.」「It may not be blood in your pee if: you've recently eaten beetroot … you're taking a new medicine … it's happening during your period」. <https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/blood-in-urine/>；NHS. - Notes: "No pain" is not a reason to brush it off. The UK National Health Service (NHS) clearly states that if there is blood in your urine, you should see a doctor promptly—even if there are no other symptoms, it is the first time, the amount is tiny, or you are not completely sure it is blood. ### 28. If You Have Erectile Dysfunction, Check Your Heart First—Do Not Treat It as Just a Bedroom Issue

- In plain terms: Men with erectile dysfunction are about 40% more likely to develop cardiovascular disease later and about 60% more likely to suffer a heart attack. Men with intermediate baseline cardiovascular risk had a higher relative increase than those at high or low risk. ### 30. Use condoms for the entire duration of sex, and never share needles

- Cost: One or two yuan each. The hard part is not the cost; it is using one every single time, right from the start.
- In plain terms: Using condoms every single time and from start to finish reduces the risk of heterosexual HIV transmission by over 70%. Using them "most of the time" protects barely better than not using them at all. - Benefit: A review pooled 25 studies covering 10,676 heterosexual couples where one partner was infected with HIV and the other was not (serodiscordant couples). The authors concluded that although condoms are not completely foolproof, "consistent use reduces HIV transmission by more than 70%." The World Health Organization (WHO) notes that correct and consistent condom use is one of the most effective tools for preventing sexually transmitted infections, including HIV. However, condoms cannot protect against ulcers located outside the covered area, such as syphilis or genital herpes.

### 32. Tell someone nearby the moment a suicidal thought surfaces, and hand over those next few dozen minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is speaking up in the moment and admitting that you cannot be left alone for the next few hours.
- In plain terms: In one interview study, nearly half of people who survived a suicide attempt said that 10 minutes or less elapsed between the thought first surfacing and taking action. Three-quarters had contact with someone nearby during that window. - Benefit: In a study where 82 suicide attempt survivors were interviewed within 3 days of admission: 47.6% (39 people) reported that 10 minutes or less elapsed from the first suicidal thought of this episode to the actual act. ### 33. Do not count on resuscitation as a safety net: after swallowing pesticides or inhaling gas, emergency care might save your life, but not your lungs or brain

### 35. Do not trade "living with one kidney is fine" for cash: the remaining one works double duty, and 86% of kidney sellers report worse health

- Cost: Free. Just get the full ledger straight first.
- In plain terms: In the US, among carefully screened formal kidney donors, 31 per 10,000 developed kidney failure after 15 years. Among equally healthy people who did not donate, it was 4 per 10,000. In an Indian study of kidney sellers, 86% reported worse health 6 years later, and three-quarters were still in debt.
- Benefit: A US study compared 96,217 living kidney donors with 20,024 matched healthy non-donors drawn from the Third National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES III). The estimated 15-year risk of developing end-stage renal disease (ESRD, kidney failure requiring dialysis or transplantation) was 30.8 per 10,000 among donors (95% CI 24.3–38.5) versus 3.9 per 10,000 among healthy non-donors (95% CI 0.8–8.9, P<0.001). In another US cohort of 3,698 donors, 11 developed ESRD (180 cases per million person-years, compared with 268 per million person-years in the general population). ### 36. Never pick up, dismantle, or scrap metal parts of unknown origin, and stay far away from the trefoil radiation symbol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. If you spot an unfamiliar metal cylinder, a lead pot, or parts stripped from industrial equipment, steer clear—do not pick them up, take them apart, or put them in your pocket.
- In plain terms: Radiation is invisible, and radioactive sources are solids sealed inside small metal capsules, so "it looks harmless" means nothing. The odds of an ordinary person running into one are tiny, with under 1 incident per 10,000 sources each year. But if you do cross paths with one, the fallout is devastating: in 1987 in Brazil, people took apart a radiotherapy machine from an abandoned clinic, and 4 people died.
- Benefit: According to educational materials from the National Nuclear Safety Administration, a radioactive source is solid radioactive material permanently sealed inside a container or covered by a tight protective cladding. Industrial level gauges, flaw-detection machines, and hospital gamma knives all use them. Without shielding, exposure to a Category III radioactive source can cause permanent injury within a few hours and become fatal within days to weeks. X-rays are invisible to the naked eye, and gamma rays are electromagnetic waves just like them. Protection against external radiation relies on three things: cutting exposure time, increasing distance, and adding shielding. Gamma rays have high penetrating power, requiring thick concrete walls or lead to block them. As of 2025, there are roughly 178,000 active radioactive sources and 355,000 radiation-generating devices nationwide. The annual radiation accident rate has held steady at under 1 incident per 10,000 sources. The classification works as follows: losing, having stolen, or losing control of a Category I or II radioactive source constitutes a major radiation accident. Category III constitutes a serious radiation accident. Losing or having Category IV or V sources stolen, or exposing personnel beyond annual dose limits, counts as an ordinary radiation accident. Official domestic case: In June 2020, during gamma-ray flaw detection on a blast furnace piping renovation project at Ningxia Iron and Steel Group's ironmaking plant, a Category II iridium-192 source exposed three workers. Worker Chen suffered Grade III acute radiation skin injury on his left hand. Worker Liu suffered Grade IV on his left thumb and index finger, and Grade III on his middle, ring, and little fingers. Worker Fan suffered Grade I–II on his left hand. Radiation doses for all three workers severely exceeded national regulatory limits; radiation safety licenses for the two responsible companies were revoked, and they were fined 70,000 yuan. International case: In 1987 in Goiânia, Brazil, a teletherapy unit at an abandoned clinic was dismantled. A 50.8 TBq cesium-137 source inside was extracted, and the capsule containing the source was ruptured. Approximately 112,000 people were monitored. Among them, 249 individuals suffered internal or external contamination. Of those 249, 129 had moderate to severe contamination, and 50 required close medical supervision. Ultimately, 4 people died.

### 41. Measure ice thickness before stepping onto wild ice; stay off if it is under 15 cm, and avoid river ice and ice-fishing holes

- Cost: The easiest way is to use a supervised, official rink, which requires no extra spending. If you insist on going out on wild ice, bring an ice auger or ice chisel to drill holes and measure thickness. Buy a pair of ice safety picks—two spiked handles worn around your neck across your chest. Together, these cost a few dozen to a couple hundred yuan. Measuring every few steps also takes extra time.
- In plain terms: Official Chinese guidelines say ice must be at least 15 cm thick before adults can skate on it. The baseline in the US and Canada is 10 cm, and that applies only to new, clear ice. White ice is only half as strong, so required thickness doubles. On the same body of water, ice can be a foot thick here and only an inch or two thick a few steps away, so you must measure as you go.
- Benefit: Public science outreach from the China Meteorological Administration states that adults can skate on ice once it reaches 15 cm, which is also the required thickness for opening outdoor rinks. Wild ice often has holes cut by anglers, which rarely refreeze solidly. On the same body of water, ice might be a foot thick in one spot and only an inch or two thick a few feet away. ### 44. Get an ECG if your heart races or beats irregularly; if atrial fibrillation is found, ask a doctor about anticoagulants to prevent stroke, and do not substitute aspirin

- Cost: An electrocardiogram (ECG) costs several tens of yuan. Warfarin is very inexpensive, but it requires regular blood draws to adjust the dose. ### 46. Ventilate, test the air, wear protective gear, and post a watcher outside before entering confined spaces like septic tanks, manholes, or cellars—never jump in after someone who collapses, and call 119 and 120

- Cost: Ventilating, testing the air, protective equipment, and dedicated monitoring all require buying gear, hiring people, and spending time. For household cleanouts, prioritize hiring a trained and properly equipped team; check with local service providers for rates. If safety conditions are not met, do not go down yourself. The hardest part is resisting the urge to jump in when someone collapses inside.
- In plain terms: Confined spaces like septic tanks, manholes, and root cellars easily trap lethal gases or run out of oxygen, knocking a person unconscious within seconds. Before anyone goes inside, thoroughly ventilate the space, test the air to confirm it is safe, put on protective equipment, and have a watcher stationed outside the entire time—if even one of these steps is missing, do not enter. Simply opening the hatch and waiting a while does not make the space safe. If you see someone collapse inside, do not jump down after them; call 119 and 120 immediately.
- Benefit: "Confined spaces" refer to enclosed or semi-enclosed spaces not designed for continuous human occupancy, such as septic tanks, manholes, sewage sumps, cellars, and storage vats. An analysis by the Ministry of Emergency Management of serious confined-space accidents in industrial and commercial enterprises since 2013 showed that poisoning and asphyxiation accounted for 96.8% of incidents and 96.9% of fatalities. The main hazards were hydrogen sulfide, carbon monoxide, nitrogen, and carbon dioxide. Accidents involving inadequate protective measures and blind rescue attempts accounted for 87.4% of incidents and 86.8% of deaths. The ministry's 2020 safety guide noted that nearly 80% of accidents saw casualties multiply due to blind rescue attempts, and more than 50% of confined-space fatalities were would-be rescuers. In 2018, the State Council Work Safety Committee reported eight confined-space accidents: all eight initially involved only one person in distress, but because others rushed in blindly to save them, each incident ended with three to four people dead. In 2019, at a paper mill in Dongguan, three workers cleaning a sewage basin lost consciousness after inhaling hydrogen sulfide; six colleagues descended into the pit one after another to rescue them, and seven people died in total. In 2020, at a noodle factory in Jiaozuo, one worker fell into an ingredient vat in a fermentation workshop; coworkers attempted a blind rescue, and six people died in total. An investigation by the U.S. National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) into 70 fatal confined-space incidents between 1983 and 1993, which claimed 109 lives, found that 39 victims (36%) entered solely to rescue someone else; of those 39, only four were trained professionals such as police officers or firefighters. A 1986 NIOSH alert estimated that about 60% of all confined-space fatalities were would-be rescuers. The safety manual notes that hydrogen sulfide smells like rotten eggs, but at high concentrations it rapidly paralyzes the sense of smell; levels above 1000 mg/m³ can cause death within seconds. High concentrations of carbon dioxide can cause unconsciousness and collapse within seconds. The manual mandates that if the scene lacks proper rescue conditions, personnel must call 119 and 120 immediately, and "under no circumstances should a forced entry rescue be attempted." Hauling a victim out from the outside is only feasible if the trapped person was wearing a full-body harness connected to a lifeline leading outside. Order No. 13 of the Ministry of Emergency Management mandates that industrial and commercial operations must "ventilate first, test next, and operate only after approval." Enterprises must provide testing, ventilation, protective, and rescue equipment tailored to the hazards, and a safety watcher must remain present for the entire duration. Ventilation and air testing must continue during entry, and entry is prohibited if conditions fail to meet safety standards. Watchers are required to halt any blind rescue attempts that lack safety precautions. The Health Bureau of Anshun warns that vegetable cellars kept sealed for long periods deplete oxygen and build up carbon dioxide from plant respiration; cellars must be thoroughly ventilated by opening vents before entry. However, simply airing out the space cannot replace gas detection, protective gear, and outside monitoring (China / United States).

### 47. Screen with an endoscopy if you are 45 or older and at high risk for stomach or esophageal cancer (high-incidence area, family history, or positive H. pylori)

- Cost: A standard endoscopy (gastroscopy) costs several hundred yuan, while painless (sedated) endoscopy costs several hundred yuan more; prices vary by region. You must fast beforehand, and the procedure takes up half a day in total. If choosing painless endoscopy, an escort must accompany you. - In plain terms: For high-risk individuals aged 45 and older, having an endoscopy is linked to about a 50% lower chance of dying from stomach or esophageal cancer. - Benefit: The 2024 protocol from China's National Health Commission defines high-risk individuals as those aged 45 or older who meet at least one of the following criteria: 1) living in a county with a high incidence of stomach cancer (age-standardized incidence rate > 20 per 100,000) or esophageal cancer (> 15 per 100,000); 2) having a parent, child, or sibling who had these cancers; 3) testing positive for Helicobacter pylori (counts for stomach cancer only); 4) smoking, heavy drinking, consuming a high-salt diet or frequent preserved foods, or—for esophageal cancer—frequently consuming scalding hot food and drinks; 5) a history of chronic atrophic gastritis, gastric ulcers, gastric polyps, Barrett's esophagus, reflux esophagitis, or related diseases. ### 49. If you cough or bring up phlegm for over two weeks or have blood in your sputum, get tested for tuberculosis at a designated hospital; if diagnosed, finish the entire medication course and do not stop just because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Going to a local designated tuberculosis hospital for a chest X-ray and sputum tests takes about half a day. Designated hospitals provide some tests and treatments for free, with specific free items decided locally; you can call the local Chinese CDC to check before going. If diagnosed, you must take daily medication: standard pulmonary tuberculosis requires 6 to 8 months, along with follow-up visits as scheduled by your doctor. The hardest part is continuing the pills for several more months even after the cough has completely cleared up.
- In plain terms: If you have a cough or phlegm lasting more than two weeks, or blood in your sputum, get checked at a designated tuberculosis hospital. Without treatment, about 70% of people with sputum-positive tuberculosis die within 10 years. In China, about 95% of newly diagnosed and relapsed patients who start treatment either get cured or complete their medications on schedule. You must finish the full course: stopping on your own encourages drug resistance, and treatment succeeds in only about 70% of multidrug-resistant cases in China.
- Benefit: According to the WHO, approximately 10.7 million people fell ill with tuberculosis worldwide in 2024, and 1.23 million died from it. China accounted for 6.5% of the world's new cases. By WHO estimates, approximately 696,000 people developed tuberculosis in China in 2024, and about 28,000 died from it. Of the estimated new cases, about 76% were diagnosed and notified. The *Administrative Measures for Tuberculosis Prevention and Control* specify that coughing or expectorating for more than 2 weeks, hemoptysis, or blood-streaked sputum are considered suspected symptoms of pulmonary tuberculosis. The consequences of untreated disease come from a systematic review pooling studies prior to the advent of antituberculosis drugs: for untreated smear-positive pulmonary tuberculosis, 10-year mortality ranged from 53% to 86%, with a weighted average of 70%. Sputum smear-positive means bacteria are directly detected in the sputum. For smear-negative, culture-positive cases, about 20% died within 10 years. For treatment outcomes, WHO data for China show that among approximately 549,000 (548,839) new and relapse patients who started treatment in 2023, about 95% were treated successfully (defined as cured or completing treatment on schedule), with a mortality rate of about 2.9%. In China in 2024, about 2.9% of new patients and 17% of previously treated patients had rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis. Rifampicin is the primary first-line antituberculosis drug. Among 9,927 multidrug-resistant patients who began treatment in 2022, 6,783 were successfully treated, or about 68%. The WHO currently recommends three categories of drug-resistant tuberculosis regimens: two 6-month regimens, several 9-month regimens, and long regimens lasting 18 to 20 months. The 9-month regimens are only given to patients without fluoroquinolone resistance. In China in 2024, more than half of multidrug-resistant or rifampicin-resistant patients were still receiving the long regimen. The WHO states that stopping medication early or taking it irregularly breeds drug resistance in the body's tuberculosis bacteria. National health education materials note that standard pulmonary tuberculosis requires 6 to 8 months of full-course treatment, while drug-resistant tuberculosis requires 18 to 24 months. These materials also emphasize that irregular treatment readily leads to drug resistance, which results in lower cure rates and much higher costs. A Cochrane review pooled 5 randomized controlled trials that shortened standard 6-month therapy to 4 months by substituting moxifloxacin: the relative risk of relapse after treatment was RR 3.56 (95% CI 2.37–5.37), meaning the probability of relapse was about 3.6 times that of a 6-month regimen (Global / China).

- Notes: If someone in your household is diagnosed with infectious pulmonary tuberculosis, household members, close coworkers, and classmates should also get tested at a designated hospital. The *Administrative Measures for Tuberculosis Prevention and Control* require designated hospitals to screen these close contacts. An analysis pooling 95 studies from low- and middle-income settings found that among close contacts, about 3.1% already had active tuberculosis disease, and about 51.5% had latent infection. Latent infection means the bacteria are present in the body, but have not caused active disease and are not contagious. The 2024 national plan requires screening close contacts for tuberculosis infection and providing preventive treatment to those with latent infection. Implementation varies across regions, so ask the designated hospital. National educational materials note that people living with HIV, people with diabetes, people with pneumoconiosis, older adults, and individuals with compromised immune systems face higher risks and should be screened regularly every year. For HIV, see Item 31 of this section (Get tested once for HIV); for blood glucose, see Item 8 of this section (Check fasting blood glucose).
