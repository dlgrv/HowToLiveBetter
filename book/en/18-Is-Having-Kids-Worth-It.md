# 18. Is raising a child worth it?

This section breaks down the financial and time costs involved. Much like Section 10, which examined the costs of marriage, we’re simply laying out the numbers here — without offering any definitive conclusions. If you’re already pregnant and need to follow the necessary steps, please refer to Section 27.
There are 18 key considerations to keep in mind when deciding whether raising a child is worthwhile.
### 1. Start by counting what you can claim: the national childcare subsidy is 3600 yuan per child per year until age 3, and the individual income tax special additional deduction is 2000 yuan per child per month
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Apply for the subsidy where the child's hukou (户口 — household registration) is registered; a single visit finishes it. Fill in the tax deduction in the Individual Income Tax app on your phone, or have your employer fill it in.
- In plain terms: A child under 3 can receive 3600 yuan a year in childcare subsidy, and it is not taxed. A child born before 2025 is paid for the months that remain. Separately, from the child's birth until full-time schooling ends, 2000 yuan per child per month can be taken off a parent's taxable income. If a single parent takes the whole deduction, someone in the 3% bracket pays 720 yuan less for the year, and someone in the 10% bracket pays 2400 yuan less.
- Benefit: The plan issued by the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council provides: from January 1, 2025, a subsidy is paid for infants and toddlers under 3, until they turn 3, at 3600 yuan per child per year. A child born before January 1, 2025 who is still under 3 is paid for the months that qualify. Either parent, or another guardian, applies. The subsidy is "exempt from individual income tax." When dibao (低保 — means-tested minimum subsistence allowance) and similar assistance are assessed, it "does not count as household or individual income." 3600 yuan is the "national baseline," and "funds needed for a local increase are borne by the local budget." The plan also provides that "governments below the county level may not set their own childcare-subsidy policy or standard." A provincial or municipal government that wants another childcare subsidy, or a higher standard, files it for the record with the competent department at the level above. On the tax side, the State Council in 2022 created a deduction for care of children under 3. In 2023 it raised that deduction together with the children's-education deduction; from January 1, 2023, both are 2000 yuan per child per month. The children's-education deduction covers the time from when the child turns 3 until primary school, and also full-time academic education from primary school through a doctorate. Parents "may choose to have either of them deduct 100% of the standard, or each deduct 50%." Once chosen, it cannot be changed during that year. Worked example: for a child, the deduction is 2000 yuan times 12 months, 24000 yuan in a year. If a single parent takes all of it, that person pays 720 yuan less in the 3% bracket and 2400 yuan less in the 10% bracket. If it is split, each deducts 12000 yuan, and pays 360 yuan less in the 3% bracket and 1200 yuan less in the 10% bracket. The bracket is based on taxable income for the whole year: 3% on the part that does not exceed 36000 yuan, and 10% on the part above 36000 yuan up to 144000 yuan.

- Notes: This is the national baseline. The plan allows a provincial or municipal government to raise the standard or to issue another childcare subsidy; a government below the county level cannot issue its own. Whether your area adds more, and how to claim it, is in your province's implementation plan. Added up over 3 years, it is 10800 yuan, only a small piece of the cost of raising a child. Do not treat it as the reason to have a child or not. If the tax deduction was not filled in during the year, it can still be added at the year-end reconciliation; see Section 5, item 2 (individual income tax reconciliation). Someone whose taxable income for the whole year is already 0 pays no less after the deduction. If the deduction crosses a bracket, the tax saved falls between the figures from the brackets. If both parents pay tax, having the parent in the higher bracket take the whole deduction saves the household more.

### 2. Maternity leave is 98 days; the maternity allowance is paid by the maternity insurance fund at the employer's average monthly wage for the prior year; the father's paternity leave and both parents' parental leave follow the provincial regulation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Your employer files the claim; you do not have to make the trip yourself.
- In plain terms: Having a child comes with at least 98 days of maternity leave, with more days added for a difficult birth or a multiple birth. During that time the maternity insurance fund pays the maternity allowance. If your employer did not enroll you in maternity insurance, the employer pays it. The father's paternity leave, and parental leave for both parents, follow your province's regulation. In Beijing, paternity leave is 15 days, and until the child turns 3 each parent gets 5 working days of parental leave a year.
- Benefit: The number of maternity-leave days is set by the State Council. "Female employees are entitled to 98 days of maternity leave, of which 15 days may be taken before the birth; a difficult birth adds 15 days; for a multiple birth, each additional 1 baby adds 15 days." The money paid during maternity leave is the maternity allowance. For someone already in maternity insurance, it is "paid by the maternity insurance fund at the standard of the employer's average monthly wage for employees in the prior year." If you are not enrolled, the employer pays you at the wage you had before the leave. The father's paternity leave, and parental leave for both parents, are set by each province's population and family planning regulation. Beijing: the mother gets another 60 days of extended maternity leave, and the father gets 15 days of paternity leave. Until the child turns 3, the couple "each receive 5 working days of parental leave a year," and wages must not be cut during that leave. Shanghai: the mother gets another 60 days of maternity leave, and the father gets 10 days of paternity leave. Until the child turns 3, "each of them may take 5 days of parental leave a year," paid as for a normal day at work. Guangdong: the mother gets 80 days of reward leave, and the father gets 15 days of paternity leave. While the child is under 3, each parent gets 10 days of parental leave a year, and wages are paid as usual during the leave. Sichuan, the 2025 edition: the mother gets 90 days of extended maternity leave, and the father gets 30 days of nursing leave. While the child is under 3, the couple each get 10 working days of parental leave a year, and the leave counts as attendance. In 2024 the General Office of the State Council required localities to make sure that "maternity leave, childbirth reward leave, paternity leave, parental leave, and other leave for having a child are actually granted."

- Notes: 98 days is the national minimum. The regulations of the 4 provinces above each add another 60 to 90 days of childbirth reward leave, for a total of 158 to 188 days. How many days another province adds is in that province's regulation. Which side pays wages during the reward leave differs by province. To check your own province, search the National Laws and Regulations Database (flk.npc.gov.cn) for the province name plus "population and family planning regulation," and use the version marked in force. In some provinces paternity leave is called nursing leave. Parental leave is counted in working days in some provinces and in calendar days in others. The day counts for the 4 provinces above are taken from the regulations as of October 2026, and they may be revised later.

### 3. Know this: an employer may not cut your pay or dismiss you for pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing.
- In plain terms: If your employer cuts your pay or fires you because you are pregnant, gave birth, or are breastfeeding, that is illegal. While the child is under 1 year old, you get 1 hour a day to breastfeed, and the employer also may not assign you overtime or night shifts. A violation of that is fined 1000 to 5000 yuan per person. How to claim is in Section 19.
- Benefit: The State Council provides: "An employer may not, because a female employee is pregnant, gives birth, or is breastfeeding, reduce her wages, dismiss her, or end her labor or employment contract." During these 3 periods the employer cannot pay you less, and cannot dismiss you or end the contract. While you are breastfeeding an infant under 1 year old, you have 1 hour of breastfeeding time each day. The employer also may not extend your working hours or assign a night shift. Overtime or a night shift during breastfeeding is fined not less than 1000 yuan and not more than 5000 yuan per person. For a pay cut or a dismissal, this regulation sets no fine. Someone dismissed illegally goes to labor arbitration and asks to be reinstated or paid compensation.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第五条、第九条、第十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Notes: If you are in fact dismissed illegally, the path for a claim is in Section 19. The evidence that matters is the written notice of a transfer or a pay cut, and the chat record. Keep both.

### 4. Count the time cost of raising a child by whose time is taken up, not by how hard it feels
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What it costs is time. Before a child turns 3, that time is basically taken up for the whole day. The hard part is that it is like this every day. You cannot grit your teeth for a few days and be done.
- In plain terms: Turn the hours spent watching the child each day, and the number of times you are woken at night, into time. Then multiply by the hourly wage of the person doing the work. The figure is usually far larger than the money spent on formula and diapers. So it is better to agree the split before the child is born.
- Benefit: Turn the time spent watching the child each day, and the number of times you are woken at night, into hours. Then multiply by the hourly wage of the person doing that work. The figure is usually far larger than the money spent on formula and diapers. Watching the child, cooking, and getting up at night are unpaid, and they still go into the cost the same way. The method is the same as the housework calculation in Section 10. Agree the split first, then decide whether to have a child.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:作者经验，无直接文献；口径见第 10 节
- Notes: The figure does not need to be exact. What helps is both of you, before the birth, talking through the split against the same sheet, so you are not arguing after the birth. If you go back to work after the birth and the employer transfers you or cuts your pay, how to respond is in Section 19, item 23 (do not agree to a transfer and a pay cut).

### 5. Split the money into 3 stretches: ages 0 to 3, compulsory education, and after compulsory education
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no fixed sum. It varies widely by city and by how the child is cared for. The hard part is sitting down and calculating the 3 stretches separately, instead of naming a figure by feel.
- In plain terms: Money from ages 0 to 3 is mostly about how the child is cared for: you, grandparents, or someone you hire. From primary school through junior high, it is mostly housing and spending outside school. After that, it is mostly the path through the next level of school. The 3 stretches are spent in completely different ways. Adding them together only produces a frightening total.
- Benefit: The large part from ages 0 to 3 is how the child is cared for: you, grandparents, or someone you hire. During compulsory education (primary school through junior high), the large part is housing and spending outside school. After that, the large part is the path through the next level of school. These 3 stretches of money are spent in completely different places. Added together, they only produce a frightening total.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:作者经验，无直接文献
- Notes: First count the money you can definitely receive: the childcare subsidy, the maternity allowance, and yibao (医保 — basic medical insurance) reimbursement. Then see how much is still short. Do not decide from the online claim that raising a child costs so-and-so many tens of thousands of yuan. That kind of figure has no clear scope. You do not know what it counted and what it left out.

### 6. Keep a separate account for having a child for your elders, for the marriage, and for old age
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What it costs is 1 serious conversation. The hard part is saying it plainly, without talking around it.
- In plain terms: Someone else wanting you to have a child can be a factor you weigh. The time and the money, though, are on your own account. "Raising a child to provide for your old age" needs its own line, because it bets on another person's means and willingness 20 years later. Also write down the consequences of not having a child, and compare the sides.
- Benefit: The approach is the same as in Section 10. Other people's expectations can be a factor you weigh. The cost goes on your own account, not on theirs. "Raising a child to provide for your old age" especially needs its own line, because it assumes another person's means and willingness 20 years later.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:作者经验，无直接文献；同类分析见第 10 节
- Notes: Write down the consequences of not having a child, and set them next to the consequences of having a child. An account written on only a single side is not an account.

### 7. If you plan to wait a few more years to have a child, count the woman's age first: around 40, the chance of conceiving without treatment and the success rate of in vitro fertilization both fall markedly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It costs no money. What it costs is 1 serious conversation. The hard part is putting having a child, work, and buying a home on the same timeline, instead of only pushing the birth later.
- In plain terms: The older the woman is, the harder it is to conceive without treatment. Just past 40, the chance of conceiving in a given month is only about half the chance just past 30. After a pregnancy starts, the share of pregnancies that miscarry also rises, to about a third at 40 to 44. Having the egg fertilized outside the body does not make up for that. At 43 and 44, only about 5% of embryo transfers end in a birth.
- Benefit: For conceiving without treatment, a U.S. follow-up study looked at women aged 30 to 44, with no history of infertility, who had just started trying. Compared with ages 30 and 31, the chance of conceiving in a given month was about 53% lower at 40 and 41 (ratio 0.47, 95% CI 0.28-0.78), and about 59% lower at 42 to 44 (0.39, 0.16-0.93). At 38 and 39 it was about 30% lower (0.70, 0.48-1.01); the range just touches "no difference." Ages 32 and 33 did not differ from 30 and 31. Women who had never been pregnant had a lower chance at every age. In a similar Danish study, the chance for women aged 35 to 40 was 0.77 times the chance for women aged 20 to 24. The man's age matters much less: for men aged 35 to 39 the figure is 0.95. For miscarriage, Norwegian registry records of about 420000 pregnancies in 2009 to 2013 show the share was lowest at 25 to 29, at 9.8%. It was 10.8% at 30 to 34, 16.7% at 35 to 39, 32.2% at 40 to 44, and 53.6% at 45 and older. For the embryos' chromosomes, a U.S. fertility center tested 15169 in vitro fertilization embryos. Chromosomal abnormalities rose steadily with age after 26. Among people doing in vitro fertilization at 26 to 37, the share whose cultured embryos were all chromosomally abnormal was 2% to 6%; at 42 it was 33%, and at 44 it was 53%. For the success rate of in vitro fertilization, preliminary 2022 figures from the UK fertility regulator, using the woman's own eggs and a fresh embryo transfer, give the share of transfers that led to a birth as 35% at 18 to 34, 26% at 35 to 37, 18% at 38 and 39, 10% at 40 to 42, and 5% at 43 and 44. The domestic rule: the 2003 Ministry of Health Norms on Human Assisted Reproductive Technology state that it is "prohibited to provide human assisted reproductive technology to couples who do not meet the state population and family planning laws and regulations, or to single women." In 2024 the General Office of the State Council required localities to bring suitable assisted-reproduction procedures into yibao (医保 — basic medical insurance) reimbursement. In July 2025 the National Healthcare Security Administration said that all 31 provinces and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps had fully included assisted reproduction in reimbursement, and that in 2024 more than 1000000 person-times received the benefit.
