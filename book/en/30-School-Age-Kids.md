# 30. Children after starting school

Section 20 covers the first year after a child is born. Section 18 covers how much money and time raising a child costs. The years in between, ages 3 to 18, are in this section. The item on screen time by age starts at age 0, because the official tiers are 0–3, 3–6, and primary/secondary school; splitting them would make the guidance harder to use. This section answers one question: when “don’t delay schoolwork” collides with “the body,” which things cannot wait. They cannot wait because they have time windows; once missed, they cannot be made up. Whether schoolwork should be that intense is not discussed here — only the windows and the numbers.

The first eight items, item 12 (cycloplegic refraction), item 13 (pit and fissure sealants), items 15 through 19, and item 21 (head hit during sport) are scored on death and lasting physical or mental harm. Among them, items 16 and 17 are about how to parent at home: no hitting or yelling, and take a parenting class that teaches concrete skills. Item 18 is about not sending a child to a closed “internet addiction” school. Item 19 is about teaching a child which body parts are private and to tell you if they were touched. Item 9 (do not buy products that “cure myopia”) and item 20 (do not ride on the road under age) are scored on money. Item 10 (written rules on sleep, homework, PE, and rankings), item 11 (leave of absence with enrollment kept), and item 14 (when a child loves games) are scored on time. Drowning, traffic, helmets, and HPV vaccine are in Section 1. Infants are in Section 20. Scams against children and game top-ups are in Section 5. Red lines minors themselves must not cross are in Section 9. How to recognize an emergency and call a car is in Section 13. Children after a parent dies are in Section 29. None of that is repeated here.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below (numbers in parentheses are item numbers).

**Care that cannot wait**: sudden severe pain — go to hospital at once (item 1); do not put off treatment to wait for exams (item 2); the annual student health-check report (item 7).

**Eyes and teeth**: enough outdoor time, 2 hours a day (item 4); screen rules by age (item 5); deeper myopia degrees (item 6); do not buy products that “cure myopia” (item 9); cycloplegic refraction (item 12); pit and fissure sealants (item 13).

**At school**: report bullying the same day (item 3); written rules on sleep, homework, PE, and rankings (item 10); leave of absence with enrollment kept (item 11).

**Mental health and parenting at home**: depression screening (item 8); when a child loves games (item 14); when a child says they like the same sex (item 15); do not hit when disciplining (item 16); take a parenting class (item 17); do not send to an internet-addiction school (item 18); know private body parts (item 19).

**Roads and playing fields**: do not ride on the road under age (item 20); after a head hit in sport, do not play again that day (item 21).
### 1. Go to the hospital immediately if a child reports sudden, worsening severe pain—do not make them tough it out until school is over
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: Free. Take half a day off work and take your child to the emergency room.
- In plain terms: Some medical conditions are measured in hours. If a boy suddenly develops severe pain on one side of his scrotum, it may be testicular torsion—the blood vessels attached to the testicle twist, cutting off its blood supply. Treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles can be saved; delayed to 19 to 24 hours, that drops to 42.5%. Do not wait until school lets out for this kind of pain, and go to the hospital even if the pain has already lasted for hours.
- Benefit: A systematic review pooled 30 case series covering 2,116 patients with testicular torsion. Among them, 1,283 cases were broken down into 6-hour windows from pain onset: testicular salvage rates were 97.2% for 0 to 6 hours, 79.3% for 7 to 12 hours, 61.3% for 13 to 18 hours, 42.5% for 19 to 24 hours, 24.4% for 25 to 48 hours, and 7.4% beyond 48 hours. Grouped into broader intervals, the salvage rate was 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% for 13 to 24 hours, and 18.1% beyond 24 hours. The authors concluded that the salvageable window can extend beyond the traditionally cited 6 to 8 hours; therefore, seeking emergency care is still worthwhile even after prolonged pain, rather than assuming it is already too late. In US pediatric inpatient data from 2000, 2003, and 2006, 2,443 boys aged 1 month to under 18 years underwent surgery for testicular torsion. Incidence peaked twice: during the first year of life and at early puberty.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>；Zhao LC, Lautz TB, Meeks JJ, Maizels M. (2011). Pediatric testicular torsion epidemiology using a national database: incidence, risk of orchiectomy and possible measures toward improving the quality of care. Journal of Urology, 186(5), 2009-2013. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2011.07.024>
- Notes: Tell boys in advance: if you ever feel sudden, severe pain down there, speak up immediately—there is no need to be embarrassed. Testicular torsion is most common in boys just entering puberty. In the author's experience, many children feel too shy to mention it and try to tough it out. Other emergencies that run on a strict clock include acute appendicitis (which can perforate if delayed), diabetic ketoacidosis (a severe complication of diabetes), and meningitis. The hourly progression of damage for these has not been individually audited in this section. The author summarizes their shared red flags: sudden onset, steady worsening, and feeling completely different from any past illness. For how to identify medical emergencies and call an ambulance, see Section 13.

### 2. Do not delay necessary medical treatments just to "wait until exams are over" — some treatment windows follow bone growth, not exam schedules
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: Free in itself; the only decision is whether to act earlier or later. Taking time off school and attending follow-up visits takes time, and the actual cost of medical treatment is separate. The hard part is that the brace must be worn for at least 18 hours a day, which the child has to endure continuously.
- In plain terms: Scoliosis is a condition where the spine curves sideways. A brace is a rigid vest worn on the torso to hold the spine in place. If worn while bones are still growing, 75% of children do not progress to a 50-degree curve by the time their bones finish growing, compared with only 42% of those who simply watch and wait without a brace. The window for bracing closes when bone growth stops, not when school entrance exams end — waiting until after exams may mean missing it completely.
- Benefit: A multicenter trial enrolled 242 patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis who met the criteria for bracing. Of these, 116 were randomized and 126 chose their own treatment preference. Treatment failure was defined beforehand as curve progression to 50 degrees or more; treatment success was defined as not reaching this threshold by the time skeletal maturity was achieved. Children in the bracing group were instructed to wear the brace for at least 18 hours per day. Analyses were conducted based on the initial assigned group (intention-to-treat), regardless of whether participants actually wore the brace for the recommended duration. In the randomized cohort, the success rate was 75% in the bracing group versus 42% in the observation group (odds ratio OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16). In the combined randomized and preference cohorts, the rate was 72% versus 48% (adjusted odds ratio OR 1.93, 95% CI 1.08–3.46). A greater number of hours of daily brace wear was significantly associated with higher success rates (P < 0.001). The trial was stopped early owing to the clear efficacy of bracing.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is used as an example here because it has randomized controlled trials and a clearly defined window of opportunity. It also happens to be a key screening item during student physical examinations (see Item 7). In the author's experience, similar conditions include strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and elective surgeries such as those for congenital heart disease. Delaying treatment for these conditions may lead to worse outcomes or higher costs. These examples have not been verified individually in this section; consult a specialist regarding specific treatment windows. If school attendance is a concern, students in compulsory education can apply for a formal leave of absence of up to 1 year while retaining their student status (see Item 11).

### 3. Report school bullying the same day and demand written action; call the police directly if it involves physical violence, extortion, or spreading rumors
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: Free. Contact the homeroom teacher as well as the school's anti-bullying committee. Call 110 to report to the police. The hard part is standing firm against pressure to "not make a big deal out of it."
- In plain terms: Children bullied by peers are significantly more likely to have suicidal thoughts and suicide attempts. Upon receiving a report, schools are required to investigate immediately, administer disciplinary action if bullying is confirmed, and by law cannot cover up serious cases—they must report them to the police. Report it on the very same day, insist on the formal determination procedure, and keep a written paper trail.
- Benefit: Across 34 studies totaling 284,375 children and adolescents, peer bullying was associated with suicidal ideation, with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37). In an additional 9 studies involving 70,102 individuals, being bullied was associated with suicide attempts, with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.55 (95% CI 1.95–3.34). Results remained consistent across sex, age groups, and study quality. The association between cyberbullying and suicidal ideation was stronger than that for offline bullying. Article 21 of the *Provisions on the Protection of Minors in Schools* lists five categories of conduct: hitting, kicking, slapping, scratching/biting, pushing/shoving, and pulling/dragging; verbally abusing, mocking, and calling insulting nicknames; snatching, extorting, or deliberately damaging property; malicious exclusion and isolation; and fabricating facts to defame or spread rumors via the internet. It further specifies that intentional or malicious actions by a party with an advantage in age, physical strength, or numbers that cause personal injury, property loss, or psychological harm can be determined as bullying. Article 22 requires school faculty and staff who notice abnormal student emotions or physical injuries to promptly investigate and report them. Article 23 requires schools, upon receiving a report, to investigate immediately, refer the matter to the student bullying governance body for formal determination and resolution, and notify parents to participate. Where bullying is confirmed, educational disciplinary measures or penalties must be imposed. Serious bullying that violates public security administration or constitutes suspected crimes must not be concealed and must be reported promptly to public security organs and education administrative departments (Nationwide, effective September 1, 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>；教育部等十一部门 (2017). 加强中小学生欺凌综合治理方案（教督〔2017〕10 号，三（一））. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5294435.htm>
- Notes: The warning signs highlighted in Article 22 are noticeable emotional abnormalities and physical injuries. If unexplained injuries appear on a child or their mood suddenly changes, ask questions first. This meta-analysis synthesized observational tracking data without randomized controlled groups, so it cannot prove that bullying alone causes suicide. However, the direction and magnitude of the risk are clear. The *Plan for Strengthening Comprehensive Governance of Bullying Among Primary and Secondary School Students* also requires strictly distinguishing bullying from ordinary roughhousing and play among students. Schools are legally obligated to make a formal determination and cannot simply brush incidents off as "kids just messing around."

### 4. Have the child spend at least 2 hours outdoors each day — among unpaid, drug-free options, this is the only myopia-prevention method backed by randomized trials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. But time must be cleared each day, often squeezed from homework and online classes. The hard part is doing it every day, not a few times.
- In plain terms: An extra 40 minutes of outdoor class each day in grade 1 cut new myopia over three years from about four in ten to about three in ten. The health commission’s dose is at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor time a day, or 14 hours a week in total. Cloudy days count.
- Benefit: Grade-1 students in 12 Guangzhou primary schools were assigned by lot at school level (intervention schools 952 people, control schools 951, mean age 6.6 years). The intervention added one 40-minute outdoor activity class each school day and encouraged parents to take children out more after school. Over 3 years, new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group (259 of 853 eligible) and 39.5% in controls (287 of 726). Difference −9.1 percentage points (95% CI −14.1 to −4.1, P<0.001); the true gap sits roughly between 4 and 14 percentage points fewer. Over 3 years, refractive progression (spherical equivalent change) was −1.42 D versus −1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group progressed a little less. D is the unit for refractive power. Axial length (front-to-back eye length) growth did not differ between groups (P=0.07). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents (防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条) requires at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor activity a day, or 14 hours a week cumulative. It notes that even on cloudy days outdoor activity has a protective effect, and asks to avoid midday high heat and strong sun. Drug prevention of myopia also has lot-assigned trials. In Hong Kong, 474 children aged 4 to 9 without myopia were split into three groups and used eye drops once nightly for two years. With 0.05% atropine, myopia over two years was 28.4%; with placebo, 53.0%. With 0.01% atropine it was 45.9%, not different from placebo
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；Yam JC, Zhang XJ, Zhang Y, et al. (2023). Effect of Low-Concentration Atropine Eyedrops vs Placebo on Myopia Incidence in Children: The LAMP2 Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA, 329(6), 472-481. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.24162>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: This trial ran only 3 years, and participating children all started at age 6. A 9.1 percentage-point gap in myopia rates only keeps some children from becoming myopic; it does not guarantee no myopia. The same ten core points also give a few practices. Reading and writing: “one chi, one fist, one cun” (一尺一拳一寸). After 20 minutes of near work, look into the distance for 20 seconds or more. For primary and secondary students, non-study screen use should not exceed 15 minutes at a time or 1 hour a day in total. “Being outdoors” is not the same as “PE class”; standing outside at recess counts. Authors of the atropine trial say more trials are needed to confirm, and long-term safety still needs watching. Whether to use drops and at what strength, ask an eye doctor. The author found no other unpaid, drug-free method with a lot-assigned trial.

### 5. No screens for ages 0–3, avoid screens for ages 3–6, and cap non-study use at 1 hour a day for school students
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,孩子 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is taking screens away from children, and for parents to look at their own screens less in front of them.
- In plain terms: The National Health Commission recommends zero screen time for children aged 0 to 3, and avoiding screens as much as possible for ages 3 to 6. For primary and secondary students, non-study screen use should not exceed 15 minutes at a time and 1 hour a day. The reasoning is that long hours of close-up screen viewing tend to bring on nearsightedness earlier. But the evidence linking screens to nearsightedness is relatively soft; what has proven real impact in trials is 2 hours outdoors every day.
- Benefit: The fourth point of the *Ten Core Knowledge Points for Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents* states verbatim: "Infants and toddlers aged 0–3 should not use electronic screen products such as mobile phones, tablets, or computers; young children aged 3–6 should avoid exposure to and use of electronic screen products such as mobile phones, tablets, or computers as much as possible; for primary and secondary school students, non-study use of electronic screens should not exceed 15 minutes per session, and cumulative daily use should not exceed 1 hour." The same point notes that "prolonged, close-up use of electronic screen products easily depletes children's hyperopia reserve and is an important cause of the early onset and high incidence of myopia in children and adolescents." The 2018 implementation plan by eight government agencies including the Ministry of Education sets identical requirements for parents, adding: "The younger the child, the shorter continuous screen time should be." The research evidence is much weaker: a systematic review included 33 studies, with 11 pooled in meta-analysis, spanning participants from 3 months to 33 years old. For mobile phones and tablets alone, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Combined with computers, it was 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors concluded only that it was "potentially associated," noting that none of the 33 studies had an objective and reliable measurement of screen time.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: Grade B was assigned because this is an official tiered guideline rather than a figure measured in trials. The lower bound of the study's odds ratio lands right at 1.00, and the authors only went so far as to call it "potentially associated." Treat this as an easy-to-follow house rule, not as "no screens means no nearsightedness." What is actually backed by randomized trials is Item 4 (2 hours outdoors). Preschoolers also need eye exams. The same document states that regular refraction screening should be conducted at ages 1–3, 4–6, and after age 7 to check how much hyperopia reserve remains (see Item 12 for cycloplegic refraction). The link between screen time and children's language skills was also evaluated in a meta-analysis pooling 42 studies with 18,905 participants. More screen time correlated with weaker language skills (r = −0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). However, co-viewing and educational programming were positively correlated (r = 0.16 and 0.13). This is correlation, not causation; it simply shows that substituting screens for talking with children is a losing trade.

### 6. Treat myopia as lasting structural damage: higher diopters mean greater lifetime risks of retinal detachment, macular degeneration, and glaucoma
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: Free. There is nothing to do for this step itself; actionable steps are covered in items 4 and 5 (two hours outdoors daily, age-based screen limits).
- In plain terms: Nearsightedness does not cure itself, prescriptions do not reverse, and it is not simply a matter of putting on glasses. Compared with people who have normal vision, individuals with myopia face far higher risks of macular degeneration and retinal detachment, with the steepest rise occurring above −6.00 D. The higher a child's prescription climbs, the greater the health risks they will carry into adulthood.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies published up to June 2019, categorizing myopia into three tiers: mild (−0.50 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), and high (≤−6.00 D). Compared with non-myopic individuals, the odds ratios for myopic maculopathy were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34), respectively. For retinal detachment, the odds ratios were 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract, odds ratios were 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, the odds ratio was 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91) for mild myopia, and 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52) for moderate and high myopia combined. For visual impairment among individuals aged 60 and older, the odds ratios across the three tiers were 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). This aligns with the official Chinese guideline *Ten Core Knowledge Points for the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents*: severe myopia is defined as higher than −6.00 D; in this population, the incidence of blinding eye diseases—including cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic maculopathy, retinal detachment, and myopic optic neuropathy—is significantly higher than in the general population, and "myopia is preventable and controllable, but irreversible."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: The pooled studies were all observational studies, and the odds ratios are unusually large. The confidence interval for macular degeneration in high myopia spans from 230 to 3104, indicating substantial heterogeneity across individual studies. These figures should be interpreted by order of magnitude rather than as precise values. The visual impairment data in the Benefit section was calculated specifically for individuals aged 60 and older. The authors inferred from this that these complications mostly manifest in middle and older age. Myopia is not solved simply by putting on glasses; it is well worth the effort to delay its onset and slow its progression. For follow-up examinations after diagnosis, see item 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Avoid purchasing products claiming to cure myopia; see item 9 (curing myopia). 〔600〕 〔600〕 〔600〕

### 7. Take a close look at your child’s annual school health report and follow up on any abnormalities right away
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Under China’s Compulsory Education Law, all school health check fees are covered by school operating budgets, so parents pay nothing extra.
- In plain terms: Primary and secondary schools run one student health check a year. On the report, focus on overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries. Do not glance at height and weight and put the sheet away. If any of these are abnormal, take the child to a specialist that same year and get it clear.
- Benefit: The 2021 Administrative Measures for Health Checks of Primary and Middle School Students requires all such schools to run one health check per year for every enrolled student. The physical exam covers the head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, and lymph nodes, while the eye exam checks external eye condition, distance vision, and refractive errors (degree of myopia or hyperopia). The individual report issued to each student must include all test results, an overall assessment, and tailored health guidance. Overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal curvature issues, poor eyesight, and dental cavities are all designated as priority areas for follow-up. Local education authorities are also required to keep records of students with health problems and conduct regular follow-ups. All costs for these compulsory education health checks are paid by school budgets, with no extra charges for parents (issued nationwide in September 2021).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: This grade is assigned because the document is a set of administrative rules for schools, not a study on the actual effectiveness of these checks. It guarantees that schools will run annual checks covering the listed items and follow up on any abnormalities, but does not confirm that all identified issues will receive timely intervention. These health checks are only a preliminary screening tool, not a formal diagnosis. For poor eyesight, a pupil dilation refraction test at an ophthalmology clinic is required (see Item 12). For spinal curvature issues, a visit to an orthopedics or spinal surgery specialist is necessary (see Item 2 — do not delay treatment to wait until after exams). Parents are responsible for arranging these follow-up appointments for their children.

### 8. Screen kids aged 12 to 18 for depression once based on US preventive guidelines; do not treat school mental health surveys as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: 0 to several tens of yuan for a screening questionnaire at a community health center or hospital outpatient clinic. Calling the 12355 and 12356 hotlines is free.
- In plain terms: US preventive guidelines recommend screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depression, while evidence for children aged 11 and younger is insufficient. In the US, suicide is the second leading cause of death among youth aged 10 to 19. Annual mental health surveys at school are general screenings and cannot serve as a medical diagnosis. If a child says they feel miserable, book an appointment directly in psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening for major depressive disorder in adolescents aged 12 to 18 (Grade B recommendation). Grade B means there is moderate certainty that the net benefit is moderate, making it worth doing. For screening major depressive disorder in children aged 11 and younger, the evidence is insufficient (I statement, meaning evidence is lacking and no recommendation can be given). For specifically screening suicide risk in children and adolescents, evidence on benefits and harms is also insufficient (I statement). The task force found no evidence on how often screening should occur, so the optimal screening interval remains unclear. It notes that repeat screening may be most productive in adolescents with depression risk factors. The document points out that suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19. It also notes that major depression in children and adolescents is strongly linked to many later problems. These issues include recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and increased risks of suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and suicide death. The "Ten Measures to Further Strengthen Mental Health Work for Primary and Secondary School Students" requires building a three-tier service system consisting of homeroom teachers, full-time and part-time mental health teachers, and psychiatrists. Each student undergoes a psychological assessment generally no more than 1 time per school year. The document also calls for advancing mechanisms for psychiatric referral and medical treatment as well as post-recovery return to school. It calls for leveraging the 12355 youth service desk and 12356 psychological assistance hotline (nationwide, issued October 2025).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>；US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: Screening（Screening Intervals 一段）. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-depression-suicide-risk-children-adolescents>
- Notes: Grade B is assigned because the source provides a recommendation grade rather than an effect size that can be converted into numbers. Screening once is the author's minimum baseline; those with depression risk factors can be rescreened at intervals. This remains a US recommendation, and China has no corresponding official screening guideline. A positive screening result only indicates that a further clinical assessment is needed; it is not a diagnosis. For managing suicidal ideation, see Section 1, Item 25. ### 12. If poor vision is detected, go to the hospital for cycloplegic refraction and follow up at the recommended intervals as advised by the doctor

### 13. Get dental sealants once permanent molars come in
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: A few tens to one or two hundred yuan per tooth. No anesthesia or drilling is needed, and each tooth takes only a few minutes. Many areas offer free public programs for eligible children.
- In plain terms: Dental sealant coats the deep grooves on the chewing surface of back teeth with resin so food particles cannot get trapped inside. Two years after application, sealed teeth develop fewer cavities, and protective benefits remain visible after four years. Exactly how much risk drops depends on the child's baseline risk of tooth decay.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review synthesized 38 trials involving 7,924 children aged 5 to 16 years, comparing resin-based sealants with no sealant. For the first permanent molars of children aged 5 to 10 years, the odds ratio (OR) for developing dental caries at 24 months was 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19). This estimate came from 7 trials with 1,548 children randomized and 1,322 evaluated, representing moderate-quality evidence. An odds ratio cannot be directly treated as the ratio of cavity percentages between the two groups. The actual risk reduction depends on how many teeth would otherwise decay without sealant. At 48 to 54 months, the odds ratio was OR 0.21 (0.16–0.28), with sealed teeth still developing fewer cavities. The authors concluded that resin-based sealants prevent caries better than no sealant at 24 months. Across 4 trials that reported adverse events, none occurred. There is insufficient evidence to determine which sealant material is superior among other types such as glass ionomer.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>

Many regions include sealants for age-eligible children as a free public program; consult your local Chinese CDC or community health service center. ### 17. Take a parenting class that teaches concrete techniques if your child is hard to manage and you keep resorting to yelling or hitting
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: Under the law, parenting schools run by schools or kindergartens and online parenting schools set up by provincial-level or higher governments are non-profit public-interest services. The evidence-backed classes meet weekly or every other week for 1 to 2 hours per session, running from 4 to 24 weeks. You have to practice the techniques at home. ### 19. Teach children about private body parts, to speak up if touched or sworn to secrecy; believe them first without scolding, then call the police and seek medical care

### 21. If a child hits their head during sports and gets a headache, dizziness, or feels dazed, pull them from play for the rest of the day; seek emergency care immediately if they vomit repeatedly, grow increasingly drowsy, or their headache worsens

- Cost: Free. Pull the child from play and do not let them practice or compete for the rest of the day. The hard part is when the game is close, the child insists they are "fine," and coaches and parents both want them to keep playing.
- In plain terms: Kids who keep playing after a concussion take an average of 44 days to recover, compared to about 22 days for those pulled off the field immediately. If something feels off after a head hit, keep them off the field for the rest of the day until a doctor clears them to return. Repeated vomiting, growing so drowsy they cannot be woken up, worsening headaches, slurred speech, or seizures are all red flags that call for an immediate trip to the emergency room.
- Benefit: A concussion is a temporary disruption of brain function after a blow to the head, and imaging scans usually show nothing abnormal. A US study tracked 69 athletes who suffered concussions during sports (average age about 15). The 35 athletes removed from play immediately took an average of 22.0 days to recover, while the 34 who continued playing with symptoms averaged 44.4 days (P=0.003, unlikely to be by chance). Those who kept playing were also far more likely to have a recovery dragging beyond 21 days (the original study stated "8.80 times more likely" without specifying the exact ratio type). The 2022 Amsterdam International Consensus Statement on Concussion in Sport stated that athletes who exhibit loss of consciousness, seizures, unsteadiness, confusion, behavioral changes, or memory loss must be removed from play immediately. They should not return to competition or practice that day, unless an experienced healthcare provider conducts an immediate on-site evaluation and rules out a concussion. Guidelines from the US CDC for parents and coaches are even more direct: remove the athlete immediately upon suspected symptoms, do not allow return to play that day, and wait until a healthcare provider gives medical clearance. The guidelines emphasize not assessing concussion severity on one's own, as only a healthcare provider can evaluate it.
