# 30. Children Once They Start School

Section 20 covered the first year of life, and Section 18 counted the money and time it takes to raise a child; the stretch in between — the dozen or so years from age 3 to 18 — was left empty. This section fills that gap and answers only one question: when "don't let it interfere with studying" and "the body" collide, which things cannot wait — because they have time windows, and once a window closes it is irreversible. Whether schoolwork should be that much of a grind is outside the scope of this section; it only gives the windows and the numbers.

The first six items are framed around mortality and health endpoints, item 7 is money, and items 8 and 9 are time. Drowning, traffic, helmets, and HPV vaccination are covered in Section 1, infants and toddlers in Section 20, children being scammed and in-game top-ups in Section 5, the red lines minors should not cross themselves in Section 9, recognizing acute illness and hailing a ride in Section 13, and children after a parent's death in Section 29; none of that is repeated here.
### 1. Take your child to the hospital immediately if they suddenly have severe, worsening pain — do not let them endure it until school ends
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; take half a day off and go to the emergency department
- In plain terms: some illnesses are counted in hours. If a boy suddenly has severe pain on one side of the scrotum, it is testicular torsion: the vessel the testicle hangs on has twisted, and the blood supply is cut off. Treated within 6 hours, the testicle is saved in 97.2% of cases; dragged out to 19–24 hours, only 42.5%. This pain cannot wait for the school day to end, and it cannot wait for the exam to be over.
- Benefit: a systematic review that located, by the book, 30 case series covering 2,116 testicular torsion patients and pooled them: among 1,283 cases tallied in 6-hour intervals, testicular survival was 97.2% at 0–6 hours, 79.3% at 7–12 hours, 61.3% at 13–18 hours, 42.5% at 19–24 hours, 24.4% at 25–48 hours, and 7.4% beyond 48 hours; on the cumulative measure it was 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13–24 hours, and 18.1% beyond 24 hours. The authors conclude that the organ can survive longer than the usual 6–8 hours cited — so even if the pain has already lasted a long time, still go get it treated; do not skip it on the grounds that "it is too late anyway"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: tell boys in advance: if there is sudden severe pain down below, say so at once — there is nothing to be embarrassed about. Testicular torsion is most common in adolescent boys, and many put up with it because it is too awkward to bring up. Also counted in hours are acute appendicitis (drag it out and it perforates), diabetic ketoacidosis, and meningitis; this section has not verified one by one how much worse each gets with each hour of delay. Their shared signal: it comes on suddenly, keeps getting worse, and is unlike any previous episode. For recognizing an emergency and getting to hospital, see Section 13.

### 2. Do not postpone indicated treatment until after exams — some windows follow bone age, not the exam calendar
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; the decision is about doing it early or late, not about whether to spend money; leave from school and follow-up visits take time, and the treatment itself is priced separately. The hard part is that the brace must be worn at least 18 hours a day, and the child has to put up with it the whole time
- In plain terms: scoliosis is a spine that curves sideways. A brace is a hard-shell vest worn on the body to hold the spine in place. Worn while the bones are still growing, it markedly lowers the share of curves that end at surgery: 75% success in the braced group versus 42% in the observation-only group. That window follows the time the bones finish growing, not the high-school or college entrance exams. Wait until exams are over, and what is often left is surgery as the only road.
- Benefit: a multicenter trial that enrolled 242 adolescents with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis who met the conditions for bracing; 116 were assigned by lot and 126 chose for themselves whether to wear the brace. Success and failure were defined in advance: a curve growing past 50 degrees counted as treatment failure, still under that degree when the bones finished growing counted as success; counted by the group you started in, whether or not you actually wore it enough. Success was 75% in the randomized bracing group versus 42% in the randomized observation group (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16) — about 4 times the chance of success compared with observation alone. Pooling the randomized and self-selected groups gives 72% versus 48% (OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46 after equalizing the differences the two groups started with — still about 1.9 times). The more hours per day the brace was worn, the higher the success rate (P<0.001 — this relationship is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because the brace clearly worked
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: scoliosis is the example here because it has a randomized trial, a clear time window, and happens to be one of the key items in student health checks (see item 7). In the same category are strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and the kind of congenital heart disease surgery that can be scheduled; the common thread is that the later they are done, the worse the result or the bigger the price. Ask a specialist about the specific window. Missing class is not the problem: during compulsory education a child can suspend schooling for up to 1 year with enrollment status kept — see item 11.

### 3. Report bullying to the school the same day and demand written handling; call the police directly if it involves beatings, money extortion, or spreading rumors
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; go to the head teacher, and also to the school's student-bullying governance body; call 110 if you need the police. The hard part is standing up to persuasion like "do not blow this up"
- In plain terms: a child bullied by peers is about 2.2 times as likely to have suicidal thoughts as one who was not bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have attempted suicide. When a school receives a bullying report it must investigate immediately, and if it determines bullying occurred it must discipline the offending student. When something is serious enough to violate public-security administration or may constitute a crime, the school is not allowed to cover it up — it must report to the police. So report it the same day, demand the formal determination procedure, and keep written records; do not settle for "have the teacher have a word with him."
- Benefit: 34 studies covering 284,375 children and adolescents pooled together: peer bullying was associated with suicidal thoughts, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37) — the chance of such thoughts about 2.2 times that of a child not bullied. Another 9 studies covering 70,102 people found bullying associated with suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. The results held regardless of sex, age, or the quality of the study itself. The link between cyberbullying and suicidal thoughts was stronger than for offline bullying. Article 21 of the Regulations on School Protection of Minors lists five categories of conduct: one, beating, kicking, slapping, grabbing and biting, shoving, and pulling. Two, verbal abuse, mockery, and insulting nicknames. Three, seizing, forcibly taking, or deliberately damaging property. Four, malicious exclusion and isolation. Five, fabricating facts online to defame someone or spreading rumors. The same article provides that where the side with the advantage in age, physical strength, or numbers does these things deliberately or maliciously, causing personal injury, property loss, or mental harm, it may be found to be bullying. Article 22 requires school staff who notice abnormal emotions or physical injuries in a student to promptly inquire and report. Article 23 requires the school, on receiving a report, to investigate immediately, submit it to the bullying governance body for determination and handling, and notify parents to take part. Where bullying is found, educational discipline or a disciplinary sanction must be imposed; serious bullying that violates public-security administration or may constitute a crime must not be concealed and must be promptly reported to the public security organs and the education administrative department (nationwide, in force from September 1, 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Notes: if a child has injuries no one can explain, suddenly refuses to go to school, or keeps losing belongings, start by asking about the signals listed in Article 22. This pooled analysis of multiple studies is only tracking records with no comparison groups, so it cannot prove that bullying alone causes suicide — but the direction and the size of the risk are clear. The Plan for Strengthening the Comprehensive Governance of Bullying Among Primary and Secondary School Students additionally requires a strict distinction between bullying and ordinary play-fighting between students. In other words, the school is obliged to make a determination and cannot wave it off with "kids were just messing around."

### 4. Make sure the child spends at least 2 hours outdoors every day — the only myopia-prevention method currently backed by a randomized trial
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; but it takes time out of every day, which mostly has to be squeezed out of homework and online classes. The hard part is doing it every single day, not a few times and done
- In plain terms: 12 primary schools in Guangzhou ran a trial with assignment by lot: first graders were given an extra 40-minute outdoor activity class every day. Three years later, 30.4% of children at these schools were myopic versus 39.5% at the schools without the extra class — 9.1 percentage points less. The health commission's target is at least 2 hours outdoors in daylight every day, or 14 hours added up over a week. Overcast days still count, and standing outside during recess counts too.
- Benefit: first graders at 12 primary schools in Guangzhou, assigned by lot with the school as the unit (952 children in intervention schools, 951 in control schools, mean age 6.6). The intervention was one extra 40-minute outdoor activity class on every school day, plus encouraging parents to take children out more after school. Over 3 years, new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group — 259 of 853 eligible children. The control group was 39.5% — 287 of 726. The difference between the groups was -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001), meaning the real gap is likely somewhere between 4 and 14 percentage points less. The deepening of prescriptions over 3 years (change in spherical equivalent) was -1.42 D versus -1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04) — the intervention group's eyes deteriorated slightly less. D is the unit prescriptions are measured in. Growth in axial length (the eye front to back) showed no visible difference between the groups (P=0.07). The Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents require at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor activity per day, or 14 hours accumulated per week; they note that outdoor activity has a protective effect even on overcast days, and they require avoiding the hot, high-sun period in the early afternoon
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: this trial ran only 3 years, and the children all started at age 6. The 9.1 percentage point gap in myopia rates means some children are kept from becoming myopic — it is not "guaranteed no myopia." The same Ten Core Messages give a few practices: hold reading and writing at "one chi, one fist, one cun"; after 20 minutes of near work, look into the distance for at least 20 seconds; for primary and secondary students, screen use for non-learning purposes is capped at 15 minutes per session and 1 hour per day in total. "Being outdoors" and "having PE class" are not the same thing — standing outside during recess counts too.

### 5. No screens from 0 to 3, as little as possible from 3 to 6, and for primary and secondary students no more than 1 hour a day for anything but learning
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; the hard part is taking the screen out of the child's hands, and the harder part is parents themselves looking at theirs less in front of the child
- In plain terms: the national health commission's tiers are strict: no screens from 0 to 3, avoid them as much as you can from 3 to 6, and for primary and secondary students no more than 15 minutes per session and no more than 1 hour per day of screen time for anything but learning. The reason is that long hours of close-up eye use burn through the "hyperopia reserve" early, and myopia arrives ahead of schedule. The research evidence on screens and myopia is not as solid as this rule — the only method with a randomized trial behind it is 2 hours outdoors a day.
- Benefit: Article 4 of the Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents reads: "Infants and toddlers aged 0–3 do not use phones, tablets, computers, or other screen-based electronic products; for children aged 3–6, avoid as much as possible their contact with and use of phones, tablets, computers, and other screen-based electronic products; for primary and secondary school students, a single session of electronic screen use for non-learning purposes should not exceed 15 minutes, and cumulative daily use should not exceed 1 hour." The same article states that "long hours of close-up use of screen-based electronic products readily deplete children's hyperopia reserve and are an important reason myopia starts early and is common among children and adolescents." The 2018 implementation plan from the Ministry of Education and seven other departments gives parents the same requirements and adds one line: "the younger the child, the shorter each stretch of electronic product use should be." The research side is much weaker: a systematic review took in 33 studies, 11 of which entered the pooled calculation, on subjects from 3 months to 33 years old. Counting only phones and tablets, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Adding computers in, it was 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors' conclusion goes only as far as "possibly associated," and they state that not one of the 33 studies had a reliable measure of screen time
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: graded B because what the state gives is a tiered rule, not a number measured out by a trial. On the research side, the lower bound of the odds ratio sits right on 1.00, and the authors would only say "possibly associated." So use this rule as a house rule that is easy to enforce — not as "no screens and no myopia." The rule with a randomized trial behind it is item 4 (2 hours outdoors). Preschoolers need eye checks too: the same document says periodic refractive screening is due at ages 1–3, 4–6, and after 7, to see how much hyperopia reserve is left; how the check is done is in item 12 (cycloplegic refraction). The link between screens and children's language ability has also been meta-analyzed: 42 studies, 18,905 people — the longer the screen time, the weaker the language scores (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). But co-viewing, and watching programs with educational content, were positively associated in reverse (r=0.16 and 0.13). This is correlation, not causation; it only shows that swapping screen time for talking with your child is a bad trade.

### 6. Treat myopia as an injury that stays with you: the deeper the prescription, the more retinal detachment, macular disease, and glaucoma later in life
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; this item itself asks nothing of you — the things to do are in items 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, screens managed by age)
- In plain terms: myopia does not go away on its own, and the prescription does not come back down. Nor is it just a matter of "wearing glasses." Compared with people who are not myopic, the chance of myopic maculopathy is more than ten times higher with mild myopia and hundreds of times higher above 600 degrees. For retinal detachment it is about 3 times higher with mild myopia and about 13 times higher above 600 degrees. So the earlier a child becomes myopic and the faster the prescription climbs, the bigger the risk they carry into the future.
- Benefit: a systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies up to June 2019, splitting prescriptions into three tiers: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), and high (≤−6.00 D, that is, above 600 degrees). Compared with people who are not myopic, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were, in order, 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). For retinal detachment, 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract, 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, mild myopia was 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91) and moderate and high myopia pooled were 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52). For visual impairment after age 60, the three tiers were 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents say the same: above 600 degrees counts as high myopia, and in this group the rates of blinding eye diseases such as cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic maculopathy, retinal detachment, and myopic optic neuropathy are clearly higher than in other groups — and "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: everything pooled here is observational research, and the odds ratios are unusually large. The confidence range for high myopia against macular degeneration runs from 230 to 3104, which shows the studies underneath differ greatly — read the numbers as orders of magnitude, not as precise values. Most of these complications only show up in middle and old age, so this is not "what happens tomorrow" but a bill that takes decades to pay off. Myopia itself is not a disability; most people see normally once corrected with glasses. The use of this item is to swap "myopia just means a pair of glasses and that is that" for "it is worth the effort to delay it and keep it under control." How to follow up after diagnosis is in item 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Do not buy products claiming to cure it — see item 9 (curing myopia).

### 7. Read the annual student health check report yourself, and take any abnormal items to a hospital that same year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; during compulsory education the check fee is paid from the school's public operating funds, with no separate charge to parents
- In plain terms: primary and secondary schools organize 1 student health check each year. The report sheet is required to list overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries as key guidance items — exactly the things most easily put off with "let's wait and see." Do not glance at height and weight and file the sheet away. If any of these items is abnormal, take the child to the right specialist that same year to get it clarified; do not wait for the next check.
- Benefit: the Administrative Measures for Health Checks of Primary and Secondary School Students (2021 edition) require primary and secondary schools to organize 1 health check per year for enrolled students. The surgical examination covers head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, and lymph nodes. The ophthalmic examination covers external eye, distance vision, and refraction (the degree of myopia or hyperopia). The individual report sheet must include the results found, an overall assessment, and health guidance recommendations, with overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries (tooth decay) as mandatory guidance priorities. Education administrative departments must keep files on students with health problems and follow up — write it down, then come back and ask after it. During compulsory education, health check fees are paid from schools' public operating funds, with no separate charge to parents (nationwide, issued September 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: graded B because this is a document governing how schools act, not a study of effects. What it guarantees is "checked every year, these items checked, abnormalities followed up" — it does not guarantee that once something is found someone will deal with it. The health check is only a first pass at screening, not a diagnosis. Poor vision goes to ophthalmology for cycloplegic refraction (item 12). Abnormal spinal curvature goes to orthopedics or spinal surgery (item 2 — do not postpone treatment while waiting for exams to end). Both of these steps need a parent to take the child there personally.

### 8. Have a 12-to-18-year-old screened once for depression, and do not treat the school's psychological assessment as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 to a few dozen yuan to fill in a screening questionnaire at a community clinic or hospital outpatient service; the 12355 and 12356 hotlines are free to call
- In plain terms: the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depression, on the grounds that the benefit works out to be moderate and worth doing. For age 11 and under the evidence is not enough. Suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19. The psychological assessment schools run each school year is a census, not a diagnosis. If the child says they feel unwell, or eating, sleep, interests, or grades change markedly in a short time, go straight to psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: the US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (B recommendation). A B grade means moderate certainty and moderate net benefit — worth doing. For screening children aged 11 and under for major depression, the evidence is insufficient (an I statement, meaning the evidence is not enough to give a recommendation). For screening children and adolescents specifically for suicide risk, the evidence on both benefit and harm is insufficient (an I statement). The document notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19. It also notes that major depression in children and adolescents is closely tied to many later problems: recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and elevated risk of suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts, and suicide death. The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools require building a three-tier service system: head teachers, full-time and part-time mental health teachers, and psychiatrists. Every student takes a psychological assessment each school year, generally no more than 1 time. The document also requires advancing mechanisms for referral to psychiatric care and return to school after recovery, and calls for making use of the 12355 youth service desk and the 12356 psychological assistance hotline (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: graded B because what the original gives is a rating of recommendation strength, not an effect that can be converted into numbers. It is also a US recommendation; China has no corresponding official screening guidance. A positive screening result only means another evaluation is needed — it is not a diagnosis. For what to do when there are suicidal thoughts, see Section 1, item 25. For how to respond at home, see Section 29.

### 9. Do not buy products or services claiming to "cure myopia" or "reduce the prescription"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0; on the contrary, it saves this money
- In plain terms: the market regulator's own words are "with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." "Rehabilitation," "recovery," "reducing the prescription," "myopia cure," "nemesis of myopia," and "prescription repair" are listed outright as misleading marketing phrases to be investigated and punished strictly. When you see words like these, rule the product out on the spot — no need to compare prices. Eye-care devices and the like count as medical devices, and e-commerce platforms have a duty to check whether sellers are qualified to sell them.
- Benefit: the notice on the special rectification campaign run by the General Administration of Market Regulation's General Office from November 2021 to March 2022 states plainly: "with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." The notice requires strict punishment under the law of marketing that uses misleading phrases such as "rehabilitation," "recovery," "reducing the prescription," "myopia cure," "nemesis of myopia," or "prescription repair" to sell myopia prevention and control products for children and adolescents. It requires a harder crackdown on marketing that trades under the name of traditional Chinese medicine, or falsely borrows TCM theory and techniques, to deceive consumers. It also requires cracking down hard on two kinds of false and illegal advertising: advertising that uses a patient's name or image for before-and-after treatment comparisons, and advertising that makes guaranteed promises about the safety or efficacy of treatment. The notice requires e-commerce platforms to strictly fulfill their duty to review the business qualifications of sellers of eye-care devices and other medical devices and special commodities — that is, the platform is responsible for checking whether a seller is qualified to sell such things (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 市场监管总局办公厅 (2021). 关于开展儿童青少年近视防控产品违法违规商业营销宣传专项整治行动的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Notes: graded B because the basis is the regulator's enforcement position, not a number measured out by testing products in controlled trials. But on "myopia cannot be cured" the health authorities and the regulators say the same thing: the Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents put it as "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed." The two things with real evidence are in item 4 (outdoors) and item 12 (proper refraction, glasses fitting, and follow-up). Blue-light glasses are covered in Section 6.

### 10. Sleep, homework, PE, and rankings all have explicit written rules — raise them when the school falls short
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: 0; know the provisions, and when necessary raise them with the school or the local education administrative department. The hard part is speaking up
- In plain terms: all of these are in black and white: primary school students sleep 10 hours a day, junior high 9 hours, senior high 8 hours. Morning classes start generally no earlier than 8:20 for primary schools and 8:00 for secondary schools. In 2025 two more were added: at least 2 hours of combined physical activity per day, and no ranking of students by exam scores. If your child is chronically short of sleep, check against these provisions first rather than blaming the child for dawdling.
- Benefit: the Notice on Further Strengthening Sleep Management for Primary and Secondary School Students provides: primary school students should sleep 10 hours a day, junior high students 9 hours, senior high students 8 hours. Morning classes start generally no earlier than 8:20 in primary schools and 8:00 in secondary schools. Schools may not require students to arrive early for unified teaching activities. Primary students' bedtime is generally no later than 21:20, junior high no later than 22:00, senior high no later than 23:00. Off-campus tutoring must end no later than 20:30, and live online tutoring no later than 21:00. From 22:00 to 8:00 the next day, no game services may be provided to minors (nationwide, issued March 2021). The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools add several provisions: fully implement at least 2 hours of combined physical activity per day for primary and secondary students; encourage adopting a "15-minute recess"; do not rank students by exam scores; strictly prohibit assigning mechanical, repetitive, or punitive homework; encourage setting aside one "no-homework day" per week (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部办公厅 (2021). 关于进一步加强中小学生睡眠管理工作的通知（教基厅函〔2021〕11 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/02/content_5597443.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>

### 11. If the child is at breaking point, they can suspend schooling — the school must keep their enrollment, for up to 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: 0; the application is filed with the school by the parents or other legal guardians. The hard part is first accepting that the child will stop for a while
- In plain terms: suspending schooling is a formal institution. The parents or other legal guardians apply; the school confirms and reports to the higher-level student-status management department for approval. A single application covers no more than 1 year, and if the suspension must continue when it expires, a new application is filed. During the suspension the school must keep the student's enrollment status. So "suspending schooling means ruin" does not hold institutionally. The concrete procedures are set by each province — ask the school's academic affairs office or the local education bureau.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Administrative Measures for Student Status of Primary and Secondary School Students sets out how suspension works. A student's suspension is applied for by the parents or other legal guardians. The school confirms and reports to the higher-level student-status management department for approval. When the student returns, the school must promptly handle the relevant procedures. A single suspension application covers no more than 1 year; if continued suspension is genuinely needed after it expires, a new application must be filed. During the suspension the school must preserve the student's enrollment status — the student's standing and place are kept. The concrete methods for suspension and return are drawn up by the provincial education administrative department (nationwide, issued January 2025). The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools require advancing the building of mechanisms for referral to psychiatric care and return to school after recovery, and guiding schools to draw up contingency plans for sudden psychological crisis incidents on campus
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部 (2025). 中小学生学籍管理办法（教基〔2025〕1 号，第十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002620.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: before going to do it, clarify three things: what medical documentation is needed, how the enrollment status is recorded in the system during the suspension, and whether returning means rejoining the original grade or repeating a year. Graded B because the conditions for suspension, the materials to submit, and how the return is handled are all set by provincial education departments — the national level only builds the frame. The Regulations on School Protection of Minors additionally require compulsory-education schools to register and file records of students who drop out, suspend schooling, or take long leaves — so this is a path schools have walked before, not a special case made for you alone.

### 12. If a check finds poor vision, get a cycloplegic refraction at a hospital, then recheck at the intervals the doctor gives
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few dozen to a few hundred yuan for the examination and glasses. After dilation the child will be sensitive to light and blurry at close range, taking half a day to a day or two to wear off
- In plain terms: cycloplegic refraction starts with eye drops that relax the muscle inside the eye that handles focusing, and only then measures the prescription. It is done at a hospital and is the most accurate way to diagnose myopia. The health commission states plainly that these drops cause no harm to healthy eyes and that things return to normal once the drug is stopped. Do not treat a single computer refraction at an optical shop as a diagnosis, and do not refuse to fit the child for glasses because you "fear the prescription will climb."
- Benefit: the Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents set out what cycloplegic refraction is. A cycloplegic agent is used first, then the refraction. A cycloplegic agent is the eye drops that relax the muscle in the eye responsible for focusing. Cycloplegic refraction is a medical refraction done at a hospital, and it is the "gold standard" for diagnosing myopia — that is, the most accurate method there is. Cycloplegic drugs cause no harm to healthy eyes; after use there may be short-term sensitivity to light and blurry near vision, and things return to normal once the drug is stopped. Once myopia is diagnosed, intervene promptly and scientifically through glasses and other correction. Properly fitted glasses effectively correct blurred vision and slow myopia progression. For those whose corrected vision is normal with glasses, preschool children and primary school students recheck every 3 to 6 months, and junior and senior high students every 6 to 12 months, with replacement of glasses decided by the specific situation. Children should receive periodic refractive screening (prescription checks) at ages 1 to 3, 4 to 6, and after 7, to monitor the hyperopia reserve (nationwide, issued July 2023)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: graded B because this is the practice laid out in an official public-education document, not a primary study with concrete numbers. High myopia leads on to retinal detachment and the like, so rechecks are not only about replacing glasses. "Poor vision" found at the student health check is only a screening result; it still needs a full ophthalmic examination at a hospital — see item 7.

### 13. Get pit-and-fissure sealants on the permanent molars once they erupt
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few dozen to one or two hundred yuan per tooth; no anesthesia and no drilling, a few minutes per session. Many localities have free programs for children of the target age
- In plain terms: pit-and-fissure sealing coats the grooves on the chewing surface of the big back teeth with resin and seals them shut, so food debris cannot get packed in. At 24 months after sealing, the chance of tooth decay is clearly lower than without sealing. If 16% of tooth surfaces in unsealed children decay, about 5.2% do in sealed ones. If 40% do in unsealed children, about 6.25% do in sealed ones. The authors' summary is 11 to 51 percentage points less decay than without sealing at 24 months, still effective at 48 months.
- Benefit: 38 trials covering 7,924 children aged 5 to 16, located and pooled by Cochrane by the book. Resin sealants were compared with no sealant. For first permanent molars in children aged 5 to 10, at 24 months after sealing the OR for tooth decay was 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19) — about one-eighth the chance of decay compared with no sealing. This figure comes from 7 trials with 1,548 children assigned by lot and 1,322 finally examined, moderate-quality evidence. If 16% of tooth surfaces in the control group decay within 24 months, the sealed figure is 5.2% (3.13%–7.37%). At a control rate of 40% it is 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%). At 70% it is 19% (12.3%–27.2%). At 48 to 54 months after sealing the OR is 0.21 (0.16–0.28), still about one-fifth of no sealing. The authors conclude that resin sealants produce 11% to 51% less decay than no sealing at 24 months. Among the 4 trials that recorded adverse events, none appeared. On which of the other sealant materials, such as glass ionomer, works better, the evidence is not enough
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Notes: the first permanent molar (the big tooth that will never be replaced) generally comes in around age 6, the second around age 12. Seal while the tooth has just come in and is still sound; once it has decayed, the only option is a filling. Many regions list pit-and-fissure sealing for children of the target age as a free program — ask your local CDC or community health service center. This section did not find a nationwide document that can be verified word for word, so it does not state coverage or age ranges. Dental caries is also a key guidance item in student health checks — see item 7.
