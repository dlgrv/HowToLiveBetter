# 30. Children after starting school

Section 20 covers the first year after a child is born. Section 18 covers how much money and time raising a child costs. The years in between, ages 3 to 18, are in this section. The item on screen time by age starts at age 0, because the official tiers are 0–3, 3–6, and primary/secondary school; splitting them would make the guidance harder to use. This section answers one question: when “don’t delay schoolwork” collides with “the body,” which things cannot wait. They cannot wait because they have time windows; once missed, they cannot be made up. Whether schoolwork should be that intense is not discussed here — only the windows and the numbers.

The first eight items, item 12 (cycloplegic refraction), item 13 (pit and fissure sealants), items 15 through 19, and item 21 (head hit during sport) are scored on death and lasting physical or mental harm. Among them, items 16 and 17 are about how to parent at home: no hitting or yelling, and take a parenting class that teaches concrete skills. Item 18 is about not sending a child to a closed “internet addiction” school. Item 19 is about teaching a child which body parts are private and to tell you if they were touched. Item 9 (do not buy products that “cure myopia”) and item 20 (do not ride on the road under age) are scored on money. Item 10 (written rules on sleep, homework, PE, and rankings), item 11 (leave of absence with enrollment kept), and item 14 (when a child loves games) are scored on time. Drowning, traffic, helmets, and HPV vaccine are in Section 1. Infants are in Section 20. Scams against children and game top-ups are in Section 5. Red lines minors themselves must not cross are in Section 9. How to recognize an emergency and call a car is in Section 13. Children after a parent dies are in Section 29. None of that is repeated here.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below (numbers in parentheses are item numbers).

**Care that cannot wait**: sudden severe pain — go to hospital at once (item 1); do not put off treatment to wait for exams (item 2); the annual student health-check report (item 7).

**Eyes and teeth**: enough outdoor time, 2 hours a day (item 4); screen rules by age (item 5); deeper myopia degrees (item 6); do not buy products that “cure myopia” (item 9); cycloplegic refraction (item 12); pit and fissure sealants (item 13).

**At school**: report bullying the same day (item 3); written rules on sleep, homework, PE, and rankings (item 10); leave of absence with enrollment kept (item 11).

**Mental health and parenting at home**: depression screening (item 8); when a child loves games (item 14); when a child says they like the same sex (item 15); do not hit when disciplining (item 16); take a parenting class (item 17); do not send to an internet-addiction school (item 18); know private body parts (item 19).

**Roads and playing fields**: do not ride on the road under age (item 20); after a head hit in sport, do not play again that day (item 21).
### 1. If a child says sudden pain is getting worse, go to the hospital at once — do not make them tough it out until after school
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. Take a half day off and take the child to emergency care.
- In plain terms: Some illnesses are counted in hours. Sudden severe pain on one side of a boy’s scrotum may be testicular torsion — the vessel feeding the testis twists and blood supply stops. Treated within 6 hours, the testis is saved in 97.2% of cases. Wait until 19 to 24 hours and only 42.5% remain. This pain will not wait until after school or until exams are over.
- Benefit: Following the rules, 30 case series with 2116 patients with testicular torsion were collected and pooled. Of these, 1283 were timed in 6-hour bands. From pain onset, testis salvage was 97.2% at 0 to 6 hours, 79.3% at 7 to 12 hours, 61.3% at 13 to 18 hours, 42.5% at 19 to 24 hours, 24.4% at 25 to 48 hours, and 7.4% after 48 hours. In broader bands: 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13 to 24 hours, and 18.1% after 24 hours. The authors conclude salvage can still happen past the usual 6 to 8 hour talk. So even if pain has gone on a long time, still get care — do not stay home because “it’s too late anyway.” In U.S. pediatric hospital data for 2000, 2003, and 2006, 2443 boys from 1 month to under 18 years had surgery for testicular torsion. Age of onset has two peaks: the first year of life, and early puberty
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>；Zhao LC, Lautz TB, Meeks JJ, Maizels M. (2011). Pediatric testicular torsion epidemiology using a national database: incidence, risk of orchiectomy and possible measures toward improving the quality of care. Journal of Urology, 186(5), 2009-2013. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2011.07.024>
- Notes: Tell boys in advance: if there is sudden severe pain down there, say so at once — no need to be shy. Testicular torsion is common in boys just entering puberty. Many kids are too embarrassed to speak and tough it out; that is the author’s experience. Other hour-counted problems include acute appendicitis (delay can lead to perforation), diabetic ketoacidosis, and meningitis. This section did not verify hour-by-hour harm for each of those. The author’s shared signal: sudden onset, keeps getting worse, unlike any prior episode. How to recognize emergencies and call a car is in Section 13.

### 2. Do not put off needed treatment to “wait until exams are over”; some windows follow bone age, not the exam calendar
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money; the only choice is early or late. Leave and follow-ups take time; the cost of treatment itself is separate. The hard part is that a brace must be worn at least 18 hours a day, and the child must keep putting up with it.
- In plain terms: Scoliosis is a sideways bend of the spine. A brace is a hard shell vest that holds the spine. Worn while bones are still growing, 75% of children never reach a 50-degree curve by the end of growth; observation alone is only 42%. The wearable window follows when bones finish growing, not the high-school or college exams — wait until after exams and you may miss it.
- Benefit: A multi-hospital trial enrolled 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who met criteria for bracing. Of these, 116 were assigned by lot and 126 chose for themselves whether to wear a brace. Success and failure were defined in advance: curve growing past 50 degrees was failure; still below that at skeletal maturity was success. Brace-group children were asked to wear at least 18 hours a day. Results were analyzed by initial assignment, whether or not they actually wore enough. Assigned brace group success 75%, assigned observation only 42% (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16), roughly 4 times the chance of success versus observation alone. Combining the lot-assigned and self-chosen groups: 72% versus 48% (after leveling baseline differences, OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46, still about 1.9 times). More hours worn per day meant higher success (P<0.001; this link is unlikely to be chance). The trial stopped early because the brace worked
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is the example here because it has lot-assigned trials and a clear time window. It is also a focus item on student health checks (see item 7). In the author’s experience, similar cases include strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and congenital heart disease surgeries that can be timed. The later they are done, the worse the result may be, or the higher the cost. This section did not verify each one; ask a specialist about the exact window. If missing class is the fear, during compulsory education a leave of absence can last up to 1 year with enrollment kept — see item 11.

### 3. If a child is bullied, report it to the school the same day and ask for a written process; for hitting, taking money, or spreading rumors, call the police at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. Go to the homeroom teacher and the school’s student-bullying governance body. To call the police, dial 110. The hard part is resisting pressure to “not make a big deal of it.”
- In plain terms: Children bullied by peers are about 2.2 times as likely to have suicidal thoughts as those not bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have attempted suicide. Schools that receive a report must investigate at once; if bullying is found, they must discipline or punish, and serious cases must not be hidden — they must be reported to public security. So report the same day, ask for the formal finding process, and keep a written record.
- Benefit: 34 studies with 284375 children and adolescents pooled. Peer bullying linked to suicidal thoughts, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37), about 2.2 times the chance versus not bullied. Another 9 studies, 70102 people: bullying linked to suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. Results held across sex, age, and study quality. The link of cyberbullying to suicidal thoughts was stronger than offline bullying. Article 21 of the Regulations on School Protection of Minors (未成年人学校保护规定) lists five kinds of conduct: beating, kicking, slapping, grabbing/biting, shoving, pulling; insults, mockery, humiliating nicknames; snatching, forcing goods, or deliberate damage to property; malicious exclusion and isolation; fabricating facts online to defame or spread rumors. The same article says that when the side with an advantage in age, body, or numbers does these things willfully or maliciously and causes bodily harm, property loss, or mental harm, it may be found to be bullying. Article 22 requires staff who notice abnormal mood or bodily injury to inquire promptly and report. Article 23 requires the school, on receiving a report, to investigate at once, submit it to the bullying-governance body for finding and handling, and notify parents to take part. If bullying is found, educational discipline or a disciplinary sanction must follow. Serious bullying that violates public-security administration or may be a crime must not be concealed and must be reported promptly to public security and education authorities (nationwide, in force from 1 September 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>；教育部等十一部门 (2017). 加强中小学生欺凌综合治理方案（教督〔2017〕10 号，三（一））. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5294435.htm>
- Notes: Article 22 names the signals: clear mood changes and bodily injury. If a child has injuries they cannot explain, or mood suddenly goes wrong, ask first. This pooled analysis only follows records and has no assigned controls, so it cannot prove bullying alone causes suicide. The direction and size of the risk are clear. The Plan for Comprehensive Governance of Bullying Among Primary and Secondary Students (加强中小学生欺凌综合治理方案) also requires a strict distinction between bullying and ordinary rough play among students. So the school must make a finding; a line like “kids just playing” is not enough.

### 4. Have the child spend at least 2 hours outdoors each day — among unpaid, drug-free options, this is the only myopia-prevention method backed by randomized trials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. But time must be cleared each day, often squeezed from homework and online classes. The hard part is doing it every day, not a few times.
- In plain terms: An extra 40 minutes of outdoor class each day in grade 1 cut new myopia over three years from about four in ten to about three in ten. The health commission’s dose is at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor time a day, or 14 hours a week in total. Cloudy days count.
- Benefit: Grade-1 students in 12 Guangzhou primary schools were assigned by lot at school level (intervention schools 952 people, control schools 951, mean age 6.6 years). The intervention added one 40-minute outdoor activity class each school day and encouraged parents to take children out more after school. Over 3 years, new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group (259 of 853 eligible) and 39.5% in controls (287 of 726). Difference −9.1 percentage points (95% CI −14.1 to −4.1, P<0.001); the true gap sits roughly between 4 and 14 percentage points fewer. Over 3 years, refractive progression (spherical equivalent change) was −1.42 D versus −1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group progressed a little less. D is the unit for refractive power. Axial length (front-to-back eye length) growth did not differ between groups (P=0.07). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents (防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条) requires at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor activity a day, or 14 hours a week cumulative. It notes that even on cloudy days outdoor activity has a protective effect, and asks to avoid midday high heat and strong sun. Drug prevention of myopia also has lot-assigned trials. In Hong Kong, 474 children aged 4 to 9 without myopia were split into three groups and used eye drops once nightly for two years. With 0.05% atropine, myopia over two years was 28.4%; with placebo, 53.0%. With 0.01% atropine it was 45.9%, not different from placebo
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；Yam JC, Zhang XJ, Zhang Y, et al. (2023). Effect of Low-Concentration Atropine Eyedrops vs Placebo on Myopia Incidence in Children: The LAMP2 Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA, 329(6), 472-481. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.24162>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: This trial ran only 3 years, and participating children all started at age 6. A 9.1 percentage-point gap in myopia rates only keeps some children from becoming myopic; it does not guarantee no myopia. The same ten core points also give a few practices. Reading and writing: “one chi, one fist, one cun” (一尺一拳一寸). After 20 minutes of near work, look into the distance for 20 seconds or more. For primary and secondary students, non-study screen use should not exceed 15 minutes at a time or 1 hour a day in total. “Being outdoors” is not the same as “PE class”; standing outside at recess counts. Authors of the atropine trial say more trials are needed to confirm, and long-term safety still needs watching. Whether to use drops and at what strength, ask an eye doctor. The author found no other unpaid, drug-free method with a lot-assigned trial.

### 5. Ages 0 to 3: no screens; ages 3 to 6: as little as possible; primary and secondary students: non-study use no more than 1 hour a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard parts are taking the screen out of the child’s hands, and parents themselves looking less in front of the child.
- In plain terms: The national health commission says ages 0 to 3 get no screens, and ages 3 to 6 should avoid them as much as possible. For primary and secondary students, non-study use is no more than 15 minutes at a time and no more than 1 hour a day. The reason is that long near work makes myopia come early. Evidence that screens cause myopia is weaker; what has been shown to work is 2 hours outdoors a day.
- Benefit: Article 4 of Ten Core Knowledge Points for Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents (防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条) says in the original wording: “Infants and toddlers aged 0–3 shall not use phones, tablets, computers, or other screen electronic products; children aged 3–6 shall avoid contact with and use of phones, tablets, computers, and other screen electronic products as much as possible; for primary and secondary students, non-study screen use should not exceed 15 minutes per session, and cumulative daily use should not exceed 1 hour.” The same article states that “prolonged near use of electronic screen products readily depletes children’s hyperopia reserve and is an important cause of early and high rates of myopia in children and adolescents.” The 2018 implementation plan from the Ministry of Education and seven other departments gives parents the same requirements and adds “the younger the age, the shorter continuous use of electronic products should be.” Research evidence is much weaker: a systematic review took in 33 studies, 11 entered the pooled calculation, ages from 3 months to 33 years. Counting only phones and tablets, the odds ratio for myopia was 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Combined with computers it was 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors conclude only “may be related,” and state that none of the 33 studies had a reliable measure of screen time
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: Grade B because officials give tiered rules, not numbers from a trial. The lower bound of the research odds ratio sits at 1.00, and the authors only dare say “may be related.” Treat it as a household rule that is easy to run, not as “no screens means no myopia.” What has a lot-assigned trial behind it is item 4 (2 hours outdoors). Preschool eyes should also be checked. The same document says ages 1–3, 4–6, and after 7 should all have regular refractive screening to see how much hyperopia reserve remains. How to check is in item 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Screens and children’s language ability were also pooled in a meta-analysis of 42 studies with 18905 people. Longer screen time went with weaker language (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). But co-viewing and educational content went the other way (r=0.16 and 0.13). That is association, not cause — it only shows that swapping screens for talking with the child is a poor trade.

### 6. Treat myopia as lasting damage: the higher the degree, the more retinal detachment, macular disease, and glaucoma later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. This item itself needs no action; what to do is in items 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, screen rules by age).
- In plain terms: Myopia does not heal on its own, and the degree does not go back down. It is also not only a “wear glasses” matter. Versus people without myopia, the chance of myopic macular disease is over ten times higher in mild myopia and hundreds of times higher above 600 degrees. Retinal detachment is about 3 times higher in mild myopia and about 13 times higher above 600 degrees. So the deeper a child’s myopia climbs, the more risk they carry later.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies before June 2019 into three degree bands: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), high (≤−6.00 D, above 600 degrees). Versus people without myopia, odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). Retinal detachment: 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). Posterior subcapsular cataract: 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28), 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). Open-angle glaucoma: mild 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91); moderate and high combined 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52). Odds ratios for visual impairment at age 60 and above: 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). Ten Core Knowledge Points for Preventing and Controlling Myopia in Children and Adolescents (防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条) says the same: above 600 degrees is high myopia; in that group, rates of cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic macular disease, retinal detachment, myopic optic neuropathy, and other blinding eye diseases are clearly higher than in other groups, and “myopia can be prevented and controlled but is not reversible”
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: Studies pooled were all observational, and the odds ratios are unusually large. For high myopia and macular degeneration, the credible range runs from 230 to 3104, so underlying studies differ a lot. Read the numbers as order of magnitude, not exact values. The visual-impairment set in the Benefit line is for people aged 60 and above. The author infers these outcomes mostly show up in middle and old age. Myopia is not finished once glasses are fitted — it is worth the effort to delay and control it. Follow-up after diagnosis is in item 12 (cycloplegic refraction). Do not buy products that claim a cure — see item 9 (cure myopia).

### 7. Take a close look at your child’s annual school health report and follow up on any abnormalities right away
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. Under China’s Compulsory Education Law, all school health check fees are covered by school operating budgets, so parents pay nothing extra.
- In plain terms: Primary and secondary schools run one student health check a year. On the report, focus on overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries. Do not glance at height and weight and put the sheet away. If any of these are abnormal, take the child to a specialist that same year and get it clear.
- Benefit: The 2021 Administrative Measures for Health Checks of Primary and Middle School Students requires all such schools to run one health check per year for every enrolled student. The physical exam covers the head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, and lymph nodes, while the eye exam checks external eye condition, distance vision, and refractive errors (degree of myopia or hyperopia). The individual report issued to each student must include all test results, an overall assessment, and tailored health guidance. Overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal curvature issues, poor eyesight, and dental cavities are all designated as priority areas for follow-up. Local education authorities are also required to keep records of students with health problems and conduct regular follow-ups. All costs for these compulsory education health checks are paid by school budgets, with no extra charges for parents (issued nationwide in September 2021).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: This grade is assigned because the document is a set of administrative rules for schools, not a study on the actual effectiveness of these checks. It guarantees that schools will run annual checks covering the listed items and follow up on any abnormalities, but does not confirm that all identified issues will receive timely intervention. These health checks are only a preliminary screening tool, not a formal diagnosis. For poor eyesight, a pupil dilation refraction test at an ophthalmology clinic is required (see Item 12). For spinal curvature issues, a visit to an orthopedics or spinal surgery specialist is necessary (see Item 2 — do not delay treatment to wait until after exams). Parents are responsible for arranging these follow-up appointments for their children.

### 8. Conducting a depression screening for kids aged 12 to 18 — don’t rely on school mental health assessments as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->

- Cost: Costs range from zero to just a few dozen yuan; this covers filling out a screening questionnaire at a community clinic or hospital outpatient department. Calling the 12355 and 12356 hotlines is completely free.

- In plain terms: U.S. prevention guidelines recommend screening children aged 12 to 18 for major depression; for age 11 and under the evidence is not enough. In the U.S., suicide is the second cause of death among ages 10 to 19. The school’s yearly mental-health assessment is only a census, not a diagnosis. If the child says they feel bad, go straight to psychiatry or clinical psychology.

- Benefit: In 2022, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement advising that adolescents aged 12 to 18 be screened for major depressive disorder; this recommendation falls under Category B, meaning the evidence supporting it is moderate and the benefits are also moderate, making it worthwhile. For children under 11, there is insufficient evidence to support depression screening (an I statement, which indicates a lack of evidence and no formal recommendation). Likewise, evidence regarding both the benefits and risks of screening children and adolescents specifically for suicide risk is also limited (another I statement). The document emphasizes that suicide is the second leading cause of death among 10- to 19-year-olds, and that major depression in youth is closely linked to numerous later issues, including recurrent depressive episodes and other mental disorders, as well as a heightened risk of suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts, and death. China’s “Ten Measures to Further Improve Mental Health Care for Primary and Secondary School Students” calls for establishing a three-tier support system: homeroom teachers, full- and part-time school counselors, and psychiatrists. Each student should undergo a mental health assessment no more than once per academic year. The measures also stress the need to improve referral pathways to mental health specialists and reintegration processes after recovery. Additionally, they highlight the importance of leveraging the 12355 Youth Service Hotline and the 12356 Psychological Assistance Hotline (issued nationwide in October 2025).

- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>

- Notes: This B rating reflects the original recommendation strength grading used by the task force, rather than a quantifiable measure of actual effectiveness. It represents a U.S.-based recommendation; no equivalent official screening guidelines currently exist in China. A positive screening result merely indicates the need for a follow-up evaluation — it does not constitute a formal diagnosis. Guidance on handling suicidal thoughts can be found in Section 1, Item 25, while family coping strategies are outlined in Section 29.

### 9. Do not buy products or services claiming to “cure myopia” or “reduce refractive error”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 人群=孩子 -->

- Cost: There is no cost involved; in fact, you save this money by avoiding such purchases.
- In plain terms: The State Administration for Market Regulation explicitly states that “under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured.” Phrases such as “recovery,” “restoration,” “reducing refractive error,” “curing myopia,” “myopia remedy,” and “repairing refractive error” are classified as illegal marketing claims subject to strict enforcement. If you encounter any of these terms, you can safely rule out those products without further comparison. Devices such as eye-care instruments are considered medical devices, and e-commerce platforms must verify whether sellers are qualified to sell them.

### 13. Pit and fissure sealants applied after permanent molars emerge
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->

- Cost: Each application costs just a few dozen to a couple of hundred yuan — specifically, between 100 and 200 yuan per tooth. No anesthesia is needed, no tooth grinding is required, and the whole procedure takes only a few minutes. Many regions offer free sealant programs for children who meet the age criteria.

- In plain terms: Pit and fissure sealing fills the grooves on the chewing face of big molars with resin so food scraps cannot pack in. Two years after sealing, the chance of cavities is about one eighth of not sealing. Without sealing, about 16% of tooth surfaces decay; with sealing, only about 5%. It still works four years later.

- Benefit: This conclusion comes from 38 separate trials involving 7,924 children aged 5–16, all selected and pooled by Cochrane according to strict methodological standards. The studies compared children who received resin‑based sealants with those who did not. For first permanent molars in the 5–10 age group, the odds ratio after 24 months was 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19), meaning the risk of decay was roughly one‑eighth that of untreated teeth. This result is based on seven trials with 1,548 participants randomly assigned and 1,322 actually examined; the overall evidence quality is rated as moderate. If the untreated control group shows 16% decayed surfaces after two years, the sealed group shows only 5.2% (range 3.13%–7.37%). When the control group rate reaches 40%, the sealed group still stays at 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%); at a 70% control rate, the sealed group remains at 19% (12.3%–27.2%). After 48–54 months the odds ratio remains low at 0.21 (0.16–0.28), indicating a risk roughly one‑fifth that of untreated teeth. Authors conclude that resin sealants reduce cavity formation by 11%–51% compared with no sealants after two years. No adverse effects were reported in any of the four trials that monitored side‑effects; however, there is insufficient evidence to determine whether glass‑ionomer sealants perform better than other types.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>

### 21. If a child hits their head in sport and then has headache, dizziness, or fogginess, do not put them back in that day; if they keep vomiting, grow sleepier, or the headache keeps getting worse, go to emergency at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. Take the child out; no more practice or competition that day. The hard part is a tight game, the child saying “I’m fine,” and coaches and parents wanting them back in.
- In plain terms: Children who keep playing after concussion take on average 44 days to recover; those taken out at once about 22 days. If something is off after a head hit, do not play again that day — wait until a doctor says yes. Repeated vomiting, growing sleepiness and not waking, headache getting worse, slurred speech, or seizures are signals to go to emergency at once.
- Benefit: Concussion is a temporary disruption of brain function after a head hit; imaging usually shows nothing. A U.S. study followed 69 athletes with sport concussion, mean age about 15. Those who kept playing had 8.80 times the chance of recovery dragging past 21 days versus those taken out. A 2022 Amsterdam international sport-concussion meeting issued a consensus. It says loss of consciousness, seizures, unsteadiness, confusion, behavior change, or inability to remember events means immediate removal. Those people should not return to competition or training that day. The exception is when experienced medical staff check on the spot and confirm it is not from concussion. CDC guidance for parents and coaches is more direct: with suspected symptoms, leave at once, do not return that day, wait until a doctor says return is OK. It also says do not judge severity yourself — only medical staff can assess. It lists signals for emergency care at once: seizures; not recognizing people or places; repeated nausea and vomiting; odd behavior, growing confusion or agitation. Also growing sleepiness after passing out and not waking when called; slurred speech, weak or numb limbs. And headache that keeps getting worse without relief; unequal pupils or double vision (U.S. / multi-country)
