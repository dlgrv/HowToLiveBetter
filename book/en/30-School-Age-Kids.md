# 30. Children Once They Start School

Section 20 covered the first year of life, and Section 18 counted the money and time it takes to raise a child; the stretch in between — the dozen or so years from age 3 to 18 — was left empty. This section fills that gap and answers only one question: when "don't let it interfere with studying" and "the body" collide, which things cannot wait — because they have time windows, and once a window closes it is irreversible. Whether schoolwork should be that much of a grind is outside the scope of this section; it only gives the windows and the numbers.

The first six items are framed around mortality and health endpoints, item 7 is money, and items 8 and 9 are time. Drowning, traffic, helmets, and HPV vaccination are covered in Section 1, infants and toddlers in Section 20, children being scammed and in-game top-ups in Section 5, the red lines minors should not cross themselves in Section 9, recognizing acute illness and hailing a ride in Section 13, and children after a parent's death in Section 29; none of that is repeated here.
### 1. Take your child to the hospital immediately if they suddenly have severe, worsening pain — do not let them endure it until school ends
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; take half a day off and go to the emergency department
- In plain terms: some illnesses are counted in hours. If a boy suddenly has severe pain on one side of the scrotum, often with nausea and vomiting, it is testicular torsion: treated within 6 hours, the testicle is saved in 97.2% of cases; delay to 7–12 hours and the rate drops to 79.3%, at 13–18 hours it is 61.3%, at 19–24 hours 42.5%, and past 48 hours only 7.4%. There is no "take a painkiller first and see," and no waiting for the school day or the exam to end. The same rule applies to any pain that comes on suddenly, keeps getting worse, and is unlike anything before.
- Benefit: a systematic review of 30 case series covering 2,116 testicular torsion patients: among 1,283 cases tallied in 6-hour intervals, testicular survival was 97.2% at 0–6 hours, 79.3% at 7–12 hours, 61.3% at 13–18 hours, 42.5% at 19–24 hours, 24.4% at 25–48 hours, and 7.4% beyond 48 hours; on the cumulative measure it was 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13–24 hours, and 18.1% beyond 24 hours. The authors conclude that the organ can survive longer than the usual 6–8 hours cited, so aggressive treatment is warranted even if the pain has lasted a long time already
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: testicular torsion is most common in adolescent boys, and many endure it because it is too embarrassing to mention — spell this out to boys in advance: when this happens, they must speak up at once. Other conditions also counted in hours are acute appendicitis (delayed, it perforates), diabetic ketoacidosis, and meningitis; this section does not verify their time curves one by one, and the shared signal is "sudden onset, continuously worsening, unlike any previous episode." For recognizing acute illness and how to get to a hospital, see Section 13.

### 2. Do not postpone indicated treatment until after exams — some windows follow bone age, not the exam calendar
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; the decision is about doing it early or late, not about whether to spend money; leave from school and follow-up visits take time, and the treatment itself is priced separately (a brace requires wearing it at least 18 hours a day)
- In plain terms: adolescent idiopathic scoliosis has a clear window — bracing while the bones are still growing markedly lowers the share of curves that progress to needing surgery: in the randomized groups, bracing succeeded 75% of the time versus 42% for observation alone, and wearing it for enough hours worked better; the trial was stopped early because the brace clearly worked. That window tracks skeletal maturity, not the middle-school or college entrance exams. Wait until exams are over, and what you often get is "surgery is the only option left."
- Benefit: a multicenter trial of 242 adolescents with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis who had an indication for bracing (116 randomized, 126 self-selected), with the primary endpoints being curve progression beyond 50 degrees (treatment failure) versus not reaching that degree at skeletal maturity (treatment success): in the intention-to-treat analysis, success was 75% in the randomized bracing group versus 42% in the randomized observation group (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16); combining the randomized and self-selected cohorts gave 72% versus 48% (propensity-score-adjusted OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46); hours of wear per day were significantly positively associated with success (P<0.001); the trial was stopped early because bracing was effective
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: scoliosis is used as the example because it has a randomized trial, a clear time window, and happens to be one of the key items in student health checks (see item 5). In the same category are strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and elective surgery for congenital heart disease — the common thread is that the later they are done, the worse the outcome or the higher the cost; ask a specialist about the specific window. Missing school is not the problem: during compulsory education, a suspension of schooling can last up to 1 year with enrollment status preserved; see item 9.

### 3. Report bullying to the school the same day and demand written handling; call the police directly if it involves beatings, money extortion, or spreading rumors
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; go to the head teacher and the school's student-bullying governance body; call 110 for the police
- In plain terms: a child bullied by peers is about 2.2 times as likely to have suicidal thoughts as one who was not bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have attempted suicide; the link between cyberbullying and suicidal thoughts is stronger than for offline bullying, and this holds regardless of sex and age. Institutionally you have a firm handle: on receiving a bullying report, the school must investigate immediately, and if it determines that bullying occurred it must impose educational discipline or a disciplinary sanction on the offending student; for serious bullying that violates public-security administration or may constitute a crime, the school must not conceal it and must report it to the public security organs. So what you do is report it the same day, demand the formal determination procedure, and keep written records — not "have the teacher have a word with him."
- Benefit: a meta-analysis of 34 studies covering 284,375 children and adolescents: peer bullying was associated with suicidal ideation, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37); in 9 studies covering 70,102 people it was associated with suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34); the results were not moderated by sex, age, or study quality, and the association between cyberbullying and suicidal ideation was stronger than for traditional bullying. Article 21 of the Regulations on School Protection of Minors lists five categories of conduct — beating, kicking, slapping, grabbing, biting, shoving, and pulling; verbal abuse, mockery, and insulting nicknames; seizing, forcibly taking, or deliberately damaging property; malicious exclusion and isolation; and fabricating facts to defame someone or spreading rumors online — and provides that bullying may be found where the side with the advantage in age, physical strength, or numbers acts deliberately or maliciously and causes personal injury, property loss, or mental harm. Article 22 requires school staff, upon noticing abnormal emotions or physical injuries in a student, to promptly inquire and report. Article 23 requires the school, upon receiving a report, to investigate immediately, submit it to the bullying governance body for determination and handling, and notify parents to take part; where bullying is found, it must impose educational discipline or a disciplinary sanction, and for serious bullying that violates public-security administration or may constitute a crime it must not conceal it and must promptly report to the public security organs and the education administrative department (nationwide, in force from September 1, 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Notes: the meta-analysis is observational evidence and cannot prove that bullying alone causes suicide, but the direction and magnitude of the risk are clear. The Plan for Strengthening the Comprehensive Governance of Bullying Among Primary and Secondary School Students additionally requires a strict distinction between bullying and ordinary roughhousing between students — meaning the school is obliged to make a determination and cannot wave it off with "kids just playing around." If a child has injuries no one can explain, suddenly refuses to go to school, or keeps losing belongings, start by asking about those signals from Article 22.

### 4. Make sure the child spends at least 2 hours outdoors every day — the only myopia-prevention method currently backed by a randomized trial
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0, but it takes time out of every day, which mostly has to be squeezed out of homework and online classes
- In plain terms: a randomized trial across 12 primary schools in Guangzhou added a 40-minute outdoor activity class every day for first graders and encouraged more time outside after school; three years later, 30.4% were myopic versus 39.5% at the control schools — 9.1 percentage points less. The health commission's target is at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor time daily, or 14 hours accumulated per week; overcast days still count. What works is being outdoors itself, not any particular sport.
- Benefit: a cluster-randomized trial of first graders in 12 primary schools in Guangzhou (952 children in intervention schools, 951 in control schools, mean age 6.6): the intervention added one 40-minute outdoor activity class on every school day and encouraged parents to increase outdoor activity outside school; cumulative 3-year myopia incidence was 30.4% in the intervention group (259 of 853 eligible children) versus 39.5% in the control group (287 of 726), a difference of -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001); 3-year change in spherical equivalent was -1.42 D versus -1.59 D (difference 0.17 D, P=0.04); axial-length growth did not differ significantly (P=0.07). The Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents requires at least 2 hours of daytime outdoor activity per day or 14 hours accumulated per week for children and adolescents, notes that outdoor activity has a protective effect even on overcast days, and requires avoiding the hot, high-sun period in the early afternoon
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: this trial was tested only within a 3-year intervention window and only in children starting at age 6; the absolute difference in myopia incidence was 9.1 percentage points — not "no myopia." The same Ten Core Messages also give the reading-and-writing "one chi, one fist, one cun" posture rule, looking into the distance for at least 20 seconds after every 20 minutes of near work, and — for primary and secondary students — no more than 15 minutes per session and no more than 1 hour accumulated per day of screen use for non-learning purposes. "Being outdoors" and "having PE class" are not the same thing; standing outside during recess counts too.

### 5. Read the annual student health check report yourself, and take any abnormal items to a hospital that same year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0; during compulsory education the check fee is paid from the school's public operating funds, with no separate charge to parents
- In plain terms: primary and secondary schools organize 1 student health check each year. Surgery checks the spine, ophthalmology checks distance vision and refraction, and the report is required to list overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries as key guidance items — exactly the things most easily put off with "let's wait and see." So do not glance at height and weight and file the sheet away: if any of these items is abnormal, take the child to a specialist that same year to get it clarified, and do not wait for the next check.
- Benefit: the Administrative Measures for Health Checks of Primary and Secondary School Students (2021 edition) require primary and secondary schools to organize 1 health check per year for enrolled students; the surgical examination covers head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, and lymph nodes, and the ophthalmic examination covers external eye, distance vision, and refraction; the individual report sheet must include objective results, an overall assessment, and health guidance recommendations, with overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal spinal curvature, poor vision, and dental caries as mandatory guidance priorities; education administrative departments must keep files on students with health problems and follow up; during compulsory education, health check fees are paid from schools' public operating funds (nationwide, issued September 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: grade B because this is a work specification, not an effectiveness study — it guarantees "checked every year, these items checked, abnormalities followed up," not that a finding will definitely be dealt with. The health check is screening, not diagnosis: poor vision should go to ophthalmology for cycloplegic refraction (item 10), abnormal spinal curvature to orthopedics or spinal surgery (item 2), and a parent has to take the child to both of those steps personally.

### 6. Have a 12-to-18-year-old screened once for depression, and do not treat the school's psychological assessment as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 to a few dozen yuan for a questionnaire at a community clinic or hospital outpatient service; the 12355 and 12356 hotlines are free
- In plain terms: the US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depression; this tier of recommendation means "moderate net benefit, worth doing." For age 11 and under the evidence is insufficient, and so is the evidence for specifically screening for "suicide risk." The same document notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19. Schools in China run a psychological assessment once each school year, but that is a census, not a diagnosis — if the child says they feel unwell, or you see their eating, sleep, interests, or grades change markedly in a short time, go straight to psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: the US Preventive Services Task Force 2022 recommendation statement: screen adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (B recommendation, moderate certainty, moderate net benefit); evidence is insufficient for screening children aged 11 and under for major depression (I statement); the benefits and harms of screening children and adolescents for suicide risk are uncertain (I statement); the document notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19, and that major depression in children and adolescents is closely associated with recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and increased risk of suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and suicide death. The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools require building a three-tier service system of head teachers, full-time and part-time mental health teachers, and psychiatrists, with each student receiving psychological assessment generally no more than 1 time per school year; they push forward mechanisms for referral to psychiatric care and return to school after recovery, and call for making use of the 12355 youth service desk and the 12356 psychological assistance hotline (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: grade B because this is a recommendation grade, not a convertible effect size, and it is a US recommendation; China has no corresponding official screening guidance. A positive screen only means further evaluation is needed, not a diagnosis. For what to do when there are suicidal thoughts, see Section 1, item 25; for how to respond at home, see Section 29.

### 7. Do not buy products or services claiming to "cure myopia" or "reduce the prescription"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0; on the contrary, it saves this money
- In plain terms: the market regulator's own words are "with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured," and it directly lists "rehabilitation," "recovery," "reducing the prescription," "myopia cure," "nemesis of myopia," and "prescription repair" as misleading marketing claims to be investigated and punished strictly; those trading under the name of traditional Chinese medicine, using patients' images for before-and-after efficacy comparisons, and giving guaranteed promises of efficacy are all within scope. So when you see such words you can rule the product out immediately, without comparing prices. Eye-massage devices and the like are medical devices, and e-commerce platforms are required to vet the sellers' qualifications.
- Benefit: the notice on the special rectification campaign run by the General Administration of Market Regulation's General Office from November 2021 to March 2022: it states plainly that "with current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured," and requires strict punishment under the law of marketing that uses misleading phrases such as "rehabilitation," "recovery," "reducing the prescription," "myopia cure," "nemesis of myopia," or "prescription repair" to sell myopia prevention and control products for children and adolescents; it strengthens the crackdown on marketing that trades under the name of traditional Chinese medicine or falsely borrows TCM theory and techniques to deceive consumers; it cracks down hard on false and illegal advertising that uses a patient's name or image for before-and-after treatment comparisons or makes guaranteed promises about the safety or efficacy of treatment; and it requires e-commerce platforms to strictly fulfill their duty to review the business qualifications of sellers of eye-care devices and other medical devices and special commodities (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 市场监管总局办公厅 (2021). 关于开展儿童青少年近视防控产品违法违规商业营销宣传专项整治行动的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Notes: grade B because this is an enforcement position rather than a controlled study of product efficacy, but "myopia cannot be cured" is the consistent position of health and regulatory authorities — the Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents say "myopia can be prevented and controlled, but not reversed." The two things with real evidence are in item 4 (outdoors) and item 10 (proper refraction, glasses fitting, and follow-up). Blue-light glasses are covered in Section 6.

### 8. Sleep, homework, PE, and rankings all have explicit written rules — raise them when the school falls short
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: 0; know the provisions and, when necessary, raise them with the school or the local education administrative department
- In plain terms: these are all in black and white: primary school students sleep 10 hours a day, junior high 9, senior high 8; morning classes generally start no earlier than 8:20 for primary schools and 8:00 for secondary schools, and schools may not require students to arrive early for unified teaching activities; bedtime is generally no later than 21:20, 22:00, and 23:00 respectively; offline tutoring must end no later than 20:30 and live online tutoring no later than 21:00; from 22:00 to 8:00 the next day, no game services may be provided to minors. In 2025 these were joined by: at least 2 hours of combined physical activity per day, encouragement of a 15-minute recess, and no ranking of students by exam scores. If your child is chronically short of sleep, check against these provisions first rather than blaming the child for dawdling.
- Benefit: the Notice on Further Strengthening Sleep Management for Primary and Secondary School Students: primary school students should sleep 10 hours a day, junior high students 9 hours, senior high students 8 hours; morning classes generally start no earlier than 8:20 in primary schools and 8:00 in secondary schools, and schools may not require students to arrive early for unified educational and teaching activities; primary students' bedtime is generally no later than 21:20, junior high no later than 22:00, senior high no later than 23:00; off-campus tutoring must end no later than 20:30, live online tutoring no later than 21:00, and from 22:00 to 8:00 the next day no game services may be provided to minors (nationwide, issued March 2021). The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools: fully implement at least 2 hours of combined physical activity per day for primary and secondary students, encourage adopting a "15-minute recess," do not rank students by exam scores, strictly prohibit assigning mechanical, repetitive, or punitive homework, and encourage setting aside one "no-homework day" per week (nationwide, issued October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部办公厅 (2021). 关于进一步加强中小学生睡眠管理工作的通知（教基厅函〔2021〕11 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/02/content_5597443.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: both are departmental normative documents, and implementation varies widely across localities and schools — hence grade B for this item. Their use is to let you know which requirements the school is already supposed to meet, so you have grounds when you raise them. For the adult sleep accounting, see Sections 2 and 3.

### 9. If the child is at breaking point, they can suspend schooling — the school must keep their enrollment, for up to 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: 0; the application is filed with the school by the parents or other legal guardians
- In plain terms: suspending schooling is a formal institution: the parents or other legal guardians apply, the school confirms and reports to the higher-level student-status management department for approval; a single application covers no more than 1 year, and if continued suspension is genuinely needed when it expires, a new application is filed; during the suspension the school must keep the student's enrollment status, and when the student returns it must process the paperwork promptly. The 2025 Ten Measures also specifically call for building "mechanisms for referral to psychiatric care and return to school after recovery." So "suspending schooling means ruin" does not hold institutionally; the concrete procedures are set by each province — ask the school's academic affairs office or the local education bureau.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Administrative Measures for Student Status of Primary and Secondary School Students: a student's suspension from schooling is applied for by the parents or other legal guardians, confirmed by the school, and reported to the higher-level student-status management department for approval; when the student returns, the school must promptly handle the relevant procedures; the period of a suspension application does not exceed 1 year, and if continued suspension is genuinely needed after the period expires, a new application must be filed; during the suspension the school must preserve the student's enrollment status, and the specific methods for suspension and resumption are formulated by provincial education administrative departments (nationwide, issued January 2025). The Ten Measures for Further Strengthening Mental Health Work in Primary and Secondary Schools require advancing mechanisms for referral to psychiatric care and return to school after recovery, and guiding schools to draw up contingency plans for sudden psychological crisis incidents on campus
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部 (2025). 中小学生学籍管理办法（教基〔2025〕1 号，第十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002620.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: grade B because the specific conditions, documents, and resumption arrangements for suspension are set by provincial education administrative departments, with only the framework defined nationally. Before doing it, clarify three things: what medical documentation is needed, how enrollment status is marked during the suspension, and whether returning means rejoining the original grade or repeating a year. The Regulations on School Protection of Minors additionally require compulsory-education schools to maintain a reporting and filing system for students who drop out, suspend schooling, or take long leaves, so this is a path schools have walked before, not an exception.

### 10. If a check finds poor vision, get a cycloplegic refraction at a hospital, then recheck at the intervals the doctor gives
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few dozen to a few hundred yuan for the examination and glasses; after dilation the eyes are sensitive to light and near vision is blurry, needing half a day to a day or two to recover
- In plain terms: cycloplegic refraction is the gold standard for diagnosing myopia; the health commission states plainly that dilating drugs cause no harm to healthy eyes, with short-term light sensitivity and blurry near vision after use, returning to normal once the drug is stopped. If myopia is confirmed, get glasses promptly to correct it; for those whose corrected vision is normal with glasses, preschool and primary school children recheck every 3 to 6 months, junior and senior high students every 6 to 12 months. Do not treat a single computer refraction at an optical shop as a diagnosis, and do not refuse glasses because you "fear the prescription will climb."
- Benefit: the Ten Core Messages for Myopia Prevention and Control in Children and Adolescents: cycloplegic refraction is a medical refraction performed after a cycloplegic agent relaxes the eye's accommodation, and it is the "gold standard" for diagnosing myopia; dilating drugs cause no harm to healthy eyes, and after use there may be short-term light sensitivity and blurry near vision, with normal function returning once stopped; once myopia is diagnosed, intervene promptly and scientifically through glasses and other correction — properly fitted glasses effectively correct blurred vision and slow myopia progression; for those with normal corrected vision, preschool children and primary school students recheck every 3 to 6 months and junior and senior high students every 6 to 12 months, with replacement of glasses decided by the specific situation; children should receive periodic refractive screening at ages 1 to 3, 4 to 6, and after age 7 to monitor hyperopia reserve (nationwide, issued July 2023)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: grade B because this is the practice laid out in an official public-education document, not a primary study with effect sizes. High myopia brings complications such as retinal detachment, so rechecks matter for more than just replacing glasses. "Poor vision" found at the school check is only a screening result; take it to a hospital for a full ophthalmic examination — see item 5.

### 11. Get pit-and-fissure sealants on the permanent molars once they erupt
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few dozen to one or two hundred yuan per tooth, no anesthesia and no drilling, a few minutes per session; many localities have free programs for children of the target age
- In plain terms: sealing the grooves on the chewing surface of permanent molars with resin makes tooth decay over 24 months clearly less likely than leaving them unsealed: converted from the control groups' caries levels in the trials, if the control group runs 16% the sealed group is about 5.2%, at 40% it is about 6.25%, and at 70% it is about 19%; the authors' summary is 11 to 51 percentage points less caries than no sealing at 24 months, still effective at 48 months. Of four trials reporting adverse events, none reported any adverse events.
- Benefit: a Cochrane systematic review of 38 trials covering 7,924 children aged 5 to 16: resin sealants versus no sealant, at 24-month follow-up of first permanent molars in children aged 5 to 10, OR 0.12 for caries (95% CI 0.08–0.19, 7 trials, 1,548 randomized, 1,322 assessed, moderate-quality evidence); if 16% of tooth surfaces in the control group develop caries within 24 months, the sealed figure is 5.2% (3.13%–7.37%), at a control rate of 40% it is 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%), and at 70% it is 19% (12.3%–27.2%); at 48–54 months of follow-up the OR is 0.21 (0.16–0.28); the authors conclude that resin sealants reduce caries by 11% to 51% relative to no sealant at 24 months; no adverse events occurred in the 4 trials that reported them; evidence on the relative effectiveness of glass ionomer sealants and between different sealant materials is insufficient
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Notes: the first permanent molars generally erupt around age 6 and the second around age 12; seal them while they have just come in and are still sound — once decayed, they can only be filled. Many regions list pit-and-fissure sealing for children of the target age as a free service; ask your local CDC or community health service center. This section did not obtain a nationwide document that can be verified verbatim, so it does not state coverage or age ranges. Dental caries is also one of the key guidance items in student health checks — see item 5.
