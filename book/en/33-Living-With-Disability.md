# 33. Living after disability

This section is about life after a disability has already happened. How to prevent disability is in Section 1 and Section 13; it is not repeated here.

The four score types are kept separate and are not converted into one another. Items on emergencies and complications are scored on mortality. Items on subsidies, benefits, and lawsuits are scored on money. Items on schooling and paperwork are scored on time. Items on psychiatric hospitalization, capacity, and guardianship are scored on personal liberty.

The WHO fact sheet on disability (残疾), published in March 2023, gives three figures. About 1.3 billion people worldwide have significant functional difficulty — 16% of the world population, roughly one in six. Some people with disabilities die up to 20 years earlier than people without disabilities. Their risk of depression, asthma, diabetes, stroke, obesity, or poor oral health is twice as high. The fact sheet states that these health gaps come from unfair conditions people with disabilities face: discrimination, poverty, barriers to school and work, and barriers inside the health system itself. So half the items in this section are about the body, and half are about procedures and rights.

Five topics already covered elsewhere are only pointed to here, not repeated. How to get a disability certificate, the seven categories, and levels 1 to 4 are in Section 24, item 10 (disability certificate). When to do a disability assessment is in Section 24, item 9 (disability assessment). How to claim the two disability subsidies is in Section 7, item 8 (the two subsidies). Work-capacity assessment after a work injury and how grades convert into money are in Section 19, item 14 (work-capacity assessment). How to apply for long-term care insurance for severe disability is in Section 17, item 7 (long-term care insurance). That item sits in the “older people at home” section, but the benefit has no age floor — younger people can claim it too.

Most items in this section benefit you and your family, the top two beneficiary tiers. Places that involve other people are noted separately in Notes.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below (numbers in parentheses are item numbers).

**Body and rehabilitation**: sudden severe headache after spinal cord injury (item 1); switch the wheelchair cushion to a pressure-relieving type (item 5); do not buy therapies that claim to cure paralysis (item 6); do rehabilitation at a proper facility (item 16); get a hearing aid when hearing declines (item 17); after spinal cord injury, do not leave an indwelling catheter in long term without a plan (item 21).

**Benefits and subsidies**: go to the Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) and ask about every benefit at once (item 7); apply for rehabilitation aid for a disabled child (item 8); apply for subsidies for ramps, handrails, and bathroom modifications (item 9); personal income tax reduction (item 11).

**Work and rights**: say at job search that you hold a disability certificate (item 10); if you are refused hire because of disability, you can sue (item 20).

**School and travel**: take a guide dog into public places (item 12); apply for reasonable accommodations on the gaokao (高考) (item 13); schools must not refuse a disabled child (item 14); you can still take a driving test with both lower limbs missing (item 15).

**Mental health and caregivers**: suicide risk in the first decade after disability (item 2); caregivers must look after their own health (item 4).

**Mental disorders and guardianship**: psychiatric hospitalization is voluntary in principle (item 3); capacity must be determined by a court application (item 18); guardians follow the statutory order (item 19).
### 1. After spinal cord injury, sudden severe headache and sweating: first help the person sit upright, loosen clothing, and call 120
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Three actions, done in a few dozen seconds.
- In plain terms: In someone with a spinal cord injury, part of the body’s alarm wiring is cut. A full bladder or tight clothes may not be felt, yet blood pressure can shoot up. The signal is sudden severe headache and sweating. Help the person sit upright, loosen anything tight, check whether urine is being held, and call 120 at the same time.
- Benefit: This episode is called autonomic dysreflexia. A systematic review searched Medline, CINAHL, EMBASE, and PsycINFO, included 31 studies, and of those 6 were randomized controlled trials. The review states that non-drug management during an episode is three steps: put the patient in an upright position, loosen tight clothing, and remove the triggering stimulus. Those three steps rest on clinical consensus and physiology data and are Level 5 evidence. The review body text states that episodes are most common with injury at thoracic level 6 or above, and the most common triggers are bladder or bowel irritation. Stimuli below the injury level set off a large sympathetic discharge, blood pressure rises sharply, and sensation below the injury is partly or wholly gone. Symptoms include severe headache and heavy sweating above the injury level; there may also be no symptoms at all. Untreated, it can lead to intracranial hemorrhage, seizures, or death. No study specifically measured the effect of sitting up during an episode, but when people with spinal cord injury go from lying flat to sitting or tilting, resting blood pressure falls clearly. Antihypertensive drugs are used only when blood pressure stays high; among them prazosin has Level 1 evidence, and nifedipine and prostaglandin E2 have Level 2 evidence. The review also states that prevention mainly targets urogenital and surgical triggers, and most of that evidence is only Level 4 or Level 5
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Krassioukov A, Warburton DE, Teasell R, Eng JJ; Spinal Cord Injury Rehabilitation Evidence Research Team (2009). A systematic review of the management of autonomic dysreflexia after spinal cord injury. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 90(4):682-695. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2008.10.017>
- Notes: Grade B because those three on-the-spot actions have no randomized controlled trial behind them, only consensus and physiological reasoning. The review itself says most of these methods are backed only by uncontrolled studies. The drug paragraph is for clinicians; do not give blood-pressure medicine at home. Laying the person flat raises blood pressure further, so sit them upright. Family and aides need these three steps too. Do not tough it out as an ordinary headache.

### 2. The first decade after becoming disabled is a high-risk period for suicide; this shouldn’t be dismissed as “just a matter of staying positive.”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No cost at all. It only requires a phone call or a single appointment booking.
- In plain terms: Long-term follow-up at two UK spinal cord injury centres found this group’s suicide rate is five times that of the general population. Over nine-tenths of the suicides happened in the first decade after injury. For people whose injury itself came from a suicide attempt, the risk is higher still.
- Benefit: This study tracked 2,304 patients with traumatic spinal cord injuries who were newly admitted between 1991 and 2010, survived the first year after injury, and had residual neurological impairments at discharge, with follow-up lasting until the end of 2014. Among them, 63 patients (2.7%) had suffered their injury as a direct result of a prior suicide attempt. By the end of the follow-up period, 533 patients had died, of which 4.2% died by suicide; 91% of all suicides occurred within the first ten years after injury. The age-standardized suicide mortality rate was 62.5 cases per 100,000 people per year (95% CI 36.4–88.6), which is five times the average suicide rate of 12.2 cases per 100,000 people in England and Wales in 2014. Compared to patients with other causes of injury, those whose injury was triggered by a prior suicide attempt had a 4.32-fold higher risk of death overall (P<0.001), and a 9.46-fold higher risk of suicide specifically (P<0.001).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Savic G, DeVivo MJ, Frankel HL, Jamous MA, Soni BM, Charlifue S (2018). Suicide and traumatic spinal cord injury - a cohort study. Spinal Cord, 56(1):2-6. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sc.2017.98>
- Notes: This research was limited to spinal cord injury patients only, so the findings should be extrapolated with caution to other causes of disability such as blindness, amputation, or burns. Its main takeaway is the time window of heightened risk: this elevated risk does not fade away immediately after discharge, and requires close monitoring for a full decade. For support services, please refer to Section 29, Item 11 (12356). To prevent accidental harm, avoid storing sleeping pills or pesticides at home; see Section 1, Item 25 (thoughts of suicide). If feelings of grief and emotional distress persist for more than six months, seek help from a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist; see Section 29, Item 8 (grief lasting over half a year).

### 3. Psychiatric hospitalization is voluntary in principle; only two situations allow it without the person’s consent
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Memorize the two situations so you can say them when needed.
- In plain terms: A family member or workplace cannot keep someone in a psychiatric hospital with one sentence. The law recognizes only two situations, and only after a diagnosis of serious mental disorder: the person has harmed themselves or is at that risk, or has endangered others or is at that risk. The first still needs guardian consent for admission. For the second, if there is objection, a second diagnosis can be demanded within three days, and afterward an independent appraisal can be commissioned.
- Benefit: Article 30 of the Mental Health Law (精神卫生法) states that “hospitalization for mental disorders follows the voluntary principle.” Inpatient treatment shall be given only when diagnosis and assessment show a serious mental disorder and one of the following applies. The first is “harm to self has already occurred, or there is a risk of harm to self.” The second is “harm to others’ safety has already occurred, or there is a risk of harm to others’ safety.” Article 31: for the first situation, the facility carries out inpatient treatment only with guardian consent; without it, it must not. Article 32: for the second situation, if the person or guardian objects, a second diagnosis may be demanded within three days of receiving the diagnostic conclusion, by two psychiatrists other than those who made the first diagnosis; if there is still objection to the second diagnosis, the person may independently commission a licensed appraisal body for a medical appraisal of mental disorder. Article 35: if the second diagnosis or appraisal report cannot confirm a serious mental disorder, or shows the patient does not need inpatient treatment, the facility must not hospitalize them. Article 78: intentionally sending a person who does not have a mental disorder into a medical facility as a mental-disorder patient incurs liability for damages (nationwide, in force from 1 May 2013, amended 2018)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:中华人民共和国精神卫生法（2018 年修正，第三十条、第三十一条、第三十二条、第三十五条、第七十八条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7448a066d>
- Notes: The reverse is also true: it is not that no one can ever be taken for care. Article 28 says that if a person suspected of a mental disorder has already harmed themselves or others’ safety, or is at that risk, close relatives, the workplace, or the local public security organ shall take immediate measures to stop it and send the person to a medical facility for mental-disorder diagnosis. A facility that receives such a referral must not refuse to make a diagnosis. So “sent for diagnosis” and “kept as an inpatient” are two doors; the first is lower, the second higher. Article 75 lists facility duties; improper restraint or isolation, or diagnosing a person without a mental disorder as having one, can bring discipline up to revocation of the practice certificate. What to do when someone nearby has an urge to hurt others is in Section 8.

### 4. Keep an eye on the health of whoever provides long-term care at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Delegate part of the caregiving duties and go get a medical checkup yourself.
- In plain terms: Caregiver burnout is not a sign of weakness. A US study followed more than eight hundred older adults over four years. Caregivers looking after a disabled spouse who reported mental strain had a 63% higher risk of dying compared to non-caregivers. Those who provided care without feeling strained showed no such increase. What needs watching is not caregiving itself, but the chronic strain.
- Benefit: A prospective population-based cohort study conducted across four US communities from 1993 to 1998, with an average follow-up of 4.5 years. It included 392 caregivers and 427 non-caregivers aged 66 to 96, all living with their spouses. Within 4 years, 103 participants (12.6%) died. After adjusting for sociodemographic factors, prevalent disease, and subclinical cardiovascular disease, caregivers who reported mental or emotional strain had a 63% higher mortality risk than non-caregiving controls (relative risk RR 1.63, 95% CI 1.00–2.65). Neither caregivers who reported no strain (RR 1.08, 95% CI 0.61–1.90) nor individuals whose spouse was disabled but who did not provide care (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.73–2.58) showed elevated adjusted mortality.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Schulz R, Beach SR (1999). Caregiving as a risk factor for mortality: the Caregiver Health Effects Study. JAMA, 282(23):2215-2219. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.282.23.2215>
- Notes: Caveat: the lower bound of the confidence interval in this study sits right on 1.00, so the possibility of "no difference" is not entirely ruled out. The study population consisted of older American adults living with a spouse, caring for an impaired elderly partner; applying this to caregivers of younger family members with disabilities is an extrapolation. What it demonstrates is the direction of effect: those under heavy strain face higher risk. Offload caregiving tasks whenever possible, and consult your local disabled persons' federation and civil affairs bureau for respite care and adult day care services. Caregivers must not stop taking medications for their own chronic conditions—see Section 16, Item 1 (take medications fully as prescribed). The beneficiary is you. 〔800〕

### 5. Switch to a pressure-relieving wheelchair cushion and inspect the sitting bones and tailbone daily
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A pressure-relieving cushion costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. Checking daily takes one minute.
- In plain terms: In nursing homes, older residents who used pressure-relieving wheelchair cushions developed significantly fewer pressure ulcers over their sitting bones than those using standard segmented foam cushions, though there was no noticeable difference once tailbone sores were included. Everyone in the trial was first fitted with a properly sized wheelchair, so the chair itself must be fitted to the person's measurements rather than buying a generic off-the-shelf model. The UK National Health Service (NHS) also recommends checking the skin daily for signs of pressure ulcers.
- Benefit: This randomized clinical trial was conducted across 12 nursing homes, enrolling 232 residents aged 65 and older between 2004 and 2008 who used a wheelchair for at least 6 hours daily and had a Braden scale score of 18 or lower. All participants were first fitted with an appropriately sized wheelchair and then randomized into two groups. One group used skin-protection cushions (air, viscous fluid with foam, or gel with foam), while the other group used a 7.6 cm thick segmented foam cushion. Follow-up lasted 6 months or until a pressure ulcer developed. For ischial tuberosity pressure ulcers, there were 8 cases (6.7%) in the segmented foam group versus 1 case (0.9%) in the skin-protection cushion group (P = 0.04). When ischial and sacrococcygeal pressure ulcers were combined, there were 21 cases (17.6%) in the segmented foam group versus 12 cases (10.6%) in the skin-protection cushion group (P = 0.14), showing no statistically significant difference. The recommendation to inspect the skin daily is based on pressure ulcer prevention guidance from the UK National Health Service (NHS) for home carers, which highlights two key points: using specially designed mattresses and cushions, and checking the skin daily for signs of pressure ulcers.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Brienza D, Kelsey S, Karg P, et al. (2010). A randomized clinical trial on preventing pressure ulcers with wheelchair seat cushions. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 58(12):2308-2314. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2010.03168.x>；NHS. Pressure ulcers (pressure sores)：「use specially designed mattresses and cushions」「check your skin every day for symptoms of a pressure ulcer」. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/pressure-sores/>（2026 年 11 月 4 日查阅）
- Notes: Grade B was assigned for two reasons. First, the sample size was only 232 participants; the P-value of 0.04 for the primary endpoint barely crossed the significance threshold, and the secondary endpoint showed no statistically significant difference. Second, the study population consisted of elderly nursing home residents, so generalizing the findings to younger wheelchair users is an extrapolation. In the trial, all participants were first fitted with a properly sized wheelchair, meaning proper fit and the cushion work together—do not just swap the cushion alone. For pressure ulcer prevention in bedridden individuals, including alternating-pressure air mattresses and scheduled repositioning, see Chapter 17, Item 8 (Pressure Ulcers). For how to apply for subsidies for basic assistive devices, see Item 7 of this chapter (Check thoroughly with the local Disabled Persons' Federation).

### 6. Do not buy treatments or devices claiming to cure paralysis, blindness, or deafness
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free; in fact, it saves money. The hardest part is convincing family members who cling to hope and refuse to listen.
- In plain terms: Advertising law draws a hard line. Ads for medical care, pharmaceuticals, and medical devices are strictly forbidden from guaranteeing results, citing cure rates or success rates, or using spokespeople and testimonials. Claims like "walk again after three courses of treatment" or "look how many patients have recovered" are illegal ads on their face—there is no need to waste time analyzing their claimed scientific principles. Furthermore, any advertisement outside licensed healthcare, drugs, and medical devices is completely banned from mentioning disease treatment at all.
- Benefit: Article 16 of the Advertising Law stipulates that advertisements for medical care, pharmaceuticals, and medical devices shall not contain: "(1) Assertions or guarantees of efficacy or safety; (2) Stated cure rates or effective rates; (3) Comparisons with the efficacy or safety of other pharmaceuticals or medical devices, or with other medical institutions; (4) Endorsements or testimonials by advertising spokespersons." Article 17 stipulates that with the exception of advertisements for medical care, pharmaceuticals, and medical devices, "no other advertisements may involve disease treatment functions, nor use medical terminology or wording likely to confuse the promoted goods with pharmaceuticals or medical devices." Article 4 provides that advertisements shall not contain false or misleading content and shall not deceive or mislead consumers; advertisers are responsible for the veracity of advertising content (National, 2021 Amendment).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:中华人民共和国广告法（2021 年修正，第四条、第十六条、第十七条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817ab231eb017abd6bd860052d>
- Notes: There is only one rule of thumb: see if they make absolute promises. A rehabilitation department in a legitimate hospital will never promise you can stand up in a set number of treatment courses. If you encounter such marketing, report it to 12315 or the local market supervision authority. Dietary supplement sales pitches follow the exact same playbook; see Section 6, Item 10 (dietary supplements). If you have already bought them and want a refund, follow the procedures for online shopping and prepaid consumer disputes; see Section 5, Item 29 (follow e-commerce platform rules for online purchases). The beneficiaries are yourself and your family.

### 7. Once the disability certificate is issued, visit the county-level disabled persons' federation and ask about every available benefit in one go
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to the county-level disabled persons' federation for a morning of inquiries. Later, you will need to visit more than one government department.
- In plain terms: The disability certificate itself does not pay out money, but multiple benefits require it to apply. These benefits are handled separately, and each one requires its own application—none arrive automatically. On your first trip to the disabled persons' federation, go through the checklist item by item; asking all at once saves multiple back-and-forth trips later.
- Benefit: At least six benefits link to the certificate, each with its own legal basis. The living allowance for disabled people in financial hardship and the nursing care subsidy for severely disabled people are covered in Section 7, Item 8 ("Two Subsidies"). Rehabilitation assistance for children with disabilities is in Item 8 of this section. Subsidies for configuring basic assistive devices are based on Article 26 of the *Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation of Persons with Disabilities*. Subsidies for home accessibility renovations are in Item 9 of this section (ramps, handrails, and bathroom modifications). Proportional employment and employment services are in Item 10 of this section (proactively disclosing certificate status). Individual income tax reductions are in Item 11 of this section. Article 26 of the *Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation of Persons with Disabilities* also specifies that the state provides basic rehabilitation services to poor and severely disabled rural and urban residents by carrying out priority rehabilitation projects, and "provides subsidies for the provision of basic assistive devices in accordance with relevant state regulations." Article 20 provides that relevant departments of local people's governments at or above the county level and disabled persons' federations shall use community resources to establish rehabilitation facilities or purchase services to conduct community-based rehabilitation, including rehabilitation guidance, daily living skills training, rehabilitation care, assistive device fitting, information consulting, and referrals (nationwide, effective July 1, 2017). Article 21 of the same regulations stipulates that rehabilitation services require a prior needs assessment, followed by an individualized plan based on the assessment results. A 2016 State Council document states that "areas with the capacity to do so may provide subsidies for the configuration of basic rehabilitation assistive devices for poor and severely disabled residents in urban and rural areas." A 2026 three-year action plan issued by departments including the civil affairs bureau specifies the need to "standardize and strengthen the subsidy system for the adaptation and service of basic assistive devices for persons with disabilities." Disability resulting from work-related injuries follows a separate channel. The *Measures for the Administration of Assistive Device Configuration under Work-Related Injury Insurance* governs items such as prosthetics, orthotics, artificial eyes, dentures, and wheelchairs. It stipulates that configuration must be confirmed by the labor capacity appraisal committee beforehand. Expenses for installation, maintenance, and training are paid by the work-related injury insurance fund in accordance with regulations. If an employee obtains an assistive device on their own without prior confirmation or at a non-contracted facility, the handling agency will not cover the cost.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十条、第二十一条、第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>；国务院 (2016). 关于加快发展康复辅助器具产业的若干意见（国发〔2016〕60 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2016-10/27/content_5125001.htm>；民政部等 (2026). 康复辅助器具产业扩能提质三年行动方案（2026-2028年）（民函〔2026〕48 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7075249.htm>；人力资源社会保障部、民政部、国家卫生计生委令第 27 号 (2016). 工伤保险辅助器具配置管理办法（第二条、第十五条、第二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5074060.htm>
- Notes: The procedure for applying for the certificate itself is covered in Section 24, Item 10 (disability certificate). For items such as wheelchairs, prosthetics, and hearing aids, always clarify the assessment and fitting procedures required for subsidies before purchasing them. The work-injury system explicitly requires confirmation prior to fitting; items purchased independently beforehand are not reimbursed. Work-injury identification and labor capacity appraisal are covered in Section 19, Item 14 (labor capacity appraisal). Regarding subsidies for assistive devices, the State Council document specifies that "areas with the capacity to do so may" provide them, meaning whether they exist and how much is paid depends on local policy. The following points are based on author experience: write down the exact official name of every item while inquiring, and check the original policy documents by name on the local disabled persons' federation and government websites once home—do not rely solely on verbal statements from counter staff. Inquiring once is also not a permanent fix; check back every year or two, as policies change.

### 8. If a child is under 7 and has a disability or autism, apply to the county Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) for rehabilitation aid
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 人群=婴幼儿,孩子 -->
- Cost: No money. File one application at the county Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联), then pick one facility from the designated list.
- In plain terms: The state has a rehabilitation-aid system for children with disabilities and autism from ages 0 to 6, covering surgery, assistive devices, and rehab training. Recipients start with families in need; where local finances allow, age and household-income limits can be widened. Aid under the rules runs to age 6 — do not wait until school starts. Pick the facility from the Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) designated list.
- Benefit: The State Council Opinions on Establishing a Rehabilitation Aid System for Children with Disabilities state that eligible recipients are children aged 0–6 with vision, hearing, speech, physical, or intellectual disability, and children with autism, who meet the conditions. That includes children with disabilities in urban and rural dibao households, registered poverty households, children raised in child-welfare institutions, orphans with disabilities, children with disabilities covered by extreme-poverty support, and children with disabilities from other economically difficult households. How “other economically difficult households” are defined is set by people’s governments at county level and above. The document also states that “where conditions allow, localities may widen the age range for rehabilitation aid for children with disabilities, and may relax limits on household economic conditions.” Funding standards are set by people’s governments at county level and above by local finances, numbers of recipients, and disability categories, with a dynamic adjustment mechanism. Applications go to the county Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) where the child’s hukou is (or where the residence permit was issued). After approval, the guardian chooses a designated rehab facility. Designated facilities are selected by the Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) at county level and above together with health, education, and civil-affairs departments “on open, competitive principles.” Costs at designated facilities are settled directly between finance and the facility. “Costs of rehab services at a non-designated facility that the county Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) has approved go by settlement rules set by that federation with same-level finance.” Provincial governments were to issue local systems and supporting policies by the end of September 2018. The higher basis is Article 26 of the Regulations on Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation of Persons with Disabilities (残疾预防和残疾人康复条例): the state establishes a rehabilitation-aid system for children with disabilities and shall “gradually make surgery, assistive-device configuration, and rehab training free for children aged 0–6 with vision, hearing, speech, physical, or intellectual disability and for children with autism” (nationwide, from 2018)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2018). 关于建立残疾儿童康复救助制度的意见（国发〔2018〕20 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-07/10/content_5305296.htm>；国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Notes: Aid projects and standards are set by local governments at county level and above; first check your province’s measures for rehabilitation aid for children with disabilities on the local Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) website. Designated facilities are chosen by the federation with relevant departments; ask the federation for the list. The document requires the federation and departments to strengthen entry and exit oversight of designated facilities, run periodic checks and comprehensive assessments, and keep a blacklist. To use a facility off the list, get county Disabled Persons’ Federation (残联) approval first; how money is settled is set locally. Going without approval has no settlement rule in the document. The beneficiary is your child, second tier. Facilities that promise a “full cure” are handled under item 6 of this section (claims to cure paralysis).

### 9. Apply to county-level governments or above for subsidies on home ramps, handrails, and bathroom modifications—check with your local disabled persons' federation first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Applying is free. Most modifications are subsidized, though you may still need to cover a portion out of pocket. Requires trips to the disabled persons' federation and the subdistrict office.
- In plain terms: The Barrier-Free Environment Creation Law states that county-level governments and above must support and guide home accessibility modifications, providing appropriate subsidies to eligible households with disabled persons or seniors. Subsidy amounts and eligible items are determined locally, so start by asking your local disabled persons' federation.
- Benefit: Article 19 of the Barrier-Free Environment Creation Law states: "People's governments at or above the county level shall support and guide the renovation of barrier-free home facilities. Appropriate subsidies shall be granted to eligible households with persons with disabilities or older persons." Article 18 provides that for existing residential buildings, residential communities, public buildings, public places, transportation facilities, and urban and rural roads that do not meet barrier-free construction standards, people's governments at or above the county level shall formulate targeted barrier-free renovation plans and organize their implementation based on actual conditions. Article 22 states: "The State supports the installation of elevators or other barrier-free facilities in existing multi-story residential buildings in old urban communities to provide convenience for persons with disabilities and older persons." The same article provides that people's governments at or above the county level and their relevant departments shall "give play to the role of grassroots community organizations" to promote elevator installations in existing multi-story residential buildings. Article 62 provides that any organization or individual has the right to submit opinions and suggestions on strengthening and improving the barrier-free environment to relevant competent government departments, and to file complaints or reports regarding violations of this Law; competent departments receiving complaints or reports "shall handle them promptly and provide a response." Article 63 provides that where violations of this Law harm public interest, the People's Procuratorate may issue procuratorial recommendations or initiate public interest litigation (national, effective September 1, 2023).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法（第十八条、第十九条、第二十二条、第六十二条、第六十三条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>
- Notes: Graded B because the law only stipulates that "appropriate subsidies shall be granted"—exact subsidy amounts and eligibility criteria are left entirely to local authorities, with no unified national figures. Start by checking the local disabled persons' federation website for "barrier-free home modifications for households with severe disabilities facing hardship." ### 16. Do rehabilitation at a proper facility with a rehabilitation medicine department; intensity follows the therapist — more is not always better (evidence from stroke)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Inpatient rehab several hundred yuan a day; the out-of-pocket part varies by place. Time is counted in months. Practice every day; it is dull.
- In plain terms: After a stroke, people admitted to an organized stroke unit are more often still alive and still living at home one year later. But do not add intensity on your own: starting earlier, more often, and with more volume within 24 hours after stroke led to worse three-month outcomes. How much and when to practice — follow rehab medicine and the therapists.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review included 29 trials and 5902 participants, comparing organized inpatient stroke-unit care with other care. The review describes a stroke unit as a team using standard approaches to common post-stroke problems and providing coordinated multidisciplinary care. At final follow-up (median 1 year), the odds ratio for a poor outcome (death, disability, or need for institutional care) was 0.77 (95% CI 0.69–0.87, credible range), for death 0.76 (95% CI 0.66–0.88), and for death or dependence 0.75 (95% CI 0.66–0.85); evidence quality moderate. In absolute terms, per 100 people in stroke-unit care, 2 more survive, 6 more live at home, and 6 more live independently. Separately, the AVERT trial randomized 2104 patients across 56 acute stroke units in 5 countries to “usual care” or “usual care plus very early high-intensity activity.” At three months, a good outcome (modified Rankin Scale 0–2) was 480 people (46%) in the very-early group versus 525 (50%) in usual care; adjusted odds ratio 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90, P=0.004). Deaths were 88 versus 72, with no difference shown (odds ratio 1.34, 95% CI 0.93–1.93, P=0.113)
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: Contested: both bodies of evidence are from stroke; applying them to spinal cord injury, amputation, burns, and other causes of disability is an extrapolation. ### 17. When hearing declines, get a hearing aid; the reason is to hear now, not to prevent dementia
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Hearing aids cost several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan; most are out of pocket. Fitting is required, plus several return visits for tuning.
- In plain terms: The claim that “hearing aids prevent dementia” has not held up so far. A nearly thousand-person three-year trial showed no overall difference. A difference appeared only in the subgroup already at higher risk of cognitive decline. So the reason to get a hearing aid is to hear now, talk with people, and hear cars when crossing the street — not for the brain decades later.
- Benefit: The ACHIEVE trial ran in 4 U.S. communities and enrolled 977 people aged 70 to 84 with untreated hearing loss and no clear cognitive impairment. They were randomized to hearing intervention (hearing counseling and hearing aids) or health-education control, with follow-up every 6 months. The primary endpoint was 3-year change in global cognitive score. In the main analysis pooling both source populations, the two groups did not differ significantly: hearing intervention −0.200 (95% CI −0.256 to −0.144), control −0.202 (95% CI −0.258 to −0.145), difference 0.002 (95% CI −0.077 to 0.081), P=0.96. A pre-specified sensitivity analysis showed the intervention effect differed between the two source populations (interaction P=0.010). Participants from that long-term cardiovascular-health observational study were older, had more risk factors for cognitive decline, and had lower baseline cognitive scores. In that subgroup of 238 people, hearing intervention reduced 3-year cognitive decline by 48% (difference 0.191, 95% CI 0.022–0.360, P=0.027). In the other 739 newly recruited healthy volunteers, the groups did not differ significantly (difference −0.061, 95% CI −0.151 to 0.028, P=0.18). The investigators concluded that hearing intervention may reduce 3-year cognitive change in people at higher risk of cognitive decline, and not in those at lower risk. The trial reported no serious adverse events attributed to the study
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lin FR, Pike JR, Albert MS, et al.; ACHIEVE Collaborative Research Group (2023). Lancet, 402(10404):786-797. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01406-X>
- Notes: Contested: the line “hearing aids cut cognitive decline by nearly half” comes from a 238-person subgroup in this trial, not the primary result, which showed no difference between groups. ### 18. Apply to a court to declare an adult legally incapacitated—family cannot decide on their own

- In plain terms: Whether an adult lacks legal capacity or has limited legal capacity can only be determined by a court. ### 19. An adult’s guardian follows the statutory order; if the ward harms someone, the guardian pays
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. If there is a dispute, go to the residents’ committee, villagers’ committee, civil affairs, or the court.
- In plain terms: After an adult loses capacity, guardians follow this order: spouse; parents and children; other close relatives; last, other persons or organizations agreed to by the residents’ committee and the like. Disputes are designated by the residents’ committee, villagers’ committee, or civil affairs; disagree and go to court. While clear-minded, a person may name a future guardian in writing. If the ward harms someone, pay first from the ward’s own money; the guardian covers the shortfall.
- Benefit: Article 28 of the Civil Code (民法典): for an adult with no civil capacity or limited civil capacity, the following persons with guardianship capacity serve as guardian in order: “(1) spouse; (2) parents, children; (3) other close relatives; (4) other individuals or organizations willing to serve as guardian, but subject to consent of the residents’ committee, villagers’ committee, or civil-affairs department at the ward’s domicile.” Article 31: if there is a dispute over who the guardian is, the residents’ committee, villagers’ committee, or civil-affairs department at the ward’s domicile designates; anyone who disagrees with the designation may apply to a people’s court for designation, or apply directly to a people’s court. Designation “shall respect the ward’s true wishes and follow the principle most favourable to the ward.” Before designation, if rights and interests are unprotected, the residents’ committee, villagers’ committee, a relevant organization provided by law, or civil affairs serves as temporary guardian. Article 33: “An adult with full civil capacity may, in advance and in writing, agree with close relatives or other individuals or organizations willing to serve as guardian on who their guardian will be.” Article 1188: “If a person with no civil capacity or limited civil capacity causes harm to another, the guardian bears tort liability. If the guardian has fulfilled guardianship duties, tort liability may be reduced.” If the person has property, compensation is paid from that property; the shortfall is paid by the guardian. Article 1189: if the guardian entrusts guardianship duties to another, the guardian still bears tort liability; the trustee who is at fault bears corresponding liability (nationwide, in force from 1 January 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:中华人民共和国民法典（第二十八条、第三十一条、第三十三条、第一千一百八十八条、第一千一百八十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Notes: Article 33 is voluntary guardianship. Naming someone in writing while the person is clear can avoid most later disputes; how to draft it is in Section 17, item 1 (voluntary guardianship). ### 20. If you are denied a job, refused probation completion, or underpaid due to disability, you can sue—but calculate the process costs first

- Cost: Attorney fees run from several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan out of pocket. Ordinary first-instance civil proceedings are required by law to conclude within 6 months, but can be extended. You must preserve evidence yourself.
- In plain terms: The law prohibits discrimination against persons with disabilities in recruitment, probation completion, promotion, professional title evaluation, pay, and social insurance. You can file a complaint with regulatory authorities or file a lawsuit in court. However, even if you win the lawsuit, the opposing party is not required to pay your attorney fees. File a complaint first, apply for legal aid at the same time, and only then consider a court case.
- Benefit: Article 3 of the Law on the Protection of Persons with Disabilities: "Discrimination based on disability is prohibited. Insulting or infringing upon persons with disabilities is prohibited." Article 38: "No discrimination against persons with disabilities may occur in the hiring, completion of probation, promotion, professional title evaluation, labor remuneration, welfare benefits, rest and leave, social insurance, and other aspects of employees." The same article also provides that the employing unit of an employee with a disability shall provide appropriate working conditions and labor protection based on the employee's characteristics, and renovate the workplace, work equipment, and living facilities according to actual needs. Article 64: "Whoever violates this Law by discriminating against persons with disabilities in employee recruitment and other areas shall be ordered to rectify by the competent authority; disabled laborers may file a lawsuit with the people's court in accordance with the law." Article 13: Employing units shall not discriminate against disabled employees in promotions, rank advancement, professional title evaluation, remuneration, social insurance, or welfare benefits. ### 21. After spinal cord injury, if you cannot void urine, decide the voiding method with the doctor; do not leave an indwelling catheter unmanaged long term; check kidneys and bladder on a lifelong schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Self intermittent catheterization four to six times a day, a few minutes each time; catheters must be bought ongoing. Rechecks every one to two years, urology or rehab. The hard part is doing it every day for decades.
- In plain terms: After spinal cord injury, if you cannot void, the bladder must be emptied by catheterization or similar means. In UK data spanning seventy years, over one-tenth of deaths were in the genitourinary system. People with long-term indwelling catheters had about twice the complication rate of those on timed self-catheterization. Whatever the method, kidneys and bladder need lifelong scheduled checks.
- Benefit: Two UK spinal cord injury centres reviewed 5483 people with traumatic spinal cord injury. They were injured from 1943 to 2010, all survived the first year after injury, and were followed to the end of 2014. 2322 died; 2170 had reliable causes of death. Leading causes were respiratory 29.3%, circulatory 26.7%, cancer 13.9%, genitourinary 11.5%. After age adjustment, genitourinary mortality was higher than in the general population; it has fallen over the years but remains elevated. Another U.S. study reviewed records of 316 people with traumatic spinal cord injury, mean follow-up 18.3 years after injury, grouped by voiding method. Complication rates: long-term transurethral indwelling catheter 53.5%, clean intermittent catheterization 27.2%, spontaneous voiding 32.4%, suprapubic cystostomy 44.4%. Complications included infection, stones, urethral injury, and imaging abnormalities. This was a chart comparison only, not randomized. The 2026 European Association of Urology guidelines state that transurethral indwelling catheters have many complications and high urinary-tract infection risk — avoid them when possible. Suprapubic cystostomy is similar, only milder. The guidelines also state that long-term follow-up is lifelong, to check upper-tract damage and risk of kidney failure. Recheck intervals in most cases are no longer than one to two years. High-risk people roughly get ultrasound about every half year, and annual physical exam and urine tests. Section 3.4.2.e of the guidelines states that intermittent catheterization and indwelling catheters are both standard for people who cannot empty the bladder, and recommends joint decision by doctor and patient. In people with spinal cord injury, quality-of-life scores on intermittent catheterization are worse than on indwelling catheters, especially with more than 4 urinary-tract infections a year. In one observational study, 42.1% of people on intermittent catheterization stopped within 12 months, mainly for inconvenience, leakage, and more infections. The guidelines state silicone catheters are better because they scale less, and latex allergy is common in neurological disease. Section 3.5.1 lists the most common UTI signals in these patients: fever, new or worse leakage, worse spasms, fatigue, cloudy urine with stronger odour, flank or bladder pain, and autonomic dysreflexia
