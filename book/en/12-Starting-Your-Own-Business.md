# 12. Starting a business: don't bet the family savings

This section counts only money and legal liability—not life expectancy. When you open a shop or form a company, which steps can wipe out your own money, family money, and your spouse's money together? What does the law require, and can you wall them off in advance? Items written from experience only, with no literature behind them, are grade C.

Items in this section are grouped by theme below; numbers in parentheses are item numbers.

**Before opening, don't bet the nest egg**: Only put in money you can afford to lose (item 1); don't sign personal guarantees on company loans (item 2); pick the right entity before you open (item 3); don't be a nominal shareholder (item 4); check franchise filing before joining (item 5); ask the right questions before paying someone to run your online store (item 24); when raising money for a business, only approach relatives, friends, and employees (item 26); put a partnership shop in a written agreement (item 28).

**Registration and licenses**: Fix name, premises, business scope, and registered capital before registration (item 6); don't open until licenses are in hand (item 7); before leasing a shopfront, check whether that unit can host your trade (item 27).

**Food**: See which regulatory tier you fall into (item 8); prepackaged food labels (item 9); ordinary food must not claim to treat disease (item 10); criminal lines for food (item 11).

**Tax, annual reports, and invoices**: File on time even with zero income (item 12); issue invoices only for real transactions (item 13); anyone posing as the tax bureau demanding payment is a scam (item 14); file last year's annual report online in the first half of each year (item 25).

**Contracts and hiring**: When taking money, write deposit (定金), not booking-fee wording (订金) (item 15); sign employment contracts within the first month (item 16); pay wages per contract and law (item 17); follow procedures on the job (item 22).

**Products and sourcing**: Sell first, then make (item 18); run samples through a mass-production checklist (item 19); keep purchase receipts and upstream supplier details (item 20); use your own designs or buy a license (item 21).

**Exit**: If you lose money, exit by the book (item 23).
### 1. Only put in money you can afford to lose; don't touch family savings or borrow to open
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before you start, set a cap: you could lose every yuan of it and still pay the mortgage, support the kids, and see a doctor. Don't invest above that cap. Family money and borrowed money don't count toward the cap. The hard part is when the business looks like it's picking up and you want to pour in more.
- In plain terms: Debts of a sole proprietorship come out of assets in your name. If it's unclear whether the whole family ran the business, creditors can reach family property. A company doesn't fully shield you either: when the company can't pay, what you promised as subscribed capital but haven't paid yet can be called in early. Size your stake by «I could lose it all and still pay the mortgage and medical bills».
- Benefit: Losing money isn't the only risk. For a sole proprietorship, if you ran the business alone, debts are paid from property in your name (personal property). If the family ran it together or who ran it is unclear, debts are paid from family property. Debts both spouses signed count as joint marital debt; later assent by one spouse counts too. A loan in one spouse's name still counts as joint debt if the creditor shows it went into the couple's shared business. For companies there is another rule: subscribed capital is what a shareholder promised at registration. When the company can't pay a due debt, creditors may demand early payment of the portion not yet due (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第五十六、一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四十七、五十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>
- Notes: «Affordable to lose» means losing it all wouldn't force you to sell the home or borrow from relatives to plug the hole. This round found no verifiable official statistics on how many firms survive or how long they last on the National Bureau of Statistics or State Administration for Market Regulation sites, so the text gives legal consequences only, not headcount figures—grade B.

### 2. Don't sign personal guarantees on company loans; spouses should stay off the signature line
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. When a bank, supplier, or landlord wants a «shareholder personal joint-and-several guarantee» or a spouse's signature, pause. That page is you giving up «company debt stays with the company» (limited liability). Don't sign if you can avoid it. If you must, choose «general guarantee» only and write a clear cap on how much you guarantee. The hard part is «no signature, no loan»—you have to hold the line.
- In plain terms: «Joint-and-several guarantee» means if the company doesn't pay, the creditor can skip the company and come straight to you. «General guarantee» means sue or arbitrate against the company first, sell company assets, and only then collect any shortfall from you. If your spouse signs too, both households' assets are in play and the company's «limited» liability stops protecting you personally.
- Benefit: Guarantee wording means «if the debtor doesn't pay, you pay». With «joint-and-several guarantee», creditors need not pursue the company first—they can demand payment from you directly. With «general guarantee», they must sue or arbitrate against the company and have the court enforce on its assets first; only the remainder can be collected from you. Debts both spouses signed count as joint marital debt; later assent counts too. A spouse's signature puts both sides' family assets on the hook (nationwide, from 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第六百八十一、六百八十七、六百八十八、一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: The author's experience is that banks lending to small and micro enterprises often require shareholder personal joint-and-several guarantees; this book found no official statistics. Loans are still available; know what that page costs before you sign, and keep guaranteed amounts within the «affordable to lose» cap in item 1. General rules for IOUs and guarantees: section 8, item 18.

### 3. Pick the right entity before opening: sole props and general partners pay to the end; only LLCs are «limited»
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Register on the local government services portal—a few hours once. After you form a limited liability company, monthly bookkeeping and tax filing are ongoing: your time if you do it yourself, or paid bookkeeping, with prices varying by region.
- In plain terms: Sole proprietors and general partners owe without limit—pay what you owe until it's cleared. LLC shareholders owe only up to what they subscribed at registration. But whatever you subscribe must be paid in within 5 years: write 1000000, you may owe up to 1000000. Mix company books with household books or use the company to dodge debt, and «limited» can fail. With only one shareholder, you must prove the books are not mixed.
- Benefit: Sole proprietorship debts are paid from personal property or from family property. General partners in an ordinary partnership bear unlimited joint and several liability—pay in full until cleared, and a creditor may demand the entire debt from one partner alone. Limited partnerships are different: limited partners are liable only up to subscribed capital. LLC shareholders are also liable only up to subscribed capital. «Limited» fails in three situations. First, subscribed capital must be fully paid within 5 years of formation, and when the company can't pay due debts, creditors may demand early payment. Second, capital withdrawn after contribution must be returned. Third, using the company as a shell to dodge debt and seriously harming creditors makes you personally jointly liable for company debts. One-shareholder companies are stricter: that shareholder must prove company funds and personal funds are separate; if not, personal liability follows. Courts mainly look at whether money and books are separate. Shareholders casually spending company money without books, or mixing company and personal accounts, count as mixing (nationwide, from July 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第五十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；全国人大常委会 (2006). 合伙企业法（2006 年修订，第二条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2006/content_413955.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四、二十三、四十七、五十三、五十四条；第二十三条第三款：「只有一个股东的公司，股东不能证明公司财产独立于股东自己的财产的，应当对公司债务承担连带责任。」）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>；最高人民法院 (2019). 全国法院民商事审判工作会议纪要（第 10 点「人格混同」）：「最主要的表现是公司的财产与股东的财产是否混同且无法区分」「公司账簿与股东账簿不分，致使公司财产与股东财产无法区分的」. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/199691.html>
- Notes: Keep company and personal accounts separate; don't take customer payments on a personal card; don't pay household bills from the company; keep complete books. Don't inflate registered capital for show.

### 4. Don't be a nominal shareholder or hold shares for someone else
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. When someone asks to «borrow your ID for a shareholder slot» or «hold shares in your name for now», refuse.
- In plain terms: If your name is on the register, you're a shareholder in law. Pay subscribed capital on time; miss the deadline and you owe make-up plus company losses. If another founder didn't really pay in at formation, you share liability for the gap. Someone else runs the business; the debt sits on your name.
- Benefit: A registered shareholder is a legal shareholder. Pay subscribed capital in full by the deadline in the articles; otherwise pay the shortfall and compensate company losses. If a founder didn't contribute at formation, other founders at formation share joint liability for that gap. When the company can't pay due debts, creditors may demand early payment of capital not yet due. The operator isn't on the register; the obligation is still yours (nationwide, from July 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2023). 公司法（2023 年修订，第四十七、四十九、五十、五十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202312/content_6923395.htm>；最高人民法院 (2020 修正). 关于适用《中华人民共和国公司法》若干问题的规定（三）（法释〔2011〕3 号，2014 年法释〔2014〕2 号、2020 年法释〔2020〕18 号两次修正，第二十四、二十五条）：「如无法律规定的无效情形，人民法院应当认定该合同有效」「实际出资人未经公司其他股东半数以上同意，请求公司变更股东……人民法院不予支持」「名义股东处分股权造成实际出资人损失，实际出资人请求名义股东承担赔偿责任的，人民法院应予支持」. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808181799def980179ac07a9ca117c>（国家法律法规数据库）；最高人民法院 (2025). 关于适用《中华人民共和国公司法》若干问题的解释（征求意见稿）第九十条. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/477881.html>
- Notes: A nominee agreement between the nominal holder and the actual investor is valid unless a statutory invalidity applies. The actual investor can't get their name on the register without consent from over half of the other shareholders. If the nominal holder sells or pledges shares, courts apply Civil Code good-faith acquisition rules: a buyer who didn't know, paid a fair price, and registered may keep the shares; the investor can only sue the nominal holder. A draft new interpretation was released in September 2025; when finalized and in force it replaces the current text. As of September 2026, the 2020 amended version remains in force—this item follows it. Risks of being a nominal legal representative: section 8, item 28.

### 5. Before franchising, check MOFCOM filing, get written disclosure, and put a cooling-off period in the contract
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. On the Ministry of Commerce «Commercial Franchise Information Management System» site, check whether the brand is filed. Before signing, require written disclosure of everything they must tell you, together with the contract text. A few hours.
- In plain terms: By rule the franchisor needs at least 2 company-owned stores open 1 year, and must file with commerce authorities within 15 days after signing. Before signing it must give you 12 topics in writing—fees, when deposits return, how existing stores perform, and more. No filing, no disclosure, or pay-before-you-see-contract: walk away.
- Benefit: Regulations require the franchisor to have at least 2 directly operated stores running 1 year or more. Within 15 days of the first franchise contract it must file with the commerce department; no filing: fine 10000–50000 yuan; still unfiled after the deadline: 50000–100000 yuan plus public notice. Before signing it must deliver written disclosure on 12 items—including fee types and amounts, deposit refund conditions, franchisee count and how stores are doing, audited report summaries for the last 2 years, and litigation and arbitration in the last 5 years. The contract must also state that the franchisee may unilaterally terminate within a period after signing. Concealment or false disclosure lets the franchisee cancel the contract (nationwide, from May 2007).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2007). 商业特许经营管理条例（国务院令第 485 号，第七、八、十二、二十二、二十三、二十五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_4179.htm>；商务部 (2012). 商业特许经营信息披露管理办法（商务部令 2012 年第 2 号，第五、九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2012/content_2177025.htm>；商务部. 商业特许经营信息管理系统. <https://txjy.syggs.mofcom.gov.cn/>
- Notes: No filing, no disclosure file, or pay-before-reading contract: leave. «Cooling-off» is the post-signing window for unilateral exit; the regulation only says «a certain period», so get the day count written in. On disclosed average sales, gross, or net profit for existing franchisees, ask how the brand sourced the numbers—the rules require disclosure of sources. Schemes that skip «franchise» and only charge «store operations» or «opening training» fees: item 24.

### 6. Before registration, fix name, premises, scope, and registered capital; with complete papers you can get the license on the spot
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Registration itself costs nothing. Enterprise and sole-proprietorship registration fees have not been collected since 1 January 2015. Do it yourself on the local government services site or at the service hall—a few hours once. Seal carving, paid bookkeeping, and a registered address (address hosting) are market services, not government fees.
- In plain terms: Registration is free; the registration fee has not been charged since 2015—if someone charges you, it is not the registration fee. Only five material types are required: application, identity or eligibility proof, premises documents, articles or partnership agreement, plus anything else the rules name. Complete papers: license on the spot; if not on the spot, within 3 working days; complex cases get 3 more working days.
- Benefit: The regulation fixes the materials as five items. «(1) Application form». «(2) Applicant qualification documents and natural-person identity proof». «(3) Domicile or main business premises documents». «(4) Articles for companies, non-company enterprise legal persons, farmer specialized cooperatives (unions), or partnership agreement». «(5) Other materials required by laws, administrative regulations, and the State Council market supervision department». In plain language: one application; proof of who you are or that you're eligible; proof you may use the shop or office; articles or partnership agreement; plus anything else named. Companies also submit appointment documents and ID for the legal representative, directors, supervisors, and senior managers. Sole props don't file articles. The name is «declared by the applicant according to law»—you choose and report it. Registered capital uses «subscribed registration»—state how much you promise; you need not pay at the counter. Business scope is chosen from the authority's published categories. Complete and correctly formatted materials are «confirmed and registered on the spot». If not finished on the spot, «within 3 working days», with up to 3 more for complex cases. «The business license issue date is the market entity's establishment date»—the printed date is when the company or shop is born (nationwide, from March 2022).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2021). 市场主体登记管理条例（国务院令第 746 号，第十、十三、十四、十六、十九、二十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5636139.htm> ; 市场监管总局 (2022). 市场主体登记管理条例实施细则（市场监管总局令第 52 号，第十二、二十五、二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/02/content_5676403.htm> ; 财政部、国家发展改革委 (2014). 关于取消、停征和免征一批行政事业性收费的通知（财税〔2014〕101 号，附件 1）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2014-12/23/content_5023513.htm> ; 国务院办公厅 (2018). 关于进一步压缩企业开办时间的意见（国办发〔2018〕32 号，第二部分第三项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-05/17/content_5291643.htm>
- Notes: Registration itself is free. Charges are for agency help, seal carving, and similar market services. Seal prices are market-set. A 2018 General Office of the State Council document says applicants choose the seal maker themselves and forbids designating one—so you need not accept «must use the designated shop». How much registered capital you write does not dress up the storefront; it only caps your liability, and it must be paid within 5 years—see item 3. If scope includes licensed activities, don't open until licensed—see item 7.

### 7. Licensed trades: don't open until the license is issued
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Before opening, check whether your line needs a license. Catering and selling food need a food business license. Selling only others' prepackaged food needs no license—file for record instead. Open only after the license or filing is done.
- In plain terms: Opening without a license is handled by the department that governs that license under its rules. With no business license and no specific penalty rule: ordered to stop, illegal gains confiscated, plus a fine of up to 10000 yuan. Selling monopoly or restricted goods, or doing securities, futures, insurance, or payment settlement—serious cases are illegal business operation; the lowest tier is up to 5 years.
- Benefit: Operating without authorization is handled by the department the law designates. No license where the law doesn't spell out a penalty: stop, confiscate gains, fine up to 10,000 yuan. Heavier track: monopoly or restricted goods without permission, or securities, futures, insurance, or payment settlement without approval—serious cases are illegal business operation, up to 5 years' imprisonment or short-term detention plus a fine of 1–5 times illegal gains; especially serious, above 5 years (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国务院 (2017). 无证无照经营查处办法（国务院令第 684 号，第五、六、十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-08/23/content_5219861.htm>；市场监管总局 (2023). 食品经营许可和备案管理办法（市场监管总局令第 78 号，第四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10606/202307/content_6894763.html>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第二百二十五条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Notes: Finance, medical, and training each have their own licensing laws; this item only checked generic enforcement and criminal articles. Milk tea and snack shops are catering—food business license required. Renovate-first, trial-before-license is a common trap. Food tiers and whether fresh meat and vegetables need a license: item 8.

### 8. Categorize your food business before starting: production and catering require a permit, prepackaged food needs filing, and fresh produce needs no permit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The permit itself is free. However, facilities and premises must meet regulatory standards, and on-site inspections plus permit processing take several weeks. Meat sellers must also retain the quarantine certificate and meat quality inspection certificate for every batch.
- In plain terms: If you make and sell your own food—such as running a snack shop, braised deli, bakery, or homemade pickles—you must obtain a permit, while small workshops and food stalls follow provincial rules. If you only sell prepackaged food that meets standard definitions, you do not need a food business permit, but you must complete a record filing. Selling fresh produce like raw meat and vegetables requires no permit at all. If you operate without a required permit, you can be fined 50,000 to 100,000 yuan even if the total value of the goods is under 10,000 yuan.
- Benefit: Food Safety Law: Doing food business requires obtaining a permit first. Producing food, selling food, and providing catering services all require a permit according to law. "However, sales of edible agricultural products and sales exclusively of prepackaged food do not require a permit. Those exclusively selling prepackaged food shall file a record with the local food safety supervision and administration department of the people's government at or above the county level." Edible agricultural products refer to unprocessed goods such as fresh meat and vegetables. Whether an item qualifies as prepackaged food must be judged according to applicable standards, not merely by whether someone else made it or whether it has packaging; see Item 9 (Prepackaged Food Labeling) for the specific definition. "Specific administrative measures for food production and processing small workshops and food vendors shall be formulated by provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the Central Government"; rules for these two categories are set by each province. For those operating a food business without a permit, illegal income, food items, tools, equipment, and raw materials are all confiscated. If the total value of the goods (goods value) calculated at retail price is less than 10,000 yuan, an additional fine of 50,000 to 100,000 yuan is imposed. If the goods value is 10,000 yuan or more, a fine of 10 to 20 times the goods value is imposed. Regulations on the Administration of Pig Slaughtering: Pigs may only be slaughtered at designated slaughterhouses, subject to centralized official quarantine. "Except for rural individuals slaughtering pigs for self-consumption who are not subject to designated slaughtering, no entity or individual may engage in pig slaughtering activities without designation." "Pig products sold or processed into meat products by entities and individuals, as well as those produced and operated by catering service providers and communal dining units, must be pig products from designated pig slaughterhouses that have passed quarantine and meat quality inspection." Therefore, the pork you sell or use must come from designated slaughterhouses and pass both quarantine and meat quality inspection. Anyone slaughtering pigs privately outside designated slaughterhouses will be ordered to shut down, with the pigs, slaughtering tools, equipment, and illegal income confiscated. The fine amounts match the penalty bracket for operating a food business without a permit above. Animal Epidemic Prevention Law: Animal products being sold or transported must be accompanied by quarantine certificates and quarantine marks. Failure to do so will result in an order to rectify and a fine of up to 100% of the value of equivalent qualified goods. Judicial interpretation governs punitive damages, which are compensation awarded on top of a refund. If food produced or sold by small workshops or food vendors fails to meet food safety standards and the buyer demands ten-fold compensation, the court shall support it (national).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025 年第三次修正). 食品安全法（第三十五、三十六、一百二十二条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=7b5a76d0461745a08d3f964916b87ef3>；国务院 (2021). 生猪屠宰管理条例（国务院令第 742 号，第二、二十三、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/22/content_5626534.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2021 修订). 动物防疫法（第五十一、一百条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080817703add2017737395a973e31>；最高人民法院 (2024). 关于审理食品药品惩罚性赔偿纠纷案件适用法律若干问题的解释（法释〔2024〕9 号，第四条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/bf531a70471f10d377788f28d3d8f8.html>
- Notes: Thresholds for small workshops, small catering establishments, and food vendors are set by each province, so specific numbers are omitted here. Visit the official website of your provincial market supervision administration and search for "Regulations on the Administration of Food Small Workshops, Small Catering, and Food Vendors" to find your local version. Selling homemade food online does not exempt you from permits simply because it is "online"; whatever requires a permit offline still requires one. For entity registration and business licenses, see Item 6 (Determine Name, Premises, and Business Scope Before Registration). To check whether other industries require permits, see Item 7 (Industries That Require Permits). For the compensation amounts buyers can claim, see Section 5, Item 31 (Ten-Fold Compensation for Food).

### 9. Check labels before selling prepackaged food: list the production date, shelf life, and ingredients as required
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: A few cents to tens of cents per printed label. Making sure the date and ingredients are filled out correctly for every batch takes some time.
- In plain terms: Whether something counts as prepackaged food depends on whether it is pre-portioned and pre-packaged with a standardized weight or volume marked on it, not just whether it comes in a bag. Food weighed and bagged only when purchased, made and sold on the spot, or packaged as restaurant takeout does not become prepackaged food simply because of a bag or takeout box. If prepackaged food is missing required label details, selling goods worth under 10,000 yuan carries a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan. Bulk food sold loose must also display details like its name and date.
- Benefit: As of October 2026, the current GB 7718-2011 requires prepackaged food to be pre-portioned or packaged in packaging materials and containers, with a standardized net weight or volume indicated on the package. Items bagged and weighed only at the time of customer purchase cannot be judged as prepackaged food solely because they are in a bag. General labeling rules for prepackaged food do not apply to food freshly prepared and sold on the spot, nor does restaurant takeout qualify simply because it is placed in a meal box. The Food Safety Law requires labels on the packaging of prepackaged food. For food sold directly to consumers, unless explicitly permitted to be omitted by standards, the label must state the product name, specifications, net content, production date, ingredients or formulation table, producer's name, address, and contact information, and shelf life. It must also include the product standard code, storage conditions, common names of food additives used as specified in national standards, and production license number. Any additional items required by laws, regulations, or food safety standards must also be marked. When selling bulk food, the container and outer packaging must state the food name, production date or production batch number, shelf life, and the producer's or dealer's name, address, and contact information. Anyone who produces or sells prepackaged food without a label, or whose labels or instructions do not comply with this law, faces confiscation of both the illegal income and the food. If the total value of the goods (calculated by retail price) is under 10,000 yuan, an additional fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan is imposed. ### 11. Know the criminal lines in the food trade: selling diseased meat or substandard stock is a crime; adulterating with toxic substances starts at up to five years in prison regardless of sales revenue
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. Follow the playbook from item 8 (checking which regulatory tier your food business falls under first): keep quarantine certificates and purchase receipts on file, and include in your ingredient list only substances with verifiable official approval numbers.
- In plain terms: The previous items cost you money in fines; this one puts people in prison. Selling meat from animals that died of disease, meat that failed inspection, or food with pesticide (农药) residues, veterinary drug residues, or heavy metals severely over the limits carries up to 3 years in prison. It only has to be "sufficient to cause" severe food poisoning—no one actually needs to get sick first. If you adulterate food with toxic or harmful non-food raw materials, even the lowest sentencing tier carries up to 5 years in prison, no matter how little money you made.
- Benefit: Article 143 of the Criminal Law governs substandard food production and sales. If the food you produce or sell fails food safety standards and is "sufficient to cause a severe food poisoning incident or other severe foodborne illness," the crime is constituted. The penalty is fixed-term imprisonment of up to 3 years or criminal detention, plus a fine. If it causes severe harm to human health or involves other serious circumstances, the sentence is 3 to 7 years in prison plus a fine. For especially serious consequences, the penalty is 7 years to life imprisonment, accompanied by fines or total confiscation of property. Article 144 governs adulteration: mixing toxic or harmful non-food raw materials into food produced or sold, or knowingly selling such food, carries a sentence of up to 5 years in prison plus a fine. Causing severe harm to human health or other serious circumstances raises this to 5 to 10 years plus a fine. Causing death or other especially serious circumstances is punished under Article 141 (10 years to life imprisonment, or the death penalty). A joint judicial interpretation by the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate explicitly defines what constitutes being "sufficient to cause a severe food poisoning incident or other severe foodborne illness": (1) containing pathogenic microorganisms, pesticide residues, veterinary drug residues, biotoxins, or heavy metal contaminants that severely exceed standard limits; (2) involving meat or meat products from livestock, poultry, game, or aquatic animals that died of disease, died of unknown causes, or failed inspection and quarantine; (3) involving products whose production and sale are expressly prohibited by the state for disease prevention or control; or (4) special medical purpose formulas or infant supplementary foods whose nutritional components severely deviate from food safety standards. Using excessive food additives or using them outside permitted categories to an extent capable of causing the above harms is prosecuted under Article 143. What counts as "toxic and harmful non-food raw materials" is strictly objective: it includes substances banned from food by laws and regulations, as well as substances published on official lists by competent State Council departments (such as banned pesticides and veterinary drugs prohibited in food-producing animals). Furthermore, Article 144 recognizes an additional scenario as "other serious circumstances": producing or selling amounts between 100,000 and 200,000 yuan when conducted on or around primary/secondary school campuses, daycare centers, or nursing homes, or when targeted at minors or the elderly. Running an unauthorized slaughterhouse for pigs outside designated slaughter facilities constitutes illegal business operations if serious; nationwide thresholds define "serious circumstances" as reaching a production/sales volume of 100,000 yuan or illegal earnings of 50,000 yuan.

### 15. When you take money, write "earnest deposit", not "booking payment". Write the penalty as a number, and review a payment term as if you were lending
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Write “earnest deposit” in the contract. The amount does not exceed 20% of the contract total. Take the money before you deliver. Before you agree to let the other side take the goods and pay later (a payment term), check them first.
- In plain terms: Only the words “earnest deposit” bring the double rule: if you break the deal, you do not get this money back; if they break it, they return double. If they go under, it is a bad debt.
- Benefit: Earnest money counts only once the money has actually been handed over (it is established on actual delivery). The part above 20% of the contract total (the subject amount of the main contract) is not counted as earnest money. If the agreed penalty is absurdly higher or absurdly lower than the actual loss, the court may, at one side's request, adjust it down or up. If both a penalty and earnest money are written into the contract, a real breach lets you claim only one of them. You cannot take both (nationwide, from 2021).

### 17. Pay wages per contract and law; put raises, loans, and bonuses in writing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. State wages clearly in the contract. Raises, bonuses, advances, and loans all need a signed paper—no oral promises. Don't pay staff from your personal pocket on the company's behalf. The hard part is sticking to this when acquaintances ask.
- In plain terms: Wages must be paid on time and in full per contract and law. If wages are owed, the employee can ask a court for a payment order. Oral promises on raises, bonuses, or advances lose in a dispute if you can't prove them. Money you lend an employee isn't forced into writing by law between natural persons, but write an IOU with amount, interest, and repayment date.
- Benefit: A labor contract must specify how wages are paid and how social insurance is handled. The employer must pay wages timely and in full per the contract and state rules. Unpaid wages allow a direct application for a court payment order. An owner lending personal money to an employee is a loan contract. The Civil Code says: «A loan contract shall be in writing, except where natural persons agree otherwise.» An owner lending to an employee is exactly a loan between two natural persons—the law does not force writing. Contract content «generally includes loan type, currency, purpose, amount, interest rate, term, and repayment method.» Writing an IOU covering those items is the author's advice. Oral bonus promises without paper leave the party without evidence at a disadvantage (nationwide).

### 18. Sell before building: validate buyer interest with pre-orders and small batches before funding production
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a few hundred yuan to make a sample or a landing page. Then find a few dozen real customers willing to prepay or place a small order. This adds a few weeks compared to jumping straight into production. The hard part is resisting the urge to produce inventory first.
- In plain terms: A study from Italy found that entrepreneurs who frame hypotheses and test them against market feedback run better businesses. They are also more likely to pivot in time, without giving up earlier overall. Following this logic, if no one is willing to prepay or place a small order, do not assume people will buy it once it is built.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial tracked 116 Italian startups for about a year. A randomized controlled trial randomly divides participants into two groups, provides training to only one, and compares outcomes between the two. The result: entrepreneurs trained to make decisions by framing hypotheses and rigorously testing them with market feedback achieved better performance. They were also more likely to pivot to alternative ideas in time, without higher rates of early abandonment. The authors refer to projects that look promising but ultimately fail as "false positives." Their explanation is that this scientific approach reduces the likelihood of sinking more money into such dead ends (Italy, published 2020). Using pre-orders and small trial orders to validate demand is the practical rule this book suggests based on that logic.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Camuffo A, Cordova A, Gambardella A, Spina C (2020). A Scientific Approach to Entrepreneurial Decision Making: Evidence from a Randomized Control Trial. Management Science 66(2):564-586. <https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249>
- Notes: Rated B because there is only this single study, with a small sample size, conducted in Italy. The title, source, and abstract were verified via Crossref and Semantic Scholar; the exact effect sizes from the full text were not verified in this section. In practice: if nobody is willing to prepay or place a small order, do not believe that "if you build it, they will come."

### 19. Run the sample through a mass-production checklist before you commit to a production run

### 20. Keep invoices and supplier records for every purchase batch; skip goods priced far below market—if staff buy counterfeits for the company, the owner can still be the one convicted
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. For each batch keep purchase contract, invoice, payment record, and the upstream party's business license and authorization. Set an internal rule: whoever buys signs; never accept prices clearly below market. The hard part is passing up cheap stock that looks profitable.
- In plain terms: Selling counterfeits: illegal gains of 30000 yuan, or sales of 50000 yuan, meets the criminal threshold—up to 3 years. Unsold warehouse stock counts if goods value reaches three times the sales threshold. «I didn't know it was fake» is not yours alone to decide: buying far below market without a good explanation supports knowledge. Keeping purchase contract, invoice, payment trail, and upstream identity is the path to the no-damages defense.
- Benefit: Knowingly selling goods bearing counterfeit registered trademarks: illegal gains of 30000 yuan meets the criminal threshold; sales of 50000 yuan likewise. Up to 3 years plus a fine, or a fine alone. At ten times those levels: 3–10 years plus fine. Unsold stock still counts: goods value at three times the sales threshold or more is also a crime. «Knowing» need not be admitted. A 2025 SPC and SPP interpretation lists five situations where knowledge is presumed unless you prove otherwise: (1) knowing the registered trademark on the goods was altered, swapped, or covered; (2) forging or altering the trademark owner's authorization, or knowing it was forged or altered; (3) prior criminal or administrative penalty for selling counterfeits, then selling the same kind again; (4) no reasonable grounds, buying or selling far below market; (5) after discovery by administrative or judicial authorities, moving or destroying infringing goods and accounting records, or providing false proof. When a company commits this offense, «the unit is fined, and the directly responsible managers and other directly responsible persons» are punished under the same article—so even if staff sourced the goods, the person who approved still faces judgment. Trademark law leaves an out: selling without knowing the goods infringe another's registered trademark, «if you can prove lawful acquisition and identify the provider, you bear no compensation liability». Whether that defense works depends entirely on invoices and upstream identity; without them you can't use it (nationwide; interpretation effective 26 April 2025).

Beyond criminal liability there is administrative penalty: infringing goods and tools confiscated; illegal turnover of 50000 yuan or more can draw a fine of up to 5 times turnover. If stock already bought turns out wrong, stop selling, seal it, and contact the brand or market regulators—don't keep selling, and don't destroy goods and books; destruction fits presumption (5) above. Artwork rules: item 21. ### 21. Art on products, packaging, tags, and ads must be yours or licensed—recoloring or adding icons is not «changed enough»

On trademarks, «likely to cause confusion» is infringement by itself.

The criminal threshold: for profit, without the copyright owner's permission, you reproduce and distribute fine-art works. ### 23. Close down properly if you lose money: use simplified deregistration if eligible, file for bankruptcy if insolvent, and never just abandon the business
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Simplified deregistration can be handled on your own: post a public notice for 20 days on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System. If you still have outstanding debts, settle the accounts first—collect what is owed and pay what you owe; this step is liquidation. Filing for bankruptcy requires applying to the court and takes several months. The hardest part is simply facing the loss instead of dragging your feet and doing nothing.
- In plain terms: If you have no outstanding debts, unpaid wages, taxes, or social insurance, and all investors sign a written commitment, you can deregister through the simplified process after a 20-day notice with no objections; sole proprietors do not even need a public notice. If you fail to file annual reports for 2 consecutive years, stay on the irregular operations list without fixing it, and cannot be reached, the business license is revoked, and the responsible legal representative cannot serve again for 3 years.
- Benefit: You owe nothing and no one owes you anything, or all accounts have been settled. No unpaid wages, no unpaid social insurance, no unpaid taxes. If an enterprise has its business license revoked, any legal representative personally responsible for it cannot serve as a legal representative, director, supervisor, or senior executive of any company for 3 years, counted from the date of revocation. ### 24. Before paying for e-commerce store management, training courses, or "no-inventory" setups, ask whether extra fees will follow and where sales actually come from

### 25. File your annual report online every year between January 1 and June 30 if you run a company or are self-employed, even with zero business—filing is free
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: It costs nothing. Log in to the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System yourself to fill it out once a year. Sole proprietors can also submit a paper copy. The annual report is submitted to the market regulation department and is completely separate from filing taxes monthly or quarterly with the tax bureau. Filing your taxes does not mean you filed your annual report. The tricky part is that it only happens once a year, making it easy to forget.
- In plain terms: Company owners and sole proprietors must file an annual report for the previous year online during the first half of every year (sole proprietors can also submit on paper). If you do not file, you are placed on the list of enterprises with abnormal operations, though you can apply for removal after filing late. Filing is completely free. Anyone calling to say you missed filing and directing you to a mini-program or WeChat official account to pay an agency fee is a scammer—check the official publicity system yourself first. Ringleaders of such scams have been sentenced to over 10 years in prison.
- Benefit: Companies must "submit the previous year's annual report to the market regulation department through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System between January 1 and June 30 each year, and publish it to the public." Those failing to file within the deadline are placed on the list of enterprises with abnormal operations "and subject to administrative penalties according to law." The abnormal operations list is displayed on the publicity system, accessible to anyone. Entities on the list face restrictions or bans in government procurement, project bidding, and similar areas. Sole proprietors must also file their previous year's annual report between January 1 and June 30 each year, either online or on paper. Sole proprietors who began business in the current year start filing the following year. Sole proprietors can choose whether to make the contents of their annual report public, but failing to file will still result in being placed on the abnormal operations list. Those listed for failure to file can apply to the market regulation department where registered to be removed once they catch up on unfiled years. A company that fails to file for 2 consecutive years, is placed on the list without rectifying it, and cannot be reached at its registered address will have its business license revoked. An enterprise's annual report must be publicized to society. Even if a sole proprietor chooses not to publicize the details, the system will still display that they "have submitted the annual report." Therefore, whether you have filed and whether you are on the list can be verified simply by logging into the publicity system yourself. The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) has warned of a scam. The administration clarified that "annual reporting is free of charge." Filing the report and applying for removal from the abnormal operations list "must be done through official channels such as the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System." ### 26. Raise business funds only face-to-face with family, friends, and staff; never solicit money on social media or let them recruit outsiders for you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Free. When borrowing money or bringing in partners, talk face-to-face, one-on-one, and strictly with relatives, friends, and existing employees you already know. Do not post on social media feeds, do not post in group chats, do not print flyers, and do not hold promotional pitch events. If a relative or friend offers to "ask around for you," stop them immediately. The hardest part is resisting temptation when cash is tight, because recruiting more people always looks like the quickest way to get funded.
- In plain terms: Borrowing money or pooling equity solely from relatives, friends, and existing employees—without letting them recruit outsiders—is not a crime. What breaks the law is publicly soliciting money from strangers while promising repayment of principal, interest, or investment returns. Taking in 1 million yuan or collecting money from 150 people is enough to trigger a criminal trial, starting at up to 3 years in prison. If the money was used for legitimate business operations and fully returned before prosecution, criminal punishment can be waived.
- Benefit: Article 176 of the Criminal Law penalizes "illegally absorbing public deposits or absorbing public deposits in disguised form, disrupting financial order." Sentences range up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment or criminal detention, alongside or solely with a fine. For huge amounts or other serious circumstances, the sentence is 3 to 10 years; for especially huge amounts or other especially serious circumstances, 10 years or more. A judicial interpretation by the Supreme People's Court stipulates that all four conditions must be met simultaneously: (1) raising funds without lawful approval from relevant authorities, or under the guise of legitimate business operations; (2) publicly promoting to society via the internet, media, pitch meetings, promotional flyers, mobile messages, etc.; (3) promising to repay principal and interest or provide returns in cash, physical goods, equity, etc., within a specified period; and (4) absorbing funds from unspecified members of the public. The same article explicitly adds: "Absorbing funds from specific targets within family, friends, or one's own organization without public promotion does not constitute illegally absorbing public deposits or absorbing public deposits in disguised form." However, joint guidance from the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate, and the Ministry of Public Security outlines two exceptions that are still treated as taking funds from the public: first, raising funds from relatives, friends, or employees while knowingly allowing them to collect money from strangers on your behalf; second, hiring outsiders as employees specifically to absorb money from them. Three thresholds trigger criminal prosecution (meeting any single one suffices): collecting 1 million yuan or more, pooling funds from 150 people or more, or causing direct economic losses of 500,000 yuan or more. Collecting 5 million yuan or more, involving 500 people or more, or causing direct losses of 2.5 million yuan or more pushes the offense into the 3-to-10-year sentencing bracket. Amounts are calculated based on the gross total collected. If funds were primarily used in normal business production and operations and are fully refunded before indictment, criminal punishment can be waived. Cases that are noticeably minor and cause little harm are not prosecuted as crimes. Even if criminal thresholds are not reached, administrative penalties apply: the local lead authority handling illegal fund-raising can impose a fine of 20% to 100% of the funds raised. For institutional fund-raising, legal representatives and directly responsible personnel receive a warning and a fine between 500,000 and 5,000,000 yuan (National, judicial interpretation effective March 1, 2022).

### 27. Check whether a space allows your business before signing a lease: converting a home into a shop requires unanimous consent from the entire building, and restaurants producing cooking fumes cannot open in residential buildings
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

Second, the building type: is it purely residential, mixed commercial-residential, or a standalone commercial property, and does anyone live upstairs? Third, if opening a food business, check whether there is a dedicated fume exhaust duct, then ask the local market supervision bureau whether you can obtain a food business license. The hard part is doing your own verification even when landlords and brokers claim, "No problem, the previous tenant ran a shop here."
- In plain terms: Converting a residential apartment into a commercial shop requires unanimous consent from every other owner in the same building, plus any owners in other buildings who can prove they are affected. If neighbors sue demanding that you restore the property to its original state or pay damages, the court will support them. Owners in other buildings within the same residential compound qualify only if they prove that their property value or quality of life is affected. If converted without their unanimous consent, a court must support their requests to cease operations, restore the original condition, or compensate for losses; pleading that "a majority of owners agreed" is not accepted by the court. ### 28. When several people open a shop together, put capital, profit splits, who decides, who draws wages, and how to exit into a written agreement before opening
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Sit down once or twice with the partners, write about ten items clearly, and each sign. Partnership-agreement templates are online; fill the ten items listed in the Partnership Enterprise Law one by one. The hard part is acquaintances feeling that «talking money hurts the relationship» and not wanting to start.
- In plain terms: Without an agreement, the law sets default rules for you. Decisions need unanimous consent; profits follow each person's actual capital paid in; the person who watches the shop every day draws no wage by default. With no partnership term agreed, anyone may exit after reasonable advance notice to the others. Changing those defaults only works if written into the agreement in advance.
- Benefit: Partnership Enterprise Law Article 4: the partnership agreement is made in writing by unanimous negotiation of all partners. Article 18 lists ten items the agreement should cover—including capital contribution form, amount, and payment deadline; profit distribution and loss sharing; who executes partnership affairs; admission and withdrawal; dispute resolution; dissolution and liquidation; and breach liability. Partners who don't form a partnership enterprise and only work together under a Civil Code partnership contract follow that contract; where the contract is silent, Civil Code defaults apply. Partnership decisions, unless the contract says otherwise, need unanimous partner consent. A partner who executes partnership affairs may not claim pay for that alone, unless the contract says otherwise. Profits and losses follow the contract. If silent: negotiate first; if negotiation fails, by actual capital ratios; if ratios can't be figured, equal shares. Partners bear joint liability for partnership debts; anyone who pays more may seek contribution from the others. With no partnership term agreed, it is an open-ended partnership—a partner may terminate at any time with reasonable advance notice to the others. For a partnership enterprise with no term agreed, a partner may withdraw with 30 days' notice to the others. The agreement also may not assign all profits to only some partners, or dump all losses on only some (nationwide).
