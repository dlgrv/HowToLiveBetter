# 14. Account and Information Security

Scope: money and personal information. If someone else logs into your account, you lose money first. They will also use your account to scam people in your contact list. Your identity is effectively taken too.
### 1. Turn on two-factor authentication for email, payment, and social accounts; prefer phone pop-up confirmation, with SMS codes second
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. Set it once per account; two or three minutes each.
- In plain terms: Two-factor authentication means that besides your password, you confirm once more that it is really you when you log in. With a pop-up on your phone that you tap to confirm, more than nine out of ten phishing account takeovers are blocked. Old methods like answering “where did you last log in?” or “what is your backup email?” only block around one in ten phishing takeovers.
- Benefit: Google counted 350,000 real account takeover attempts (also called account hijacking). One class uses device-based verification, such as a pop-up on your phone for you to confirm, or plugging in a security key. That class blocked “more than 94% of phishing-related hijacking attempts and 100% of automated hijacking attempts.” Phishing means tricking you into entering your password on a fake site; automated means machines trying leaked passwords in bulk. Another class uses knowledge-based verification, such as where you last logged in or what your backup email is. That class “blocked as little as 10% of phishing hijacking and 73% of automated hijacking”
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Doerfler P, Thomas K, Marincenko M, et al. (2019). Evaluating Login Challenges as a Defense Against Account Takeover. The World Wide Web Conference (WWW '19). <https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313481>
- Notes: The same study also found that this verification sometimes locks out the real user. 52% of genuine users did not get in on the first try, but 97% eventually did. Turn it on for email first, because most other accounts can recover passwords through email. Keep verification codes to yourself; what reading a code aloud for someone else can cost you is in Section 8, item 45 (reading verification codes).

### 2. Give your email its own password; do not reuse it on any website
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. A password manager can store it so you do not have to memorize it. The hard part is breaking the habit of one password everywhere.
- In plain terms: Passwords stolen from other sites get tried on your email directly. Once someone is in your email, every account that can reset its password through that email is lost too. So the email password stands alone; do not use it anywhere else.
- Benefit: Trying leaked account-and-password pairs from elsewhere against your logins is called credential stuffing; it is the easiest attack, and your email gets tried this way too. Once someone is in your email, every account that uses it for password recovery goes with it. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency recommends a different strong password for every account, at least 16 characters, stored in a password manager
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:US CISA. Use Strong Passwords. <https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords>
- Notes: If you cannot memorize them, use your browser’s built-in password manager. It remembers each site’s password for you, which is far stronger than reusing one password everywhere. Do not store passwords in WeChat favorites or notes.

### 3. Set a screen lock on your phone and a PIN on your SIM card
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. Set the screen lock and the SIM PIN once each.
- In plain terms: The SIM card is the small card in your phone; SMS verification codes arrive through it. If you lose the phone, whoever finds it can pull out the card, put it in another phone, collect verification codes, and reset your accounts one by one. A PIN on the card means that when the card moves to another phone, you must enter that code at startup first, and that path is cut off.
- Benefit: After a phone is lost, the fastest move for whoever finds it is to put the SIM in another phone and collect your SMS verification codes. With those codes they can reset your accounts one by one. With a SIM PIN, another phone asks for the code at startup, so they cannot use the card to receive codes. In October 2020 the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工信部) reported a real case. After a user’s phone was stolen, they did not suspend the line in time. Wrongdoers used “mobile number plus verification code” to log into a government service app and obtained the user’s ID number and other data. They then changed the mobile service password and tricked customer service into unsuspending the already-suspended card. Finally they exploited a “forgot password” flaw on a lending platform, reset the payment password, and stole loan funds. MIIT used the case to remind users to set a SIM PIN promptly and to suspend the line immediately when a phone is lost. For the screen-lock half, the US Federal Communications Commission advises setting a strong password on the phone to limit others’ use; when the phone is lost or stolen, that helps stop unauthorized charges and misuse of personal data
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:工业和信息化部 (2020). 工信部提醒！强化个人信息保护意识. 中国政府网转载工信微报，2020-10-22. <https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202010/22/464049/article.html>；US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device>
- Notes: Set the PIN under “SIM card lock” in phone settings. Factory default codes are usually 1234 or 0000. Three wrong entries in a row require the carrier’s PUK code to unlock, so write the PIN on paper after you set it. Rated C because both sources are official reminders plus one case, with no study quantifying how much money a PIN saves. What to do first when a phone is lost is in item 4 (if your phone is lost, follow this order).

### 4. If your phone is lost, do this in order: suspend the SIM, remote-lock the phone, change passwords, report to police, freeze bank cards
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. The full sequence takes about ten minutes.
- In plain terms: Order matters more than speed. Step one, suspend the SIM, and the verification-code lifeline is cut. Then remote-lock the phone, change email and payment passwords from a computer, report to police for a receipt, and freeze bank cards as needed. Without your phone, you can still call the carrier from someone else’s phone to suspend the line.
- Benefit: Order matters more than speed. First, suspend the SIM so others cannot receive your verification codes. Second, remote-lock the phone and wipe its contents. Third, from a computer, change email and payment passwords. Fourth, report to police and get a receipt. Last, freeze bank cards as needed. The US Federal Communications Commission also says that even if you think you only misplaced the phone, remote-lock it; if it was stolen, report to police at once with model and IMEI (the phone’s identity number), and tell the carrier immediately
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device>；步骤顺序是作者经验；补办身份证见第 7 节，冒名贷款见第 8 节关于征信的一条
- Notes: Save the customer service numbers for the three major carriers in advance: China Mobile 10086, China Unicom 10010, China Telecom 10000. Also note which city you registered the number in; customer service will ask. You can suspend the line from another person’s phone the same way.

### 5. If your card is fraudulently charged, suspend or freeze it and report to police first, then demand compensation from the bank: proving “you made the charge” is the bank’s job
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. As soon as something on the card looks wrong, suspend or freeze it immediately. Keep police reports, suspension records, and transaction notices from the bank. If the physical card is still with you, make a small balance inquiry or deposit nearby to leave a record that the real card was in your hands when it happened. The hard part is not arguing with customer service first; suspend first.
- In plain terms: If someone fraudulently uses your card, you do not have to prove “that was not me.” The bank must prove the charge was made by you or with your authorization; if it cannot, it must compensate you. That assumes you suspend or freeze as soon as you find out. If you delay suspension, you bear the extra losses that pile up afterward.
- Benefit: Supreme People’s Court rules split “who must prove what.” If you say it was counterfeit-card fraud or online fraud, you go first with evidence. Counterfeit-card fraud means someone copied your card and used the copy. Usable proof includes effective legal documents, where the physical card was during the transaction, where the transaction occurred, account statements, transaction alerts, police reports, suspension records, and so on. **Conversely, if the issuing bank or a non-bank payment institution (third-party payment) says the cardholder made the charge or authorized it, they must produce the evidence.** After you notify the bank, if it does not verify promptly or does not provide or preserve transaction records and surveillance video, and evidence is lost because of that, the bank bears the consequence of lacking proof. Once liability is established: debit card (savings card) holders may demand that the bank return stolen deposits with interest and compensate losses; credit card holders may demand return of unauthorized overdraft principal, interest, and penalties, and compensation for losses, and courts will not support the bank demanding repayment of that overdraft from you. You may also demand that the bank promptly remove related negative credit records (nationwide, effective 25 May 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:最高人民法院 (2021). 关于审理银行卡民事纠纷案件若干问题的规定（第四、五、七、十四、十五条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/304771.html>
- Notes: Two situations leave you liable. First, if you did not keep your card, password, or verification codes safe and you were at fault (the original text: failure to fulfill the duty of safekeeping), you bear loss in proportion to fault. So do not tell others your password or forward verification codes (see item 1, prefer phone pop-up for two-factor authentication). Second, if you do not suspend in time and losses grow, you bear the extra part. So the first step is always suspend or freeze, not argue with customer service. Third-party payment institutions follow the same rules. If their marketing promises “pay first, investigate later” in concrete terms, you can hold them to that advance payment. Money you were tricked into transferring yourself follows a different path; see Section 8, item 2 (if you discover fraud, call 110 or 96110 immediately to request a payment stop).

### 6. Every so often, check logged-in devices and authorized apps on your accounts; remove what you no longer use
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. A few minutes each time. The hard part is that nothing reminds you; you have to remember to look.
- In plain terms: “Logged-in devices” means the phones and computers that can still use your account right now. If an unfamiliar device appears on the list, or software you stopped using still connects to your account, change the password first, then sign out everywhere. For email, also check whether any auto-forwarding was set without your knowledge.
- Benefit: The logged-in-devices list records which phones and computers can still use the account. The authorized-apps list records third-party apps you allowed to sign in with the account. Unfamiliar devices and long-unused third-party apps are the easiest traces to spot. The UK National Cyber Security Centre lists “logins or login attempts from unfamiliar places or at unusual times” as signs of account compromise. It also says criminals often add a forwarding rule in email so every message to you is copied to them, which they use to reset your passwords. Its recommended order is: remove unknown forwarding rules, change the password, then sign out all devices and apps. After sign-out, anyone else needs the new password to use the account. The US Federal Trade Commission likewise advises signing out all devices after regaining an account and deleting forwarding rules you did not set
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:UK NCSC. Recovering a hacked account. <https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/recovering-a-hacked-account>；US FTC. How To Recover Your Hacked Email or Social Media Account. <https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recover-your-hacked-email-or-social-media-account>；两份都是账号出事以后的处理指南，「隔一段时间主动去看」是作者经验
- Notes: WeChat, Alipay, email, Apple ID, and Android accounts all have this screen. If you see an unfamiliar device, change the password first, then choose sign out everywhere; that is the order the UK NCSC gives. Rated C because both sources are official how-to guides, with no study on how much periodic checks reduce losses.

### 7. Do not tap “accept all” just to use an app: for non-essential data, refusing must not cost you the service
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No cost. The hard part is not tapping “accept all.”
- In plain terms: When an app asks for your information, if it is not required to provide the service, the app cannot deny you access because you refuse. It may only collect what it actually needs. A map app needing your location is required; a flashlight app needing your contacts is not.
- Benefit: The law states two rules. First, you may not refuse products or services on the grounds that the person does not consent or withdraws consent, except where processing that information is necessary to provide the service. Second, collection must be limited to the minimum needed for the purpose; only what is usable may be collected
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法. 中国人大网. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>：第六条「收集个人信息，应当限于实现处理目的的最小范围，不得过度收集个人信息」；第十六条「个人信息处理者不得以个人不同意处理其个人信息或者撤回同意为由，拒绝提供产品或者服务；处理个人信息属于提供产品或者服务所必需的除外」；第十五条「基于个人同意处理个人信息的，个人有权撤回其同意。个人信息处理者应当提供便捷的撤回同意的方式」
- Notes: The test is whether the information is necessary to provide this service. Location is necessary for a map; contacts are not for a flashlight. After installing an app, open app permissions in phone settings and turn off what is not necessary. When you truly need a permission, choose allow only this once.

### 8. You have the right to view, copy, correct, and delete your personal information; if refused, you can sue
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No cost. You only need to complain or sue if the other party stalls. A real lawsuit starts at several months, and you pay your own lawyer fees, so complaining first is usually better value. The hard part is following up when they drag their feet.
- In plain terms: You may require a company to let you view, copy, correct, and delete your information. When service stops, retention periods end, or you withdraw consent, the company should delete on its own. If it refuses you, it must state the reason; if it still will not act, you can sue in court. Closing an account and deleting personal information are two steps; after closing the account you must request deletion separately.
- Benefit: In several situations the company must delete proactively: service stopped, agreed retention period ended, you withdrew consent, the original collection purpose was achieved, and so on. If it did not delete, you may demand deletion. If it refuses your exercise of these rights, it must state the reason. You may sue directly in court
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2021). 个人信息保护法. 中国人大网. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>：第四十五条「个人有权向个人信息处理者查阅、复制其个人信息……个人请求查阅、复制其个人信息的，个人信息处理者应当及时提供」；第四十六条更正、补充权；第四十七条列了五种应当主动删除的情形，含「（一）处理目的已实现、无法实现或者为实现处理目的不再必要」「（二）个人信息处理者停止提供产品或者服务，或者保存期限已届满」「（三）个人撤回同意」，「个人信息处理者未删除的，个人有权请求删除」；第五十条「个人信息处理者应当建立便捷的个人行使权利的申请受理和处理机制。拒绝个人行使权利的请求的，应当说明理由」「个人可以依法向人民法院提起诉讼」
- Notes: Closing an account and deleting personal information are separate; after closing, request deletion separately. Before changing phones or selling an old phone, sign out of all accounts and unlink them on the old device, then factory reset. The law gives you a post-hoc deletion right; it cannot claw back what already leaked.

### 9. Facial recognition is not mandatory: if other methods can do the same job, they cannot make face scan the only option; if you refuse, they must offer alternatives
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No cost. When asked to scan your face, ask “is there another way to verify?” If they say no, demand one. The hard part is asking out loud on the spot.
- In plain terms: As long as another method can achieve the same purpose, they cannot require face scan alone. If you refuse face verification, they must offer card swipe, password, ID document, or similar alternatives, and they may not force you with “then we cannot serve you.” No one may install face recognition equipment inside hotel guest rooms, public baths, changing rooms, or restrooms.
- Benefit: The Administrative Measures for the Safe Application of Facial Recognition Technology (人脸识别技术应用安全管理办法) state: “Where the same purpose can be achieved or equivalent business requirements met, and other non-facial-recognition methods exist, facial recognition must not be the sole verification method. Where an individual does not agree to identity verification via facial information, other reasonable and convenient methods shall be provided.” The measures also state: “No organization or individual may, under the pretext of handling business or improving service quality, mislead, defraud, or coerce an individual into accepting facial recognition to verify identity.” If processing relies on consent, there must be “separate consent given voluntarily and explicitly with full knowledge”: asked separately for this matter, and you agree separately. You may withdraw consent, and the processor must provide a convenient way to withdraw. Processing facial information of minors under 14 requires consent from a parent or other guardian. Face recognition devices in public places must be “necessary for maintaining public security” and carry prominent notices. Inside private spaces in public venues such as hotel guest rooms, public baths, public changing rooms, and public restrooms, no organization or individual may install them. Facial information shall be stored on the device and must not be transmitted over the internet, except as otherwise provided by laws or administrative regulations, or with separate consent (nationwide, effective 1 June 2025)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家互联网信息办公室、公安部 (2025). 人脸识别技术应用安全管理办法（第 19 号令，第十条、十二条、十三条，2025 年 6 月 1 日起施行）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/21/c_1744174262156096.htm>
- Notes: Common cases are residential gate access, rental platforms, gyms, and hotels asking you to enroll your face. When they say “the system only supports face scan,” quote the measures verbatim: “Where the same purpose can be achieved or equivalent business requirements met, and other non-facial-recognition methods exist, facial recognition must not be the sole verification method.” Then demand card, password, or ID alternatives. If they still refuse, report to the local cyberspace authority (网信部门). Where national rules require face verification for identity, such as some financial and government scenarios, follow those rules. Unlike a password, a face cannot be changed after leak, so treat it more carefully than a password. Entities storing facial information on 100,000 or more people must file with provincial-level or higher cyberspace authorities within 30 working days; that filing is also a question to ask about whether they are legitimate. Rights to access, correct, and delete personal information are in item 8. Someone using your face to take out their own loan is a different matter; see Section 8, item 45 (helping with a face scan).

### 10. Do not hand code, keys, or private material to unknown “AI proxy gateways,” especially when letting AI run commands automatically
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Official direct access is usually pricier than proxy gateways, and topping up is less convenient. The pull is that proxies are cheap and one endpoint can call several model vendors
- In plain terms: A proxy sits between you and the model vendor; it can see and alter what you send and what comes back. Researchers measured 400-plus proxies: 9 injected malicious code into replies, 17 tampered with cloud service keys the researchers had planted on purpose. Where AI runs commands automatically, one altered command can be enough for someone else to control the computer.
- Benefit: In June 2026 the Ministry of State Security issued a risk alert. Some proxies retain user submissions on servers; some intercept privately and resell data to other model vendors for training. Some hide backdoors that implant malicious code on user devices, steal account keys and cloud credentials, or even install remote-control software. The alert recommends official direct connections or properly licensed platforms, and not using platforms of unknown origin without operating credentials or security safeguards. Desensitize personal privacy and project material before use, manage keys, and rotate them on a schedule. On abnormal billing, unexplained account bans, or odd data, stop use immediately, rotate keys, run antivirus, and preserve evidence. Researchers from UC Santa Barbara and others measured in 2026. They bought 28 paid proxies from Taobao (淘宝), Xianyu (闲鱼), and overseas shops, and collected 400 free proxies from public communities. One paid and eight free injected malicious code into returned tool calls. Tool calls are commands AI has your computer run, such as installing a package. Two timed their attacks; the paper’s example is targeting only fully automated sessions. Seventeen touched Amazon Web Services keys the researchers had planted on purpose. One moved funds from a researcher’s Ethereum wallet; the wallet held very little, under $50. The demonstrated tampering was subtle: swap `requests` for the near-identical `reqeusts` in an install command, hard to spot at a glance (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:国家安全部 (2026). 「AI中转站」，风险要防范. <https://www.szzg.gov.cn/2026/xwzx/szkx/202606/t20260608_5331487.htm>（数字中国建设峰会官网转载国家安全部微信公众号）；Liu H, Shou C, Wen H, Chen Y, Fang RJ, Feng Y. (2026). Your Agent Is Mine: Measuring Malicious Intermediary Attacks on the LLM Supply Chain. arXiv:2604.08407. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08407>
- Notes: Rated B because there is only one study, and it is a preprint (published before formal peer review). Most measured proxies were free; only 28 were paid, so you cannot infer what share of all proxies are malicious. Some media wrote the theft as $500,000; the paper says under $50. Benefit magnitude is set at medium by consequence: loss from stolen keys and code varies widely, and the study has no amount to apply mechanically. If you already used an unknown proxy, revoke and regenerate keys at the model vendor, and rotate cloud and repository keys together. When traffic goes through a proxy, do not let AI run commands fully automatically; review commands before allowing them. Operators running proxy gateways face criminal liability; see Section 11, item 19 (AI proxy gateways). The beneficiary is you.
