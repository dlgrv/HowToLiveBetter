# 2. Don't Die Slowly

This section covers only two types of chronic risks: those with a major impact on all-cause mortality, and those backed by solid evidence. A few items are labeled "randomized trial," meaning participants were randomly divided into groups to compare outcomes; these findings are the most reliable. All other figures come from studies that merely tracked people over time without assigning them to groups. The ratios calculated by such studies (like HR and RR) carry two types of distortion: the people were already different to begin with, and some only made lifestyle changes after getting sick. These numbers therefore show only the direction and rough scale of an effect—they do not guarantee that doing the same will cut your risk by that exact amount.

The items in this section are grouped by topic below, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Tobacco, alcohol, and betel nut**: Quit smoking as early as possible (item 1), keep homes and cars smoke-free (item 2), get medication before quitting smoking (item 3), set a quit date (item 4), visit a smoking cessation clinic or call 12320 (item 5), consider e-cigarettes only if quitting fails (item 6), do not chew betel nut (item 8), drink less or not at all (item 19), do not quit cold turkey alone if you get the shakes (item 20), count how much you drink in a week (item 21).

**Things to eat and drink less**: Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages (item 7), eat less processed meat (item 18), cut down on ultra-processed foods (item 28), do not drink scalding hot tea or soup (item 30), throw away the whole package if food gets moldy (item 46), avoid raw freshwater fish and shrimp (item 47).

**Things to swap in or eat more of**: Switch to low-sodium salt (item 9), eat a small handful of nuts daily (item 22), swap red meat for fish and poultry (item 23), replace refined grains with whole grains (item 24), drink tea more than three times a week (item 25), drink three to four cups of coffee a day (item 26), eat at least 5 servings of fruit and vegetables (item 27), eat chili peppers more than four times a week (item 33), drink one or two servings of dairy daily (item 34), no need to give up eggs (item 35).

**Exercise and weight**: Walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day (item 11), play racquet sports (item 14), build up short bursts of vigorous activity (item 15), do strength training every week (item 16), avoid sitting for long stretches (item 17), keep BMI (body mass index) between 20–25 (item 32), count daily dog walks toward your step total (item 44), screen for prediabetes (item 45).

**Sleep and night shifts**: Sleep around 7 hours a night (item 13), keep naps under half an hour (item 37), catch up on sleep the next day after staying up late (item 38), switch off night shifts as early as possible if you can (item 39).

**Kitchen oil and smoke**: Do not burn coal or wood for cooking and heating (item 29), avoid unpackaged raw-pressed peanut oil (item 40), replace lard and butter with vegetable oil (item 41), run the range hood while cooking (item 42).

**Dental care, medication, and other habits**: Brush thoroughly and clean between teeth (item 10), take medication regularly as prescribed (item 12), get some sun outdoors during the day (item 31), take baths when possible (item 36), get evaluated for hormone therapy if experiencing hot flashes around menopause (item 43).
### 1. Quit smoking as early as possible
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, and actually saves money. A pack a day costs about 20–30 RMB, so quitting keeps that cash in your pocket. The hard part is enduring withdrawal, which typically takes a few weeks to several months.
- In plain terms: In US studies, smokers lived on average over 10 years less than non-smokers. Quitting before age 40 wipes out about 90% of the excess risk of dying from continued smoking. The earlier you quit, the more years of life you win back. If you keep smoking after a bone fracture, the bone is much more likely to fail to heal, so do not smoke until it has fully knit back together.
- Benefit: An observational cohort study in the US found that current smokers have a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than never-smokers. Quitting before age 40 eliminates about 90% of the excess mortality risk associated with continued smoking. Quitting at ages 25–34 gained back about 10 years of life, at 35–44 about 9 years, and at 45–54 about 6 years (reported as life-years). Similar cohort studies in China found that in the 2010s, urban male smokers had 1.65 times the mortality risk of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and rural males had 1.22 times the risk. After 10 years of voluntary smoking cessation, this excess risk nearly disappeared. The same holds after a fracture: a meta-analysis of 19 observational studies found that smokers had higher odds of nonunion (the bone failing to heal) compared to non-smokers, with an odds ratio of about 2.3 (OR 2.32, 95% CI 1.76–3.06, adjusted for confounders), with similarly elevated risks in tibial and open fractures. In a Swedish randomized trial, among 50 patients who joined a 6-week smoking cessation program after emergency fracture surgery, 20% developed at least one postoperative complication, compared to 38% among 55 patients who did not. For planned elective surgeries, starting a cessation program 4 to 8 weeks before surgery with weekly counseling and nicotine replacement therapy reduced postoperative complications by about 58% (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.27–0.65; 2 trials, 210 participants).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Jha P 等 (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z 等 (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>；Oberg M 等 (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>（二手烟那两个数字）；Scolaro JA 等 (2014). Cigarette smoking increases complications following fracture: a systematic review. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.M.00081>；Nåsell H 等 (2010). Effect of smoking cessation intervention on results of acute fracture surgery: a randomized controlled trial. J Bone Joint Surg Am. <https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.I.00627>；Thomsen T 等 (2014). Interventions for preoperative smoking cessation. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002294.pub4>（骨折和手术那几个数字）
- Notes: Breathing secondhand smoke is also deadly: in 2004, approximately 603,000 deaths worldwide were attributable to secondhand smoke, accounting for about 1% of total global mortality. Beyond not smoking yourself, avoid secondhand smoke and especially protect children from exposure. Concrete strategies to quit are covered in the next four items: Item 3 (smoking cessation medications), Item 4 (setting a quit date), Item 5 (smoking cessation clinics), and Item 6 (e-cigarettes). The nonunion statistics come from observational studies; smokers may differ from non-smokers in other ways that cannot be fully adjusted for. The trial on cessation after fracture included only 105 patients; when the authors recalculated using an odds ratio, the difference was not statistically significant (OR 2.51, 95% CI 0.96–6.9).

### 2. Don't smoke at home or in the car, and don't let guests smoke indoors
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time. The hard part is speaking up to ask family members and guests not to smoke inside.
- In plain terms: In 2004, secondhand smoke killed roughly 603,000 people worldwide, and 28% of them were children. People exposed to secondhand smoke face a higher risk of high blood pressure (hypertension), heart disease, and stroke. Breathing it in at home is even more harmful than outside the home.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 deaths worldwide were attributable to secondhand smoke (about 1.0% of all worldwide deaths), with children accounting for 28%. Pooling 57 studies, comparing people exposed to secondhand smoke with unexposed individuals yielded an odds ratio of approximately 1.28 for hypertension. The odds ratio was 1.39 for heart disease, 1.50 for myocardial infarction, and 1.36 for stroke. The risk from exposure at home was higher than from exposure outside the home.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet：「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」，「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research：57 项研究，「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Start with the home, because people spend more time there, and pooled studies show that secondhand smoke exposure at home is more harmful than outside the home. Children bear 61% of the total disease burden caused by secondhand smoke. For quitting smoking yourself, see Item 1 in this section.

### 3. Don't Rely on Willpower Alone to Quit Smoking; Get Medication First: Varenicline Can More Than Double Your Success Rate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and gum are over-the-counter medications that can be bought right at pharmacy counters. A full course lasts 8 to 12 weeks and costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs that require a visit to a smoking cessation clinic or a pulmonology department. This expense roughly balances out against the money saved on cigarettes over the same period.
- In plain terms: People taking varenicline succeed in quitting at more than double the rate of those taking a placebo. Those using replacement aids like nicotine patches or gum succeed roughly half again as often as those using no medication. Using a patch together with gum pushes success higher by more than another fifth.
- Benefit: Combining 41 randomized trials with 17,395 participants, the smoking cessation success rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51, indicating the credible range; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline was also superior to bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and to using a single nicotine replacement product alone (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Comparing nicotine replacement therapy to no medication across 133 trials and 64,640 participants, the success rate was about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). Adding a fast-acting form (gum, lozenge) to a patch was about 27% higher than using a single form alone (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37; 16 trials, 12,169 participants; high-certainty evidence). Side effects: compared to placebo, people taking varenicline experienced nausea at about 2.6 times the rate (RR 2.61), insomnia was about 37% more common (RR 1.37), and abnormal dreams were also more frequent. The review notes that nausea is the most common, mostly mild to moderate, and typically subsides over time. The instructions for a US nicotine patch outline a three-step taper: first use a 21 mg patch for 6 weeks, then a 14 mg patch for 2 weeks, and finally a 7 mg patch for 2 weeks before stopping.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>（恶心、失眠的相对风险在全文 Results 的 Adverse events 一节）；Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>；上海市卫生健康委员会 (2021). 选对药物，让戒烟轻松一点：「市场上可见的戒烟药物主要有三种，即尼古丁替代疗法药物、安非他酮、伐尼克兰」，「尼古丁替代疗法药物属于非处方药（OTC），可通过药店柜台购买；而安非他酮、伐尼克兰属于处方药，须到医院戒烟门诊或呼吸内科就诊，凭医师处方经药师调配后才能得到」. <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>；Haleon (2025). NICODERM CQ (nicotine) patch, extended release. DailyMed 说明书：「use according to the following 10-week schedule: STEP 1 … 21 mg patch/day … Weeks 1-6 … 14 mg … Weeks 7-8 … 7 mg … Weeks 9-10」. <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=93b2d1b9-83c1-40b5-b6af-90c38c8d6cef>（贴片减量那一句）
- Notes: Only the three classes above are sold domestically as smoking cessation medications, and only nicotine replacement therapy is over-the-counter. Common reactions to varenicline include nausea, vivid dreams, and poor sleep. Anyone with a psychiatric history should disclose it to their doctor, as recommended by the author. A course of nicotine replacement therapy generally lasts 8 to 12 weeks; patches should be tapered step-by-step according to the package instructions or medical advice before stopping. Medication only eases the discomfort during the initial weeks of withdrawal; it does not resolve situational cravings, so it should be combined with Item 4 (set a quit date) and Item 5 (visit a smoking cessation clinic) in this section.

### 4. Set a quit date, either stopping all at once or cutting down gradually to zero
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. It just means picking a day on the calendar and telling family and coworkers in advance.
- In plain terms: Stopping abruptly or cutting down gradually until you quit completely are both valid options. In one UK study, after six months 22% of those who stopped all at once stayed smoke-free, compared with only 15.5% of those who cut down gradually. However, pooling more studies together shows no clear winner over the long term. If you choose to cut down first, the goal is still zero cigarettes.
- Benefit: In 2016, a UK study randomized 697 smokers into two groups. One group quit abruptly on their quit date, while the other reduced smoking by 75% over the two weeks leading up to the quit date. Both groups received behavioral support from nurses and used nicotine replacement therapy before and after quit day. At week 4, 49.0% (95% CI 43.8–54.2, the confidence interval) in the abrupt group had not relapsed, compared with 39.2% (34.0–44.4) in the gradual group, a relative risk of 0.80 (0.66–0.93). By month 6, 22.0% (18.0–26.6) in the abrupt group and 15.5% (12.0–19.7) in the gradual group had not relapsed, a relative risk of 0.71 (0.46–0.91). Participants who originally preferred cutting down also had lower 4-week success rates than those who preferred stopping all at once (38.3% vs. 52.2%). A 2019 Cochrane review pooled 22 studies and 9,219 people comparing smoking cessation rates after at least 6 months. Comparing cutting down first with stopping abruptly yielded a relative risk (RR) of 1.01 (95% CI 0.87–1.17). The evidence is of moderate certainty; it does not show which approach is better in the long run, nor does it prove they are completely equivalent.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>；Lindson N, Klemperer E, Hong B, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Aveyard P (2019). Smoking reduction interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 9, CD013183. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013183.pub2>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308（提前用尼古丁替代品那一段）. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Contested. While the 2016 trial favored stopping abruptly, it cannot replace the broader synthesis of multiple studies in the 2019 review. The review's estimate still carries uncertainty; the long-term success rate for gradual reduction might be slightly higher or slightly lower. If you choose to cut down gradually, the goal remains quitting completely, not just smoking fewer cigarettes indefinitely. Additionally, both groups in that trial began using nicotine replacement therapy before quit day, a strategy supported by its own evidence: starting early improves quit rates by about 25% compared with waiting until quit day (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44; 9 trials, 4,395 people; moderate-certainty evidence). Evidence supporting pre-treatment does not mean abrupt quitting is always better than gradual reduction.

### 5. Visit a smoking cessation clinic, or call 12320 to check for local quit-smoking services
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Registration fees range from a few to a few dozen yuan per visit. Behavioral support typically involves 4 to 8 sessions, lasting 30 minutes to an hour each, usually totaling under 5 hours. Quitline calls are free.
- In plain terms: Medication and regular check-ins are two different things, but combining them works much better. People who take medication alongside dedicated follow-up quit at nearly double the rate of those who only hear brief advice. Phone calls count too: when quitlines proactively call back multiple times, quit rates are about 25% to 38% higher.
- Benefit: Across 52 trials with 19,488 participants, combining medication with behavioral support increased smoking cessation rates to 1.83 times that of usual care or brief advice (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68–1.98, the credible range; high-quality evidence). In the 43 trials recruited in healthcare settings, it was 1.97 times (1.79–2.18). For telephone counseling alone: among people who called a quitline themselves, receiving multiple proactive callbacks increased quit rates by about 38% compared with receiving only materials or a single call (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19–1.61; 14 trials, 32,484 participants); for smokers who did not call a quitline but received telephone counseling provided by study staff, quit rates were about 25% higher (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15–1.35; 65 trials, 41,233 participants). Both represent moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3>；Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4>；中国疾病预防控制中心 (2021). 「中国戒烟平台」微信小程序正式上线：「在健康中国行动控烟行动工作组指导下中国疾病预防控制中心和世界卫生组织驻华代表处联合制作了中国权威戒烟资源库『中国戒烟平台』微信小程序。小程序内容主要包括：戒烟热线、戒烟门诊详细信息，以及线上戒烟服务资源等」；同一站点页脚标注「健康咨询电话：12320」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Notes: Finding services in China: search for the "China Smoking Cessation Platform" (中国戒烟平台) WeChat mini-program for a national directory of quitlines and cessation clinics, or call 12320 to ask if your area has a clinic. Most of the trials above were conducted outside China, and service intensity varies widely between clinics, so take these figures as directional guidance. For medications to pair with support, see Item 3 in this section (Smoking cessation medications).

### 6. Consider e-cigarettes only if you cannot quit otherwise; never touch them if you do not smoke
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A device plus pods costs several dozen to several hundred yuan per month. In China, only tobacco-flavored products are legally sold, and they can only be purchased in physical shops holding a tobacco monopoly retail license.
- In plain terms: Switching to nicotine e-cigarettes helps about 61% more people quit smoking than traditional nicotine replacement therapies, and this conclusion is based on high-certainty evidence. However, long-term safety data are still lacking, so they cannot be treated as harmless. These trials only involved people actively trying to quit; if you do not smoke, do not touch them.
- Benefit: A living systematic review included 80 randomized controlled trials with 29,861 participants. Compared with nicotine replacement therapy, nicotine e-cigarettes increased quit rates by about 61% (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23–2.12; high-certainty evidence, 11 trials, 4,114 participants). In absolute terms, this translates to about 4 additional people quitting per 100 (ranging from 1 to 7 more). The incidence of serious adverse events was roughly comparable between the two groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02; moderate-certainty evidence). Compared with behavioral support alone or no support, nicotine e-cigarettes increased quit rates by about 75% (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39–2.20; low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11>；国家烟草专卖局 (2022). 电子烟管理办法（国家烟草专卖局公告 2022 年第 1 号，2022 年 5 月 1 日施行）第十八条：从事电子烟零售业务，要向烟草专卖行政主管部门申领烟草专卖零售许可证。第二十二条：「禁止向未成年人出售电子烟产品。」第二十三条第二款：「任何个人、法人或者其他组织不得通过本办法规定的电子烟交易管理平台以外的信息网络销售电子烟产品、雾化物和电子烟用烟碱等。」第二十六条：「禁止销售除烟草口味外的调味电子烟和可自行添加雾化物的电子烟。」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>；World Health Organization (2023-12-14). Urgent action needed to protect children and prevent the uptake of e-cigarettes：「E-cigarettes as consumer products are not shown to be effective for quitting tobacco use at the population level」「it is not recommended that governments permit sale of e-cigarettes as consumer products in pursuit of a cessation objective」「Whilst long-term health effects are not fully understood」. <https://www.who.int/news/item/14-12-2023-urgent-action-needed-to-protect-children-and-prevent-the-uptake-of-e-cigarettes>；Department of Health and Social Care (2023-04-11). Smokers urged to swap cigarettes for vapes in world first scheme：「almost 1 in 5 of all smokers in England will be provided with a vape starter kit alongside behavioural support」. <https://www.gov.uk/government/news/smokers-urged-to-swap-cigarettes-for-vapes-in-world-first-scheme>；最高人民检察院 (2021). 江苏苏州吴江区：办案检察官揭秘披着潮流外衣的新型毒品：「这种“上头”电子烟内含有被国家整类列管的合成大麻素成分」. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>
- Notes: Controversial. In 2023, the World Health Organization (WHO) stated that when sold as consumer products, there is no evidence showing e-cigarettes help populations quit smoking. It does not recommend that governments allow open commercial sales for smoking cessation, suggesting they should be regulated as medicines if used at all. It also emphasized that long-term health consequences remain unclear and raised concerns that children and young people may take up smoking because of them. England took the opposite approach in 2023, distributing e-cigarette starter kits alongside quit counseling to about one million smokers. In China, e-cigarettes are not completely banned, but they are tightly restricted under the Measures for the Administration of Electronic Cigarettes. Only tobacco flavors are permitted; fruit flavors and open-system refillable e-liquids are prohibited. Purchases must be made in brick-and-mortar stores holding a tobacco monopoly retail license. Selling through unauthorized websites, online shops, or social media—outside the unified national e-cigarette transaction management platform—is illegal. Sales to minors are strictly prohibited. Consequently, any fruit-flavored e-cigarettes still available are sold through illicit channels beyond regulatory oversight. This is precisely how "spiked e-cigarettes" laced with synthetic cannabinoids spread (see Section 22, Item 4: do not accept vape pods from others). The systematic review evaluated only regulated nicotine products, not unregulated or illicit e-liquids. In order of priority, try prescription cessation medications first (Item 3 in this section), as their evidence is more robust and they are less expensive.

### 7. Cut out sugary drinks, and switching to sugar-free is not a fix
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing and takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea actually saves money. The hard part is breaking the habit of drinking sweet beverages, including diet ones.
- In plain terms: In US studies, people who had 2 or more sugary drinks a day had about a fifth higher risk of death compared to those who rarely drank them. Having 1 to 2 a day was also over a tenth higher. Switching to diet drinks showed no benefit either: people who had 2 or more glasses of artificially sweetened drinks a day actually had about a quarter higher risk.
- Benefit: Two large US cohort studies (observational, no randomized groups) followed roughly 118,000 people with 36,000 deaths. Compared to drinking less than 1 serving per month, drinking 2 or more servings per day was associated with an approximately 21% higher risk of death (HR 1.21); drinking 1–2 servings per day was about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC cohort study (observational, no randomized groups) included 450,000 people across 10 countries. Comparing those who drank 2 or more glasses per day with those who drank less than 1 glass per month: sugar-sweetened soft drinks were associated with about an 8% higher risk (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16); artificially sweetened drinks (diet versions) were about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35); and all soft drinks combined were about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Malik VS 等 (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: The controversy centers on sugar-free drinks. The association for artificially sweetened beverages was actually stronger than for sugary ones, which is very likely reverse causation: people who are already overweight or have developed diabetes are more likely to switch to diet versions. So do not use this to claim that artificial sweeteners are more toxic. However, it at least shows there is no evidence supporting the claim that "switching to sugar-free makes everything fine." Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea is the safest bet. In addition, these studies were merely observational cohorts without randomized trials; people who consume more sweet drinks tend to have poorer overall lifestyles to begin with, so the numbers may be somewhat inflated.

### 8. Do not chew betel nut
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time; it also saves the money spent on buying betel nut. The hard part is breaking the chewing habit.
- In plain terms: People who chew betel nut have about a 20% higher risk of dying over the same period than those who do not. The risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is about 50% higher. Oral cancer and esophageal cancer risks come on top of that.
- Benefit: Pooling 17 Asian studies covering 388,000 people, betel nut chewers had about a 21% higher risk of death than non-chewers (relative risk 1.21, P = 0.02); the mortality analysis accounted for 179,600 of those participants. The risk of developing diabetes was about 47% higher (1.47), and metabolic syndrome was about 51% higher (1.51). Betel nut itself is also an established risk factor for oral and esophageal cancers. Oral cancer: pooling 50 studies, in the Indian subcontinent, chewing betel nut without tobacco carried about 2.56 times the risk of oral and oropharyngeal cancer compared to non-chewers (95% CI 2.00–3.28); with tobacco, about 7.74 times (5.38–11.13). In Taiwan, chewing betel quid without tobacco carried about 10.98 times the risk (4.86–24.84). In both India and Taiwan, higher daily consumption and longer duration of chewing meant higher risk. Esophageal cancer: pooling 12 Asian studies, betel nut chewers had an odds ratio of about 3.05 (2.41–3.87) for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. For those who both chewed betel nut and smoked, the odds ratio was about 6.79 (4.71–9.79). Because esophageal squamous cell carcinoma is an uncommon outcome, the odds ratio approximates relative risk. After quitting: pooling 13 studies, those who quit chewing betel quid with tobacco had about a 34% lower risk of head and neck cancer than current chewers (RR 0.66, 0.54–0.81); those who quit quid without tobacco had about a 27% lower risk (RR 0.73, 0.56–0.95). Quitting for 20 years or more lowered the risk by about 63% (RR 0.37, 0.22–0.61).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>；Guha N, Warnakulasuriya S, Vlaanderen J, Straif K (2014). Betel quid chewing and the risk of oral and oropharyngeal cancers: a meta-analysis with implications for cancer control. International Journal of Cancer, 135(6), 1433-1443. <https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.28643>；Akhtar S (2013). Areca nut chewing and esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma risk in Asians: a meta-analysis of case-control studies. Cancer Causes & Control, 24(2), 257-265. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10552-012-0113-9>；Shah K, Boffetta P, Seyyedsalehi MS (2025). Reversal of head and neck cancer risk after the cessation of use of smokeless tobacco and betel quid use: meta-analysis. - Notes: The more years someone chews and the more they chew per day, the higher the risk of oral cancer. After quitting, the risk gradually decreases over time. In Indian studies, betel quid with tobacco was more dangerous than without tobacco. In Taiwan, most chewing involves quid without tobacco, and the risk remains very high.

### 9. Switch your household salt to low-sodium salt (potassium-enriched salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan more per bag than regular salt. Just grab it when grocery shopping, taking no extra time. The taste is virtually identical.
- In plain terms: In rural China, people who had a prior stroke or were 60 or older with high blood pressure (hypertension) switched their household salt to low-sodium salt. Followed for nearly five years, their chance of dying was about 12% lower, and their chance of having a stroke was about 14% lower.
- Benefit: A randomized controlled trial conducted in rural China with 20,995 participants—all of whom had had a prior stroke or were 60 or older with hypertension—followed subjects for 4.74 years. Results: the group using low-sodium salt had about a 12% lower risk of death than the regular-salt group (RR 0.88). Stroke was about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events were about 13% lower (RR 0.87). There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups in episodes of high blood potassium (hyperkalemia).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>；O'Donnell M 等 (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>（争议方 PURE）
- Notes: Controversy. The PURE study only tracked an observational cohort without random assignment. It reported that people excreting less than 3 g of sodium per day had about a 27% higher risk of death combined with cardiovascular events, while those excreting more than 7 g per day had about a 15% higher risk. According to its findings, both too little and too much sodium are harmful, and a moderate intake is best. However, in the rural China randomized trial cited here, low-sodium salt only replaced part of the sodium and did not suppress intake that low. In addition, note that the trial participants were high-risk older adults; healthy young people would derive far less benefit from switching salt. Anyone with impaired kidney function or taking potassium-sparing medications should consult a doctor before switching.

### 10. Brush thoroughly, clean between your teeth daily, and see a dentist if you lose teeth
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Dental floss or interdental brushes cost a few tens of yuan a year; professional dental cleanings cost 100 to 200 yuan per visit. It takes an extra two to three minutes a day. The hardest part is making interdental cleaning a daily habit. If you cannot clean well with a manual toothbrush, switch to an electric toothbrush, which costs a few hundred yuan, and replace the brush heads regularly.
- In plain terms: In a Japanese study, people who used dental floss or interdental brushes had about an 11% lower risk of dying. Older adults who lost all their teeth or had fewer than 20 teeth left also had a noticeably higher risk of dying. Electric toothbrushes remove roughly 11% to 21% more plaque than manual brushes and cause slightly less gum inflammation, though it is still unclear how much this difference really matters for overall health.
- Benefit: A Japanese study tracked 9,676 people for 6 years. Those who used interdental cleaning tools had about an 11% lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.89). Those who used tongue cleaners had about a 23% lower risk (0.77). In addition, a pooled analysis of multiple studies among community-dwelling older adults found that for those with no teeth at all, the odds ratio for death was approximately 1.87 (95% CI 1.35–2.59, the credible range). For those with fewer than 20 teeth, the odds ratio was approximately 2.04 (1.67–2.49). An odds ratio cannot be read directly as a multiple of the probability of death. Comparing electric to manual brushes: pooled results from 51 randomized trials showed that after 1 to 3 months of use, electric brushes reduced plaque by about 11%, and after more than 3 months, by about 21%. Gingivitis was reduced by roughly 6% and 11%, respectively. The review authors noted it is still unclear how important these differences are for overall health.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>；Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>；Yaacob M, Worthington HV, Deacon SA, et al. (2014). Powered versus manual toothbrushing for oral health. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002281.pub3>；Deacon SA, Glenny AM, Deery C, et al. (2010). Different powered toothbrushes for plaque control and gingival health. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004971.pub2>
- Notes: Controversial. These studies are observational tracking only. People with poor oral health often have worse overall health and lower socioeconomic status as well. It is unclear whether poor teeth drag down the body or poor general health takes a toll on teeth. However, the cost of preventive care is extremely low, and periodontitis and missing teeth directly impair eating. Among electric toothbrushes, oscillating-rotating round brush heads have been studied the most (27 trials); compared with manual brushes, they showed clear reductions in plaque and gingivitis in both the short and long term. No study has tested whether replacing missing teeth reduces mortality. Recommending tooth replacement because missing teeth impairs eating is the author's clinical judgment. As for which type of electric toothbrush is superior, independent reviews reached no conclusion due to too few trials. For how much a dental implant costs and which dental procedures are covered by yibao (basic medical insurance), see Section 24, Item 12 ("Ask for an all-inclusive price before getting dental implants").

### 11. Walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day, or accumulate 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Counted by step count, it takes 60–90 minutes a day, which can be split across commutes and errands. Counted by exercise time, it takes 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is sticking with it over the long term.
- In plain terms: People who walk around 7,800 steps a day have about a 45% lower risk of death than those who only take 3,500 steps. Counting by exercise time shows the same thing: 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week lowers the risk by about 30% compared with being inactive. Beyond a certain point, the benefit levels off; for people aged 60 and older, 6,000–8,000 steps are enough. Meeting either of the two targets is plenty.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 cohort studies (observational, tracking without intervention), covering 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths: participants were divided into four tiers by daily step count, with the lowest tier having a median of 3,553 steps/day. The other three tiers had medians of 5,801, 7,842, and 10,901 steps/day. Compared with the lowest tier, mortality risk in these three tiers was lower by about 40%, 45%, and 53% (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). For people aged 60 and older, the benefit leveled off after 6,000–8,000 steps/day. For those under 60, it leveled off after 8,000–10,000 steps/day. Another meta-analysis showed benefits beginning at roughly 3,867 steps/day; each additional 1,000 steps/day was associated with an approximately 15% reduction in mortality risk. Counted by exercise time: pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, tracking without intervention), people meeting 1–2 times the minimum guideline had an approximately 31% lower mortality risk than inactive individuals (HR 0.69). This volume is 7.5–15 MET·h/week, roughly equivalent to 150–300 minutes of brisk walking per week. Those below the minimum guideline still had an approximately 20% lower risk (HR 0.80). Reaching 3–5 times the guideline reduced risk by about 39% (HR 0.61), representing the maximum reduction; exceeding this showed no further reduction, but caused no harm (>10 times was HR 0.69). Another pooled analysis using accelerometers found that the top quartile of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity had an approximately 48% lower mortality risk than the lowest quartile (HR 0.52). Accelerometers are wearable devices that physically measure activity volume. This pooled analysis included 8 studies with 36,383 participants (mean age 62.6 years, median follow-up only 5.8 years). When the authors re-analyzed after excluding deaths within the first two years, the results remained largely unchanged, though they acknowledged that reverse causation ("moving less because one is already sick") may still introduce bias.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Paluch AE 等 (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M 等 (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>；Arem H 等 (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U 等 (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Step counts and exercise duration describe the same thing using different metrics; meeting either target is enough. Increasing from 4,000 to 7,000 steps offers the biggest payoff; there is no need to obsess over reaching 10,000 steps. Note that these are observational tracking studies: the least active group inevitably includes people who were already sick and unable to walk much. They moved less because their health was already failing, rather than dying early purely because they were inactive, so the calculated gaps are often overstated. Studies using accelerometers to physically measure activity had shorter follow-ups and older participants, so "HR 0.52, about 48% lower" may be overstated—this is this book's inference. Nevertheless, the trend that moving more is better remains clear.

### 12. Check blood lipids regularly in adulthood (every 2 to 5 years before age 40, annually from age 40); take prescribed blood pressure and lipid-lowering medications regularly without stopping on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bulk-procured blood pressure medications and statins cost a few to several dozen yuan per month. Taking a pill once a day takes almost no time. The hard part is taking them consistently and not stopping just because you feel fine. A lipid panel requires one vial of drawn blood. Adults aged 65 and older receive a free annual physical exam that includes a lipid test (see Chapter 6, Item 18 on the universal checkup package).
- In plain terms: For every 10 mmHg (millimeters of mercury) drop in systolic blood pressure (the top number), the risk of death drops by about 13% and stroke by about 27%. When statins lower "bad" LDL cholesterol by 1.0 mmol/L (millimoles per liter), the risk of death drops by about 10%. People who take their medications consistently have an approximately 29% to 45% lower risk of death than those who frequently skip doses. Check blood lipids every 2 to 5 years before age 40, and every year starting at age 40.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple randomized controlled trials shows that for every 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure (the upper number in a blood pressure reading), the risk of death is about 13% lower (RR 0.87), major cardiovascular events are about 20% lower (RR 0.80), stroke is about 27% lower (RR 0.73), and heart failure is about 28% lower (RR 0.72). Statins lower low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL). For every 1.0 mmol/L reduction in LDL, the risk of death is about 10% lower (RR 0.90), and major vascular events are about 22% lower (RR 0.78). In addition, pooling observational cohort studies (tracking without randomization) shows that individuals with medication adherence of ≥80% have a lower risk of death compared with those who frequently miss doses: about 45% lower for statins (RR 0.55) and about 29% lower for antihypertensive medications (RR 0.71). Regarding screening frequency, the Healthy China Action guidelines recommend that individuals under 40 with normal blood lipids be tested once every 2 to 5 years; individuals aged 40 and older should be tested at least once a year; and individuals at high risk for cardiovascular disease should be tested every 6 months. The 2023 Chinese Guidelines for Lipid Management keep the same recommendations for before and after age 40, but adjust the recommendation for high-risk individuals to testing based on individual clinical need.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R 等 (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>；健康中国行动推进委员会 (2019). 健康中国行动（2019—2030年），（十一）心脑血管疾病防治行动（湖南省卫生健康委员会转载）. <https://wjw.hunan.gov.cn/wjw/ztzl/knowledge/tzggzcfg/202003/t20200303_11359594.html>；Joint Committee on the Chinese Guidelines for Lipid Management (2023). 中国血脂管理指南（2023 年）. 中华心血管病杂志, 51(3), 221-255. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.cn112148-20230119-00038>；Li JJ 等 (2023). 2023 Chinese guideline for lipid management. - Notes: This advice applies only to people whose doctor has determined they need medication; healthy individuals do not need to take them. The testing intervals for blood lipids are official public health recommendations; no clinical trials have directly compared which screening frequency works best. If a family member has familial hypercholesterolemia (an inherited condition causing exceptionally high cholesterol), their first- and second-degree relatives should all have their blood lipids tested. Children should also be screened if either parent has a total cholesterol level of ≥6.2 mmol/L, or if a first- or second-degree relative had a heart attack, stroke, or sudden cardiac death before age 55 in men or before age 65 in women. The figures on "adhering to medication" come from observational cohort studies; people who take their pills faithfully tend to be more disciplined overall, which inflates the apparent benefit. The earlier figures from randomized controlled trials are much more reliable. Blood glucose control is not listed as an independent item in this section because the evidence that lowering blood glucose further reduces overall mortality is not as consistent as it is for blood pressure and lipids. Those with hypertension or diabetes should have their kidneys screened annually (see Chapter 16, Item 13 on the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio).

### 13. Sleep around 7 hours a night on a consistent schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. You need to set aside time to sleep, which for most people means trading phone scrolling for sleep. The hardest part is sticking to consistent bedtimes and wake-up times.
- In plain terms: People who regularly sleep under 7 hours have about a 12% higher risk of dying over the same follow-up period. The more irregular your schedule, the higher the risk: compared with the most erratic sleepers, other groups had a 20% to 48% lower risk of death. How consistent your schedule is predicts mortality risk even better than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Pooling 16 cohort studies (observational, no random assignment), covering 1.38 million people and 113,000 deaths: short sleepers had about a 12% higher risk of death (RR 1.12), while long sleepers had about a 30% higher risk (RR 1.30). Another meta-analysis categorized by sleep duration showed that the 7-hour group had the lowest risk. For sleep under 7 hours, each 1-hour reduction was associated with about a 6% higher risk (RR 1.06). For sleep over 7 hours, each 1-hour increase was associated with about a 13% higher risk (RR 1.13). The UK Biobank collected wrist-worn accelerometer data from about 61,000 people (observational, no random assignment) and divided participants into five quintiles based on sleep regularity. The four more regular groups had a 20%–48% lower risk of death than the most irregular group. Sleep regularity predicted mortality even better than sleep duration. Another UK Biobank study tracked sleep timing with wrist accelerometers in 51,562 people, during which 3,853 developed new cardiovascular disease. Social jetlag refers to the difference in the midpoint of sleep between workdays and weekends. People with social jetlag of 2 hours or more had about a 30% higher risk of cardiovascular disease (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). This association held true even among people with normal sleep durations.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Cappuccio FP 等 (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J 等 (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP 等 (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>；Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: The things to fix are sleeping too little and having an irregular schedule; there is no need to deliberately cut your sleep short. The higher risk seen in long sleepers is mostly reverse causation: depression, chronic illnesses, and sleep apnea cause people to sleep more. In addition, the benefit of a regular schedule is currently supported by two cohort studies, both observational with no random assignment, which on their own count as Grade B evidence. For how to make up for staying up late, see item 38 in this section ('Make up for a late night the next evening'). For how to treat chronic insomnia, see Section 3, item 26 ('Treat chronic insomnia with cognitive behavioral therapy first').

### 14. Play racket sports regularly, like tennis, badminton, or table tennis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A court fee of several tens of yuan per session. About 2 hours per week. The hard part is coordinating schedules, booking a court, and sticking to it every week.
- In plain terms: People who regularly play tennis, badminton, or table tennis have about half the overall death risk of those who do not, and their risk of cardiovascular death is about 56% lower. Swimmers have about a 28% lower death risk. In these data, running and soccer showed no clear difference.
- Benefit: A UK tracking study of 80,300 people compared participants who engaged in specific sports against those who did not. Those who played racket sports had about a 47% lower death risk (hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.69). Their risk of cardiovascular death was about 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports included tennis, badminton, and table tennis. For swimmers, the two numbers were 0.72 (about a 28% lower death risk) and 0.59; for aerobics, 0.73 and 0.64; and for cycling, 0.85 for all-cause death. Running and soccer showed no statistically significant differences.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes: Debated. Observational studies like this only track outcomes over time. People who play racket sports may already be healthier and have richer social lives. The lack of an observed difference in the running group also suggests that who chooses which sport may be driving the results. Do not conclude from this entry that running is useless; this section includes other entries based on total exercise volume.

### 15. Build up four to five minutes a day of short, vigorous bursts like climbing stairs or walking briskly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and requires no extra workout time. The challenge is remembering to take the stairs and walk faster instead of taking the easy way out.
- In plain terms: For people who never exercise, just 3 short bursts of vigorous effort a day, lasting 1–2 minutes each, are tied to about a 40% lower risk of dying and nearly half the risk of cardiovascular death compared with none at all. Vigorous effort means everyday activities like climbing stairs or hurrying somewhere on foot.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study tracked 25,200 non-exercisers for an average of 6.9 years, during which 852 people died. For those who logged 3 bursts of incidental vigorous physical activity per day lasting 1–2 minutes each, all-cause mortality and cancer mortality dropped by 38%–40%, and cardiovascular mortality dropped by 48%–49%, compared to those with none. For those accumulating 4.4 minutes of such activity per day, all-cause and cancer mortality dropped by 26%–30%, and cardiovascular mortality dropped by 32%–34%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Contested. This study used wearable devices to measure physical activity directly, which is far more reliable than asking people to recall habits on questionnaires. Still, it was an observational follow-up rather than a controlled trial, and followed participants for only 6.9 years. The participants were all non-exercisers, so this does not necessarily apply to people who already work out regularly.

### 16. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training per week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and push-ups cost nothing. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each session. The hard part is fitting it into your weekly schedule.
- In plain terms: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week have a 10%–20% lower risk of death than those who do none. Doing more does not add extra benefit. Combining strength training with aerobic exercise works best.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple observational follow-up studies (tracking without intervention): people who do strength training have a 10%–17% lower risk of death compared with those who do not. More is not necessarily better: the largest risk reduction occurs at around 30–60 minutes per week, reaching about 10%–20%, with no extra benefit beyond that. People who do both strength training and aerobic exercise have an even lower risk of death than those who do neither.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Momma H 等 (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: These studies are observational tracking, and exercise amounts were self-reported by participants. Evidence suggesting that "training too much actually worsens outcomes" is very weak, so there is no need to cap training volume out of concern. For older adults, strength training also helps prevent falls and preserve muscle mass, which is covered in Section 1. For people with degenerative knee pain, strengthening thigh muscles works better than arthroscopy; see Section 6, Item 33 (Arthroscopic debridement).

### 17. Break up long stretches of sitting: get up and move around regularly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Standing up and moving around takes very little time. The real challenge is losing track of time once seated, so set a reminder or timer.
- In plain terms: People who sit the most have more than one and a half times the increased risk of dying compared to those who sit the least. Sitting for long uninterrupted stretches adds its own separate risk on top of that. However, getting about 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity each day essentially cancels out the extra risk from prolonged sitting.
- Benefit: A US observational follow-up study used accelerometers to objectively record activity, dividing participants into quartiles based on total daily sedentary time. Those in the highest sedentary quartile had an approximately 163% higher mortality risk than those in the lowest (HR 2.63). When categorized into quartiles by the length of unbroken sitting bouts, those with the longest bouts had an approximately 96% higher risk than those with the shortest (HR 1.96), showing that total sitting time and continuous bout duration are each independently linked to risk. Another meta-analysis pooling observational studies of over one million people found that those who sat for more than 8 hours a day with almost no exercise had an approximately 59% higher mortality risk (HR 1.59) compared to those who sat under 4 hours a day and were the most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per day offset the added risk of prolonged sitting: in the most active group, even with more than 8 hours of sitting a day, the HR was only 1.04, showing no statistically significant difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Diaz KM 等 (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U 等 (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable takeaway is that how much you move matters more than how long you sit; when physical activity is sufficient, the risks of prolonged sitting largely vanish. Note that these are observational follow-up studies, and the group that sat the most included many people who were already in poor health, meaning the HR of 2.63 clearly incorporates reverse causation (being sick leads to sitting more). In addition, in the same pooled analysis, watching television for more than 3 hours a day carried an increased risk across all physical activity levels; being in the most active group merely pushed that threshold up to 5 hours.

### 18. Cut back on processed meat (ham, bacon, sausage, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, or even saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is cutting back on foods you love.
- In plain terms: People who eat the most ham, bacon, and sausage have about twenty percent higher chance of dying over the same period than those who eat the least. Having an extra serving of processed meat every day also raises that chance by about twenty percent.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational tracking without randomized grouping): the group consuming the most processed meat had an approximately 23% higher risk of death than the group consuming the least (RR 1.23). Combined red meat was linked to an approximately 29% higher risk (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat was linked to an approximately 10% higher risk (RR 1.10), but this difference was not statistically significant. Another meta-analysis looked at daily intake: each additional 1 serving of processed meat per day was associated with an approximately 23% higher risk of death (RR 1.23). Each additional 1 serving of red meat per day was associated with an approximately 10% higher risk (RR 1.10).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>；Johnston BC 等 (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621>（争议方 NutriRECS 指南）
- Notes: Controversial. Using the standard GRADE evidence-rating system, the NutriRECS guidelines rated these studies as low-certainty and issued only a weak recommendation to simply keep eating as you currently do. The dispute is about whether the evidence is strong enough, not about the direction: no studies suggest processed meat is beneficial. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect with no statistically significant difference. What truly needs reining in is processed meat.

### 19. Drink less or do not drink
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing, saves money, and takes no time. The only difficulty is the slight awkwardness of turning down a drink in social situations.
- In plain terms: Once you drink more than 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink, the earlier you die. That 100 g of pure alcohol is roughly 2.5 L of beer. At age 40, drinking 100–200 g a week shortens life by about half a year; drinking 200–350 g cuts it by 1–2 years; and drinking more than 350 g cuts it by 4–5 years. For overall health, the optimal amount is zero.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 83 prospective studies tracking 600,000 current drinkers found that the threshold for lowest mortality risk was at or below 100 g of pure alcohol per week. That amount is roughly 2.5 L of 5% beer or 300 mL of 40% spirits. At age 40, drinking 100–200 g per week shortened life expectancy by about 6 months; 200–350 g per week shortened it by 1–2 years; and more than 350 g per week shortened it by 4–5 years (reported as life-years lost). When combining all health harms, the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD 2016) found that the level of alcohol consumption that minimizes overall risk is 0 drinks per week. Another meta-analysis that adjusted for study design biases found that drinking 1.3–24 g per day had a mortality risk about 7% lower than lifetime abstainers (RR 0.93), but this difference was not statistically significant. Mortality was about 19% higher at 45–64 g per day (RR 1.19), and about 35% higher above 65 g per day (RR 1.35).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Wood AM 等 (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J 等 (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>；Di Castelnuovo A 等 (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437>（争议方）
- Notes: Controversy. The position that "light drinking is good for you" comes from a meta-analysis of 34 prospective cohort studies, which reported that light drinking was linked to up to a 17%–18% lower total mortality. It suggested limits of no more than 4 drinks per day for men and 2 drinks for women, within which mortality appeared lower. The counterargument points out that the "non-drinker" comparison groups were contaminated by former drinkers who quit due to illness and people who were already in poor health. Once a meta-analysis controlled for these confounding factors, the protective effect disappeared. The Global Burden of Disease study likewise concluded that 0 is safest. The most sound takeaway: light drinking will not noticeably shorten your life, but heavy drinking definitely will. There is no evidence to support "starting to drink for your health." For practical steps on cutting back, see item 21 of this section (count your drinks first, then see a doctor). Daily heavy drinkers must not quit abruptly on their own; see item 20 (do not quit cold turkey on your own).

### 20. Do not quit cold turkey if you drink daily and get shaky or anxious when you stop
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: An outpatient appointment in psychiatry or addiction medicine costs a few to several dozen yuan. Severe cases may require a hospital stay of a few days.
- In plain terms: People who drink heavily every day can suffer severe withdrawal if they quit abruptly. Mild cases bring shaky hands, sweating, a racing heart, and insomnia; severe cases can cause seizures or delirium tremens, a life-threatening medical emergency. A Norwegian study tracked over 30,000 people and found that among those who suffered delirium tremens, about 8% died each year afterward. If you want to quit, see a doctor first and tell them honestly how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review stated that individuals with chronic heavy alcohol use who suddenly stop drinking or drastically reduce their intake can develop alcohol withdrawal syndrome. Symptoms include tremors, autonomic hyperactivity, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures, and delirium tremens, described as "common and potentially life-threatening" in general medical care. A Norwegian nationwide registry cohort followed 36,287 individuals diagnosed between 2009 and 2015 with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state, or delirium tremens: annual mortality was 8.0% in the delirium tremens group, 5.0% in the withdrawal group, and 3.6% in the alcohol dependence group. The standardized mortality ratio for the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, the credible interval), meaning their risk of death was about 9.8 times that of the age-matched general population.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>；Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to tell if this applies to you: if you drink almost every day, if going a single day without a drink leaves you with hand tremors, sweating, palpitations, or sleeplessness, or if you need a morning drink to feel steady—meeting even one of these signs means you should not stop cold turkey on your own. Standard hospital management uses benzodiazepines to get patients through the first critical days alongside thiamine supplementation (vitamin B1). Grade B was assigned because the Norwegian cohort tracked "what happens long-term to people who developed delirium tremens," rather than "what happens when you quit on your own," so the connection is inferred. The point is not to encourage continued drinking, but to switch to a safe, supervised way to quit. For guidance on how much alcohol per week counts as heavy drinking, see item 19 (Drink less or do not drink); for steps on cutting down, see item 21 (Count your drinks before seeing a doctor).

### 21. If you want to cut back on alcohol, track your weekly drinks first and spend a few minutes talking with a doctor
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Just jot down your drink count each day. Asking during a regular clinic visit costs nothing extra either.
- In plain terms: When a doctor spends a few minutes reviewing how much you drink, explaining the risks, and helping you set a goal, people drink an average of 20 grams less pure alcohol per week a year later—about half a liter of beer. Talking for longer brings no extra benefit. For those who already cannot stop, two medications abroad have been shown to work: for about every 12 people treated, 1 more avoids slipping back into heavy drinking.
- Benefit: Across 34 randomized trials totaling 15,197 participants, people who received brief interventions drank an average of 20 g less pure alcohol per week at one year compared with those receiving minimal or no intervention (95% CI: 12 to 28 g less; moderate-quality evidence). Average baseline consumption across these studies was 244 g per week. A brief intervention was defined as advice or lifestyle counseling totaling no more than 5 sessions and under 60 minutes overall; longer counseling provided no additional benefit. Regarding medications: across 122 randomized trials totaling 22,803 participants, acamprosate allowed 1 additional person to avoid returning to any drinking for every 12 people treated (95% CI: 8 to 26); oral naltrexone at 50 mg daily allowed 1 additional person to avoid returning to heavy drinking for every 12 people treated (8 to 26).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>；Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit magnitude is rated "small" because both bodies of evidence measured consumption amounts rather than mortality, and by the rules of this book, surrogate endpoints receive a "small" rating. Most medication trials were conducted abroad, most participants met diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence, and all trials included concurrent psychosocial interventions; these figures represent added benefits on top of that baseline. Indications and prescription availability for these two medications domestically depend on your doctor and package inserts; do not purchase them online on your own. If withdrawal symptoms have already appeared, see item 20 in this section (do not quit abruptly on your own).

### 22. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Eating 28 grams a day costs around 200–300 yuan a year. Easy to grab and eat, takes no extra time.
- In plain terms: People who regularly eat nuts have about a 20% lower risk of dying compared to those who do not. Eating them just once a week already shows a difference, with about an 11% lower risk. Eating a handful every day shows the clearest benefit.
- Benefit: Two US follow-up studies tracking 119,000 people over more than 3 million person-years (person-years means the number of participants multiplied by the years each was followed). Compared to non-nut eaters: eating nuts less than once a week was associated with an approximately 7% lower mortality risk (hazard ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, the confidence interval); once a week was about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93); 2–4 times a week was about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90); 5–6 times a week was about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91); 7 or more times a week was about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy plain, unsalted nuts. Nuts are fairly calorie-dense, so do not binge on them as casual snacks. Note that these studies simply followed a group of people over time; nut eaters tend to have healthier overall lifestyles, so take these figures with a grain of salt.

### 23. Swap some red meat for fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Zero extra cost and takes no time. This is switching the type of meat you eat, not buying more. The tricky part is adjusting your taste preferences.
- In plain terms: Eating two extra servings of processed or red meat a week is linked to about a 3% higher risk of death. Swapping them for the same amount of fish or poultry shows no noticeable increase. The effect here is on the smaller side for this chapter—worth doing if it is convenient, but nothing to obsess over.
- Benefit: Combining 6 US cohort studies of nearly 30,000 people: two additional servings of processed meat per week were associated with about a 3% higher risk of death (hazard ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05). Two additional servings of unprocessed red meat per week were also linked to about a 3% higher risk (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same intake of poultry showed an HR of 0.99 (0.97–1.02); fish was 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither showed a statistically significant difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The difference from two weekly servings is very small, so do not expect swapping meat to add years to your life. When spending the same money on meat, fish and poultry are simply better default choices.

### 24. Replace some refined grains with whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats, and whole-wheat flour cost slightly more than white rice and refined flour, but take no extra time. The main challenge is adjusting to the texture.
- In plain terms: People who eat an extra 90 g of whole grains a day have about a 17% lower risk of dying over the same follow-up period. That 90 g is roughly 3 servings. The risk continues to drop up to about 210–225 g a day.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational tracking, no randomized groups): eating an extra 90 g of whole grains per day was linked to an approximately 17% lower risk of death (RR 0.83). That 90 g is about 3 servings. This benefit continued to increase up to 210–225 g per day. In another meta-analysis, each additional serving per day was linked to about an 8% lower risk (RR 0.92).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Aune D 等 (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: You do not have to switch entirely to whole grains; swapping half already puts you in the range with the clearest benefits. Keep in mind that these are observational tracking studies: people who eat more whole grains often have healthier habits overall, which inflates the numbers. Findings also varied considerably across studies (I² 83%, where a higher percentage means greater inconsistency between study conclusions).

### 25. Drink tea more than three times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few dozen to a few hundred yuan a year. Brewing a cup takes only a few minutes.
- In plain terms: People who regularly drink tea have about a 15% lower risk of death. Starting from age 50, they live an average of 1.26 years longer and gain an extra 1.41 years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: In a Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR) where half were followed for at least 7.3 years, habitual tea drinkers had about a 15% lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90). Starting from age 50, habitual tea drinkers gained 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and 1.26 more years of life expectancy.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>；茶叶霉菌毒素：Cui P 等 (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830>；Zhou H 等 (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452>；绿茶提取物与肝损伤：Hu J 等 (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Notes: The limitation is that this is an observational follow-up study, and a high proportion of male tea drinkers in China also smoke and drink alcohol. The authors adjusted for some of these confounding factors, but residual bias cannot be fully ruled out. In addition, do not drink tea scalding hot; see item 30 in this chapter for safe drinking temperatures. Some worry about mycotoxins in tea leaves. In domestic testing of 158 dark tea samples, 2 detected aflatoxins, but calculated against regular tea consumption, the intake was well below international acceptable levels. Another study tested 352 tea samples for 16 mycotoxins; only the average ochratoxin A level in dark tea exceeded the regulatory limit. Neither study calculated an actual dietary risk based on standard Chinese tea consumption. Store tea in a dry place and discard any that becomes damp or moldy. Green tea extract capsules are entirely different: swallowing high concentrations of catechins in large single doses can damage the liver, whereas brewed tea does not carry this risk. This recommendation refers strictly to brewed tea; do not substitute it with extract supplements.

### 26. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, keeping total caffeine under 400 mg, without sugar or sweet foam toppings; pregnant women, women at high risk of fractures, and people who get palpitations, anxiety, or insomnia from coffee are excluded
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brewing at home costs 1–2 yuan a day. Making a cup takes just a few minutes of your time. If buying large cups, watch how many milligrams of caffeine you drink in a day.
- In plain terms: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day have about a 17% lower risk of death than non-drinkers. On days when people drink coffee, extra heartbeats from the lower chambers (premature ventricular contractions) increase, and blood pressure in people with hypertension rises for several hours after drinking. Over the years, however, regular coffee drinkers do not develop more new arrhythmias. Caffeine content varies widely from cup to cup, so keep total intake under 400 mg a day.
- Benefit: An umbrella review synthesized 201 meta-analyses whose underlying primary studies were all observational without random assignment. For all-cause mortality, the umbrella review cited a dose-response meta-analysis: compared with non-drinkers, people drinking 3–4 cups a day had a 17% lower risk of death (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88). At 7 cups a day, the reduction narrowed to 10% (relative risk 0.90, 95% CI 0.85–0.96), with no rebound in risk observed. A study from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) tracked 40,725 adults for a median follow-up of 9.8 years, also observational. The study divided coffee drinkers into two patterns. The morning pattern (drinking concentrated between 4:00 and 11:59, with almost none in the afternoon or evening) accounted for 36% of participants. Compared with non-drinkers, their all-cause mortality risk was 12% lower (hazard ratio 0.88, 95% CI 0.81–0.96); after further adjusting for short sleep and sleep difficulties, it was 16% lower (hazard ratio 0.84, 95% CI 0.74–0.95). Cardiovascular mortality risk was 19% lower (hazard ratio 0.81, 95% CI 0.70–0.94); after adjusting for sleep, it was 31% lower (hazard ratio 0.69, 95% CI 0.55–0.87). The all-day pattern (drinking across morning, afternoon, and evening) accounted for 14%, and showed no clear difference in all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.90–1.10). Neither group showed a clear difference in cancer mortality. Daily caffeine limits have official backing. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and Health Canada both set the upper limit for healthy adults at 400 mg per day across all sources combined, including tea, cola, and energy drinks. EFSA also recommends spacing intake throughout the day, with no single dose exceeding 200 mg. Caffeine content per cup varies widely. In EFSA tables, a 200 mL cup of filter coffee contains about 90 mg. In Health Canada tables, an 8 oz (237 mL) cup of brewed coffee averages 135 mg. In US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) tables, a 12 oz (about 355 mL) cup of brewed coffee contains 113–247 mg. Thus, the FDA equates 400 mg to roughly 2 to 3 12-oz cups, while the review equates it to roughly 4 cups. The heart and blood pressure do react in the hours after consumption. A randomized trial had 100 adults wear electrocardiogram monitors for 14 days, randomly assigning them daily via text message to drink caffeinated coffee or avoid caffeine. On coffee days, premature ventricular contractions averaged 154 per day versus 102 on caffeine-free days (rate ratio 1.51, 95% CI 1.18–1.94). Premature atrial contractions were 58 versus 53, showing no clear difference. On coffee days, participants slept 36 minutes less at night. Another meta-analysis looked specifically at patients with hypertension. A single dose of 200–300 mg caffeine raised systolic blood pressure by an average of 8.1 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure by 5.7 mmHg. The increase appeared within one hour and lasted at least 3 hours. In 3 studies where coffee was consumed daily for two weeks, blood pressure was no higher than when abstaining. Long-term, the UK Biobank tracked 386,258 people for an average of 4.5 years, also observationally. Each additional daily cup of coffee was associated with a 3% lower risk of new-onset arrhythmia (hazard ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.96–0.98). An American Heart Association 2026 scientific statement summarized randomized trials in habitual coffee drinkers, finding that caffeinated coffee reduced atrial fibrillation episodes while increasing premature ventricular contractions.
- Evidence grade: A

Neither study compared whether noon or 2:00 PM is a better cutoff. Chapter 3, Item 4 (no caffeine after 2:00 PM) addresses sleep and should be calculated backward from your personal bedtime, which does not conflict with this recommendation. The 400 mg threshold is a safety upper limit, not a required target dose. Decaffeinated coffee offers similar, slightly smaller benefits, so there is no need to drink extra coffee just to reach that number. Pregnant women should not exceed 200 mg per day (EFSA) or 300 mg per day (Health Canada). Women at high risk of fractures are excluded from this advice. People with anxiety or insomnia are also excluded; this is the book authors' clinical judgment, as the umbrella review drew no conclusions on these groups. Those who experience palpitations should cut back or switch to decaf, which is likewise this book's judgment. People with atrial fibrillation do not need to quit coffee universally. In one trial of patients after electrical cardioversion for persistent atrial fibrillation, those drinking about 1 cup of coffee a day had fewer recurrences than abstainers (47% versus 64%). The 2023 US atrial fibrillation guidelines also do not recommend caffeine cessation to prevent episodes, though individuals whose symptoms are triggered by coffee should reduce intake. For other arrhythmias, evidence is lacking, so follow your doctor's advice. Adding sugar did not appear to negate the mortality benefit, but drinks with syrups and sweet foam toppings have not been studied and carry heavy sugar loads, so avoiding them is still recommended. 〔725〕 〔386〕 〔258〕

### 27. Eat 5 Servings (About 400 g) of Fruits and Vegetables Daily
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few to a dozen yuan a day. Takes a little time each day to wash and chop. The hard part is buying them consistently and eating enough every single day.
- In plain terms: Eating an extra 200 g of fruits and vegetables a day is linked to about a 10% lower risk of death. People who eat 5 servings a day have about a 13% lower risk than those who eat only 2. In US studies, eating more than that did not lower the risk any further.
- Benefit: In pooled analyses of cohort studies (observational tracking, not randomized trials), eating an extra 200 g of fruits and vegetables daily was linked to an approximately 10% lower risk of all-cause mortality (RR 0.90), a benefit that extended up to 800 g per day. A meta-analysis combining two large US cohorts and 26 other prospective studies found that people who ate 5 servings daily had about a 13% lower risk of death than those who ate only 2 (HR 0.87). The optimal intake was 2 servings of fruit plus 3 servings of vegetables, with no further risk reduction beyond that level.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Aune D 等 (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD 等 (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: The studies differ on where the benefit caps out: the US study found no further reduction beyond 5 servings per day. In addition, these are observational tracking studies with obvious confounding—people who eat more vegetables tend to have different income, education, and exercise levels. Therefore, a risk reduction of roughly 10% is best viewed as an upper ceiling.

### 28. Cut back on ultra-processed foods (chips, instant noodles, pastries, ready meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money spent—just swapping what you buy. You need to cook for yourself or spend time picking minimally processed ingredients, which takes up some time every day. The hard part is constantly fighting the inertia of "whatever is convenient."
- In plain terms: People who eat the most ultra-processed foods have about 21% higher odds of dying over the same period compared to those who eat the least, and have about 50% higher odds of dying from cardiovascular disease. However, this evidence is rated as fairly uncertain, so take these numbers with a grain of salt.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled multiple meta-analyses, whose underlying primary studies were observational only (tracking diets without randomized assignment). Results: compared to those who ate the least, people who ate the most ultra-processed food had an approximately 21% higher risk of all-cause mortality (RR 1.21) and an approximately 50% higher risk of cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.50). The evidence classifications for these two outcomes were "highly suggestive" and "convincing," respectively. However, under the standard GRADE framework, the certainty of evidence was rated as low or very low.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Lane MM 等 (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes: The debate centers on three points. First, this classification system (NOVA) lumps together foods with vastly different nutritional profiles. Second, it heavily overlaps with sugary drinks and processed meat (Items 7 and 18), making it hard to disentangle which component is actually driving the harm. Third, under the standard GRADE framework, the certainty grade is low. Therefore, once you have already tackled those two habits, how much additional benefit this rule provides remains unclear. The counter-evidence is simply the low certainty rating given by the umbrella review itself; currently, no primary studies reach the opposite conclusion. Tackle Items 7 and 18 first (cut out sugary drinks and eat less processed meat) before worrying about this one.

### 29. Switch from coal and firewood to electricity or gas for cooking and heating
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Rural households need new stoves plus fuel costs, running several hundred to over a thousand yuan a year. It is a one-time switch and takes no ongoing time. Most city residents already use electricity or gas.
- In plain terms: People who cook with coal or firewood have about a 10% higher risk of death; for heating, the risk is about 14% higher. Those who have already switched to electricity or gas have a 13%–33% lower risk of death compared to those still burning coal or firewood.
- Benefit: A prospective cohort study on chronic diseases in China tracked 271,000 adults without cardiovascular disease (observational only, no intervention groups). People who used solid fuels like coal or firewood for cooking had about an 11% higher risk of death than those using electricity or gas (HR 1.11). For heating with solid fuels, the risk was about 14% higher (HR 1.14). Among people who had already switched from solid fuels to clean fuels, the death risk for cooking was about 13% lower (HR 0.87), and for heating it was about 33% lower (HR 0.67), compared to those still burning solid fuels. Outdoor PM2.5 is another factor: pooling 104 cohort studies, every 10 µg/m³ increase in long-term exposure was associated with about an 8% higher risk of death from natural causes (RR 1.08). Natural causes refer to non-accidental deaths from illness.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Yu K 等 (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>；Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: Studies like this are observational follow-ups. Families that switched fuels also tended to be wealthier, so the hazard ratio of 0.67 is confounded by household economic status. As for outdoor PM2.5, what individuals can do is limited—essentially moving, wearing masks, or using air purifiers. There are no studies on air purifiers with mortality as an endpoint, so this section gives no numbers.

### 30. Let hot drinks cool down before drinking; avoid scalding tea, soup, and coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free; just wait two to three extra minutes each time. The hard part is holding back when you are eager for a sip.
- In plain terms: In a study from a high-incidence area for esophageal cancer in Iran, drinking very hot tea carried about 8 times the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (a type of esophageal cancer) compared to drinking warm tea. Even drinking tea that was just "hot" roughly doubled the risk. Drinking it less than two minutes after pouring carried about 5 times the risk compared to waiting more than four minutes.
- Benefit: A study in a high-incidence region of northern Iran compared 300 patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma to 571 cancer-free controls. Compared with drinking warm tea, drinking "hot" tea had an odds ratio (OR) of 2.07 (95% CI 1.28–3.35), while drinking "very hot" tea had an OR of 8.16 (95% CI 3.93–16.9). Drinking tea less than 2 minutes after pouring had an OR of 5.41 (95% CI 2.63–11.1) compared with waiting 4 minutes or longer. Because esophageal squamous cell carcinoma is an uncommon outcome, the odds ratio closely approximates relative risk.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>；Yu C, Tang H, Guo Y, et al. (2018). Hot tea consumption and its interactions with alcohol and tobacco use on the risk for esophageal cancer: a population-based cohort study. Annals of Internal Medicine, 168(7), 489-497. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2000>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classifies beverages consumed at temperatures above 65 °C as Group 2A, meaning probably carcinogenic to humans; the same evaluation concluded that coffee itself is not carcinogenic. A prospective cohort study in China followed 456,000 participants for a median of 9.2 years. Those who drank burning-hot tea and consumed 15 g or more of alcohol per day had the highest risk of esophageal cancer: compared with those who rarely drank tea and had less than 15 g of alcohol daily, the hazard ratio was 5.00 (HR 5.00, 95% CI 3.64–6.88). For those who smoked and drank burning-hot tea daily, the hazard ratio was 2.03 (HR 2.03, 95% CI 1.55–2.67). The authors concluded that scalding tea combined with excessive alcohol intake or smoking substantially increases the risk of esophageal cancer.

### 31. Step outside for some sun during the day—don't stay in the dark all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Ten to twenty minutes a day is plenty, easily covered during your commute or lunch break.
- In plain terms: In a Swedish study, women who deliberately avoided the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter than those with the highest sun exposure. Non-smokers who avoided the sun lived about as long as smokers who got the most sun.
- Benefit: A Swedish study tracked 29,500 women for 20 years. Compared with the group with the highest sun exposure, women who actively avoided the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter. The authors noted that "nonsmokers who avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy similar to smokers in the highest sun exposure group."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: Caveats: Sweden is at a high latitude with limited sunshine, so these findings cannot be applied directly to China. Furthermore, people who avoid the sun may already be frail and less inclined to go outdoors, as the authors speculated. The study authors also noted that women who frequently get sun exposure face a higher risk of skin cancer. Do not stay out until you burn, and use shade or protection under intense midday sun.

### 32. Keep your BMI between 20 and 25, and lose weight if overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Diet and exercise take up time every day. This is the hardest item in this section to stick with: it requires sustained discipline to eat less and move more, and keeping weight off is harder than losing it in the first place.
- In plain terms: Risk of death is lowest with a body mass index (BMI) between 20 and 25. A BMI of 27.5–30 comes with about a 20% higher risk, 30–35 is about 45% higher, and 35–40 is about 94% higher. For East Asians with a BMI above 25, every 5-unit increase raises the risk by about 39%.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of individual participant data from 239 prospective cohort studies (observational only, no intervention). The analysis restricted the sample to three groups: never-smokers, people without chronic diseases at baseline, and those who survived at least 5 years after enrollment. Results: Mortality risk was lowest at a BMI of 20–25. A BMI of 25–27.5 was about 7% higher (HR 1.07); 27.5–30 was about 20% higher (HR 1.20); 30–35 was about 45% higher (HR 1.45); 35–40 was about 94% higher (HR 1.94); and 40–60 was about 176% higher (HR 2.76). For individuals with a BMI above 25, each 5 kg/m² increase was associated with about a 39% higher risk in East Asian populations (HR 1.39).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>；Flegal KM 等 (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905>（争议方）
- Notes: Controversial. One camp argues that being slightly heavier helps people live longer. In their meta-analysis, overweight individuals had about a 6% lower mortality risk, and those with mild obesity had about a 5% lower risk. The disagreement centers on whether to exclude three groups of data: smokers, people with preexisting disease, and the first few years of follow-up after enrollment. The rationale for excluding them is that serious illness often causes people to lose weight first (reverse causation). When the analysis pooling 239 studies excluded these three groups, the risks of being overweight reappeared. Also note that no randomized controlled trial has proven that weight loss itself lowers all-cause mortality. The benefits above come from comparing people of different weights against each other, which does not guarantee that losing weight will reduce risk by that exact amount. If you want to lose weight, you do not need to stress over meal timing; neither eating breakfast nor 16:8 intermittent fasting offers extra benefits; see Section 6, Item 26 (Do not count on breakfast or 16:8 fasting to help manage your weight).

### 33. Eat chili peppers four or more times a week

- Cost: Free, and takes no extra time.
- In plain terms: In an Italian study, people who ate chili peppers four or more times a week had about a 23% lower risk of death compared to those who rarely ate them, and cardiovascular deaths were roughly one-third lower.
- Benefit: In an Italian study of 22,800 people followed for a median of 8.2 years, 1,236 deaths occurred. Those eating chili peppers 4 or more times a week had an approximately 23% lower risk of all-cause mortality (hazard ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90) and about 34% lower cardiovascular mortality (0.66, 0.50–0.86) compared with rare consumers.

- Sources:Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: The main caveat is that this observational study took place within the context of a Mediterranean diet; regular chili consumers may have had an overall diet closer to traditional local patterns. In addition, for people with acid reflux (gastroesophageal reflux disease, GERD), hemorrhoids, or irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), spicy food can aggravate symptoms, so do not force it. ### 34. Have one or two servings of milk or yogurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few dollars a day; takes no extra time.
- In plain terms: People who have more than two servings of milk or yogurt a day have about a 17% lower risk of dying than those who have none.
- Benefit: The PURE cohort study followed 136,000 people across 21 countries for an average of 9.1 years, recording 6,796 deaths. Those who consumed more than two servings of dairy per day had about a 17% lower risk of death compared to non-consumers (hazard ratio 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96), trend P=0.0052.

### 35. No need to quit eggs: up to one a day is fine
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and takes no extra time.
- In plain terms: Eating an extra half a whole egg a day is linked to about a 7% higher risk of death. Replacing that half-egg with egg whites, fish, poultry, or nuts actually lowers mortality risk. However, another set of large studies found no link between eating up to one egg a day and cardiovascular disease, so there is no need to give up eggs.
- Benefit: A US tracking study of 521,000 people recorded 129,000 deaths. Each additional half of a whole egg per day was linked to roughly a 7% higher risk of death (hazard ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08). Replacing that half an egg with equivalent amounts of egg whites, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts and legumes was linked to lower overall mortality, as well as lower mortality from cardiovascular disease, cancer, and respiratory disease. On the other hand, three large US cohort studies totaling 216,000 people followed for up to 32 years found that those eating at least one egg a day had no significant difference in cardiovascular disease risk compared to those eating less than one egg a month (HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.82–1.05). In the same paper, a meta-analysis pooling 33 cohorts and 1.72 million people showed that an extra egg per day did not change cardiovascular risk (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.93–1.03). The study authors concluded that up to one egg per day is not associated with cardiovascular disease.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>；Drouin-Chartier JP, Chen S, Li Y, et al. (2020). Egg consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease: three large prospective US cohort studies, systematic review, and updated meta-analysis. BMJ, 368, m513. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m513>；U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, U.S. Department of Agriculture (2015). 2015–2020 Dietary Guidelines for Americans, Chapter 1：「The Key Recommendation from the 2010 Dietary Guidelines to limit consumption of dietary cholesterol to 300 mg per day is not included in the 2015 edition, but this change does not suggest that dietary cholesterol is no longer important to consider」「individuals should eat as little dietary cholesterol as possible while consuming a healthy eating pattern」. <https://www.dietaryguidelines.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/2015-2020_Dietary_Guidelines.pdf>
- Notes: This remains heavily debated: large long-term cohort studies and pooled meta-analyses have found no link between eating one egg a day and cardiovascular disease. The 2015 edition of the US Dietary Guidelines removed the previous daily limit of 300 mg of dietary cholesterol. However, it also noted that this does not mean dietary cholesterol is unimportant, and people should eat as little dietary cholesterol as possible while eating a healthy diet. Currently, up to one egg a day shows no association with cardiovascular disease. The phrase "up to one a day" in the title is based on this finding; higher intakes have not been directly tested in these studies.

### 36. Take baths if you can, not just showers
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Slightly higher water and electricity bills. Half an hour each time.
- In plain terms: In a Japanese study, people who took baths almost every day had about a 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events and about a 46% lower risk of brain hemorrhage compared with those who bathed less than twice a week.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people aged 40 to 59 followed for 19 years recorded 2,097 cardiovascular events. Compared with taking a bath no more than twice a week, bathing almost every day was associated with an approximately 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events (hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84, a credible range). Coronary heart disease was about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke was about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Brain hemorrhage was about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>；消費者庁. 年末年始、高齢者の事故に注意しましょう（页面没写发布日期，数字引自日本厚生劳动省 2024 年人口动态统计）：「「浴槽内での及び浴槽への転落による溺死及び溺水」による死亡者数は、令和6年に7,776人となっています」「暖かい室内と寒い脱衣所や浴室との温度差などによる急激な血圧の変動や、熱いお湯をはった浴槽内に長くつかることによる体温上昇での意識障害などが挙げられています」. <https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/consumer_safety/caution/caution_084/>
- Notes: The caveat: these findings reflect a Japanese population with an established bathtub culture; many households elsewhere lack the setup to soak every day. In addition, do not make the water too hot or stay in too long. For older adults and people with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, excessive water temperatures and prolonged soaking are dangerous. In Japan, 7,776 people drowned in bathtubs in 2024. According to Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency, common triggers include sharp temperature differences between cold rooms and warm bathrooms in winter, and soaking in hot water for too long. Never take a bath, visit hot springs, or use a sauna after drinking alcohol; see Section 22, Item 11 (Avoid hot springs after drinking alcohol).

### 37. Keep naps under half an hour and never over an hour; find the root cause if you cannot get through the day without a one- to two-hour nap
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Just set an alarm before you lie down; it takes almost no willpower.
- In plain terms: People who nap for less than an hour show no increased risk of death or chronic disease, and their minds stay sharper. For those who sleep longer than an hour, the risk of coronary heart disease is roughly 30% higher, and risks of diabetes and obesity are about 20% higher. So set an alarm to wake yourself after half an hour. If you cannot get through the day without sleeping for an hour or two, that is a warning sign to get a medical checkup.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses covering 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review systematically reviews existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses. The conclusion: for people napping under 60 minutes, neither all-cause mortality nor chronic disease risks increased, while cognitive improvements were the most pronounced (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00, the credible range). Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes produced the greatest gains in athletic performance (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). For people napping longer than 60 minutes, the risk of coronary heart disease was roughly 30% higher, and risks of diabetes and obesity were about 20% higher. Another study fitted 1,338 older adults aged 56 and above with actigraphy wristwatches to measure actual daytime naps rather than relying on self-reported questionnaires. Over up to 19 years of follow-up, 926 participants died. Each additional hour of daytime napping was associated with an approximately 13% higher risk of death (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). Each additional nap episode per day was linked to a 7% higher risk (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). Morning nappers had a roughly 30% higher risk than those who napped in the early afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Du P, Li J, Hua Z, 等 (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>；Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, 等 (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>；Dashti HS 等 (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes: Controversial. Both studies above are observational and did not randomize participants. People who take long naps are already more likely to suffer from sleep apnea, anemia, hypothyroidism, depression, or other chronic conditions. It is therefore unclear how much of the "higher mortality among long nappers" is driven by napping itself. Causal inference using genetics (Mendelian randomization across 453,000 and 541,000 participants) supports only one finding: more frequent daytime napping is causally linked to slightly higher blood pressure and waist circumference. It found no causal link between napping and coronary heart disease or diabetes. Do not treat "napping less" as a direct way to reduce mortality. The recommendation to "find the root cause" is the author's advice (Grade C): if daytime fatigue forces you into long naps, check your nighttime sleep first, screen for sleep apnea if you snore or gasp for air, and get a complete blood count and thyroid panel. For how power naps boost alertness, see Section 3, Item 11 (10-minute power nap when drowsy in the afternoon). For ideal nighttime sleep duration, see Item 13 in this section (sleep 7 hours each night). For screening and treating snoring and apnea, see Section 3, Item 27 (sleep apnea).

### 38. Catch up on lost sleep the very next night, not over the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Those extra one or two hours of sleep are hours you already crave.
- In plain terms: People who lose sleep and fail to catch up the following night face about a 15% higher risk of death compared to regular sleepers, and about 42% higher if the sleep loss was severe. For those who did catch up the next night, no clear increase in risk was seen. So if you stay up late, head to bed earlier the very next evening.
- Benefit: In the UK Biobank (85,618 participants, mean age 61.8 years, daily sleep tracked with wrist wearables, median follow-up of 8 years), researchers calculated "sleep-debt nights" based on each person's individual sleep need, defined as sleeping at least 2.5 hours less than needed. The night immediately following sleep loss was designated as the "catch-up night"; sleeping more than usual on that night counted as catching up. Participants were categorized into five patterns. Compared with regular sleepers, those who lost sleep without catching up had an approximately 15% higher risk of all-cause mortality (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31). Those with a cumulative sleep debt exceeding 3.5 hours who did not catch up had an approximately 42% higher risk (HR 1.42, 95% CI 1.24–1.63). Neither catch-up group showed a statistically significant increase; for those who caught up after sleep loss, the HR was 1.12 (95% CI 0.98–1.28). Among naturally short sleepers, losing sleep without catch-up was associated with a roughly 19% higher risk (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.01–1.40), and severe sleep loss without catch-up was roughly 38% higher (HR 1.38, 95% CI 1.17–1.63). These findings were replicated in another cohort of 4,586 participants from a US health survey.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, 等 (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This was an observational study with no intervention or random assignment. People who failed to catch up on sleep may have been busier or in poorer health to begin with, a bias that cannot be entirely ruled out. "No elevated risk detected in the catch-up groups" simply means no statistically significant difference was found; their point estimates were still above 1, so catching up does not mean you are completely off the hook. Therefore, make up for lost sleep as quickly as possible, but do not use this as an excuse to stay up late routinely. In this study, catching up meant getting extra sleep on the first night right after sleep loss, not saving it all for the weekend. Establishing a weekly routine of staying up on weekdays and sleeping in on weekends is a different issue known as social jetlag, which is itself linked to cardiovascular disease (see Item 13 in this section: sleep around 7 hours a night on a regular schedule). The recommendation to wake up at a fixed time even on weekends (see Section 3, Item 2) does not conflict with this advice: this item encourages going to bed earlier the next evening, not sleeping in until noon the following day.

### 39. Switch away from night shifts as early as you can: cardiovascular risk climbs the longer you stay
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Switching roles or changing jobs may mean earning less and losing night-shift allowances. Job hunting takes time, and making the leap is hard. If you factor this in when choosing a job in the first place, the cost is zero.
- In plain terms: People who work night shifts have about 13% more cardiovascular disease and about 27% more cardiovascular deaths than those who work day shifts. The risk keeps climbing the longer you stay: for every 5 additional years of night shifts, cardiovascular disease increases by another 7% or so. Counted in years, the toll adds up, so switch away as early as you can.
- Benefit: Pooling 23 cohort studies (observational tracking only, no randomized grouping): night-shift workers had an approximately 13% higher risk of cardiovascular events (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16) and an approximately 27% higher risk of cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.18–1.36). By duration: each additional 5 years of night-shift work was associated with an approximately 7% increase in cardiovascular disease incidence (RR 1.07, 95% CI 1.04–1.09) and an approximately 5% increase in cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.05, 95% CI 1.03–1.06). By specific disease: coronary heart disease incidence was about 22% higher (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.16–1.28), coronary heart disease mortality was about 22% higher (RR 1.22, 95% CI 1.10–1.36), ischemic heart disease mortality was about 39% higher (RR 1.39, 95% CI 1.06–1.84), and stroke mortality was about 49% higher (RR 1.49, 95% CI 1.04–2.12). Stroke incidence was not statistically significant (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, 等 (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. - Notes: These studies are purely observational. People who work night shifts inherently differ from daytime workers in smoking habits, body weight, and income, so confounding cannot be adjusted away completely. Evidence for night shifts causing cancer is far weaker than popular claims suggest. In a meta-analysis of 12 studies covering 12,132 breast cancer cases, people who had ever worked night shifts had only a 5% higher risk, with the confidence interval crossing 1 (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.96–1.14). The subgroup with over 20 years of night shifts had a roughly 25% higher risk (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.01–1.55), but after sensitivity analysis by the authors, this association lost statistical significance; after adjusting for potential publication bias, the estimate approached 1, leading the authors to conclude that the link is "far from established." Another study tracking Chinese men for 16.1 years with 8,202 cancer cases found no association between night shifts and overall cancer or most common cancers; only pancreatic cancer in the 11- to 20-year cumulative category showed a roughly 59% increase (HR 1.59, 95% CI 1.09–2.31). Therefore, this recommendation is calculated strictly on cardiovascular risks, not cancer. This book does not argue that everyone must abandon night-shift jobs; it simply lays out the cumulative math over time so you can decide for yourself. For those currently working nights, fix what you can control first: on quitting smoking, see item 1 in this chapter (Quit smoking as early as possible); on blood pressure and blood lipids, see item 12 (Take prescribed medication regularly for hypertension and dyslipidemia); on catching up on sleep after night shifts, see item 38 (Catch up on sleep the next evening after staying up late). Two additional strategies are worth trying: consolidating food intake during daytime hours, and using bright light combined with strict daytime blackout conditions to shift your circadian clock. The latter has been proven in trials to genuinely shift the biological clock. Under ordinary indoor lighting, the circadian rhythm remained completely unchanged across six consecutive night shifts; switching to 7,000 to 12,000 lux of bright light plus near-total daytime darkness fully inverted it within four days. **Researchers recommend combining all three: bright light at night, strict blackout conditions for daytime sleep, and wearing sunglasses on the morning commute home to block daylight.** In a 50-person simulated night-shift trial, bright night light and sunglasses after work each helped on their own, and the combination yielded the best results. Among those who did not wear sunglasses, the circadian clock only shifted forward and failed to delay, which the authors attributed to daylight blocking the shift on the commute home. In another 67-person trial, among participants whose circadian rhythms naturally ran early, bright light at night fully inverted almost all of them, and darker sunglasses offered no additional shift; for those under ordinary indoor lighting, however, darker sunglasses were progressively more helpful. The endpoints of both strategies were limited to metabolic markers and sleep; no randomized trial has tested whether following them actually lowers cardiovascular risk. On days off, reverting to a daytime routine will shift the clock back again. Dosages, raw figures, additional sources, and how the body perceives time are detailed in [Body clock and night shifts](../../docs/research/生物钟和夜班.md).

### 40. Buy prepackaged cooking oil with an SC license number instead of bulk workshop-pressed peanut oil

- Cost: Just switch where you shop; the price is roughly the same. All it takes is checking the packaging for a food production license number starting with "SC". The hard part is convincing older relatives who are used to buying home-pressed oil from small workshops.

### 41. Cook with vegetable oil instead of lard and butter, don't rotate oils for health, and don't count on flaxseed oil for heart protection
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Little to no extra cost. Vegetable oil is already the staple in most households. The hard part is breaking the habit of cooking with lard or mixing it into rice for flavor.
- In plain terms: Cutting back on saturated fats like lard and butter in favor of vegetable oil lowers heart disease and stroke events by about 17%, though it shows no clear change in overall death risk. Eating more of the main components in soybean or corn oil showed neither benefits nor harms. Consuming extra flaxseed oil also showed no drop in heart disease risk.
- Benefit: A Cochrane review pooled 15 randomized trials with 56,675 participants, each lasting at least two years. Reducing saturated fat lowered combined cardiovascular events by about 17% (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98). All-cause mortality remained virtually unchanged (RR 0.96, 0.90–1.03), as did cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.95, 0.80–1.12). Replacing saturated fat with polyunsaturated fat (the primary component of soybean and corn oils) showed no clear difference compared to replacing it with carbohydrates. Data on replacing it with monounsaturated fat (the main component of canola and olive oils) was too limited to draw conclusions. Another Cochrane review pooled 19 randomized trials with 6,461 participants: higher intake of omega-6 (linoleic acid) showed an RR of 1.00 (0.88–1.12) for all-cause mortality and an RR of 0.97 (0.81–1.15) for cardiovascular events, showing neither benefit nor harm. A third review pooled 86 randomized trials with 162,796 participants: higher intake of plant-based omega-3 (the main component of flaxseed oil and perilla oil) yielded an RR of 1.01 (0.84–1.20) for all-cause mortality and an RR of 1.00 (0.82–1.22) for coronary heart disease events, showing essentially no effect.

- Sources:Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, 等 (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. - Notes: Controversy: Some argue that the high omega-6 content in soybean and corn oils promotes inflammation, so they should be cut back or rotated out. The randomized trials in the omega-6 review above found no harm, though the authors rated the evidence quality as low. Magnitude of benefit is rated moderate based on a 17% reduction in cardiovascular events, with no change in all-cause mortality. ### 42. Turn on the range hood when stir-frying or deep-frying, from lighting the stove until cooking is done
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: If your home already has a range hood, it only adds a small amount of electricity. If not, buying and installing one costs several hundred to over a thousand yuan. The challenge is remembering to turn it on every time you light the stove, and not switching it off early just because it is noisy.
- In plain terms: Among non-smoking Chinese women, rarely or never running a range hood while cooking carries roughly double the risk of lung cancer compared to always using one. Living in a home with poor kitchen ventilation also raises the risk by about 49%, an effect most pronounced in households that burned coal.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of 10 case-control studies among non-smoking Chinese women (comparing lung cancer patients with healthy controls and looking back at past habits) found that exposure to cooking fumes was associated with an odds ratio of about 1.74 (95% CI 1.57–1.94) for lung cancer. A case-control study in Taiwan compared 1,302 non-smoking female lung cancer patients with 1,302 healthy controls; among those who had cooked for more than 10 years, using a range hood during more than two-thirds of their cooking years was associated with an odds ratio of about 0.49 (0.32–0.76) compared with using one less than one-third of the time. Because lung cancer is relatively rare, the odds ratio is close to relative risk. A prospective cohort study in Shanghai tracked 71,000 non-smoking women from 1996 to 2009 and identified 429 cases of lung cancer. Those who reported living in a home with poor kitchen ventilation had an approximately 49% higher risk of lung cancer (HR 1.49, 1.15–1.95). For women with both coal use and poor ventilation for over 20 years, the risk was about 103% higher (HR 2.03, 1.35–3.05). Looking solely at whether coal was used showed no meaningful difference (HR 1.03, 0.84–1.26). The type of cooking oil used also showed no detectable difference.

### 43. If hot flashes and night sweats around menopause disrupt your life, get evaluated by a gynecologist for hormone therapy—do not just endure them or self-medicate, and never use hormones to prevent heart disease

- Cost: A consultation at a gynecology or menopause clinic. The doctor will review your medical history to check for contraindications before prescribing hormones. Once started, you need follow-up visits as directed by your doctor. The hard part is overcoming the fear that "hormones cause cancer on contact," while also resisting the urge to buy them on your own after hearing claims about anti-aging.
- In plain terms: For hot flashes and night sweats around menopause, hormone therapy cuts the number of hot flashes by about 75% compared with a placebo. The trade-off is a slight increase in breast cancer, blood clots, and stroke, adding about 8 extra cases of each per 10,000 women each year. For those who start between ages 50 and 59, overall mortality does not increase. It should not be used to prevent heart disease or dementia.

The severity of hot flashes was also significantly reduced, with an odds ratio of 0.13 (OR 0.13, 0.07–0.23). Women taking placebo also experienced a 57.7% reduction in hot flashes, which underscores why any remedy claiming to relieve hot flashes must be proven against placebo. For risks, data come from the US Women's Health Initiative (WHI) randomized trial. In absolute numbers, this translates to an extra 7 cases of coronary heart disease, 8 cases of stroke, 8 cases of pulmonary embolism, and 8 cases of invasive breast cancer per 10,000 women per year, alongside 6 fewer cases of colorectal cancer and 5 fewer hip fractures. Among women who had undergone a hysterectomy and took estrogen alone, over a cumulative 13-year follow-up, breast cancer was actually about 21% lower (HR 0.79, 0.65–0.97). Combining both trials over 18 years of follow-up showed no change in overall mortality (HR 0.99, 0.94–1.03). Stratified by age at initiation, the group aged 50 to 59 had about a 31% lower overall mortality during the intervention phase (HR 0.69, 0.51–0.94); for ages 60 to 69, it was 1.04 (0.87–1.25), and for ages 70 to 79, it was 1.13 (0.94–1.36), neither being statistically significant. A 2017 Cochrane review focusing specifically on women aged 50 to 59 found that the only significantly elevated risk with combined estrogen-progestin therapy was venous thromboembolism, with an absolute risk under 1 in 500. For women aged 65 and older taking combined therapy, dementia cases over 4 years rose from 9 per 1,000 to 11–30 per 1,000. In 2022, the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommended against using hormone therapy to prevent chronic conditions (Grade D recommendation), concluding that it offers no net benefit.

The 24% reduction in the meta-analysis did not adjust for any factors, and in the studies that accounted for lifestyle habits, the difference vanished. ### 45. If diagnosed with prediabetes (elevated blood sugar not yet reaching diabetes), rely on eating less, losing weight, and moving more to delay diabetes, and recheck blood sugar every six months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Costs nothing; eating less can even save a little money. At least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity like brisk walking per week, which breaks down to about twenty-odd minutes a day. Cut 400 to 500 kcal per day compared to before. The hard part is sticking with it long term and keeping the weight off after losing it. Get blood drawn every six months for a recheck; a fasting blood glucose test costs around a dozen yuan.
- In plain terms: For people with elevated blood sugar that has not yet reached diabetes, eating less, moving more, and losing a little weight can lower the risk of developing diabetes by 30% to 60%. In a 30-year follow-up of China's Da Qing trial, people who did this had about a one-quarter lower risk of death and lived an average of about 1.5 years longer. However, similar trials in the United States and Finland did not see a difference in mortality.
- Benefit: The Da Qing study was a cluster-randomized trial conducted by clinic in Daqing, Heilongjiang. A total of 577 adults with impaired glucose tolerance (a form of prediabetes) were assigned to a control group or one of three intervention groups: diet only, exercise only, or both combined. The intervention lasted 6 years. Over 6 years, the cumulative incidence of diabetes was 67.7% in the control group, 43.8% in the diet group, 41.1% in the exercise group, and 46.0% in the diet-plus-exercise group. After adjusting for differences in baseline body mass index (BMI) and fasting blood glucose, the risk of developing diabetes was lower by about 31%, 46%, and 42% in the three groups, respectively. The reductions were similar between lean and overweight individuals (using a BMI cutoff of 25). At 23 years of follow-up, the intervention groups had about 41% lower cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.36–0.96), about 29% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.71, 0.51–0.99), and about 45% lower diabetes incidence (HR 0.55, 0.40–0.76). At 30 years of follow-up, the median onset of diabetes in the intervention groups was delayed by 3.96 years. Cardiovascular events were lower by about 26% (HR 0.74, 0.59–0.92). Severe microvascular complications of the retina, kidneys, and nerves were lower by about 35% (HR 0.65, 0.45–0.95). Cardiovascular mortality was lower by about 33% (HR 0.67, 0.48–0.94). All-cause mortality was lower by about 26% (HR 0.74, 0.61–0.89). Average life expectancy increased by 1.44 years (0.20–2.68). The US Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) trial randomized 3,234 people with elevated blood glucose. The lifestyle group aimed for at least a 7% weight loss and at least 150 minutes of physical activity per week. Over an average follow-up of 2.8 years, the risk of developing diabetes in the lifestyle group was 58% lower than in the placebo group (95% CI 48%–66%). The metformin group had a 31% lower risk (17%–43%), which was less effective than lifestyle changes. At an average follow-up of 15 years, the lifestyle group still had a 27% lower risk (HR 0.73, 0.65–0.83), though the difference narrowed over time. At a median follow-up of 21 years, all-cause mortality in the lifestyle group showed no difference compared to placebo (HR 1.02, 0.81–1.28), nor did cardiovascular mortality (HR 1.18, 0.77–1.81). The Finnish Diabetes Prevention Study (DPS) randomized 522 overweight individuals with impaired glucose tolerance. Over 4 years, cumulative diabetes incidence was 11% in the intervention group versus 23% in the control group. During the trial period, risk was reduced by 58%. After one-on-one coaching ended, continued follow-up showed a 43% lower risk at a median of 7 years. At an average follow-up of 10.6 years, there was no significant difference in all-cause mortality (HR 0.57, 0.21–1.58) or cardiovascular disease (HR 1.04, 0.72–1.51). Healthy China Action defines prediabetes as a fasting plasma glucose of 6.1 to 7.0 mmol/L (excluding 7.0), or a 2-hour post-load glucose of 7.8 to 11.1 mmol/L (excluding 11.1). It recommends that overweight or obese individuals reduce their BMI to or near 24, or lose at least 7% of their body weight; cut daily dietary intake by at least 400 to 500 kcal; engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week; and check fasting or 2-hour postprandial blood glucose every six months.

### 46. Throw away the entire bag if peanuts, corn, nuts, rice, or flour get moldy instead of picking through them; spit out any bitter nut immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Throwing out a bag of peanuts, nuts, rice, or flour costs a few to several tens of yuan. Store grains and nuts in a dry, well-ventilated, and cool place, buy small packages, and avoid stockpiling. The hard part is the reluctance to waste food, especially convincing older family members.
- In plain terms: Mold on peanuts and corn produces a fungal toxin (aflatoxin) that causes liver cancer. In a Shanghai study, people with this toxin detected in their urine faced about 3.4 times the risk of liver cancer compared with those without it. For people with hepatitis B who also had the toxin, the risk was about 59.4 times as high as for those with neither. Normal cooking—stir-frying, boiling, or deep-frying—can barely destroy this toxin.
- Benefit: Aflatoxins are classified as Group 1 carcinogens (proven to cause cancer in humans) by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, specifically causing liver cancer. They most commonly contaminate peanuts and corn, and standard cooking methods like stir-frying, boiling, or deep-frying barely break them down. A Shanghai cohort study followed 18,244 middle-aged men starting in 1986, collecting a single urine sample from each at enrollment. Over approximately 70,000 person-years of follow-up, 55 cases of liver cancer were identified. A nested case-control comparison matched 50 of these cases against 267 controls from the same cohort who remained cancer-free. Having detectable urinary aflatoxin biomarkers was associated with about 3.4 times the risk of liver cancer (relative risk, RR 3.4, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.1–10.0). Testing positive for hepatitis B surface antigen carried about 7.3 times the risk (2.2–24.4). Having both risk factors carried about 59.4 times the risk (16.6–212.0) compared with having neither. An earlier analysis of the same cohort adjusting for hepatitis B, smoking, alcohol use, and education found about 3.8 times the risk with detectable toxin (1.2–12.2). Qidong, Jiangsu was historically a high-incidence area for liver cancer; starting in the 1980s, the local staple switched from corn to rice. The median serum toxin biomarker level among residents dropped from 19.3 pg/mg in 1989 to undetectable (below 0.5) by 2009. Liver cancer mortality among residents under age 35 born between the 1960s and 1980s fell by more than half. These birth cohorts were born before widespread universal neonatal hepatitis B vaccination. The authors estimated that approximately 65% of the drop in liver cancer deaths could be attributed to reduced aflatoxin intake, with 83% of that benefit occurring in hepatitis B carriers. Globally, it is estimated that for the same amount of toxin ingested, the carcinogenic potency in hepatitis B carriers is roughly 30 times higher than in non-carriers. In China, an estimated 5,300 to 14,400 liver cancer cases per year among hepatitis B carriers and 1,990 to 4,430 cases among non-carriers are attributable to aflatoxin exposure.
- Evidence grade: B

### 47. Avoid raw freshwater fish and shrimp dishes, get screened for liver flukes if you have eaten them regularly, and take praziquantel as prescribed if infected
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Not eating raw fish costs nothing; the difficulty lies in changing lifelong tastes and turning down dishes during social meals. For regular consumers, getting a stool test at a hospital to check for fluke eggs carries costs that vary by region. If infected, take praziquantel at the dosage prescribed by your doctor, usually for 1 to 2 days.
- In plain terms: Eating raw freshwater fish and shrimp can cause liver fluke infection. It is a proven cancer-causing parasite, raising the risk of bile duct cancer to four to five times that of someone uninfected. Vinegar, alcohol, and wasabi cannot reliably kill it. If infected, taking medication for two days cures over nine out of ten cases.
- Benefit: *Clonorchis sinensis* is commonly known as the liver fluke. In 2009, the World Health Organization's International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) concluded that chronic infection is carcinogenic to humans, causing bile duct cancer (cholangiocarcinoma), and classified it as Group 1 (previously classified as Group 2A, probably carcinogenic, in 1994). Pooled published studies show that people infected with liver flukes have approximately 4.8 times the risk of developing bile duct cancer (relative risk 4.8, 95% CI 2.8–8.4); this estimate also includes another liver fluke species found in Southeast Asia. In the same review, hepatitis B carried a relative risk of 2.6 and hepatitis C was 1.8, making liver fluke the strongest factor. Another meta-analysis pooling 36 studies found an odds ratio of approximately 4.37 (OR 4.37, 2.84–6.72) for cholangiocarcinoma in case-control studies, and a relative risk of approximately 10.43 (RR 10.43, 2.90–37.47) in cohort studies. For cholangitis and cholecystitis, the relative risk was approximately 7.80 (RR 7.80); for gallstones, approximately 2.42 (RR 2.42); and for hepatocellular carcinoma, the odds ratio was approximately 4.69 (OR 4.69). Cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma are both rare, so the odds ratio and relative risk are comparable. No significant association was observed for liver cirrhosis. Higher infection burdens correlated with higher rates of these conditions. A 2014–2015 national survey found an overall infection rate of 0.47%, representing about 6 million people, with rates exceeding 1% in four provincial regions: Guangdong, Guangxi, Heilongjiang, and Jilin. Extrapolations from 2016–2021 surveillance data estimated an overall rate of about 0.67% nationwide in 2020 (around 9.46 million people), including roughly 8.92% in Guangxi and 2.99% in Guangdong. In 2024, fifteen government departments, including the National Disease Control and Prevention Administration, issued a prevention and control plan using 2018–2022 surveillance site average infection rates as baselines: 8.75% in Guangxi, 4.38% in Jilin, 3.16% in Heilongjiang, and 2.95% in Guangdong. In 2026, the China National Center for Food Safety Risk Assessment cautioned that seasonings such as alcohol, vinegar, and wasabi cannot effectively kill liver flukes; freezing requires at least 7 consecutive days at -20°C or below, and cooking requires the food's core temperature to reach at least 70°C for more than 10 minutes. The IARC also noted that cured, pickled, or smoked freshwater fish can still transmit the parasite. For treatment, a 2022 review of 26 trials involving 3,340 people showed that the WHO-recommended praziquantel regimen of 25 mg/kg per dose, 3 times a day for 2 days, had an estimated cure rate of 98.5% (85.4%–99.9%) against *Clonorchis sinensis*.
