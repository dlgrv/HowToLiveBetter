# 2. Don't Die Slowly

This section includes only chronic risk factors with large effects on all-cause mortality and hard evidence, ranked from highest to lowest value for money. Except where a randomized trial is specifically noted, the numbers come from observational studies; the HRs/RRs contain confounding and reverse causation, so read them as direction and magnitude, not as a discount you are guaranteed to receive by following the advice.
### 1. Quit smoking, and quit as early as possible
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: willpower during withdrawal (weeks to months); money-wise it is a negative cost — a pack a day runs about 20–30 yuan, and quitting saves it.
- In plain terms: smokers live more than ten years less than non-smokers on average; quitting before 40 wipes out about 90% of the excess death risk from continuing to smoke, and the earlier you quit, the more you get back.
- Benefit: US cohort (observational): current smokers live at least 10 years less than never-smokers; quitting before 40 eliminates about 90% of the excess mortality risk from continued smoking, and quitting at ages 25–34, 35–44, and 45–54 adds roughly 10, 9, and 6 years of life respectively (the original reports life-years). Chinese cohort (observational): among urban male smokers in the 2010s, all-cause mortality RR 1.65, rural men RR 1.22; after 10 years of sustained quitting the attributable risk nearly disappears.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Jha P 等 (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z 等 (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>；Oberg M 等 (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>（二手烟那两个数字）
- Notes: Secondhand smoke kills too: worldwide in 2004 about 603,000 people died from secondhand smoke exposure, about 1% of all deaths that year (Oberg M et al. (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>). Avoid other people's smoke even if you don't smoke yourself, and above all keep children away from it. This unit only covers how much good quitting does, not how to do it; the concrete methods are the four units that follow: unit 3 (quit-smoking medication), unit 4 (set a quit date), unit 5 (go to a cessation clinic), and unit 6 (e-cigarettes).

### 2. Don't smoke at home or in the car, and don't let guests smoke there either
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time. The hard part is speaking up to ask family and guests not to smoke indoors.
- In plain terms: about 600,000 people worldwide die each year from breathing other people's smoke, nearly a third of them children; people chronically exposed to secondhand smoke run about 30% higher risks of high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke, and what you breathe at home hurts you more than what you breathe outside.
- Benefit: in 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from secondhand smoke, about 1.0% of all global deaths, of whom 28% were children; in a meta-analysis of 57 studies, people exposed to secondhand smoke had an odds ratio for hypertension of 1.28, heart disease 1.39, myocardial infarction 1.50, and stroke 1.36, with the risk from exposure inside the home higher than outside it.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet：「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」，「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research：57 项研究，「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: The home is the place to police hardest — not only because you spend the most time there, but because the meta-analysis shows the effect of within-home exposure is larger than outside-home exposure. Children bear 61% of the disability-adjusted life years lost to secondhand smoke. For quitting yourself, see unit 1 of this section (quit smoking, and quit as early as possible).

### 3. Don't rely on willpower alone to quit; get the medication first: it more than doubles your odds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and gum are over-the-counter; you can buy them at any pharmacy counter. One course runs 8 to 12 weeks and costs a few hundred to a bit over a thousand yuan. Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs; get them from a smoking cessation clinic or a respiratory department. The money roughly cancels out against the cigarettes you stop buying over the same period.
- In plain terms: Most people can't quit on willpower alone. The share who succeed on varenicline is more than twice the share on a placebo. People using nicotine patches, gum and similar replacements do about 50% better than people using no medication. Adding gum to the patch adds another 20-odd percent.
- Benefit: Pooling 41 randomized trials with 17,395 people, the quit rate on varenicline was 2.32 times that of placebo (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline also beat bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and a single form of nicotine replacement (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Nicotine replacement versus no medication: 133 trials, 64,640 people, quit rates about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). A patch plus one fast-acting form (gum or lozenge) beat a patch alone by about 27% (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37, 16 trials, 12,169 people, high-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>；Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>；上海市卫生健康委员会 (2021). 选对药物，让戒烟轻松一点：「市场上可见的戒烟药物主要有三种，即尼古丁替代疗法药物、安非他酮、伐尼克兰」，「尼古丁替代疗法药物属于非处方药（OTC），可通过药店柜台购买；而安非他酮、伐尼克兰属于处方药，须到医院戒烟门诊或呼吸内科就诊，凭医师处方经药师调配后才能得到」. <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>
- Notes: The three drug classes above are what is sold in China; only nicotine replacement is over the counter. Common reactions to varenicline are nausea, vivid dreams and poor sleep; tell the doctor if you have any psychiatric history. A course of nicotine replacement usually runs 8 to 12 weeks; don't stop it abruptly on your own, taper as the doctor says. Medication only covers the discomfort of withdrawal during those weeks; it does not handle the situations that make you want to smoke, so use it together with unit 4 of this section (set a quit date) and unit 5 (go to a cessation clinic).

### 4. Set a quit date and stop all at once on that day; don't cut down gradually first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. You pick a day on the calendar and tell family and coworkers in advance.
- In plain terms: Many people plan to smoke a few fewer cigarettes a day and taper down to zero. One randomized trial with 697 people compared the two approaches head to head. At six months, 22% of those who quit all at once on a set date had not gone back to smoking, against 15.5% of those who tapered. Even people who would rather have tapered did better when assigned to the abrupt-quit group.
- Benefit: 697 British smokers were randomized to two groups. One stopped all at once on a quit date; the other cut its smoking by 75% over the two weeks before the quit date. Both got behavioral support from nurses and used nicotine replacement before and after the quit date. At week 4, 49.0% of the abrupt group (95% CI 43.8–54.2) had not relapsed versus 39.2% (34.0–44.4) of the tapering group, a relative risk of 0.80 (0.66–0.93). At 6 months the abrupt group was at 22.0% (18.0–26.6) and the tapering group at 15.5% (12.0–19.7), relative risk 0.71 (0.46–0.91). People who preferred tapering anyway had a lower week-4 success rate than those who preferred quitting abruptly (38.3% versus 52.2%).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308（提前用尼古丁替代品那一段）. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Contested. Cochrane used to have a review dedicated to comparing these two methods, concluding they were about the same (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.13, 10 trials, 3,760 people), but that review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best evidence right now is the randomized trial above, and it favors quitting all at once. Note that in that trial both groups started nicotine replacement before the quit date; that practice has evidence of its own: starting early beat starting on the quit day by about 25% (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44, 9 trials, 4,395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). So the sensible schedule is: set the date, put the patch on two weeks ahead, and stop all at once when the day comes.

### 5. Go to a smoking cessation clinic, or call 12320 to ask what quit services exist locally
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A visit costs a few to a few dozen yuan. Behavioral support is usually 4 to 8 sessions of half an hour to an hour, totalling under 5 hours in most cases. Calling the hotline is free.
- In plain terms: Medication and having someone check on you regularly are two different things, and they work best together. People on medication with a dedicated follow-up person quit at nearly twice the rate of people who only heard one brief piece of advice. The phone counts too: quitlines that call you back proactively raise success rates by another 20 to 30 percent.
- Benefit: Pooling 52 trials with 19,488 people, the quit rate in the medication-plus-behavioral-support group was 1.83 times that of usual care or brief advice (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68–1.98; high-quality evidence). In the 43 trials that recruited in healthcare settings it was 1.97 times (1.79–2.18). Phone counseling on its own: among people who had called a quitline themselves, those who received several proactive callbacks did about 38% better than those who only got materials or a single conversation (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19–1.61, 14 trials, 32,484 people); among people who had not called in and were offered phone counseling by the study, about 25% better (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15–1.35, 65 trials, 41,233 people). Both are moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3>；Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4>；中国疾病预防控制中心 (2021). 「中国戒烟平台」微信小程序正式上线：「在健康中国行动控烟行动工作组指导下中国疾病预防控制中心和世界卫生组织驻华代表处联合制作了中国权威戒烟资源库『中国戒烟平台』微信小程序。小程序内容主要包括：戒烟热线、戒烟门诊详细信息，以及线上戒烟服务资源等」；同一站点页脚标注「健康咨询电话：12320」. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Notes: How to find help in China: search WeChat for the mini-program called China Smoking Cessation Platform; it lists quitlines and cessation clinics around the country. You can also call 12320 to ask whether your area has a cessation clinic. Most of the trials above were done abroad, and how much service a clinic actually provides varies a lot from place to place, so treat the numbers as a direction rather than a promise. For the medication to pair with this, see unit 3 of this section (quit-smoking medication).

### 6. Consider e-cigarettes only if you cannot quit otherwise; if you don't already smoke, stay away
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Device plus pods runs a few dozen to a few hundred yuan a month. In China only tobacco flavors can be sold, and only in shops holding a tobacco monopoly retail license.
- In plain terms: Switching from cigarettes to nicotine e-cigarettes really does give higher quit rates than nicotine patches; this one has high-quality randomized trials behind it. But it is not harmless: there are no long-term safety data yet. For people who don't already smoke, it offers nothing at all.
- Benefit: A living review pooled 80 randomized trials with 29,861 people. Nicotine e-cigarettes beat nicotine replacement therapy on quit rates by about 61% (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23–2.12; high-certainty evidence, 11 trials, 4,114 people). In absolute terms that is roughly 4 more successful quitters per 100 people (1 to 7 more). Serious adverse events occurred at about the same rate in both groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02, moderate-certainty evidence). Against behavioral support alone or no support, e-cigarettes did about 75% better (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39–2.20, low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11>；国家烟草专卖局 (2022). 电子烟管理办法（国家烟草专卖局公告 2022 年第 1 号，2022 年 5 月 1 日施行）第十八条：从事电子烟零售业务，要向烟草专卖行政主管部门申领烟草专卖零售许可证。第二十二条：「禁止向未成年人出售电子烟产品。」第二十三条第二款：「任何个人、法人或者其他组织不得通过本办法规定的电子烟交易管理平台以外的信息网络销售电子烟产品、雾化物和电子烟用烟碱等。」第二十六条：「禁止销售除烟草口味外的调味电子烟和可自行添加雾化物的电子烟。」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>
- Notes: Contested. The World Health Organization does not recommend e-cigarettes as a quitting aid, on the grounds that long-term safety data are lacking and they draw young people into nicotine. The UK, by contrast, has built them into its stop-smoking services. China has not banned e-cigarette sales outright but regulates them tightly under its e-cigarette rules (电子烟管理办法, Measures for the Administration of E-cigarettes). Only tobacco flavors may be sold; fruit flavors and refillable devices are not. Sales are restricted to physical shops with a tobacco monopoly retail license. Selling on any website, online shop or messaging app outside the national e-cigarette transaction platform is illegal, and sales to minors are banned. So any fruit-flavored e-cigarette still on sale comes through illegal channels, outside all oversight. That is exactly how e-cigarettes laced with synthetic cannabinoids get around; see chapter 22, unit 4 (don't accept a pod someone hands you). The trials in the review all tested regulated nicotine products; e-liquids of unknown origin are not covered. Order of operations: try the medication in unit 3 of this section first; the evidence there is stronger and it is cheaper.

### 7. Don't drink sugary drinks; switching to sugar-free ones doesn't solve it either
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea actually saves money. The hard part is breaking the sweet-drink habit, sugar-free versions included.
- In plain terms: People who drink two or more sugary drinks a day are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than people who hardly drink any; one to two a day still means about 14% higher. Switching to sugar-free shows no benefit either: people drinking two or more artificially sweetened drinks a day run about a quarter higher.
- Benefit: Two large US cohort studies, observational with no groups assigned, covered about 118,000 people and 36,000 deaths. People drinking 2 or more servings a day had about 21% higher mortality than those having less than 1 a month (HR 1.21); at 1–2 a day it was about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC cohort, observational as well, followed 450,000 people in 10 countries. Comparing 2 or more glasses a day with less than 1 a month: about 8% higher for sugar-sweetened soft drinks (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16), about 26% higher for artificially sweetened drinks, the sugar-free kind (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35), and about 17% higher for all soft drinks taken together (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Malik VS 等 (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: The controversy sits on the sugar-free side. The association for artificially sweetened drinks is stronger than for sugary ones, and the arrow of causation is probably reversed: it is people who are already overweight or have diabetes who switch to the sugar-free version. So don't read this as artificial sweeteners being worse. It does show that the claim as long as it is sugar-free it is fine has no evidence behind it. Plain water or unsweetened tea is the safe swap. One more caveat: these are observational studies, no groups assigned, and heavy sugary-drink drinkers tend to have worse lifestyles all around, so the numbers may run high.

### 8. Don't chew betel nut
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time; you also stop paying for betel nut. The hard part is breaking the chewing habit.
- In plain terms: Betel chewers are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than non-chewers. Their risks of diabetes and metabolic syndrome run nearly 50% higher. Oral cancer and esophageal cancer come on top of that.
- Benefit: Pooling 17 Asian studies with 388,000 people: chewers' risk of death was about 21% higher than non-chewers' (relative risk 1.21, P=0.02). The mortality figure is computed on the 179,600 of them. Risk of developing diabetes was about 47% higher (1.47); metabolic syndrome about 51% higher (1.51). Betel nut is itself a known risk factor for oral cancer and esophageal cancer.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Notes: The longer the chewing years and the more chewed per day, the higher the oral cancer risk; after quitting, the risk falls over time. Betel nut with tobacco added is more dangerous still.

### 9. Replace the salt at home with low-sodium salt (potassium salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bag costs a few yuan more than regular salt. Swap it next time you shop; no extra time, and the taste is nearly unchanged.
- In plain terms: In a randomized trial of 20,000 people, those who switched their household salt to low-sodium salt were about 12% less likely to die within five years, and had about 14% fewer strokes. This came out of random assignment to two groups, so it is more trustworthy than numbers from follow-up records alone.
- Benefit: A randomized trial conducted in rural China, 20,995 people, all either stroke survivors or hypertension patients over 60, followed for 4.74 years. Result: the low-sodium salt group versus the regular salt group had about 12% lower risk of death (RR 0.88), about 14% fewer strokes (RR 0.86), and about 13% fewer major cardiovascular events (RR 0.87). For events of excessively high blood potassium, no statistical difference was seen between the groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>；O'Donnell M 等 (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>（争议方 PURE）
- Notes: Contested. The PURE study only followed people over time without assigning groups. It reported: people excreting under 3 g of sodium a day had about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events, and people excreting above 7 g a day about 15% higher; that is, too little and too much are both bad, with a middle optimum. But in the rural-China randomized trial cited here, low-sodium salt only replaces part of the sodium, so it cannot push sodium intake that low. Also note the trial participants were high-risk elderly; healthy young people switching salt would gain far less. People with impaired kidney function, or taking potassium-sparing drugs, should ask a doctor before switching.

### 10. Brush your teeth properly, clean between your teeth once a day, and replace missing teeth promptly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Floss or interdental brushes run tens of yuan a year; a professional cleaning costs one to two hundred yuan a visit. Two or three extra minutes a day. The hard part is making interdental cleaning a daily habit.
- In plain terms: People who floss or use interdental brushes daily are about 10% less likely to die over the same period. Elderly people who have lost all their teeth are about 90% more likely to die; those left with fewer than 20 teeth, about twice as likely.
- Benefit: A Japanese study followed 9,676 people for 6 years. Users of interdental cleaning tools had about 11% lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.89); users of tongue-coating cleaning tools about 23% lower (0.77). Separately, pooling multiple studies of community-dwelling elderly: people with not a single tooth had about 1.87 times the odds of death (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.35–2.59); those with fewer than 20 teeth, about 2.04 times (1.67–2.49).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>；Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Notes: Contested. These studies only follow people along; people with bad teeth tend to have worse overall health and finances to begin with. Whether bad teeth drag the body down or a failing body drags the teeth down is impossible to say. But this one costs very little, and periodontitis and missing teeth get in the way of eating in their own right.

### 11. Walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. 60–90 minutes of walking a day, which can be split into commuting and shopping. The hard part is doing it every day.
- In plain terms: People walking 5,800 steps a day are about 40% less likely to die over the same period than those walking only 3,500. At 7,800 steps, about 45% lower. Beyond that it drops a little more, but past a certain step count it stops falling. For people over 60, 6,000–8,000 steps is enough.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 cohort studies, observational with no groups assigned, covering 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths. People were sorted into four tiers by daily step count; the lowest tier's median was 3,553 steps a day. The other three tiers' medians were 5,801, 7,842 and 10,901 steps a day, with death risks about 40%, 45% and 53% lower than the lowest tier (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). For people over 60, past 6,000–8,000 steps the benefit flattens out; under 60, it flattens after 8,000–10,000. Another pooled analysis: the benefit kicks in from about 3,867 steps a day, and every extra 1,000 steps a day cuts the risk of death by about 15%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Paluch AE 等 (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M 等 (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>
- Notes: The stretch from 4,000 to 7,000 steps buys the most; there is no need to force ten thousand. Remember these are observational studies: the least-active group includes people already too ill to walk, who walked little because they were near the end, not the other way around, so the computed gaps often run large. But the more-is-better trend is clear. This and unit 14 of this section (150–300 minutes of moderate exercise a week) measure the same thing in different units; hitting either one is enough.

### 12. If you have hypertension or high blood lipids, take your medication regularly as prescribed; don't stop on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Centrally procured blood pressure drugs and statins run a few to a few dozen yuan a month. One pill a day takes hardly any time. The hard part is staying on it for good, not stopping just because you feel fine.
- In plain terms: Every 10 mmHg your blood pressure comes down, the chance of dying falls about 13% and stroke about 27%. A statin lowering LDL cholesterol by one unit cuts the chance of dying about 10%. People who take their medication on time are 30% to 45% less likely to die than people who often miss doses.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple randomized trials: every 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure — the higher of the two numbers on a reading — cut death risk about 13% (RR 0.87), major cardiovascular events about 20% (RR 0.80), stroke about 27% (RR 0.73), and heart failure about 28% (RR 0.72). Statins bring LDL cholesterol (low-density lipoprotein) down; each 1.0 mmol/L reduction cut death risk about 10% (RR 0.90), and the same reduction cut major vascular events about 22% (RR 0.78). Separately, pooling multiple cohort studies, observational with no groups assigned: people who took at least 80% of their prescribed doses had lower death risk than frequent dose-missers — about 45% lower for statins (RR 0.55) and about 29% lower for blood pressure drugs (RR 0.71).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R 等 (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Notes: This holds only for people the doctor has decided need medication; healthy people don't. The medication-adherence figures come from observational records, and people who stick to their medication tend to be more self-disciplined across the board, which inflates the benefit. The two randomized-trial figures above are more reliable. This section has no separate unit on blood sugar control: the benefit of pushing blood sugar lower for overall mortality is not as consistent as the evidence on blood pressure and lipids.

### 13. Sleep about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, but it takes time — for most people, trading phone-scrolling time for sleep. The hard part is pinning down a fixed bedtime and wake time.
- In plain terms: People who chronically sleep under 7 hours are about 10% more likely to die over the same period. The messier the schedule, the higher the risk: the most regular sleepers run 20% to nearly 50% lower than the least regular. In other words, how regular your schedule is predicts death risk better than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Pooling 16 cohort studies, observational with no groups assigned, 1.38 million people and 113,000 deaths: short sleepers' risk of death was about 12% higher (RR 1.12), long sleepers' about 30% higher (RR 1.30). Another pooling sorted by sleep duration: the 7-hour tier had the lowest risk. Under 7 hours, every hour less meant about 6% higher (RR 1.06); over 7 hours, every hour more meant about 13% higher (RR 1.13). The UK Biobank has wrist-monitor records for about 61,000 people, observational with no groups assigned. People were sorted into five tiers by schedule regularity; the four more regular tiers ran 20%–48% lower death risk than the least regular one. How regular the schedule is predicts death better than how long people sleep. Another UK Biobank study used wrist monitors to measure sleep timing in 51,562 people, during which 3,853 developed cardiovascular disease. Social jetlag is the gap between the mid-point of sleep on workdays and on weekends. People whose social jetlag reached 2 hours or more had about 30% higher cardiovascular risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). The association held just the same among people whose sleep duration was normal.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP 等 (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J 等 (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP 等 (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>；Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: What needs fixing is sleeping too little and a messy schedule; there is no need to deliberately compress sleep time. The higher risk among long sleepers is mostly the arrow of causation reversed: depression, chronic disease and sleep apnea make people sleep more. Also, schedule regularity currently rests on two cohort studies, both observational with no groups assigned; on its own that is grade B evidence. How to repay a late night is in unit 39 of this section (after a late night, catch up the very next night).

### 14. Accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise a week; brisk walking counts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is sticking with it long term.
- In plain terms: People who brisk-walk 150–300 minutes a week are about 30% less likely to die over the same period than people who don't exercise. Even those who fall short of the target run about 20% lower. Training three to five times the target brings the risk to its floor, about 40% lower. Adding more doesn't lower it further, but does no harm either.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, no groups assigned): people whose activity reached 1–2 times the guideline floor had death risk about 31% lower than non-exercisers (HR 0.69). That amount is 7.5–15 MET·h a week, roughly brisk walking 150–300 minutes a week. People below the floor still ran about 20% lower (HR 0.80). People at 3–5 times the floor ran about 39% lower (HR 0.61), and that is the deepest drop there is. Beyond that nothing more comes off, but there is no harm either (above 10 times, HR 0.69). Another pooling measured activity with accelerometers: the most active quarter of people had death risk about 48% lower than the least active quarter (HR 0.52). An accelerometer is a device worn on the body that measures movement directly.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Arem H 等 (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U 等 (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Pick either this or unit 11 of this section (7,000–8,000 steps a day); one is enough. Remember these are observational studies. The instrument-measured studies ran shorter follow-ups with older participants, and their numbers mix in people who stopped moving because they got sick first; the real benefit is not as big as risk down to 0.52 times.

### 15. Play a racket sport three times a week, 45 minutes each
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Court fees run a few dozen yuan a session. About 2 hours a week. The hard part is finding partners and a court, and showing up every week.
- In plain terms: People who regularly play tennis, badminton or table tennis are about half as likely to die over the same period as those who don't, with cardiovascular death about 56% lower. Swimmers run about 28% lower. Running and soccer showed no difference in this dataset.
- Benefit: A UK cohort study of 80,300 people compared people who did a given sport with people who didn't. Racket-sport players had death risk about 47% lower (hazard ratio 0.53), with a confidence range of 95% CI 0.40–0.69. Cardiovascular death was about 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports here means tennis, badminton and table tennis. For swimmers the two figures were 0.72 and 0.59; aerobics 0.73 and 0.64; cycling 0.85. Running and soccer showed no statistical difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes: Contested. These studies only follow people along. People who play racket sports are healthier and more socially connected to begin with. The running group showing no difference also suggests what works may be a matter of what kind of person picks which sport. Don't take this unit to mean running is useless; this section has a separate unit counting total exercise volume.

### 16. Bank four or five minutes a day of incidental vigorous activity: stairs, walking fast to get somewhere
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and no need to carve out exercise time. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and walk briskly instead of taking the easy way.
- In plain terms: For people who otherwise never exercise, just three bursts of one or two minutes of vigorous activity a day mean about 40% lower risk of death over the same period than none at all, with cardiovascular death down nearly half. Vigorous incidental activity means climbing stairs, hurrying on foot, that sort of thing.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study covered 25,200 people who did no dedicated exercise. Average follow-up was 6.9 years, during which 852 died. Some had three bouts of 1–2 minutes of incidental vigorous activity a day. Against people with none at all, their risk of death and of cancer death fell 38%–40%, and cardiovascular death risk fell 48%–49%. People totalling 4.4 minutes a day of this kind of activity had 26%–30% lower risk of death and cancer death, and 32%–34% lower cardiovascular death risk.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Contested. This study measured activity with wearable devices, more reliable than asking people to recall in questionnaires. But it is still follow-up records only, no groups assigned, and it followed people for just 6.9 years. The participants were people who did no exercise at all; the unit does not apply to people already exercising regularly.

### 17. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and push-ups cost nothing. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each. The hard part is fitting it into the weekly schedule.
- In plain terms: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength work a week are 10% to 20% less likely to die over the same period than those who don't. Training more adds nothing. Strength plus aerobic work together works best.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, no groups assigned): people who did strength training had death risk 10%–17% lower than those who didn't. More is not better; the reduction is largest, about 10%–20%, at roughly 30–60 minutes a week, and training beyond that adds no further benefit. People who did both strength and aerobic work had lower death risk than people who did neither.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Momma H 等 (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: These are observational studies, with exercise volume self-reported by participants. The evidence that overtraining turns harmful is weak; don't limit your training on its account. For older adults, strength training also prevents falls and preserves muscle; those points are in chapter 1.

### 18. Don't sit continuously for too long; get up and move every so often
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, and getting up a few times takes hardly any time. The hard part is losing track of time once you sit down; set yourself a reminder.
- In plain terms: The heaviest sitters are 2.6 times as likely to die over the same period as the lightest. Long uninterrupted sitting spells count as a further risk on top. But 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a day essentially cancels the extra risk from sitting.
- Benefit: A US cohort study, observational with no groups assigned, measured sitting with accelerometers. People were sorted into four groups by total daily sitting time: the heaviest-sitting group's risk of death was 2.63 times the lightest's (HR 2.63). Sorted again by how long a single sitting spell lasted: the longest-spell group was 1.96 times the shortest-spell one (HR 1.96). That is, total sitting and longest uninterrupted spell are each linked to risk in their own right. Another pooling combined studies of over a million people, observational with no groups assigned. People sitting more than 8 hours a day who also barely exercised had 1.59 times the death risk (HR 1.59) of people sitting under 4 hours a day who were the most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a day offsets the extra risk from sitting. The most active group showed no statistical difference even when sitting over 8 hours a day (HR 1.04).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Diaz KM 等 (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U 等 (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable conclusion: how much you move matters more than how long you sit, and with enough exercise the sitting risk largely disappears. Remember these are observational studies; the heaviest sitters include large numbers of people already ill, and a figure like 2.63 times clearly contains got sick first, then sat more. Also, in that same pooling, the risk of watching TV over 3 hours a day shows up at every level of activity; the most active group merely pushes that starting point out to 5 hours.

### 19. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausage, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, even saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is eating less of something you love.
- In plain terms: People who eat the most ham, bacon and sausage are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than those who eat the least. Each extra serving of processed meat a day adds about 20% as well.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, no groups assigned): the highest-intake group for processed meat had death risk about 23% higher than the lowest-intake group (RR 1.23). Red meat overall about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but no statistical difference was seen for that one. Another pooling worked by daily amounts: each extra serving of processed meat a day meant death risk about 23% higher (RR 1.23); each extra serving of red meat a day, about 10% higher (RR 1.10).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>；Johnston BC 等 (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621>（争议方 NutriRECS 指南）
- Notes: Contested. The NutriRECS guideline, using a standard evidence-grading system (GRADE), rated these studies not very reliable. It issued only a very weak recommendation: keep eating as you do now. What the two sides argue about is how hard the evidence is, not its direction. No study says processed meat is beneficial. Unprocessed red meat has a smaller effect and no statistical difference; the thing to control is processed meat.

### 20. Drink little or no alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, saves money, and takes no time. The hard part is a little awkwardness when declining drinks at social occasions.
- In plain terms: Past 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink, the sooner you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. At age 40, drinking 100–200 g a week costs about half a year of life; 200–350 g costs one to two years; over 350 g costs four to five. On overall health, the best value is not drinking at all.
- Benefit: Pooling 83 prospective studies, observational with no groups assigned, 600,000 current drinkers. The low point of death risk sits at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol a week, about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40-proof baijiu. At age 40, drinking 100–200 g a week costs about 6 months of life expectancy; 200–350 g a week costs 1–2 years; over 350 g costs 4–5 years. These three are reported in life-years in the original. The Global Burden of Disease study GBD 2016 added up all types of health loss: the drinking level with the lowest risk is 0 drinks a week. Another pooling corrected for the biases built into study designs. People drinking 1.3–24 g a day had death risk about 7% lower than lifetime abstainers (RR 0.93), but no statistical difference was seen for that one. Drinking 45–64 g a day ran about 19% higher (RR 1.19); over 65 g a day, about 35% higher (RR 1.35).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wood AM 等 (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J 等 (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>；Di Castelnuovo A 等 (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437>（争议方）
- Notes: Contested. The side that says a little drinking helps rests on a pooling of 34 prospective studies. It reported light drinkers' total mortality up to 17%–18% lower, with the amounts being no more than 4 drinks a day for men and 2 for women; within that amount, heavier drinkers within it had lower mortality. The other side argues the non-drinking group is contaminated by people who quit for health reasons and people in poor health to begin with. One pooling specifically subtracted these confounders, and after that the protective effect vanished. The Global Burden of Disease study also gives 0 as the safest. The safe reading: drinking little does not noticeably shorten life, drinking a lot certainly does, and starting to drink for your health has no basis. What to do if you want to drink less is in unit 22 of this section (count your drinks first, then talk to a doctor). Daily drinkers must not quit cold on their own; see unit 21 of this section (don't quit cold on your own)

### 21. If you drink every day and get shaky hands and a pounding heart when you stop, don't quit cold on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A visit to a psychiatrist or an addiction-medicine specialist costs a few to a few dozen yuan. Severe cases need a few days in the hospital.
- In plain terms: Heavy daily drinkers who stop suddenly get withdrawal reactions. Mild cases bring hand tremor, sweating, palpitations and sleeplessness; severe ones bring seizures or delirium tremens, an emergency that can kill. Norway tracked more than 30,000 people: among those who had had delirium tremens, 8% died each year afterwards. If you want to quit, go to the hospital first and state clearly how much you drink a day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states that people with long-term heavy drinking develop a withdrawal syndrome after suddenly stopping or sharply cutting down, with tremor, autonomic overactivity, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures and delirium tremens, and that in general medical care it is "common and potentially life-threatening". A Norwegian national registry cohort covered 36,287 people diagnosed between 2009 and 2015 with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state, or delirium tremens: annual mortality was 8.0% in the delirium tremens group, 5.0% in the withdrawal state group and 3.6% in the alcohol dependence group. The standardized mortality ratio in the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7), meaning a death risk about 9.8 times that of the general population of the same age.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>；Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to judge it yourself: drinking nearly every day; shaky hands, sweat, a pounding heart and sleepless nights after a single day without; needing a drink in the morning to feel normal. Any one of these, and you should not stop on your own. The hospital's standard approach is benzodiazepines to carry you through those days, along with thiamine (vitamin B1). Graded B because the Norwegian cohort says what happens to people who have had delirium tremens, not what happens when you quit cold on your own; the step from one to the other is inference. This unit is not telling you to keep drinking; it is telling you to change how you stop. How much per week counts as heavy is in unit 20 of this section (drink less or not at all); what to do if you want to cut down is in unit 22 (count your drinks first, then talk to a doctor).

### 22. To drink less, first count what you drink in a week, then spend a few minutes talking to a doctor
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Counting drinks is one note a day. Asking about it at a clinic costs nothing extra.
- In plain terms: A doctor spends a few minutes reviewing how much you drink, laying out the risks and helping you set a goal, and a year later you are drinking 20 grams of pure alcohol a week less on average, about half a liter of beer. Talking longer adds nothing. For people who can no longer stop, two drugs have proved effective abroad: for about every 12 people treated, 1 more person no longer returns to drinking heavily once started.
- Benefit: Pooling 34 randomized trials with 15,197 people, people who received brief intervention drank 20 grams of pure alcohol a week less a year later than those receiving minimal intervention or none (95% CI 12 to 28 grams less; moderate-quality evidence). Baseline drinking in these studies averaged 244 grams a week. Brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counseling in no more than 5 sessions totalling under 60 minutes; talking longer produced no extra benefit. On the drug side: pooling 122 randomized trials with 22,803 people, acamprosate adds 1 person who does not return to any drinking for every 12 treated (95% CI 8 to 26), and oral naltrexone at 50 mg a day adds 1 person who does not return to heavy drinking for every 12 treated (8 to 26).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>；Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit size is set to "small" because both bodies of evidence measure how much people drink, not mortality; under the book's rules, evidence resting only on a surrogate endpoint is recorded as "small". The drug trials were mostly run abroad, most participants met the diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence, and the trials all provided psychosocial interventions alongside, so the numbers are the benefit added on top of that base. For these two drugs' approved indications and whether they can be prescribed in China, defer to the doctor and the package insert; do not buy them online on your own. If withdrawal reactions have already appeared, see unit 21 of this section (don't quit cold on your own).

### 23. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 28 g a day, roughly two to three hundred yuan a year. Eat it as you go; takes no time.
- In plain terms: People who regularly eat nuts are about 20% less likely to die over the same period than people who don't. Even once a week shows a difference, about 10% lower. At a handful a day the difference is clearest.
- Benefit: Two US cohort studies, 119,000 people, over 3 million person-years. A person-year is the number of people times the years each was followed. Against people who ate no nuts: less than 1 serving a week, death risk about 7% lower (hazard ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96); 1 serving a week about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93); 2–4 times a week about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90); 5–6 times a week about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91); 7 or more times a week about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy plain, unsalted ones. Nuts are not low in calories; don't snack on them without limit. Remember these studies only follow a group of people for years: nut eaters have better overall lifestyles, so take the numbers with a discount.

### 24. Swap part of your red meat for fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time. This swaps one meat for another, not buys more. The hard part is adjusting your taste a little.
- In plain terms: Two extra servings a week of processed or red meat raise the chance of dying over the same period by about 3%. Swapping in the same number of servings of fish or poultry, the rise disappears. This unit's effect is on the small side for this section; it is a swap you make in passing, not something to fight over.
- Benefit: Pooling 6 US cohort studies with nearly 30,000 people. Two extra servings a week of processed meat meant death risk about 3% higher (hazard ratio 1.03), with a confidence range of 95% CI 1.02–1.05. Two extra servings a week of unprocessed red meat was also about 3% higher (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same eating pattern with poultry instead came out at 0.99 (0.97–1.02), and with fish 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither showed a statistical difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The difference from two servings a week is tiny; don't expect swapping meat to add years to your life. This unit's use is: spending the same money on meat, which kind to pick.

### 25. Swap part of your refined rice and white flour for whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats and whole-wheat noodles cost a bit more than refined grains, and no extra time. The hard part is getting used to the texture.
- In plain terms: People who eat an extra 90 g of whole grains a day are about 17% less likely to die over the same period. 90 g is about three servings. The risk keeps falling up to about 200 g a day.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, no groups assigned): an extra 90 g of whole grains a day meant death risk about 17% lower (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 servings. The benefit continues up to 210–225 g a day. In another pooling, each extra serving a day meant about 8% lower (RR 0.92).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: No need to switch everything to whole grains; switching half already lands you on the stretch where the benefit is most visible. Remember these are follow-up records only: whole-grain eaters are healthier to begin with, so the numbers run large. The results also vary a lot between studies (I² 83%, the higher this number, the less the studies agree).

### 26. Drink tea more than three times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Tens to a few hundred yuan a year. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes, hardly any time.
- In plain terms: Habitual tea drinkers are about 15% less likely to die over the same period. Counted from age 50, they live 1.26 years longer on average, with 1.41 extra years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: A Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR), half of them followed a full 7.3 years. Habitual tea drinkers had death risk about 15% lower (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90). Taking age 50 as the starting point: habitual tea drinkers had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and 1.26 more years of life expectancy.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>；茶叶霉菌毒素：Cui P 等 (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830>；Zhou H 等 (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452>；绿茶提取物与肝损伤：Hu J 等 (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Notes: The controversy: these are follow-up records only, and among Chinese men, tea drinkers smoke and drink at higher rates. The authors subtracted part of that influence, but not all of it. One more point: don't drink it scalding; see the unit in this section on drink temperature. Some worry about mold toxins in tea. China tested 158 dark-tea samples and found aflatoxin in 2; at tea-drinking amounts, the amount ingested is below the international acceptable value. Another study tested 352 tea samples for 16 mycotoxins; only ochratoxin A in dark tea exceeded the limit on average. At Chinese tea-drinking amounts, neither found a dietary risk. Keep tea leaves somewhere dry, and don't drink any that got damp and moldy. Green tea extract capsules are a different matter: swallowing concentrated catechins in one large dose harms the liver; brewed tea does not have this problem. So this unit only counts brewed tea; don't substitute extract supplements.

### 27. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, no sugar and no cream topping
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A yuan or two a day if you brew it yourself. Brewing a cup takes a few minutes, hardly any time.
- In plain terms: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day are about 17% less likely to die over the same period than people who don't drink coffee.
- Benefit: A review pooled the results of 201 pooled studies, whose underlying original studies were all observational with no groups assigned. Result: people drinking 3–4 cups of coffee a day, against non-drinkers, had death risk 17% lower (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Notes: The controversy: the original authors wrote that the evidence comes almost entirely from follow-up records, and that reliable randomized controlled trials are needed to judge whether it is causal. Meaning: compare two randomly assigned groups; follow-up records alone cannot settle causation. Drink it black; sugar and cream topping cancel out the benefit. Pregnant women, and people with arrhythmia, anxiety or insomnia, this unit does not apply.

### 28. Eat 5 servings (about 400 g) of fruit and vegetables a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few to a dozen-odd yuan a day, plus a little time each day for washing and cutting. The hard part is buying it daily and eating the full amount daily.
- In plain terms: Each extra 200 g of vegetables and fruit a day means about 10% lower chance of dying over the same period. People hitting 5 servings a day run about 13% lower than those eating only 2. Eating beyond that doesn't go lower.
- Benefit: Pooling multiple cohort studies (observational, no groups assigned): each extra 200 g of fruit and vegetables a day meant death risk about 10% lower (RR 0.90). The benefit continues up to 800 g a day. Two large US cohort studies plus a pooling of 26 studies: people hitting 5 servings a day had death risk about 13% lower than people eating only 2 (HR 0.87). The best mix is 2 servings of fruit plus 3 of vegetables; beyond that it stops falling.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD 等 (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: Hitting 5 servings is enough; no need to push to 10 — the two studies agree on that. Remember these are follow-up records with obvious confounding: people who eat more vegetables differ from others in income, education and exercise. So about 10% lower is already the ceiling.

### 29. Eat less ultra-processed food (chips, instant noodles, pastries, ready meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free — it is just buying different things. Cooking yourself, or spending time picking minimally processed ingredients, takes some time every day. The hard part is fighting the it's-convenient-so-it's-fine habit long term.
- In plain terms: People who eat the most ultra-processed food are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than those who eat the least, with cardiovascular death up about 50%. That said, this evidence has been graded as not very certain, so read the numbers with a discount.
- Benefit: A review pooled multiple pooled studies again, whose underlying original studies were all observational with no groups assigned. Result: heavy eaters of ultra-processed food had death risk about 21% higher than light eaters (RR 1.21) and cardiovascular death about 50% higher (RR 1.50). The evidence grades for these two were highly suggestive and convincing respectively. But under the standard evidence-grading system (GRADE), the certainty is low or very low.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lane MM 等 (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes: The controversy has three parts. First, this classification (NOVA) lumps foods with very different nutritional profiles into one category. Second, it overlaps heavily with sugary drinks and processed meat (units 7 and 19), so which item does the work cannot be told apart. Third, under the standard evidence-grading system (GRADE), the rating is low. So how much extra benefit this unit adds once you have done those two is hard to say. The counter-side's evidence is the low grades in that very review; no original studies reaching the opposite conclusion exist so far. So do units 7 and 19 first (no sugary drinks, less processed meat), then look at this one.

### 30. Don't burn coal or wood for cooking and heating; switch to electricity or gas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: For rural households, new stove plus fuel runs several hundred to over a thousand yuan a year. One switch and it is done; no long-term time cost. City dwellers are mostly already on electricity or gas.
- In plain terms: People who cook with coal or wood are about 10% more likely to die over the same period; those who heat with them, about 14% higher. People who have already switched to electricity or gas are 13%–33% less likely to die than people still burning coal or wood.
- Benefit: A Chinese chronic-disease prospective cohort study, observational with no groups assigned, 271,000 adults without cardiovascular disease. People using solid fuels such as coal and wood for cooking had death risk about 11% higher than people using electricity or gas (HR 1.11). Solid fuel for heating meant about 14% higher (HR 1.14). Some had already switched from solid to clean fuels; against people still burning solid fuel, their cooking figure was about 13% lower (HR 0.87) and heating about 33% lower (HR 0.67). Outdoor PM2.5 has its own account: pooling 104 cohort studies, every 10 µg/m³ higher long-term exposure meant about 8% higher risk of death from natural causes (RR 1.08). Natural causes means disease death, not accidents.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yu K 等 (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>；Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: These are follow-up records only. Households that switch fuels also tend to be better off, so the figure of risk down to 0.67 times mixes in the effect of family wealth. As for outdoor PM2.5, what an individual can do is limited: move, wear masks, run a purifier. Air purifiers have no studies with mortality as the endpoint, so this section gives no numbers for them.

### 31. Let hot things sit a while before drinking; don't drink scalding tea, soup, or coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free; wait two or three minutes longer each time. The hard part is holding back when the craving hits.
- In plain terms: Drinking very hot tea makes esophageal cancer 8 times as likely as drinking warm tea; even merely hot tea doubles it. Drinking within two minutes of pouring carries 5 times the risk of waiting four minutes or more.
- Benefit: In a high-incidence esophageal cancer region of northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people without the disease. All figures below are against people who drank warm tea. Drinking hot tea came with about 2.07 times the odds of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (odds ratio), with a confidence range of 95% CI 1.28–3.35. Very hot tea came with about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). People drinking less than 2 minutes after pouring had about 5.41 times the odds of those waiting 4 minutes or more (2.63–11.1).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies drinks above 65 °C as Group 2A, meaning probably carcinogenic. The same assessment found coffee itself not carcinogenic. Esophageal cancer runs high in China's Chaoshan and Taihang Mountain regions, linked to the habit of drinking while scalding hot.

### 32. Get out in the sun during the day; don't go all day without light
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. A dozen-odd minutes a day; the commute and the lunch break are enough.
- In plain terms: Women who deliberately avoid the sun live 0.6–2.1 years less than those who get the most sun. The original also notes that non-smokers who avoid the sun have about the same life expectancy as the smokers in the sunniest group.
- Benefit: A Swedish study followed 29,500 women for 20 years. Against the group with the most sun exposure, deliberate sun avoiders had 0.6 to 2.1 years less life expectancy. The authors wrote that non-smokers who avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy similar to smokers in the group with the most sun exposure.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: The controversy: Sweden sits at high latitude with little sunshine, so the conclusion does not transfer directly to China. And people who avoid the sun may already be frail and reluctant to leave the house. Also, don't sun yourself into a sunburn; you still need to cover up under strong midday light, because sun exposure raises skin cancer risk.

### 33. Keep BMI between 20 and 25; lose weight if overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free, but diet plus exercise take time every day. This is the most willpower-hungry unit in the whole section: holding your appetite and moving your legs for the long haul, and keeping the weight off after losing it is harder than losing it.
- In plain terms: The chance of dying is lowest in the BMI 20–25 range. At 27.5–30 it runs about 20% higher, at 30–35 about 45% higher, and at 35–40 nearly double. East Asians are more sensitive to overweight: every 5 points of BMI means about 40% higher risk.
- Benefit: Individual data from 239 prospective studies were pooled (observational, no groups assigned). The analysis kept only three kinds of people: never-smokers, people without chronic disease at enrollment, and people who lived at least 5 years after enrollment. Result: death risk is lowest at BMI 20–25. At 25–27.5 it is about 7% higher (HR 1.07), at 27.5–30 about 20% higher (HR 1.20), at 30–35 about 45% higher (HR 1.45), at 35–40 about 94% higher (HR 1.94), and 40–60 is 2.76 times (HR 2.76). Above BMI 25, every extra 5 kg/m² meant about 39% higher risk in East Asian populations (HR 1.39).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>；Flegal KM 等 (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905>（争议方）
- Notes: Contested. One side holds that a bit of fat makes you live longer. Their pooling found overweight people's death risk about 6% lower and grade-1 obesity about 5% lower; that is, slightly fat came out slightly better. The disagreement is mainly over whether to remove three kinds of data: smokers, the already ill, and the first few years after enrollment. The reason for removing them is that seriously ill people lose weight first. After the analysis pooling 239 studies removed those three, the risk of overweight reappeared. Also note: no randomized trial has shown that weight loss itself lowers overall mortality. The benefit above is a sideways comparison of people of different weights, and cannot be taken directly as a discount you collect once you lose the weight. If you need to lose weight, don't invest effort in meal timing: breakfast and 16:8 intermittent fasting bring no extra benefit; see chapter 6, unit 26 (don't expect breakfast or 16:8 to control your weight).

### 34. Eat chili peppers more than four times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time.
- In plain terms: People who eat chili peppers more than four times a week are about 23% less likely to die over the same period than people who rarely eat them, with cardiovascular death about a third lower.
- Benefit: An Italian study of 22,800 people, half of them followed a full 8.2 years, with 1,236 deaths in total. People eating chili peppers 4 or more times a week had death risk about 23% lower than rare eaters (hazard ratio 0.77), with a confidence range of 95% CI 0.66–0.90. Cardiovascular death was about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: The controversy: this is a follow-up record made against a Mediterranean diet backdrop, and chili eaters may simply eat closer to the local traditional diet overall. Also note that people with gastroesophageal reflux, hemorrhoids or irritable bowel get worse symptoms from chili; no need to force it.

### 35. Have one or two servings of milk or yogurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan a day, no time cost.
- In plain terms: People who drink two or more servings of milk or yogurt a day are about 17% less likely to die over the same period than people who drink none.
- Benefit: The PURE cohort study covered 21 countries and 136,000 people, followed on average 9.1 years, with 6,796 deaths in total. People eating two or more servings of dairy a day had death risk about 17% lower than people eating none (hazard ratio 0.83), with a confidence range of 95% CI 0.72–0.96 and a trend P=0.0052.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes: The controversy: PURE includes large numbers of low- and middle-income countries, where being able to drink milk every day itself signals a comfortable household, and that layer of confounding is hard to clean out. Also, people with lactose intolerance should switch to yogurt or low-lactose milk; no need to force down cow's milk.

### 36. No need to give up eggs, but don't eat three or four a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free and takes no time.
- In plain terms: Each extra half a whole egg a day means about 7% higher chance of dying over the same period. Swapping that half for egg white, fish, poultry or nuts actually lowers death risk across the board. No need to give up eggs; just don't eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: A US cohort study of 521,000 people, with 129,000 deaths in total. Each extra half a whole egg a day meant death risk about 7% higher (hazard ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08). Swapping that half a whole egg for the same amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts and legumes brought death risk lower, and risk of death from cardiovascular disease, cancer and respiratory disease all came down too.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Notes: Heavily contested: there are also long-term studies following large groups, and poolings of multiple studies, that found no link between eggs and mortality. The US dietary guidelines have already dropped the daily cap on cholesterol. This unit only argues against three or four a day; one egg a day is nothing to be anxious about.

### 37. Take a bath when you can, not just a quick shower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bit more on the water and electricity bill. Half an hour each time.
- In plain terms: People who bathe almost every day have about 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than people who bathe less than twice a week, and about 46% lower risk of cerebral hemorrhage.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people followed for 19 years, with 2,097 cardiovascular events in total. People who bathed almost every day had cardiovascular event risk about 28% lower than people bathing no more than twice a week (hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84). Coronary heart disease was about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Cerebral hemorrhage about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Notes: The controversy: this result comes out of the Japanese population and its bathtub culture; most households in China are not set up to bathe daily. Also, the water must not be too hot and the bath must not run too long. For elderly people and people with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, water that is too hot and baths that run too long are dangerous in themselves; every year a large number of elderly Japanese die suddenly in the bathtub.

### 38. Keep naps to half an hour, never past an hour; if you need an hour or two just to function, get checked for a cause
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Setting an alarm before the nap is all it takes; almost no willpower needed.
- In plain terms: People whose naps stay under an hour show no rise in death or chronic-disease risk, and their heads work a bit better afterwards. Those who sleep past an hour run about 30% higher risk of coronary heart disease and about 20% higher risk of diabetes and obesity. So set an alarm and wake yourself at half an hour. If you need an hour or two every day just to get through, that is a signal to have your body checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses covering 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review pools the existing pooled studies all over again. Its conclusions: people napping less than 60 minutes showed no rise in all-cause mortality or chronic-disease risk, and the clearest improvement in mental performance (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00). Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes improved physical performance the most (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). People napping more than 60 minutes ran about 30% higher coronary heart disease risk and about 20% higher diabetes and obesity risk. Another study had 1,338 adults aged 56 and older wear wrist monitors to measure their naps directly rather than relying on questionnaires. Follow-up ran up to 19 years, during which 926 died. Each extra hour of napping meant about 13% higher risk of death (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). Each extra napping day meant about 7% higher (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). People who napped in the morning ran about 30% higher than those who napped in the early afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, 等 (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>；Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, 等 (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>；Dashti HS 等 (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes: Contested. Both studies above are observational, with no groups assigned. People who nap long are more likely in the first place to have sleep apnea, anemia, hypothyroidism, depression or some other chronic disease. So how much of long-nappers die more comes from the napping itself is impossible to pin down. Causal inference built on genes (Mendelian randomization, 453,000 plus 541,000 people) supports only one thing: the more frequent the naps, the slightly higher the blood pressure and waist circumference. It found no causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So do not treat napping less as a way to lower mortality. The get-checked half is the author's advice and counts as grade C: if daytime sleepiness forces you into long naps, first examine your night sleep; snoring and breathing pauses during sleep call for a sleep apnea workup, then a blood count and thyroid tests. How a short nap refreshes you is in chapter 3, unit 11 (a 10-minute nap when the afternoon slump hits). How much to sleep at night is in unit 13 of this section (7 hours a night).

### 39. After a late night, catch up the very next night; don't bank the sleep for the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The extra hour or two is sleep you wanted anyway.
- In plain terms: More than 80,000 people wore wrist monitors that recorded how long they slept each day. People who slept short and failed to make it up the next night ran about 15% higher risk of death over the period than people with regular schedules. Those with severe shortfalls who also never made them up ran about 42% higher. The two groups that did make it up showed no detectable rise in risk. So after a late night, get to bed early the very next night.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 85,618 people with an average age of 61.8, wearing wrist monitors that logged sleep day by day, median follow-up 8 years. The study first worked out each person's own sleep need and defined a short-sleep night as sleeping 2.5 hours or more less than that need. The first night after a stretch of short sleep counted as a make-up night; sleeping more than one's need that night meant the debt was repaid. This sorted people into five patterns. Compared with people on regular schedules, all-cause mortality in the short-sleep-no-repayment group was about 15% higher (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31). Cumulative shortfall beyond 3.5 hours without repayment was about 42% higher (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). Neither repaying group showed a significant association; for short sleep followed by repayment the figure was HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among people who were already short sleepers, shortfall without repayment was about 19% higher (1.19, 1.01–1.40) and severe shortfall without repayment about 38% higher (1.38, 1.17–1.63). The result replicated in a separate US health survey of 4,586 people.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, 等 (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This is an observational study with no groups assigned. People who cannot repay may be busier or in worse health to begin with, and that layer cannot be fully cleaned out. That the repaying groups showed no rise means no difference was detected, not that repaying makes things safe; their point estimates still sit above 1. So this unit says to repay a late night as soon as you can, not that late nights are free to pile up. Two further things to keep apart. In this study, making up meant sleeping a bit longer on the first night after the shortfall, not banking it all for the weekend. Fixing skimp on weekdays, repay on weekends into a weekly rhythm is a different thing, called social jetlag, and it is itself linked to cardiovascular disease; see unit 13 of this section (about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule). The fixed wake time on weekends too is chapter 3, unit 2 (a fixed wake time, weekends included), and it does not conflict with this unit: this one tells you to go to bed early that same night, not to sleep until noon the next day.

### 40. The longer you work night shifts, the higher the cardiovascular risk; move out of them early if you can
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Transferring or changing jobs may mean less pay, and the night-shift allowance goes away. Job hunting takes time, and deciding is hard. If you simply factor this into how you pick a job in the first place, the cost is zero.
- In plain terms: Night-shift workers have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than day workers, and about 27% more cardiovascular death. And it accumulates the longer and more you do it: every extra 5 years of night shifts adds about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So run the account in years, and move out early if you can. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is far softer than the rumors.
- Benefit: Pooling 23 cohort studies, all observational with no groups assigned. Night-shift workers' risk of cardiovascular events was about 13% higher (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16). Cardiovascular death was about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). By years: every extra 5 years of night shifts, cardiovascular incidence another about 7% higher (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09) and cardiovascular death another about 5% higher (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By disease: coronary heart disease incidence about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28), coronary heart disease death about 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36), ischemic heart disease death about 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84), stroke death about 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). Stroke incidence was not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: All of these are observational studies with no groups assigned. Night-shift workers differ from others in smoking, weight and income to begin with, and the adjustment cannot fully clean that out. On night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is far softer than the rumors. In a pooling of 12 studies with 12,132 breast cancer cases, ever having worked night shifts raised risk only about 5%, with the confidence range crossing 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The 20-years-or-more tier was about 25% higher (1.25, 1.01–1.55), but the term lost significance after the authors ran an influence analysis and moved close to 1 after adjusting for possible publication bias; their words were that the association is "far from established". Another study following Chinese men for 16.1 years with 8,202 cancer cases found no association between night shifts and total cancer or most common cancers; only pancreatic cancer in the 11-to-20-years tier was about 59% higher (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this unit runs its account on the cardiovascular side, not on cancer. It does not claim that everyone should leave night work; it lays out the per-year bill for you to weigh yourself. If you are already doing the work, change what can be changed first: quitting smoking is unit 1 of this section (quit smoking, the sooner the better); blood pressure and lipids are unit 12 (if you have hypertension or high lipids, take your medication regularly as prescribed); how to repay sleep after a night shift is unit 39 (after a late night, catch up the very next night). Two further countermeasures are worth trying: keeping your eating inside the daytime, and shifting your body clock with bright light plus strict daytime blackout. The latter is the only method shown to actually move the clock: under ordinary indoor lighting, six straight night shifts left the clock unmoved; with 7,000 to 12,000 lux of bright light at night and near-total darkness by day, it flipped over entirely in four days. **The light at night must be bright enough, daytime sleep must be strictly blacked out, and sunglasses must block the morning light on the way home; all three or nothing** — skip the last one and the whole effort is wasted. Both countermeasures have been studied only down to metabolic markers and sleep; no trial has tested whether following them actually lowers cardiovascular risk. On days off, living back on a daytime schedule pushes the clock partway back again.
