# 2. Don't Die Slowly

This section includes only chronic risk factors with large effects on all-cause mortality and hard evidence, ranked from highest to lowest value for money. Except where a randomized trial is specifically noted, the numbers come from observational studies; the HRs/RRs contain confounding and reverse causation, so read them as direction and magnitude, not as a discount you are guaranteed to receive by following the advice.
### 1. Quit smoking, and quit as early as possible
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: willpower during withdrawal (weeks to months); money-wise it is a negative cost — a pack a day runs about 20–30 yuan, and quitting saves it.
- In plain terms: smokers live more than ten years less than non-smokers on average; quitting before 40 wipes out about 90% of the excess death risk from continuing to smoke, and the earlier you quit, the more you get back.
- Benefit: US cohort (observational): current smokers live at least 10 years less than never-smokers; quitting before 40 eliminates about 90% of the excess mortality risk from continued smoking, and quitting at ages 25–34, 35–44, and 45–54 adds roughly 10, 9, and 6 years of life respectively (the original reports life-years). Chinese cohort (observational): among urban male smokers in the 2010s, all-cause mortality RR 1.65, rural men RR 1.22; after 10 years of sustained quitting the attributable risk nearly disappears.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Jha P 等 (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z 等 (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>
- Notes: Secondhand smoke kills too: worldwide in 2004 about 603,000 people died from secondhand smoke exposure, about 1% of all deaths that year (Oberg M et al. (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>). Avoid other people's smoke even if you don't smoke yourself, and above all keep children away from it.

### 2. Don't smoke at home or in the car, and don't let guests smoke there either
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; the hard part is speaking up to family and guests.
- In plain terms: about 600,000 people worldwide die each year from breathing other people's smoke, nearly a third of them children; people chronically exposed to secondhand smoke run about 30% higher risks of high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke, and what you breathe at home hurts you more than what you breathe outside.
- Benefit: in 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from secondhand smoke, about 1.0% of all global deaths, of whom 28% were children; in a meta-analysis of 57 studies, people exposed to secondhand smoke had an odds ratio for hypertension of 1.28, heart disease 1.39, myocardial infarction 1.50, and stroke 1.36, with the risk from exposure inside the home higher than outside it.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet：「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」，「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research：57 项研究，「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: The home is the place to police hardest — not only because you spend the most time there, but because the meta-analysis shows the effect of within-home exposure is larger than outside-home exposure. Children bear 61% of the disability-adjusted life years lost to secondhand smoke. For quitting yourself, see item 1 in this section.

### 3. Don't drink sugar-sweetened beverages
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: zero; switching to water or unsweetened tea actually saves money.
- In plain terms: people who drink two or more sweetened drinks a day are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than those who hardly drink any, and even one to two a day adds a bit over 10%.
- Benefit: two large US cohorts (observational, about 118,000 people, 36,000 deaths): ≥2 servings a day vs <1 a month, all-cause mortality HR 1.21; 1–2 servings a day HR 1.14. The European EPIC cohort (observational, 450,000 people): ≥2 cups a day vs <1 a month, sugar-sweetened soft drinks HR 1.08, all soft drinks combined HR 1.17.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Malik VS 等 (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A 等 (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: Observational — heavy drinkers of these beverages tend to have worse overall lifestyles, so the effect may be overestimated. In EPIC, artificially sweetened drinks at ≥2 cups a day showed HR 1.26, higher than sugar-sweetened ones — probably reverse causation (people who already have metabolic disease switch to diet drinks). Don't treat diet drinks as a safe substitute to consume in bulk; the replacement of choice is water.

### 4. Don't chew betel nut
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan — you also save the money you'd spend on betel nut.
- In plain terms: betel chewers are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than non-chewers, with nearly 50% higher risks of diabetes and metabolic syndrome — and oral and esophageal cancers come on top of that.
- Benefit: meta-analysis of 17 Asian studies, 388,000 people: relative risk of all-cause mortality for chewers vs non-chewers 1.21 (P=0.02, 179,600 people), diabetes 1.47, metabolic syndrome 1.51; betel nut is itself a known risk factor for oral and esophageal cancer.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Notes: Oral cancer risk scales with years of chewing and daily amount, and declines over time after quitting. Betel nut with tobacco added is more dangerous still.

### 5. Replace the salt at home with low-sodium salt (potassium salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few yuan more per bag than regular salt; taste is nearly unchanged, no willpower needed.
- In plain terms: in a randomized trial of twenty thousand people, those who switched the household salt to low-sodium salt were about 12% less likely to die within five years, and had about 14% fewer strokes. This came out of random assignment, so it is more credible than the usual observational data.
- Benefit: randomized trial (rural China, 20,995 people with a prior stroke or hypertension at age ≥60, followed 4.74 years): low-sodium salt group vs regular salt group, all-cause mortality RR 0.88, stroke RR 0.86, major cardiovascular events RR 0.87; no significant difference in hyperkalemia events between the two groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Neal B 等 (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>
- Notes: Controversy: the PURE cohort (observational; O'Donnell M et al. (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>) reported that for estimated sodium excretion <3 g/day the composite OR for death plus cardiovascular events was 1.27, and at ≥7 g/day OR 1.15, suggesting a J-shaped sodium curve. But SSaSS was a randomized trial, and low-sodium salt only partially replaces sodium, so it does not push sodium that low. The trial population was high-risk elderly; healthy young people would gain less in absolute terms. People with impaired kidney function or taking potassium-sparing drugs should ask a doctor before switching salt.

### 6. Brush your teeth properly, clean between your teeth once a day, and replace missing teeth promptly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: dental floss or interdental brushes run tens of yuan a year and add two or three minutes a day; professional cleaning costs 100–200 yuan per visit.
- In plain terms: people who floss or use interdental brushes daily are about 10% less likely to die over the same period; elderly people who have lost all their teeth are about 90% more likely to die, and those left with fewer than 20 teeth about twice as likely.
- Benefit: Japanese cohort of 9,676 people over 6 years: users of interdental cleaning tools had an all-cause mortality hazard ratio of 0.89, users of tongue-coating cleaning tools 0.77; meta-analysis of community-dwelling elderly: edentulous (fully toothless) odds ratio for death 1.87 (95% CI 1.35–2.59), fewer than 20 teeth 2.04 (1.67–2.49).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789>；Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Notes: Contested. Observational — people with bad teeth tend to have worse overall health and finances, so the direction of causation is uncertain. But the cost is very low, and periodontitis and missing teeth impair eating in their own right.

### 7. Walk to 7,000–8,000 steps a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: about 60–90 minutes of walking, which can be split into commuting and shopping; free.
- In plain terms: people who walk 5,800 steps a day are about 40% less likely to die over the same period than those who walk only 3,500; at 7,800 steps it's about 45% lower, and beyond that the curve flattens out. For older adults, 6,000–8,000 steps is enough.
- Benefit: meta-analysis of 15 cohorts (observational, 47,471 people, 3,013 deaths): by steps quartile, medians of 5,801, 7,842, and 10,901 steps/day vs 3,553 steps/day, all-cause mortality HR 0.60, 0.55, and 0.47 respectively; for those ≥60 the benefit plateaus after 6,000–8,000 steps, for those <60 after 8,000–10,000. Another meta-analysis: benefit starts from about 3,867 steps/day, and each extra 1,000 steps/day cuts all-cause mortality by about 15%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Paluch AE 等 (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M 等 (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>
- Notes: Observational — the least-active group includes people who are already frail (reverse causation), so inflated HRs are the norm; but the dose-response is clear, the marginal gain is largest going from 4,000 to 7,000 steps, and there is no need to obsess over ten thousand. This and item 7 in this section are two ways of measuring the same thing; hitting either one is enough.

### 8. Take your blood pressure and lipid medication on schedule as prescribed, and don't stop on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: centrally procured blood pressure drugs and statins run a few yuan to a few dozen yuan a month; plus a once-a-day habit.
- In plain terms: for every 10 mmHg your blood pressure drops, your chance of dying falls about 13% and stroke about 27%; a statin that lowers LDL cholesterol by one unit cuts the chance of dying about 10%. People who take their medication on time are 30% to 45% less likely to die than those who often miss doses.
- Benefit: meta-analyses of randomized trials: for each 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure, all-cause mortality RR 0.87, major cardiovascular events RR 0.80, stroke RR 0.73, heart failure RR 0.72; with statins, each 1.0 mmol/L reduction in LDL gives all-cause mortality RR 0.90, major vascular events RR 0.78. Adherence meta-analysis (observational): statin and antihypertensive users with ≥80% adherence vs poor adherence, all-cause mortality RR 0.55 and 0.71 respectively.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ettehad D 等 (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R 等 (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Notes: This holds only for people with an indication for the drugs; healthy people don't need them. The adherence numbers are observational — "people who stick to their medication are more self-disciplined to begin with" inflates the effect, and the two randomized-trial figures above are more reliable. This section does not list blood sugar control separately: the evidence that intensive glucose lowering lowers all-cause mortality is less consistent than for blood pressure and lipids.

### 9. Sleep about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: time (for most people, swapping phone-scrolling time for sleep); a fixed schedule takes a little discipline.
- In plain terms: people who chronically sleep under 7 hours are about 10% more likely to die over the same period; the more chaotic your schedule, the higher the risk — the most regular sleepers are 20% to nearly 50% less likely to die than the least regular, so regularity matters more than duration.
- Benefit: meta-analysis (observational, 16 studies, 1.38 million people, 113,000 deaths): short sleepers all-cause mortality RR 1.12, long sleepers RR 1.30. Dose-response meta-analysis: with 7 hours as the low point, each hour below 7 gives RR 1.06, each hour above 7 RR 1.13. UK Biobank wrist-tracker data on about 61,000 people (observational): the four most regular sleep quintiles vs the least regular, all-cause mortality 20%–48% lower; regularity predicts mortality better than duration.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP 等 (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J 等 (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP 等 (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>
- Notes: The higher risk of long sleepers is mostly reverse causation (depression, chronic disease, and sleep apnea make people sleep more), so there is no need to deliberately cut sleep; short sleep and irregularity are what deserve fixing. The regularity evidence currently comes from a single cohort, which on its own is grade B.

### 10. Accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise a week; brisk walking counts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 20–45 minutes a day; free.
- In plain terms: people who briskly walk 150–300 minutes a week are about 30% less likely to die over the same period than those who don't exercise; even falling short of the target still cuts it by about 20%, and training up to three to five times that amount bottoms out the risk (about 40% lower) — beyond that it stops falling, with no harm.
- Benefit: pooled analysis of multiple cohorts (observational): meeting 1–2 times the guideline minimum (7.5–15 MET·h/week, roughly 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week) vs no exercise, all-cause mortality HR 0.69; even below the minimum HR 0.80; at 3–5 times, HR 0.61 and no further decline, though more is not harmful (≥10 times HR 0.69). Meta-analysis using accelerometer measurement: highest vs lowest quartile of moderate-to-vigorous activity HR 0.52.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Arem H 等 (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U 等 (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Observational; the accelerometer studies have short follow-up and older samples, and the HRs contain reverse causation, so the true effect should be smaller than magnitudes like 0.52. Choose this or item 4 in this section — either one.

### 11. Play a racket sport three times a week, 45 minutes each
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: court fees of tens of yuan per session; about 2 hours a week.
- In plain terms: people who regularly play tennis, badminton, or table tennis are about half as likely to die over the same period as those who don't, and about 56% less likely to die of cardiovascular disease; swimming cuts it about 28%, while running and soccer show no difference in this dataset.
- Benefit: UK cohort of 80,300 people: relative to not doing the sport, racket sports (tennis, badminton, table tennis) had an all-cause mortality hazard ratio of 0.53 (95% CI 0.40–0.69), cardiovascular death 0.44 (0.24–0.83); swimming 0.72 and 0.59; aerobics 0.73 and 0.64; cycling 0.85; no significant association seen for running or soccer.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes: Contested. Observational — racket players are healthier and more socially connected to begin with; the lack of a significant association in the running group also points to selection effects. Don't conclude from this that running is useless; this section has a separate entry on total exercise volume.

### 12. Accumulate four or five minutes a day of incidental vigorous activity like stair climbing and brisk walking
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; no need to carve out extra exercise time.
- In plain terms: for people who never otherwise exercise, just three bursts of one or two minutes of vigorous activity a day (climbing stairs, hurrying on foot) make them about 40% less likely to die over the same period than those with none at all, and nearly half as likely to die of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 25,200 non-exercisers, mean follow-up 6.9 years, 852 deaths: 3 bouts a day of 1–2 minutes of incidental vigorous activity, relative to none at all, cut all-cause and cancer mortality by 38%–40% and cardiovascular mortality by 48%–49%; a daily total of 4.4 minutes corresponded to 26%–30% lower all-cause and cancer mortality and 32%–34% lower cardiovascular mortality.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Contested. Measured by wearables rather than questionnaires, so more reliable — but still observational, and follow-up was only 6.9 years. The study population was people who did no exercise at all; it does not apply to those who already exercise regularly.

### 13. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 1–2 sessions a week of 20–30 minutes each; bodyweight squats and push-ups are free.
- In plain terms: people who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week are 10% to 20% less likely to die over the same period than those who don't, and training more stops adding benefit; it works best combined with aerobic exercise.
- Benefit: cohort meta-analysis (observational): those doing strength training vs those not, all-cause mortality 10%–17% lower; the dose-response is J-shaped, with the largest reduction (about 10%–20%) at roughly 30–60 minutes/week, no further gain beyond that; strength plus aerobic vs neither, all-cause mortality lower still.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Momma H 等 (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: Observational, self-reported exercise volume. The evidence for the rising right arm of the J-curve is thin, so don't limit training on its account. For older adults there are additional benefits — fall prevention and muscle preservation — which belong to section 1.

### 14. Don't sit continuously for too long; get up and move every so often
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: zero; set yourself a reminder.
- In plain terms: the heaviest sitters are 2.6 times as likely to die over the same period as the lightest, and long uninterrupted sitting spells count on top of that. But 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a day essentially cancels out the extra risk from sitting.
- Benefit: US cohort (observational, accelerometers): highest vs lowest quartile of total sedentary time, all-cause mortality HR 2.63; longest vs shortest quartile of continuous uninterrupted sitting HR 1.96 — total volume and bout length are each independently associated. Pooled analysis of a million people (observational): sitting >8 hours a day and barely exercising vs sitting <4 hours and most active, HR 1.59; about 60–75 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a day offsets the excess risk from sitting (the most active group sitting >8 hours had HR 1.04, not significant).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Diaz KM 等 (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U 等 (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: Observational — the heaviest sitters include many frail people, and a magnitude like HR 2.63 clearly contains reverse causation. The reliable conclusion: how much you move matters more than how long you sit, and with enough exercise the sitting risk largely disappears; TV viewing ≥3 hours/day carries risk at every activity level, and even the most active only push the threshold out to ≥5 hours (HR 1.16).

### 15. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausage, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: zero — it even saves money; a small sacrifice in taste.
- In plain terms: people who eat the most ham, bacon, and sausage are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than those who eat the least; each extra serving of processed meat a day adds about 20%.
- Benefit: meta-analysis (observational): highest vs lowest intake of processed meat, all-cause mortality RR 1.23; total red meat RR 1.29, unprocessed red meat RR 1.10 (not significant); another meta-analysis by dose: each additional serving/day of processed meat RR 1.23, red meat each additional serving/day RR 1.10.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: Controversy: the NutriRECS guideline (Johnston BC et al. (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621>) graded the evidence as low certainty under GRADE and issued a weak recommendation to "maintain current intake." The dispute is over evidence quality, not direction — no study says processed meat is beneficial. The effect for unprocessed red meat is small and not significant; the focus is processed meat.

### 16. Drink little or no alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a bit of awkwardness at social occasions; saves money.
- In plain terms: past 100 g of pure alcohol a week (about 2.5 L of beer), the more you drink the sooner you die: at age 40, drinking 100–200 g a week costs about half a year of life, 200–350 g costs one to two years, and over 350 g costs four to five. On overall health, the best value is zero.
- Benefit: pooled analysis of 83 prospective studies, 600,000 current drinkers (observational): all-cause mortality lowest at ≤100 g pure alcohol a week (about 2.5 L of 5% beer or 300 mL of 40° baijiu); at age 40, 100–200 g/week shortens life expectancy by about 6 months, 200–350 g by 1–2 years, >350 g by 4–5 years (the original reports life-years). GBD 2016: aggregating all health losses, the drinking level with the lowest risk is 0 drinks/week. Meta-analysis correcting for study-design bias: light drinking (1.3–24 g/day) vs lifetime abstainers RR 0.93 (not significant), 45–64 g/day RR 1.19, ≥65 g/day RR 1.35.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wood AM 等 (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J 等 (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>
- Notes: Contested. The J-curve side: meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies (Di Castelnuovo A et al. (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437>) reported light drinking lowered all-cause mortality by up to 17%–18%, with an inverse association persisting at ≤4 drinks/day for men and ≤2 for women. The opposing side argues "non-drinkers" are contaminated by people who quit for health reasons and by people in worse overall health; Zhao 2023 found the protective effect disappears after correcting for these biases, and GBD 2018 also ranked 0 as safest. The safe reading: drinking little does not noticeably shorten life, drinking a lot certainly does, and "starting to drink for your health" has no basis.

### 17. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 28 g a day, roughly 200–300 yuan a year.
- In plain terms: people who regularly eat nuts are about 20% less likely to die over the same period than those who don't; even once a week shows a difference (about 10% lower), and the effect is clearest at daily frequency.
- Benefit: two US cohorts, 119,000 people, over 3 million person-years: relative to eating no nuts, all-cause mortality hazard ratio less than 1 serving a week 0.93 (95% CI 0.90–0.96), once a week 0.89 (0.86–0.93), 2–4 times a week 0.87 (0.83–0.90), 5–6 times a week 0.85 (0.79–0.91), 7 or more times a week 0.80 (0.73–0.86).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Observational cohorts — nut eaters have better overall lifestyles, so discount the numbers. Choose unsalted, plain nuts; they are not low in calories, so don't snack on them without limit.

### 18. Swap part of your red meat for fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan — a substitution, not an addition.
- In plain terms: two extra servings of processed or red meat a week raise the chance of dying over the same period by about 3%; swapping in the same number of servings of fish or poultry shows no such rise. This item's effect is on the small side for this section — something to switch on a whim, not to fight over.
- Benefit: pooled analysis of six US cohorts, nearly 30,000 people: two additional servings a week of processed meat, all-cause mortality hazard ratio 1.03 (95% CI 1.02–1.05); two additional servings a week of unprocessed red meat 1.03 (1.01–1.05); poultry in the same eating pattern 0.99 (0.97–1.02), fish 0.99 (0.97–1.01), neither statistically significant.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The effect size for two servings a week is tiny — don't expect swapping meat to extend your life; the value of this item lies in which kind of meat you pick when spending the same money.

### 19. Swap part of your refined rice and white flour for whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: brown rice, oats, and whole-wheat noodles cost slightly more; the texture takes getting used to.
- In plain terms: people who eat an extra 90 g of whole grains a day (about three servings) are about 17% less likely to die over the same period, and the risk keeps falling up to roughly 200 g a day.
- Benefit: meta-analysis (observational): each additional 90 g/day of whole grains (about 3 servings), all-cause mortality RR 0.83, with benefits continuing up to 210–225 g/day; another meta-analysis, each additional serving/day RR 0.92.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716>；Schwingshackl L 等 (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: Observational — whole-grain eaters are healthier overall, so the effect is probably inflated; heterogeneity between studies is high (I² 83%). You don't have to switch everything; even half is already on the steep part of the dose-response curve.

### 20. Drink tea more than three times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: tens to a few hundred yuan a year.
- In plain terms: habitual tea drinkers are about 15% less likely to die over the same period; counted from age 50, that averages 1.26 extra years of life and 1.41 extra years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: Chinese cohort of 100,900 people, median follow-up 7.3 years (China-PAR): habitual tea drinkers had an all-cause mortality hazard ratio of 0.85 (95% CI 0.79–0.90); starting from age 50, habitual tea drinkers had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and 1.26 more years of life expectancy.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>
- Notes: Contested. Observational — among Chinese men, tea drinkers have higher rates of smoking and drinking; the authors adjusted for this, but residual confounding is inevitable. Don't drink it scalding hot; see the item in this section on beverage temperature.

### 21. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, no sugar and no cream topping
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a yuan or two a day if you brew it yourself.
- In plain terms: people who drink three to four cups of coffee a day are about 17% less likely to die over the same period than those who don't.
- Benefit: umbrella review covering a meta-analysis of 201 observational studies: 3–4 cups a day vs none, relative risk for all-cause mortality 0.83 (95% CI 0.79–0.88), i.e. 17% lower.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Notes: Contested. The original authors stated that the evidence is almost entirely observational and that "reliable randomized controlled trials are needed to judge whether this is causal." Different story for pregnant women and people with arrhythmia, anxiety, or insomnia; adding sugar or cream topping cancels out the benefit.

### 22. Eat 5 servings (about 400 g) of fruit and vegetables a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few to a dozen-odd yuan a day; plus time for washing and cutting.
- In plain terms: each extra 200 g of fruit and vegetables a day cuts the chance of dying over the same period by about 10%; people who hit 5 servings a day are about 13% less likely to die than those who eat only 2, and more than that stops lowering the risk.
- Benefit: meta-analysis (observational): each additional 200 g/day, all-cause mortality RR 0.90, with benefits continuing up to 800 g/day; two US cohorts plus a meta-analysis of 26 cohorts: 5 servings a day vs 2, all-cause mortality HR 0.87, with 2 servings of fruit + 3 of vegetables optimal, and no further reduction beyond that.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D 等 (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD 等 (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: Observational, with obvious confounding (people who eat more vegetables differ in income, education, and exercise), so RR 0.90 is an upper-bound magnitude. The two studies agree on what counts as enough: 5 servings does it, 10 is unnecessary.

### 23. Eat less ultra-processed food (chips, instant noodles, pastries, ready meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: you have to cook yourself or pick whole ingredients; moderate cost in time and willpower.
- In plain terms: people who eat the most ultra-processed food are about 20% more likely to die over the same period than those who eat the least, and 50% more likely to die of cardiovascular disease. That said, the certainty of this evidence has been graded as low, so discount the numbers accordingly.
- Benefit: umbrella review (pooling observational meta-analyses): high vs low intake of ultra-processed food, all-cause mortality RR 1.21, cardiovascular death RR 1.50; the evidence grading was "highly suggestive" and "convincing" respectively, but GRADE certainty was low/very low.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lane MM 等 (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes: Controversy: the NOVA classification lumps foods with very different nutritional profiles into one category, and it overlaps heavily with sugar-sweetened beverages and processed meat (items 2 and 10 in this section), so the independent effect is hard to isolate and the GRADE rating is low. After you have done items 2 and 10, the marginal benefit of this item is uncertain; the counter-evidence is the low-certainty grading itself in the same review — there are currently no original studies pointing the other way.

### 24. Don't drink sweetened drinks, sugar-free ones included
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan — it saves money.
- In plain terms: people who drink more than two soft drinks a day are about 17% more likely to die over the same period than those who have less than one a month; diet drinks come in about 26% higher, higher than the sugared ones — mostly because people who already have a disease are the ones who switch to diet versions. Don't conclude that sweeteners are more toxic, but don't treat them as a safe substitute either.
- Benefit: European cohort across 10 countries, 450,000 people: more than two soft drinks a day vs less than one a month, all-cause mortality hazard ratio 1.17 (95% CI 1.11–1.22); of which sugar-sweetened beverages 1.08 (1.01–1.16), artificially sweetened beverages 1.26 (1.16–1.35).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: Contested. The stronger association for artificially sweetened drinks is most likely reverse causation: people switch to the sugar-free version only after becoming overweight or diabetic. But it at least shows there is no evidence behind "just switch to sugar-free and you're fine."

### 25. Don't burn coal or wood for cooking and heating; switch to electricity or gas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: for rural households, new stove plus fuel costs several hundred to a thousand-odd yuan a year; urban residents have mostly already made the switch.
- In plain terms: people who cook with coal or wood are about 10% more likely to die over the same period, and those who heat with them about 14%; those who have switched to electricity or gas are 13%–33% less likely to die than those still burning solid fuels.
- Benefit: China Chronic Disease Prospective cohort (observational, 271,000 adults without cardiovascular disease): solid fuel vs clean fuel for cooking, all-cause mortality HR 1.11, solid fuel for heating HR 1.14; those who had switched from solid to clean fuels vs those still using solid fuels, cooking HR 0.87, heating HR 0.67. Outdoor PM2.5: meta-analysis of 104 cohorts, for each 10 µg/m³ of long-term exposure, RR 1.08 for death from natural causes.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yu K 等 (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151>；Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: Observational; households that switch fuels also tend to be wealthier, so HR 0.67 contains confounding. What an individual can do about outdoor PM2.5 is limited (moving, masks, air purifiers), and no studies have death as an endpoint for air purifiers, so this section gives no numbers there.

### 26. Let hot things sit a while before drinking; don't drink scalding tea, soup, or coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; wait an extra two or three minutes.
- In plain terms: drinking very hot tea makes esophageal cancer 8 times as likely as drinking warm tea, and merely "hot" tea still doubles it; drinking within two minutes of pouring carries 5 times the risk of waiting four minutes or more. Letting it sit for two minutes cuts most of this risk away.
- Benefit: 300 cases and 571 controls from a high-incidence esophageal cancer region in northern Iran: relative to warm tea, the odds ratio for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma was 2.07 (95% CI 1.28–3.35) for "hot" tea and 8.16 (3.93–16.9) for "very hot" tea; drinking within 2 minutes of pouring vs waiting 4 minutes or more, 5.41 (2.63–11.1).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies beverages above 65 °C as Group 2A (probably carcinogenic), while finding coffee itself non-carcinogenic. Esophageal cancer runs high in the Chaoshan and Taihang Mountain regions of China, linked to the habit of drinking while scalding hot.

### 27. Get out in the sun during the day; don't go all day without light
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan; a dozen-odd minutes a day, commuting and lunch break are enough.
- In plain terms: women who deliberately avoid the sun live 0.6–2.1 years less than those who get the most sun; the authors put it this way — non-smokers who avoid the sun have a life expectancy similar to the smokers in the sunniest group.
- Benefit: Swedish cohort of 29,500 women, 20 years of follow-up: relative to the group with the most sun exposure, sun avoiders had 0.6 to 2.1 years less life expectancy; the authors wrote that "non-smokers who avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy similar to smokers in the group with the greatest sun exposure."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: Contested. Sweden is high-latitude with little sunshine, so the conclusion doesn't transfer directly to China; people who avoid the sun may already be frail and housebound. Sun exposure raises skin cancer risk — don't burn, and still cover up in strong midday light.

### 28. Keep BMI between 20 and 25; lose weight if overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: the biggest willpower item on the list: long-term adherence to diet plus exercise, and keeping it off after losing it is harder still.
- In plain terms: the chance of dying is lowest at BMI 20–25; at BMI 27.5–30 it is about 20% higher, at 30–35 about 45% higher, and at 35–40 nearly double. East Asians are more sensitive to overweight — every 5 points of BMI adds about 40%.
- Benefit: individual-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies (observational, restricted to never-smokers with no chronic disease at baseline who survived at least 5 years): all-cause mortality lowest at BMI 20–25, 25–27.5 HR 1.07, 27.5–30 HR 1.20, 30–35 HR 1.45, 35–40 HR 1.94, 40–60 HR 2.76; above BMI 25, each 5 kg/m² in East Asian populations HR 1.39.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>
- Notes: Controversy: the "obesity paradox" side (Flegal KM et al. (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905>) pooled an HR of 0.94 for overweight and 0.95 for grade-1 obesity — i.e. mild overweight was slightly lower. The disagreement mainly stems from whether smokers, the already ill, and early follow-up (people seriously ill lose weight first) are excluded; after Global BMI excluded them, the risk of overweight reappeared. Also, no randomized trial has shown that weight loss itself lowers all-cause mortality; the benefit is inferred from cross-sectional comparison and cannot be taken directly as a discount "you get once you lose the weight."

### 29. Eat chili peppers more than four times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan.
- In plain terms: people who eat chili peppers more than four times a week are about 23% less likely to die over the same period than those who rarely eat them, and about a third less likely to die of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: Italian cohort of 22,800 people, median follow-up 8.2 years, 1,236 deaths: eating chili more than 4 times a week vs rarely, all-cause mortality hazard ratio 0.77 (95% CI 0.66–0.90), cardiovascular death 0.66 (0.50–0.86).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: Contested. An observational study set against a Mediterranean diet background — chili eaters may simply eat closer to the traditional pattern overall. People with gastroesophageal reflux, hemorrhoids, or irritable bowel will have worse symptoms from chili; no need to force it.

### 30. Have one or two servings of milk or yogurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: a few yuan a day.
- In plain terms: people who drink two or more servings of milk or yogurt a day are about 17% less likely to die over the same period than those who drink none.
- Benefit: PURE cohort, 136,000 people from 21 countries, mean follow-up 9.1 years, 6,796 deaths: two or more servings of dairy a day vs none, all-cause mortality hazard ratio 0.83 (95% CI 0.72–0.96, trend P=0.0052).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes: Contested. PURE includes many low- and middle-income countries, where drinking milk daily is itself a marker of economic means, and the confounding is hard to fully remove. People with lactose intolerance should switch to yogurt or low-lactose milk rather than forcing it down.

### 31. No need to give up eggs, but don't eat three or four a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan.
- In plain terms: each extra half whole egg a day raises the chance of dying over the same period by about 7%; swapping that half for egg white, fish, poultry, or nuts actually lowers every cause of death. No need to give up eggs — just don't eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: US cohort of 521,000 people, 129,000 deaths: each additional half whole egg a day, all-cause mortality hazard ratio 1.07 (95% CI 1.06–1.08); substituting an equivalent amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy, or nuts and legumes for that half whole egg gave lower all-cause, cardiovascular, cancer, and respiratory mortality.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Notes: Heavily contested. Other large cohorts and meta-analyses have found no association between eggs and mortality, and the US dietary guidelines have dropped the daily cholesterol cap. This item only argues against three or four a day; one egg a day is nothing to worry about.

### 32. Take a bath when you can, not just a quick shower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: water and electricity bills; half an hour each time.
- In plain terms: people who bathe almost every day have about 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than those who bathe less than twice a week, and about 46% lower risk of cerebral hemorrhage.
- Benefit: Japanese cohort of 30,000 people, 19 years of follow-up, 2,097 cardiovascular events: bathing almost every day vs no more than twice a week, hazard ratio for cardiovascular events 0.72 (95% CI 0.62–0.84), coronary heart disease 0.65 (0.45–0.94), stroke 0.74 (0.62–0.87), cerebral hemorrhage 0.54 (0.40–0.73).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Notes: Contested. A result from the Japanese population and bathtub culture; most households in China cannot bathe daily. Water that is too hot or baths that run too long are dangerous in themselves for the elderly and for people with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease — a large number of elderly Japanese die suddenly in the bathtub each year.
