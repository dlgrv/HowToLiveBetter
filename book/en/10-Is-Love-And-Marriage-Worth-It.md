# 10. Is dating and marriage worth it

This section splits "is marriage worth it" into separate accounts for money, time, health, legal risk, and relationship quality, and counts them apart. Each account uses only figures whose source can be checked. Sources are limited to official statistics, or to meta-analyses (pooling data from many studies and recalculating them). There is no moral judgment here, and no conclusion is made for you. The health accounts are counted as death risk, the time accounts as how many minutes a day, and the legal accounts as what the articles say. These three ways of counting are not converted into each other. The method for splitting the accounts, a fill-in checklist, and common mistakes are in [chapter 10 “Is dating and marriage worth it”](10-Is-Love-And-Marriage-Worth-It.md).

Items in this section are grouped by topic below, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Finding someone and dating**: meet more people (item 1), stop after the other person clearly refuses (item 2), judge interest from behaviour (item 3), take turns answering questions that go from light to deep (item 5), a long-distance relationship is not worse than one in the same city (item 6).

**Whether the relationship is good**: look at how you feel inside it (item 4), argue about the matter not the person (item 17), go to couples therapy together (item 20).

**The accounts of whether marriage is worth it**: look at the registration figures first (item 7), discount the health benefit because the data are observational (item 8), count the time account as unpaid labour (item 9), count the cost of leaving (item 15), keep a separate account of marrying for your elders (item 16), when divorcing write child support and visitation clearly (item 23).

**Registering and the premarital check**: registering needs only an identity card (item 13), have one premarital medical check (item 14), go and get the marriage certificate even after the banquet (item 21).

**Money and debt**: look first at the law's default rules for the money account (item 10), when parents pay for a home write down whether it is a loan or a gift (item 11), a large sum borrowed by one spouse (item 12), a spouse using joint money to tip streamers or transfer to someone outside the marriage (item 22).

**Same-sex partners and sexual orientation**: same-sex partners should put voluntary guardianship and a will in place (item 18), do not marry by hiding sexual orientation (item 19).
### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on just one: whether two people click cannot be predicted beforehand
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. Spend your time meeting several people rather than obsessing over the same person. The hard part is resisting the urge to fixate on just one.
- In plain terms: In two speed-dating studies, participants filled out over 100 partner-preference survey questions before meeting. The questionnaires could predict who tended to like people easily and who was broadly popular, but they could not predict whether two specific people would actually click. You only know if you have chemistry once you meet in person, so it makes sense to meet more people.
- Benefit: Across two speed-dating studies, participants completed more than 100 partner-related self-report measures (self-rated questionnaires) prior to meeting. Researchers analyzed the data using random forest models (a pattern-finding algorithm). The models could predict "how likely someone is overall to like others," explaining 4%–18% of the variance (variance refers to differences between individuals, and the surveys explained only a small fraction). They could also predict "how generally desirable someone is," explaining 7%–27% of the variance. However, they could not predict whether two specific individuals would click—no combination of pre-meeting traits and preferences could predict unique dyadic attraction. An earlier speed-dating experiment by the same team found that self-reported ideal partner preferences (such as valuing physical attractiveness or earning prospects) failed to predict actual interest in specific candidates met in person.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478-1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580>；Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245-264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: These studies only show that chemistry cannot be predicted before meeting, not that effort is pointless. Because mutual attraction can only be discovered face-to-face, investing time into meeting multiple people in real life is far more effective than betting everything on one prospect before meeting. The study participants were Western college students; dating cultures differ elsewhere, so apply these findings with appropriate context.

### 2. Stop after a clear refusal — continued pursuit is a public-order offence, not “sincerity”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money and no time. The hard part is stopping: no more messages, no more waiting for the person.
- In plain terms: If the other person has clearly refused and you still send messages repeatedly or wait for them, and you interfere with their normal life, from January (1) 2026 that is a public-order offence. You can be detained for up to 5 days, or fined up to 1000 yuan. For more serious cases, detention is 5 to 10 days. Police can also order you not to contact the person; violating that brings another 5 to 10 days of detention.
- Benefit: The Public Security Administration Punishments Law, in force from 1 January (1) 2026, lists entanglement and stalking as a separate item. The text covers “repeatedly sending obscene, insulting, or threatening information, or using harassment, entanglement, stalking, or similar methods to interfere with another person’s normal life.” The act is punishable by detention of up to 5 days, or a fine of up to 1000 yuan. For more serious cases, detention of 5 to 10 days, and a fine of up to 1000 yuan may be added. There is a further layer: with approval from a public-security responsible official, the actor can be ordered not to contact the victim for a set period. Violating that order brings another 5 to 10 days of detention.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2025 修订). 治安管理处罚法（第五十条第一款第五项、第二款，第一百三十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>（最高人民检察院转载）
- Notes: “Chasing to the end will move the other person” has no research support, but detention and no-contact orders are in black and white. Such offence records are to be sealed and not provided or made public to ordinary units and individuals, though relevant units may query them under state rules. The law looks at whether you repeatedly harassed, entangled, or stalked and interfered with the person’s normal life, not at what you meant. Continuing after a clear refusal is the situation most easily treated as interference; that last sentence is the author’s judgment.

### 3. Gauge someone's interest by their actions, not just their "signals": who initiates, who plans, who follows up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs no money and very little time. The hard part is changing habits: stop overanalyzing expressions and tone of voice, and track objective facts instead.
- In plain terms: When strangers chat for about ten minutes and try to read each other, they are fairly accurate at recognizing when the other person is not flirting, but remarkably bad at telling when they are. Do not jump to conclusions based purely on facial expressions and tone in a single conversation. People who feel stronger attraction are indeed more likely to message first and ask to meet up later, though that link is fairly weak.
- Benefit: Two studies tested how accurately people detect flirting. The first had 52 pairs of unacquainted men and women converse for 10 to 12 minutes. Afterward, each reported whether they had been flirting and whether they thought their partner was flirting. Interactions where no flirting took place were judged much more accurately, whereas interactions where flirting did occur were judged far worse. The second study showed 26 one-minute video clips to 261 outside observers, yielding consistent findings: people reliably tell when someone is not flirting, but struggle to spot when they are. A third study looked at actual behavior, combining data from three speed-dating events with 559 participants and over 6,600 dates, followed over time. The more uniquely someone liked a date during the meeting, the more likely they were to message them and ask them out later, with an average correlation around 0.15 (a weak association). This indicates that romantic interest does translate into concrete actions like initiating contact and planning dates. However, that study did not directly compare whether actions predict interest better than facial expressions or tone.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939-958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972>；Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70-77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727>（争议方）；Baxter A, Maxwell JA, Bales KL, Finkel EJ, Impett EA, Eastwick PW (2022). Initial impressions of compatibility and mate value predict later dating and romantic interest. PNAS, 119(45), e2206925119. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2206925119>
- Notes: Debate exists. Other research suggests that men may not be overestimating women's sexual intent; rather, women may underreport their own intent on questionnaires. Exactly who judges more accurately remains unsettled. Neither of the first two studies tested behavioral cues. The third study confirms that attraction translates into texting and scheduling dates, but no study has directly compared whether watching behavior is more accurate than reading expressions and tone—that remains the author's inference. The author recommends watching whether the other person initiates, whether they arrange the next meeting, and whether they follow up when you do not push forward.

### 4. Whether a relationship is good depends mainly on how you feel in it, not on the other person’s attributes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money, and little time. The hard part is changing the angle: put away the partner checklist and look at how you get along.
- In plain terms: What best shows whether a relationship is good is how you feel, not the other person’s attributes. For example, whether you feel the other person is serious and appreciates you, and how much you argue. Your own feelings are two to four times as useful as what your partner says. But whether the relationship later gets better or worse, basically no questionnaire can calculate.
- Benefit: A machine-learning analysis of 29 labs and 43 longitudinal paired datasets — longitudinal means following the same couples over time — found that the strongest predictors of relationship quality all came from how each person felt about the relationship: sensed commitment from the other, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, sensing the other’s satisfaction, and conflict. Among person-level items the strongest were life satisfaction, negative emotion, depression, avoidant attachment, and anxious attachment. These relationship items explained at most 45% of the variance at the start of the survey. Variance here is the difference in relationship quality between couples; 45% is the part those items can account for. By the end of the study they explained at most 18%. And the predictive power of “items you report yourself” was two to four times that of “items your partner reports.” Adding individual differences and the partner’s reports on top of your own relationship feelings added no further predictive power. Whether the relationship got better or worse during the study was largely unpredictable from any set of self-report measures; the original wording is “largely unpredictable from any combination of self-report variables.”
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061-19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: To judge whether this relationship is good right now, asking how you feel is more accurate than checking the other person’s attribute list, but do not expect to predict how it will go later. The account that links relationship quality to physical health is in item 17 of this section (treat relationship quality as a health account).

### 5. Take turns answering questions that get gradually deeper to build closeness—skip conversation scripts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Free. Takes 45 minutes.
- In plain terms: When two strangers take turns answering 36 questions that get progressively deeper, they feel closer after 45 minutes than people who spent the same time making small talk. What makes it work better than small talk is taking turns and opening up layer by layer. This only shows that people feel closer in the moment, not that it will develop into a lasting relationship.
- Benefit: Pairs of strangers took turns answering three sets of increasingly personal questions, totaling 36 questions, ranging from "Given the choice of anyone in the world, whom would you want as a dinner guest?" to "When did you last cry in front of another person?" After 45 minutes, their reported closeness was significantly higher than that of a control group engaging in small talk for the same duration, and even surpassed the average closeness ratings college students in the study gave to their closest real-life relationships.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363-377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: The experiment was conducted only once, with college students, and measured only immediate closeness without assessing whether it led to long-term relationships. In the study, closeness arose from mutual vulnerability—both people taking turns sharing genuine, increasingly personal details—not conversational scripts, pickup tactics, or one-sided performances. The practical takeaway for conversation is: "I share something real, then ask something equally deep," taking turns back and forth.

### 6. Long-distance relationships are not worse than same-city ones; the real hurdle is the first three months after moving in together
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Travel costs, plus time on the road. You meet less often than same-city couples. The hard part is lasting through not seeing each other.
- In plain terms: While apart, long-distance relationships are actually more stable; research attributes this to idealizing the other person more than reality, and the less you meet the more idealized they become. The real hurdle is after you move in together. In follow-up data about half of long-distance couples reached the same-city step; the other half ended while still apart. Of the half who reunited, one third broke up within 3 months.
- Benefit: Two studies reach the same conclusion. Long-distance relationships are more stable while apart than same-city ones. The reason is that idealization of the partner is markedly higher in long-distance relationships; the studies measured idealized distortion, romantic love, relationship memories, and perceived agreement. And the less often you meet, the more idealized the picture. But that stability holds only while both sides remain apart; once they become same-city, former long-distance couples are more likely to break up. The study that tracked long-distance to same-city gave these shares: about half of long-distance couples reached moving in together; the other half ended while still apart. Of the half who reunited, one third broke up within 3 months after reunion. Changes participants reported after reunion included: loss of autonomous space, knowing more of both the good and the bad about the other person, hard-to-arrange time, and more conflict and jealousy. Longer gaps between meetings while apart and stronger idealization meant less stability after reunion.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901-919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472>；Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37-54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578>；Dargie E 等 (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181-202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367>（备注里那项异地研究）
- Notes: The sample was romantic relationships among US university students, not marriages, and from an earlier period; do not apply the absolute shares directly. Another study surveyed 717 long-distance and 425 same-city people. Quality differences between the two kinds of relationship were small; whether a relationship is good depends mainly on each person’s situation and the relationship itself, not on distance. So when deciding on long distance, do not use “long distance always ends” as the reason. What to prepare for is the months after reunion, when autonomous space shrinks, mutual knowledge grows, and conflict and jealousy also grow.

### 7. Look at the registration figures before listening to elders: what the marriage rate and divorce rate each mean
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Read the marriage-registration paragraph in the Ministry of Civil Affairs annual bulletin; a few minutes.
- In plain terms: In 2024 there were 6106000 marriage registrations nationwide, down about one fifth from the year before, and 3513000 divorces. Online claims that the divorce rate is over half take these two numbers and divide them, about 57.5%. But the people divorcing and the people marrying are not the same group, so it cannot be read as half of marriages ending. The bulletin’s marriage rate of 4.3‰ and divorce rate of 2.5‰ are divided by the national population.
- Benefit: In 2024, 6106000 couples completed marriage registration nationwide under law, down 20.5% from the previous year, with a marriage rate of 4.3‰. In the same year, 3513000 couples completed divorce procedures under law, of which 2622000 were divorce registrations at civil-affairs departments and 891000 were court judgments or mediated divorces. The divorce rate was 2.5‰. The bulletin notes that the marriage (divorce) rate = number of marriage (divorce) couples that year / average total population that year × 1000‰. This is a “crude rate”; the denominator is the whole population, not the married population.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:民政部 (2025). 2024 年民政事业发展统计公报（五（二）1 婚姻登记服务、注释 5），网页版与 PDF 版. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html>、<https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>
- Notes: The common online claim of a “divorce rate above 50%” divides that year’s divorce couples by that year’s marriage couples, 351.3 / 610.6 ≈ 57.5%. That is a “divorce-to-marriage ratio.” The two numbers are not the same people, and the bulletin has no such indicator, so it cannot be read as “half of marriages end.” To calculate “the probability that a marriage eventually ends in divorce,” you have to follow the same cohort of marriages over time; this section found no official data of that kind.

### 8. Factor in the health benefits of marriage, but discount them as observational data
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Zero money and zero time. Just keep one thing in mind: these figures simply show that married people have lower death rates, meaning the two things happen together, so we cannot tell whether marriage itself actually makes you live longer.
- In plain terms: Among older adults, married people have about a 12% lower risk of dying compared to unmarried people. Looking across all ages, people who have never married face a higher risk of death than those who are married, though the gap shrinks with age. Still, this is merely a correlation—there is no telling whether marriage itself is what makes the difference. Furthermore, these figures come mainly from Western countries and should only be taken as a rough order-of-magnitude benchmark.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of older adults pooling multiple studies covered 53 comparisons and over 250,000 participants. All pooled studies were cohort studies that simply tracked participants over time, with no randomized control groups. Comparing married to unmarried individuals, the all-cause mortality RR was 0.88 (95% CI: 0.85–0.91; RR stands for relative risk, CI for confidence interval), representing about a 12% lower risk of death. Within the same study, RRs were 1.11 for widowed, 1.16 for divorced or separated, and 1.11 for never married—about 11%, 16%, and 11% higher, respectively. There was no difference between men and women. The most conservative estimate was an RR of 0.94, representing a reduction of only about 6%. Another meta-analysis comparing unmarried (never married) to married people pooled 95 publications with over 500 million participants. It found an HR of 1.24 (1.19–1.30), meaning the risk of death at any given moment was about 24% higher. This is a ratio of instantaneous hazard rates and does not equal a 24% higher cumulative probability of dying over the same period. Furthermore, the older the age, the smaller the gap. A third meta-analysis broken down by sex included 21 studies and over 7.89 million participants: for both men and women, being unmarried coincided with higher overall mortality, cancer mortality, and cardiovascular mortality. The gaps in cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality were wider in men. Compared to unmarried women, unmarried men had 31% more stroke deaths and 9% more all-cause deaths.
- Evidence grade: A (contested)
- Sources:Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Soc Sci Med 64:77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031>；Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. Am J Epidemiol 174(4):379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111>；Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Glob Health Res Policy 5:4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: The controversy lies in selection bias: people who get married may already be healthier to begin with. This is an inference made by the book's author based on study design, as none of the three meta-analyses quantified how much selection bias accounts for the observed difference. All three meta-analyses relied on observational data from existing populations, tracking participants over time without randomized control groups. The study authors themselves noted publication bias or methodological limitations. Roelfs also found that the more rigorously a study was conducted, the smaller the observed gap. Because these numbers are derived primarily from North American and European populations, they should only be used as a rough order-of-magnitude benchmark.

### 9. Discussing labor division before marriage helps clarify unpaid work responsibilities
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: There is no monetary cost. Spend one or two hours before marriage to reach a clear agreement on how household chores, childcare, and elder care — all unpaid tasks — will be divided between partners, then follow that plan. The real challenge lies in having the conversation, not in avoiding it.

- In plain terms: The national time-use survey measured unpaid work such as housework and accompanying or caring for family. Among people who did such work that day, men averaged 1 hour and 52 minutes a day, and women 3 hours and 29 minutes. The survey was not broken down by marital status, so there is no official figure for how much housework increases after marriage; this gender gap can only serve as an upper-bound reference. Division of labor can be negotiated into a different shape before you register.

- Benefit: The 2024 National Time Use Survey tracks unpaid labor, which includes housework and family care. On average, people who perform such tasks spend 2 hours and 45 minutes daily on them; men dedicate 1 hour and 52 minutes, women 3 hours and 29 minutes. The proportion of individuals engaging in unpaid labor each day is 67.5% among men and 83.9% among women. Nationwide, residents spend an average of 1 hour and 17 minutes daily on housework; among those who actually do it, the average rises to 1 hour and 59 minutes — a decrease of 28 minutes compared to 2018. Time spent caring for family averages 30 minutes per day; for those involved, it reaches 1 hour and 46 minutes. In 2018, men spent 45 minutes on housework and 17 minutes on childcare, while women spent 2 hours and 6 minutes on housework and 53 minutes on childcare.

- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:国家统计局 (2024). 第三次全国时间利用调查公报（第二号、第三号）. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html>、<https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html>；国家统计局 (2019). 2018 年全国时间利用调查公报. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>

- Notes: Both surveys analyzed data only by gender, urban/rural residence, and age; marital status was not considered. Consequently, no official statistics exist on how much housework increases after marriage; the gender disparity can only be used as a rough upper bound. These figures represent national averages, but couples are free to negotiate a division of labor that suits them — and that is precisely the value of having this discussion.

### 10. For marriage’s money account, look first at the law’s default rules, then decide whether to make a written agreement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Read the Civil Code marriage-and-family book articles on property. If needed, the two of you sign a written property agreement; the law only requires it in writing, not notarization. The hard part is talking about money in advance.
- In plain terms: Without a written agreement, the law’s default is that wages and bonuses earned after marriage, business and investment returns, and inherited or gifted property are jointly owned. Premarital property, and property a will or gift contract says is for one side only, is personal. The two of you can agree in writing that each keeps their own; once signed it binds. The side that put in more for raising children or caring for elders can claim compensation at divorce.
- Benefit: The default rules without a separate agreement are: wages, bonuses, and labor remuneration earned after marriage, returns from production, business, and investment, intellectual-property returns, and inherited or gifted property belong to both spouses jointly (Article 1062). Premarital property, personal-injury compensation, property a will or gift contract clearly gives to one side only, and articles for one person’s exclusive daily use are personal property (Article 1063). The two sides may agree in writing that premarital and postmarital property belongs to each separately, to both jointly, or partly separately and partly jointly. That agreement binds both sides in law (Article 1065). A side that bears more duty because of raising children, caring for elders, or assisting the other side’s work has the right to claim compensation at divorce (Article 1088).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人民代表大会 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典（2021 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第一千零六十二、一千零六十三、一千零六十五、一千零八十八条）. 国家法律法规数据库 <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>；条文全文见最高人民法院公报转载 <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>
- Notes: There are no official statistics for betrothal gifts, weddings, or marital homes; this item does not write figures. How much compensation under Article 1088 is first negotiated by the two sides; if they cannot agree the court decides; there is no fixed formula. A written property agreement helps both sides: the higher earner can use it, and so can the side that does more unpaid housework and care.

### 11. When parents help buy a home, state clearly on the transfer whether it is a gift or a loan
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. On the day the funds are transferred, write a one-page statement specifying whether the money is a gift to someone or a loan, and have both the contributing parents and the child sign it. If it is treated as a loan, have both spouses sign, and write the exact same phrasing in the transfer memo.
- In plain terms: Money parents put toward buying a home before marriage defaults to a gift to their own child, while money contributed after marriage counts as joint marital property unless agreed otherwise. Starting in February 2025, if one spouse's parents fully fund the purchase after marriage without explicitly stating it is only for their child, the court may award the property to that child upon divorce. The court will then decide whether and how much to compensate the other spouse. If the money is intended as a loan, write it down clearly on the transfer day and have both spouses sign.
- Benefit: Where parents contribute toward buying a home for a couple before marriage, the contribution is deemed a personal gift to their own child, unless the parents explicitly state it is a gift to both. For contributions made after marriage, any explicit agreement governs; if there is no agreement or the agreement is unclear, it is handled under the principles of joint marital property. Starting February 1, 2025, an additional rule applies: where one spouse's parents fully fund the purchase during marriage and the gift agreement does not explicitly state it is only for their own child, the court may award ownership of the home to that child upon divorce. After awarding ownership, the court weighs several factors to determine whether and how much compensation to grant the other spouse: the duration of cohabitation and mutual children raised, fault in the divorce, relative contributions to the household, and the market value of the property at the time of divorce. If one side's parents contribute partially or both sides contribute, ownership is awarded to one party based on the funding sources and proportions, with reasonable compensation awarded to the other (national).
- Evidence grade: A

### 12. A large sum one spouse borrows is not automatically your debt if you did not sign and did not later acknowledge it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Do not sign a spouse’s loan note or guarantee as “co-borrower” or “guarantor.” Also do not write in chat “we will repay this” or similar; that can be treated as you later acknowledging the debt. The hard part is not answering when pressed.
- In plain terms: Money a spouse borrowed behind your back is not automatically your debt if it clearly exceeds ordinary household spending and you did not sign and did not later acknowledge it. The creditor must prove the money went to joint life or joint business. But for home-purchase money that really became a home you live in together or own jointly, the other side can often prove that layer.
- Benefit: Debt in the following situations is joint marital debt. First, both spouses signed. Second, one side later acknowledged it — both accept the debt. Third, one side during the marriage borrowed in their own name for ordinary family daily needs. Money one side borrowed in their own name that clearly exceeds ordinary household spending is not joint marital debt. The exception is when the creditor (the party seeking repayment) can prove the money was used for the couple’s joint life, joint production or business, or was acknowledged by both. Home-purchase money clearly exceeds ordinary daily family needs; the burden of proof is on the side asserting the claim — the creditor must produce evidence. But if that money really became a home the couple live in together or own jointly, the creditor can often prove the “used for joint life” layer. So what actually helps is the step in item 11 (when parents contribute, write whether it is a loan or a gift) (nationwide, from 2021).

### 13. Registering needs only an identity card plus a signed statement — no household register, no fee, certificate issued on the spot

The statement says you have no spouse, and that you and the other person are not lineal blood relatives or collateral blood relatives within three generations. ### 14. Get a premarital checkup and disclose any major illness before marriage registration
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free or subsidized for eligible age groups in most regions. Out-of-pocket costs are only a few hundred yuan. Takes half a day.
- In plain terms: If you have a major illness and do not honestly inform your partner before registering the marriage, they can sue to annul the marriage within 1 year of the date they knew or should have known about it. A first-instance trial under standard procedure is required by rule to conclude within 6 months, though it can be extended, and you generally cover your own legal fees. Premarital checkups have been voluntary rather than a prerequisite for getting a marriage certificate since 2003. Costs depend on local policy, and it takes half a day.
- Benefit: The legal consequences of concealment are clear. The revised Marriage Registration Regulations state that "if one party suffers from a major illness, they shall truthfully inform the other party prior to marriage registration; if they fail to do so, the other party may request a people's court to annul the marriage pursuant to Article 1053 of the Civil Code." The state treats premarital medical examinations as the primary defense against birth defects. The current national targets specify that "the premarital medical examination rate and the target population coverage rate for pre-pregnancy eugenic health examinations shall remain above 70% and 80%, respectively." Policy documents also require "promoting the co-location or close proximity of premarital examination facilities and marriage registration offices for public convenience" and promote a "one-stop" service combining marriage registration, premarital medical exams, and reproductive counseling. Annulment requires court litigation: regular first-instance procedures are prescribed to conclude within 6 months, which can be extended, and legal fees are generally paid out of pocket. A premarital checkup is not a prerequisite for obtaining a marriage certificate. In a 2022 response to a CPPCC proposal, the Tai'an Municipal Health Commission stated: "While the 2003 Marriage Registration Regulations abolished mandatory premarital checkups, they explicitly required marriage registration authorities not to impose additional obligations." ### 15. Calculate your exit costs: Uncontested divorce has a 30-day cooling-off period, while contested divorce requires statutory grounds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Take a little time before marriage to read through this: if you ever need an exit, how long will it take and what procedure must be followed? The hard part is thinking about breaking up before you even marry.
- In plain terms: An uncontested divorce is not done just by signing papers: within 30 days of submitting the application, either spouse can withdraw it. Once that period ends, both spouses must appear in person within 30 days to collect the divorce certificate; failing to show up is treated as a withdrawal. If your spouse does not consent, filing a lawsuit is the only path. The court attempts mediation first; if mediation fails and statutory grounds exist—such as domestic violence, bigamy, or living apart for at least two full years—the court must grant the divorce.
- Benefit: An uncontested divorce requires signing a written divorce agreement and applying in person at the marriage registration authority (Article 1076). Within 30 days from the date the registration authority receives the application, either party may withdraw the application. Within 30 days after the cooling-off period expires, both parties must appear in person to apply for the divorce certificate; failure to apply is deemed a withdrawal of the divorce application (Article 1077). If one party does not consent, the other must file a lawsuit, and the court must conduct mediation first. If mediation fails and any of the following circumstances exist, divorce shall be granted: bigamy or cohabiting with another person; domestic violence, maltreatment, or desertion; unrepentant gambling or drug abuse despite repeated admonition; or living apart due to emotional discord for at least two full years. If the court denies a divorce and the spouses live apart for another full year, a subsequent divorce lawsuit shall be granted (Article 1079). In 2024, there were 3.513 million divorces nationwide in China, of which 891,000 were resolved through court judgments or court-mediated settlements.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人民代表大会 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典（第一千零七十六、一千零七十七、一千零七十九条）. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>；民政部 (2020). 关于贯彻落实《中华人民共和国民法典》中有关婚姻登记规定的通知（民发〔2020〕116 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-12/04/content_5567010.htm>；民政部 (2025). 2024 年民政事业发展统计公报. 题录同第 7 条（结婚率、离婚率各是什么口径）
- Notes: The cooling-off period applies only to uncontested divorces by mutual agreement. Contested divorces through the courts do not have a cooling-off period, but the process takes much longer. Court fees and attorney fees vary widely by region, so specific figures are omitted here. High exit costs do not mean you should avoid marriage, but you should understand these realities before getting married. If children are involved, see Item 23 of this chapter (Child support and visitation) for how to draft child support and visitation arrangements.

### 16. Keep a separate ledger for "marrying for your elders"—do not mix it with your own

In the other column, list what your elders gain: peace of mind, saving face, and an end to nagging. The hard part is keeping the elders' column separate and not folding it into your own.

Meanwhile, the time spent, the money spent, and the cost of walking away are borne entirely by you. Splitting what belongs to you from what belongs to them into two columns on paper makes it immediately clear whether the trade-off is worth it.
- Benefit: The elders' gains are real. But they do not automatically translate into your health benefits (Item 8) or relationship quality (Item 4: relationship satisfaction depends on how you personally feel). Meanwhile, the time ledger (Item 9), financial ledger (Items 10 to 12), and exit costs (Item 15) are borne entirely by the person entering the marriage. ### 17. Treat relationship quality as a health account: argue about the matter not the person — no name-calling, no sarcasm
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. When arguing, speak only about the matter at hand — no name-calling, no sarcasm, no dredging up old scores. The hard part is holding your tongue when angry.
- In plain terms: Couples with a better relationship tend to have somewhat better health and somewhat lower death risk. The association is not large — about as large as diet’s link to health. In one experiment, couples who kept hostility in arguments had experimentally made small wounds heal at only about 60% the speed of calmer couples.
- Benefit: Pooling 126 studies and more than 72000 people (a meta-analysis). Higher marital quality went with better physical health; the correlation r was between .07 and .21. A correlation of 0 is no relationship; 1 is perfect lockstep. The correlation with lower death risk was r = .11. Smaller cardiovascular reaction when arguing was r = -.13. The authors said these effects are all small — about as large as the link between habits such as diet and health. In another crossover experiment, 42 healthy couples each stayed in hospital twice for 24 hours. Once they had a mutually supportive talk; once they discussed a disagreement. After arguing, experimentally made blister wounds healed more slowly. Couples who showed more hostility in both sessions had wound healing at only 60% the speed of low-hostility couples. On the morning after arguing, their blood inflammation markers (IL-6, tumor necrosis factor) also rose more.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Robles TF, Slatcher RB, Trombello JM, McGinn MM (2014). Marital quality and health: a meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031859>；Kiecolt-Glaser JK 等 (2005). Hostile marital interactions, proinflammatory cytokine production, and wound healing. Archives of General Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.62.12.1377>
- Notes: Grade B because most pooled studies were follow-up only, with no randomized comparison groups. So you cannot tell whether a bad relationship makes people ill, or poor health makes the relationship worse. Some studies still found the association after subtracting age and income. Robles’s original text also notes publication bias in some results. Studies that clearly compared men and women mostly found no sex difference. This section found no pooled analysis that directly compared mortality for “a bad marriage” versus “not marrying.” So “a bad marriage hurts more than not marrying” is only an inference. The wound experiment had only 42 couples. “Argue about the matter not the person — no name-calling, no sarcasm” is the author’s practice inferred from “hostile behaviour” in the experiment; no study compared it head-on. If you argue fiercely and cannot change, go to couples therapy together — see item 20 of this section (couples therapy). If arguing turns to hitting, or long-term insults and threats, it is no longer a communication problem; it is domestic violence — see Section 8, item 43 (when you are subjected to domestic violence). To think through whether to leave, the cost of leaving is in item 15 of this section (cost of leaving).

### 18. Set up a power of attorney, voluntary guardianship, and a will while both of you are clearheaded: by law you are not close relatives, and without them you have no signing rights or inheritance rights
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Notarizing a voluntary guardianship agreement and a will at a notary public varies by region, starting at several hundred yuan. Writing a handwritten will yourself costs nothing. It takes one or two half-days in total. The hard part is sitting down together to talk about serious illness and death.
- In plain terms: Under the law, close relatives include spouses, parents, and children; an unmarried or same-sex partner does not count. Without legal paperwork, if one partner falls critically ill, the other cannot make medical decisions for them. If one partner dies, the other is not a legal heir; without a will, the surviving partner can only fight for a share of the estate by arguing they depended on the deceased for support or provided substantial care. Three documents cover each stage: a power of attorney handles specific day-to-day matters while you are alert, voluntary guardianship takes over if you lose mental capacity, and a will takes care of things after death.
- Benefit: Article 1045 of the Civil Code defines close relatives as spouses, parents, children, siblings, paternal grandparents, maternal grandparents, paternal grandchildren, and maternal grandchildren. Article 1127 lists statutory heirs: first in line are spouses, children, and parents; second in line are siblings, paternal grandparents, and maternal grandparents. A partner is on neither list. Because a partner is not an heir, without a will they can only seek a share under Article 1131. This article provides that individuals other than heirs who "depended on the deceased for support" or who "provided substantial support" to the deceased "may be allotted an appropriate share of the estate," with the exact amount decided by a judge. Article 1219 governs surgeries, special examinations, and special treatments: if medical staff cannot or should not explain the situation to the patient directly, they must explain it to the patient's "close relatives" and obtain their consent. Three legal pathways can bridge these gaps. First, Article 33 allows an adult to appoint a guardian in advance through a written agreement with "other individuals willing to act as guardian." Once the person loses full or partial capacity for civil conduct, this appointed individual serves as guardian, acting as their legal representative. Second, Article 165 stipulates that a written power of attorney must state the agent's name, authorized matters, scope of authority, and validity period, and must be signed or stamped by the principal. Article 173 lists grounds for terminating an agency (such as the agent losing capacity for civil conduct, or the death of either the agent or the principal), but the principal's own loss of capacity is not listed as an automatic termination trigger. There are also post-death exceptions under Article 174 (for example, if the agent did not know and should not have known about the principal's death, or if the heirs ratify the action, actions taken by the agent remain valid). Third, Article 1133 allows an individual to make a will bequeathing personal property to individuals outside the circle of statutory heirs. An article by the Yichang Justice Bureau published on the website of the Department of Justice of Hubei Province confirms that same-sex partners can notarize a voluntary guardianship agreement, appointing each other as future guardians (nationwide).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第二十三、三十三、一百六十五、一百七十三、一百七十四、一千零四十五、一千一百二十四、一千一百二十七、一千一百三十一、一千一百三十三至一千一百四十、一千一百四十二、一千二百一十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>（国家法律法规数据库）；宜昌市司法局 (2021). 意定监护公证：通向彩虹的公证之路. 湖北省司法厅网站「实践荟萃」栏目. <https://sft.hubei.gov.cn/fbjd/sjhc/202101/t20210108_3208602.shtml>
- Notes: Under a will, a partner inherits in the legal role of a "legatee" (recipient of a bequest), not an heir. Once you learn of the bequest, you must explicitly state that you accept it within 60 days; remaining silent is legally deemed a refusal. The partner and anyone with an interest in the estate cannot serve as witnesses to the will. If multiple wills are made, the last valid will takes precedence (see Section 17, Item 2: Later wills supersede earlier ones). Hospital practices vary on whether a voluntary guardian can directly sign surgical consent forms; ask the hospital you visit regularly in advance. If biological family members challenge these documents, resolving the dispute requires litigation: standard first-instance civil proceedings are supposed to conclude within 6 months by rule (extensions are possible), and legal fees are paid out of pocket. Therefore, notarize these documents whenever possible. For joint home purchases or pooled savings, draft a separate written agreement reflecting each person's actual financial contribution. For the practical steps to establish voluntary guardianship, see Section 17, Item 1 (Designating a future guardian in writing). Beneficiaries are yourself and your partner.

### 19. Do not marry by hiding sexual orientation, and do not treat a marriage of convenience as an easy fix: in law it is a real marriage, and the only exit is divorce

### 20. Go to couples therapy together when stuck in recurring fights and silent treatment (except in domestic violence): waiting it out rarely fixes things on its own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Costs money. Charged per session, varying by location and clinic, so no specific figure is given here. In research studies, sessions typically occurred once a week; one trial ran for about 8 months. The hardest part is that both partners must attend and commit to finishing.
- In plain terms: Couples stuck in distress who complete couples therapy together become noticeably more satisfied with their relationship, whereas those just waiting on a waitlist barely change. But the effect can fade over time: in one trial followed to year 5, about half remained noticeably improved, while about a quarter had separated or divorced. None of this applies if there is domestic violence—do not attend together.
- Benefit: A 2020 meta-analysis pooled 58 studies and 2,092 couples. After completing couples therapy, the change in relationship satisfaction before and after treatment was g = 1.12 (95% CI 0.92–1.31), which the authors characterized as a large effect size (g is an effect size metric; see the README glossary). For couples assigned to a waitlist over the same period, g = 0.12 (−0.04 to 0.29), showing no significant improvement. Couples who started out with worse relationship distress actually showed greater improvement. A 2012 review noted that couples therapy brings improvement to about 70% of couples who receive treatment. Another randomized trial followed 134 couples with chronic, severe marital discord who underwent about 8 months of two forms of behavioral couples therapy. At completion, satisfaction changes were d = 0.90 and 0.71. By year 5, 50.0% and 45.9% of couples in the two approaches, respectively, still showed noticeable improvement (defined by the authors as "clinically significant improvement"). At the same time, 25.7% and 27.9% had separated or divorced. Domestic violence requires a different approach: a 2016 meta-analysis specified that conclusions apply only to mild-to-moderate situational couple violence arising from conflict, and exclude violence characterized by coercive control or severe physical harm.

The 2020 meta-analysis included only heterosexual couples and found no domestic randomized trials in China, so take these findings with a grain of salt. Where to seek help in China: mental health law stipulates that psychotherapy must be conducted in medical institutions, and psychological counselors are not permitted to practice psychotherapy. Inquire at hospital psychiatry or clinical psychology departments about couples therapy. When seeking external counseling centers outside hospitals, verify credentials and professional training beforehand. If domestic violence is present, do not do therapy together right away: the 2016 meta-analysis noted that conclusions apply only to mild-to-moderate situational couple violence, excluding severe physical harm or coercive control; clinicians also worry about retaliatory violence at home after sessions. If you have experienced physical violence, see Section 8, Item 43 (domestic violence). Beneficiaries: yourself and your partner.

### 21. Register your marriage officially even after holding a wedding ceremony: without registration you are not legal spouses, and if your partner dies you have no spousal inheritance rights
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Free. Both partners go to the marriage registration office together; takes a few dozen minutes.
- In plain terms: If you only held a wedding without registering after February 1994, the law does not recognize you as spouses, only as cohabitants. If your partner dies, you cannot inherit their estate as a surviving spouse. If you break up, courts will not treat it as a divorce either. Registering retroactively fixes this completely, and legal validity dates back to when you first qualified.
- Benefit: The Civil Code stipulates that "a marital relationship is established upon completion of marriage registration. Those who have not registered their marriage shall complete retroactive registration." The Supreme People's Court judicial interpretation categorizes couples living together as husband and wife without registration into two groups: those who met marriage requirements before February 1, 1994 are treated as de facto marriages; after that date, courts "shall inform them to complete retroactive marriage registration." If they do not, it is treated as cohabitation. If one partner dies and the other claims inheritance as a spouse, this same rule applies. Because a spouse is a first-order heir, an unregistered partner is excluded. For cohabitation, courts do not accept petitions merely to "dissolve" the relationship, hearing only disputes over property division and child custody. Once retroactively registered, marriage validity "is calculated from the date both parties met the substantive requirements for marriage prescribed by the Civil Code." Children are unaffected: the inheritance section of the Civil Code explicitly defines "children" to include children born outside of marriage, who are first-order heirs alongside marital children.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千零四十九、一千一百二十七、一千一百三十一条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2020). 关于适用《民法典》婚姻家庭编的解释（一）（法释〔2020〕22 号，第三、六、七、八条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html>
- Notes: If your partner dies before registering, you are not an heir, but you are not necessarily left with nothing. The Civil Code provides that an "appropriate share of the estate may be distributed" to a person who provided "substantial maintenance and care" to the deceased. However, you must present evidence to fight for this yourself, and if talks break down, you must litigate. Ordinary first-instance civil procedures are scheduled to conclude within 6 months (subject to extension), legal fees are out-of-pocket, and the judge decides the amount. It is far better to complete retroactive registration while both partners are alive. Retroactive registration is identical to initial registration: both parties must appear in person; see Item 13 in this section for required materials (only an ID card is needed). Betrothal gifts (caili) can be reclaimed if the marriage was never registered; see Section 8, Item 24 (transfer betrothal gifts by bank transfer). Beneficiaries: yourself and your spouse.

### 22. When a spouse secretly tips livestreamers heavily or transfers joint money to an affair partner: petition to divide marital property without divorcing, and sue to recover third-party transfers
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Requires a lawsuit. Attorney fees come out of your own pocket; they are not included in court costs borne by the losing party. By law, standard first-instance civil trials must conclude within 6 months (subject to extension), or 3 months under summary procedures. You also have to dig up every single bank transfer and tipping record yourself.
- In plain terms: If your spouse spends marital money on huge livestream tips without consulting you, you can ask a court to divide your joint property without getting a divorce. If you do divorce later, the court can award them a smaller share or nothing at all. For money sent to an affair partner, you can sue the recipient to claw it back; claiming the money is already spent will not excuse them. But all of this takes a lawsuit, attorney fees paid out of pocket, and months of waiting for a first-instance ruling.
- Benefit: The Civil Code stipulates that spouses have equal rights of disposal over marital property. If one spouse conceals, transfers, sells off, destroys, or squanders joint assets during the marriage, the other spouse may petition the court to divide marital property without filing for divorce first. When dividing property upon divorce, the offending spouse may receive a smaller share or nothing. If discovered only after the divorce, a fresh lawsuit may be filed to demand re-division. Judicial Interpretation (II) on the Civil Code Marriage and Family Section (effective February 1, 2025) specifies two scenarios. Article 6 covers livestream tipping: if one spouse uses marital property to tip on livestream platforms without the other's consent, and the amount significantly exceeds normal household spending levels, it constitutes "squandering." Courts must support the other spouse's claim to divide marital property mid-marriage, or to award the tipping spouse a smaller share or nothing in a divorce. Article 7 covers transfers to third parties outside the marriage: if one spouse gifts marital property to another person for bigamy, cohabitation, or other breaches of the duty of marital fidelity, courts must uphold the other spouse's claim that the gift is void. Once voided, the recipient must return the funds. This also provides grounds to petition for mid-marriage property division or to award the offending spouse less or nothing upon divorce. In a model case released concurrently by the Supreme People's Court, one spouse transferred 730,000 yuan over four-plus years of marriage to a person with whom they had an improper relationship, and the recipient transferred back 170,000 yuan. The court ordered the recipient to return the net 560,000 yuan actually received, rejecting the defense that "part of the money had already been spent" (Nationwide).

- Notes: The livestream tipping provision has a specific threshold: it must occur without your consent, the amount must significantly exceed normal household consumption levels, and it must severely harm marital property; occasional small tips do not qualify. Judicial Interpretation (II) offers mid-marriage property division and a reduced divorce share as remedies for tipping; it does not authorize clawing back money from the streamer or platform. For transfers to an outside party, what can be recovered is the net amount actually received—any funds returned to the spouse must be deducted. You must gather the evidence yourself: bank and payment app statements, tipping records, and chat logs demonstrating an affair. For debts incurred by a spouse, see Item 12 of this section (large debts borrowed by one spouse). For curtailing your own tipping, see Section 5, Item 8 (don't tip livestreamers). The beneficiary is yourself. Conversely, if you are the third party receiving the money, you must return it, even if you already spent it.

### 23. Put custody, child support, and visitation schedules in writing when divorcing with minor children: the more specific the agreement, the fewer lawsuits later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 人群=婴幼儿,孩子 -->
- Cost: Writing it into an uncontested divorce agreement costs nothing. The hard part is sitting down to negotiate details: what day each month to pay, which bank account, which weekends for pickup, and how to split winter and summer breaks. If negotiations fail, you must go to court. Court filing fees for divorce cases range from 50 to 300 yuan per case. Each party pays their own attorney fees, as they are not included in the court costs borne by the losing party. Regular first-instance procedures generally conclude within 6 months (which can be extended), and summary procedures take 3 months. Any post-divorce disputes over child support or visitation require filing a separate lawsuit.
- In plain terms: Parents can negotiate who gets custody, how much monthly support is paid, and when visits happen, putting it all in writing. If you cannot agree, the court decides: for someone with a steady income, child support is typically 20% to 30% of their monthly earnings. If the other parent fails to pay what was agreed, you can sue to recover the money. If they block you from seeing the child, the court can fine or detain them, but will not physically force the child to go see you.
- Benefit: The Civil Code stipulates that the amount and duration of child support, as well as visitation arrangements, are determined first by mutual agreement; if agreement fails, the court decides. Children under two years old generally stay with the mother. For children aged two and older, custody is decided based on the "best interests of the minor child." If the child is eight or older, their own wishes must be respected. Starting in February 2025, if one party engages in domestic violence, gambling, drug abuse, cohabiting adultery, or snatching and concealing the child, the court prioritizes awarding custody to the other party. Child support amounts depend on the child's actual needs, the parents' ability to pay, and local living standards. For those with a steady income, it is generally set at 20% to 30% of total monthly income. For two or more children, the proportion may increase, but generally does not exceed 50% of monthly income. Without a steady income, it is calculated based on total annual income or industry averages using the same proportion. For example, on a monthly income of 8,000 yuan, this amounts to 1,600 to 2,400 yuan per month (our calculation). Child support is typically paid until the child turns 18. Even if an agreement or ruling sets an amount, the child may still request reasonable additional expenses when necessary, such as major medical or schooling costs. An agreement where one party pays no support is valid; however, if the custodial parent's living standard drops noticeably or the child's necessary expenses increase significantly, the child can still request support. Unpaid support under an agreement can be recovered through a lawsuit. Once a court judgment or mediation agreement takes effect, if one party fails to comply, you can apply for compulsory enforcement. If someone refuses to facilitate visitation, the court may detain or fine them, but "cannot enforce compulsory execution on the person of the child or the act of visitation" (nationwide).

When drafting the agreement, specify the exact payment date, bank account, and end date, and detail visitation down to days of the week, pickup times, and holiday splits to provide clear evidence later. If a parent or relative abducts or conceals the child, you can apply to the court for a personal safety protection order—see Section 8, Item 43 (Personal Safety Protection Orders).
