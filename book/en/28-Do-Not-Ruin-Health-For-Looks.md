# 28. Don't wreck your body for the sake of your appearance

This section collects practices that "do something to the body in order to change how it looks": starving, taking drugs, injections, surgery. What they have in common is that the decision mostly comes from comparing yourself with others, while the cost falls entirely on you — and a considerable share of it is irreversible.

This section does not judge what anyone wants to turn themselves into — that is each person's own business. It answers only one question: for the same goal, how much do the risks differ between the road of "prescription, qualifications, follow-up" and the road of "online shopping, beauty salons, upping the dose yourself". The figures mostly use health endpoints; the two medical-cosmetic items also involve criminal liability for practising medicine without a licence.
### 1. Do not control weight with extreme dieting, fasting, or self-induced vomiting; if you want to lose weight, take it from the exercise side
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — in fact it saves the money spent on weight-loss products
- In plain terms: People with anorexia nervosa die at nearly 6 times the rate of their peers — 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years, and one in five of those deaths is suicide. And the entry point is dieting: a three-year study tracking students at 44 middle schools found that girls who dieted severely were 18 times more likely to develop an eating disorder than those who didn't, moderate dieters 5 times. Looked at on its own, neither body weight nor exercise predicted new cases — only "dieting" did. If you want to control your weight, move more; don't eat so little that you're hungry.
- Benefit: Meta-analysis of 36 studies (166,642 cumulative person-years of observation in anorexia nervosa): standardized mortality ratio for anorexia nervosa 5.86, 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years, about 1/5 of deaths by suicide; bulimia nervosa standardized mortality ratio 1.93, other eating disorders 1.92. A cohort of 14–15-year-olds in 44 secondary schools in Victoria, Australia, surveyed in 6 waves over 3 years: new eating disorders in girls 21.8/1,000 person-years; risk of onset for severe dieters was 18 times that of non-dieters, for moderate dieters 5 times; after adjusting for prior dieting and psychiatric symptoms, BMI, exercise, and sex no longer predicted new eating disorders, and the authors concluded that "in adolescents, using exercise rather than dietary restriction to control weight appears to carry a lower risk of eating disorders"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Arcelus J et al. (2011). Mortality rates in patients with anorexia nervosa and other eating disorders. A meta-analysis of 36 studies. Arch Gen Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.74>；Patton GC et al. (1999). Onset of adolescent eating disorders: population based cohort study over 3 years. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.318.7186.765>
- Notes: "Severe dieting" here means chronically eating far below what you need, not skipping a meal or a late-night snack. If you are already vomiting after eating, blaming yourself after bingeing, or weighing yourself compulsively and unable to stop, see a psychiatrist or the clinical psychology department of a general hospital — this is an illness with standard treatments, not a matter of willpower. The relationship between BMI and mortality is covered in item 28 of section 2, which is about long-term weight ranges, not weight-loss methods.

### 2. Check two things before injections, thread embedding, or surgery: whether the institution's licence lists "medical cosmetology", and whether the person operating is an attending physician
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0, checking two certificates takes a few minutes; the institution should display its medical institution practice licence in the lobby, and a physician's credentials can be verified through the health authority's licensed-physician lookup
- In plain terms: Anything that breaks the skin or goes into the body — surgery, injections, medical devices — counts as a medical act, and it can only be done at an institution that holds a practice licence for medical institutions with "medical cosmetology" approved on it. The doctor doing it must hold a licensed-physician qualification and be registered; for cosmetic surgery the doctor also needs 6 or more years of clinical experience in plastic or cosmetic surgery. A beauty salon, a studio, or a hotel room doing these things is practising medicine illegally: serious cases get up to 3 years, injuring someone 3 to 10 years, causing death 10 or more. Before the procedure the doctor must tell you in writing the indications, contraindications, and risks and have you sign; if that step is skipped, the process itself is wrong.
- Benefit: Article 2 of the Chinese regulation on medical cosmetology services defines medical cosmetology as "the repair and reshaping of a person's appearance and the shape of various parts of the body by means of surgery, drugs, medical devices and other traumatic or invasive medical techniques"; Articles 8 and 24 require first obtaining a practice licence for medical institutions and having the medical cosmetology treatment category approved by the registration authority before services may be offered; Article 11 requires the attending physician to hold a licensed-physician qualification and be registered, with cosmetic surgery requiring 6 or more years, cosmetic dentistry 5 or more years, and cosmetic traditional Chinese medicine and cosmetic dermatology 3 or more years each of relevant clinical work; Article 20 requires written disclosure of indications, contraindications, medical risks, and precautions before the procedure and signed consent. Article 336 of the Chinese criminal law: a person who practises medicine without a physician's qualification and the circumstances are serious is sentenced to up to 3 years of fixed-term imprisonment, criminal detention, or public surveillance, with a fine imposed or imposed separately; where the patient's health is seriously harmed, 3 to 10 years of fixed-term imprisonment plus a fine; where the patient dies, 10 or more years of fixed-term imprisonment plus a fine
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 卫生部 (2002). 医疗美容服务管理办法（卫生部令第 19 号，第二条、第八条、第十一条、第十六条、第二十条、第二十四条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62198.htm>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第三百三十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Notes: Article 16 of the same regulation also requires medical cosmetology procedures to be performed in a medical cosmetology institution or a medical institution with a medical cosmetology department, so "home-visit services" and "private studio procedures" are non-compliant by definition. The wording of Article 20 states that "no medical cosmetology procedure may be performed on a person without or with limited legal capacity without the consent of a guardian" — a minor getting a cosmetic procedure requires a guardian's signature. Life beauty (facials, massage, manicures) is not covered, but the moment needles or scalpels are involved it becomes medical cosmetology.

### 3. Keep facial fillers away from the nose bridge, glabella, and forehead, and don't cut corners with non-standard injections
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — just a different site, or not doing it at all
- In plain terms: When filler is pushed into a facial vessel and flows backward to block the ophthalmic artery, the person goes blind on the spot. Of 48 newly reported cases of blindness after injection between 2015 and 2018, the nose accounted for 56.3%, the glabella 27.1%, the forehead 18.8%, the nasolabial fold 14.6%, and over 80% were hyaluronic acid injections. Only 20.8% of these people fully recovered their vision, 16.7% partially recovered, and the rest did not; nearly one in five also developed brain complications. No rescue method has so far been shown to work reliably, so the only option is to avoid these sites beforehand.
- Benefit: Review of the world literature (48 new cases of partial or complete vision loss after filler injection from January 2015 to September 2018): highest-risk sites were the nose 56.3%, glabella 27.1%, forehead 18.8%, nasolabial fold 14.6%; the filler was hyaluronic acid in 81.3% of cases; 43.8% had skin changes, 18.8% central nervous system complications; 10 cases (20.8%) fully recovered vision, 8 cases (16.7%) partially; the authors concluded that management varies enormously and no treatment has been proven to work consistently
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Beleznay K et al. (2019). Update on Avoiding and Treating Blindness From Fillers: A Recent Review of the World Literature. Aesthet Surg J. <https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjz053>
- Notes: This is an aggregate of cases reported in the literature with no denominator, so the "chance of going blind per injection" cannot be computed — what can be read out is only "which sites cause the most incidents and how little can be done afterwards". That is also why it is grade B. The risk-reducing practices are routine at a properly licensed institution: assessing vessel anatomy before the procedure, using blunt cannulas, injecting small amounts while withdrawing, and having hyaluronidase ready at all times — all of which require the operator to be a trained doctor, and are also the reason not to use informal channels. Verify credentials per item 2 of this section before the procedure.

### 4. Do not buy weight-loss pills, diet coffee, slimming candies, or enzyme plums that promise "fast weight loss"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — in fact it saves this money
- In plain terms: The illegal additive most often found in these products is sibutramine. It used to be a legitimate weight-loss drug until it was pulled in China in 2010: in a trial of nearly 10,000 people who took it for an average of over three years, those on the drug had roughly 30% more non-fatal heart attacks and roughly 36% more non-fatal strokes — 11.4% versus 10.0% having an event — while losing only 1.7 kg more over three-plus years. Adding it to foods and health foods is illegal, and sampling inspections that find it lead to severe penalties and referral to the police where a crime is suspected — but that all happens afterwards; you have already swallowed it.
- Benefit: SCOUT trial (9,804 people over 55 who were overweight or obese and had cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes, randomly assigned, mean treatment 3.4 years): primary endpoint (non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, resuscitation after cardiac arrest, cardiovascular death) occurred in 11.4% vs 10.0%, HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.03–1.31, P=0.02); non-fatal myocardial infarction 4.1% vs 3.2%, HR 1.28 (1.04–1.57); non-fatal stroke 2.6% vs 1.9%, HR 1.36 (1.04–1.77); cardiovascular death and all-cause death were not increased; after randomization the sibutramine group lost on average 1.7 kg more. On the basis of its safety assessment, the former State Food and Drug Administration judged that "the risks of the weight-loss treatment outweigh the benefits" and in October 2010 issued a notice halting the production, sale, and use of sibutramine preparations and APIs; sibutramine and phenolphthalein are both banned from being added to foods (including health foods)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: James WPT et al. (2010). Effect of sibutramine on cardiovascular outcomes in overweight and obese subjects. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1003114>；天津市市场监督管理委员会. 警惕部分食品中违法添加「盐酸西布曲明」和「酚酞」. <https://scjg.tj.gov.cn/tjsscjdglwyh_52651/hdpt/cjwtyxfts/202007/t20200720_2972549.html>
- Notes: SCOUT enrolled high-risk people who already had cardiovascular disease or diabetes; the trial did not show that healthy young people suffer the same harm — but it is precisely because the at-risk population cannot be identified that the regulator withdrew it outright. Another common illegal additive is phenolphthalein (a laxative); long-term overdose can cause electrolyte disturbances and trigger arrhythmias. The test is simple: a legitimate weight-loss drug is a prescription drug, available at pharmacies and hospitals with a traceable approval number; anything sold as "coffee", "candy", "plums", or "meal replacement" that promises several jin a week by definition should have no drug effect — if it does, something was added. The original text of the notice (State Food and Drug Administration Office document 〔2010〕 No. 432) is on the National Medical Products Administration website; this machine's access to it consistently returns 412, so a provincial regulator's page reporting it is cited instead, and the two phrases "risks outweigh the benefits" and "halted production, sale, and use in October 2010" can be checked verbatim there.

### 5. Do not use anabolic steroids ("muscle-building injections", "oral drugs") to gain muscle
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 — it saves the drug money; the price is accepting that muscle grows slowly
- In plain terms: Denmark tracked 545 people who used steroids, identified through positive gym doping tests, and compared them with 5,450 people of the same age: their risk of death was 3 times the control group's, and their median number of hospital visits per year was 0.81 versus 0.36. More than one in ten of them each had acne, gynecomastia, or erectile dysfunction, and checking against a second group gave the same result. In China these drugs are prescription drugs and are also on the doping list — any unit or individual that illegally produces, sells, imports, or exports them is breaking the law. So a channel that will sell them to you is itself already illegal, and no one guarantees the drug is genuine either.
- Benefit: Danish retrospective matched cohort (545 men with positive androgenic steroid results on gym doping tests from 2006-01-03 to 2018-03-01, matched with 5,450 controls; verified against 644 refusers of testing with 6,440 controls): users' mortality was 3 times that of controls, HR 3.0 (95% CI 1.3–7.0); median hospitalizations/visits per year 0.81 vs 0.36 (P<0.0001); acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each affected more than 10% of users, all significantly higher than controls (P<0.0001); the verification cohort gave consistent results. Article 7 of the Chinese anti-doping regulation: the state applies strict management to prohibited substances on the doping list, and no unit or individual may illegally produce, sell, import, or export them; the same regulation places anabolic agents and peptide hormones under prescription-drug management
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Horwitz H et al. (2019). Health consequences of androgenic anabolic steroid use. J Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12850>；国务院 (2004). 反兴奋剂条例（国务院令第 398 号，第七条、第九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63129.htm>
- Notes: Controversy: this is an observational cohort, and people who use steroids may be bigger risk-takers in other ways too, so the factor of 3 cannot be attributed entirely to the drug; the confidence interval of 1.3–7.0 is also wide. But "acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction in over one in ten" is a directly observable side effect, less affected by selection bias. "Anabolic agents" is the term these drugs go by in the regulation. Ordinary protein powder and creatine are not covered by this item; the problem is that products "claiming especially fast muscle gains" may be illegally spiked — the test is the same as in item 4.

### 6. Get weight-loss drugs with a prescription at a hospital; don't buy them from online shops that ship without a prescription
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One registration fee plus travel, a few dozen yuan and half a day
- In plain terms: By regulation, prescription drugs must be sold only against a prescription, and the pharmacy has to keep the prescription on file for at least five years; selling prescription drugs online also requires a genuine, reliable prescription under a real-name system, and the page must state clearly that "prescription drugs may be purchased and used only with a prescription, under a pharmacist's guidance". So when you see a seller that asks no questions, wants no prescription, and ships as soon as you order, there are only two possibilities: what they sell is not the drug they say it is, or the shop itself is breaking the rules — and in both cases, if something goes wrong, no one is answerable for your dose and contraindications. The few dozen yuan for the registration fee buys exactly that.
- Benefit: The Chinese regulation on the quality supervision and administration of drug trading and use requires drug retail enterprises to observe the prescription-drug/over-the-counter classification system, to sell prescription drugs only against a prescription as required, and to keep prescriptions for no less than five years; when the pharmacist or other pharmaceutical technical personnel is absent, a notice must be posted, and prescription drugs may not be sold without their review; prescription drugs may not be sold on open shelves. Article 9 of the Chinese regulation on the supervision and administration of online drug sales requires that anyone selling prescription drugs online to individuals ensure the prescription source is genuine and reliable and apply a real-name system; Article 10 requires prominent display of risk-warning information on every drug display page, and no display of the package insert and no purchasing service before the prescription review is passed; Article 8 states that vaccines, blood products, narcotic drugs, psychotropic drugs, medical toxic drugs, radioactive drugs, drug precursor chemicals, and other drugs under special state management may not be sold online
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2023). 药品经营和使用质量监督管理办法（总局令第 84 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10846/202311/content_6917322.html>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第八条至第十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Notes: The legal provisions themselves can be checked verbatim, but there is no quantitative study of "how much harm taking a drug as prescribed avoids compared with buying it yourself", so this is graded B as "hard to quantify". This item is not only about weight-loss drugs: the steroids and sex hormones in items 5 and 7 of this section fall under the same rules. If you genuinely have an indication for weight loss, the doctor first looks at BMI, comorbidities, and contraindications before deciding whether to use a drug and which one — that judgment is not something you can buy.

### 7. Use sex-hormone drugs only with a doctor's prescription and regular follow-up; don't buy them online or up the dose yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Regular registration fees and blood-draw follow-up tests, a few hundred yuan and several half-days a year
- In plain terms: This item does not discuss whether to use them, only how. In a study of transgender people followed for about four years on average, transgender women (the vast majority taking estrogen) had clearly more venous thrombosis than age-matched controls: 4.1 extra cases per 1,000 people over two years, and 16.7 over eight years; among those who started hormones during follow-up, the gap in thrombosis and ischemic stroke was even more pronounced; no overall difference was seen for heart attack. The point is that thrombosis can be tested for and intercepted early — provided someone is testing you regularly. On the path of buying online and adjusting the dose yourself, that person is precisely what's missing.
- Benefit: Kaiser Permanente electronic health record cohort (2,842 transgender women and 2,118 transgender men, mean follow-up 4.0 and 3.6 years, each matched by birth year, race, site, and enrollment date with roughly 10 times as many cisgender controls — 48,686 cisgender men and 48,775 cisgender women in total): the 2-year and 8-year risk differences in venous thromboembolism for transgender women were 4.1 per 1,000 (95% CI 1.6–6.7) and 16.7 (6.4–27.5) relative to cisgender men, and 3.4 (1.1–5.6) and 13.7 (4.1–22.7) relative to cisgender women; ischemic stroke and myocardial infarction were close across groups overall, but among transgender women who began hormone therapy during follow-up, the differences in venous thrombosis and ischemic stroke were more pronounced; the evidence for transgender men was insufficient to draw conclusions. The authors concluded that "these results may suggest the need for long-term vigilance for vascular adverse effects of estrogen in transgender hormone therapy"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Getahun D et al. (2018). Cross-sex Hormones and Acute Cardiovascular Events in Transgender Persons: A Cohort Study. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Notes: The study itself states its limitation: it cannot know how many people obtained drugs outside the system, so the split between "on hormones" and "not on hormones" was never clean — which is exactly why bringing hormone use into formal follow-up matters. It measured vascular events; it does not evaluate the indications and benefits of hormone therapy itself, which rest on a different body of evidence. The same logic applies to every use of sex hormones: contraceptives, menopausal hormone therapy, testosterone replacement — wherever people buy online, adjust doses based on forum posts, and never test for thrombosis or liver and kidney markers, the risk lies in the "no one is monitoring" part, not in the motivation for using them. The standard route in China is to be assessed, prescribed, and followed up by endocrinology or the relevant specialty at a regular medical institution.

### 8. If "feeling ugly" has reached the point of checking the mirror and thinking about fixing it over and over, get a body-image assessment before talking surgery
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One registration fee at a psychiatric or clinical psychology clinic, a few dozen yuan
- In plain terms: Pooling 33 studies, 15.04% of people seeking plastic surgery met the criteria for body dysmorphic disorder — that is, magnifying a flaw that others can barely see until it disrupts life; among those seeking cosmetic dermatology it was 12.65%, with women making up over 70% on both sides. The source of this distress is not on the face; surgery changes the face but not the cycle of "checking again and again, never satisfied", so it is easy to keep going from one procedure to the next. Spend one registration fee first to find out whether you are in this group, then decide whether to go under.
- Benefit: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 33 studies: prevalence of body dysmorphic disorder among plastic surgery attendees 15.04% (2.21%–56.67% across studies), mean age 34.54±12.41 years, 74.38% female; dermatology attendees 12.65% (4.52%–35.16%), mean age 27.79±9.03 years, 76.09% female; the authors recommended that plastic surgeons and dermatologists actively assess and identify high-risk individuals and arrange multidisciplinary management
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ribeiro RVE (2017). Prevalence of Body Dysmorphic Disorder in Plastic Surgery and Dermatology Patients: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Aesthetic Plast Surg. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0>
- Notes: The results range from 2.21% to 56.67% across studies — an enormous spread, with screening tools and populations not uniform — so all that can be read out is "this proportion is not low and is worth screening for once"; the 15.04% cannot be taken as an exact value, which is also why this is graded B. This item does not say that people who want cosmetic procedures have a psychological problem — the vast majority do not; it says that for the small group who are "repetitive, persistent, and already affected at work and in social life", surgery does not solve their problem. Cues to look for: spending long stretches of the day checking or covering up a body part, avoiding social events or photos because of it, and moving on to the next spot as soon as one is done.
