# 20. How to care for a newborn

Scope: infant mortality and money. This section only covers what affects whether the child survives or how much you spend, and only where the gap is large enough and the evidence is solid. Soothing and day-to-day parenting tips are not covered here. For tasks before birth and on the day of discharge, see Section 27.
### 1. During infancy, put the baby to sleep on their back on a firm surface; share a room but not a bed; and do not place any soft items in the crib or bed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: This costs nothing upfront. However, you’ll need to replace the mattress with a firm one, which may cost several hundred yuan. The real challenge is resisting pressure from older relatives to deviate from this rule.
- In plain terms: While the child is still an infant, lay the child face up on a firm surface that does not incline. An inclined cradle or an infant seat cannot be used as a bed. The adult and the child sleep in the same room, but not in the same bed. Do not put soft things on the bed, such as pillows, quilts, bed bumpers, or stuffed toys. In the United States alone, about 3500 infants die each year from causes related to sleep.
- Benefit: Each year, nearly 3,500 infants in the U.S. lose their lives to sleep-related issues, including sudden infant death syndrome, deaths of unknown cause, and accidental suffocation in bedding. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends a safe sleep environment consisting of: infants sleeping on their backs; using a solid, non-inclined sleeping surface; sharing a room without sharing a bed; and avoiding soft bedding and overheating. Additional protective measures include breastfeeding, keeping infants away from nicotine and alcohol, ensuring all vaccinations are up to date, and offering a pacifier during sleep.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Moon RY, Carlin RF, Hand I, Task Force on Sudden Infant Death Syndrome and the Committee on Fetus and Newborn (2022). Sleep-Related Infant Deaths: Updated 2022 Recommendations for Reducing Infant Deaths in the Sleep Environment. Pediatrics, 150(1), e2022057990. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2022-057990>
- Notes: “Do not place any soft items in the bed” means absolutely no pillows, blankets, bed rails, stuffed toys, or shaped pillows — even nursing pillows that appear perfectly safe are prohibited. Inclined cribs and baby chairs must never be used as sleeping areas. This requirement represents the biggest departure from common practice in this section, and it often requires the strongest resistance to family pressure.

### 2. Give the first dose of hepatitis B vaccine within 24 hours of birth
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Free. The hepatitis B vaccine is included in the routine national immunization program and is completely free. It is administered right at the hospital on the day the baby is born, with no extra trips needed.
- In plain terms: A baby should receive their first dose of the hepatitis B vaccine within 24 hours of birth. Among people infected with hepatitis B during infancy or early childhood, about 95% develop a chronic infection. By contrast, the vast majority of people infected in adulthood recover on their own, which is why getting this shot as early as possible is so crucial. If the mother tests positive for hepatitis B surface antigen, the newborn also needs a shot of hepatitis B immunoglobulin within 12 hours of birth.
- Benefit: According to the World Health Organization (WHO), "all infants should receive the hepatitis B vaccine as soon as possible after birth, preferably within 24 hours." This is because "about 95% of those infected during infancy and early childhood develop chronic infections." The vast majority of people infected as adults recover spontaneously (without treatment); the WHO notes that "less than 5%" of adult infections become chronic. National Health Commission guidelines for preventing mother-to-child transmission specify that for infants born to mothers who are positive for hepatitis B surface antigen, "the first dose of hepatitis B vaccine should be administered as early as possible within 12 hours of birth, along with an injection of 100 international units of hepatitis B immunoglobulin."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:World Health Organization. Hepatitis B fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hepatitis-b>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2020). 预防艾滋病、梅毒和乙肝母婴传播工作规范（2020 年版）. <https://wjw.xinjiang.gov.cn/hfpc/zhgl6/202012/08b7de496f0b40e586d4b99d00cf79c4.shtml>（新疆维吾尔自治区卫生健康委员会转载）
- Notes: If the mother tests positive for hepatitis B surface antigen, the baby also needs a dose of hepatitis B immunoglobulin right after birth. This status must be checked and confirmed during prenatal examinations rather than handled as an afterthought after delivery. For prenatal maternal hepatitis B screening, see Section 27, Item 3 (triple screening for HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B).

### 3. Complete all routine childhood vaccinations on schedule: they are entirely free, and missed doses only need to be caught up, not restarted
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,孩子,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Free. The law explicitly states that "vaccination clinics shall not charge any fees for administering national immunization program vaccines." You will need to make several trips to the vaccination clinic according to your child's age in months. Keep the vaccination certificate safe and do not lose it.
- In plain terms: All vaccines in the national immunization program are completely free, and vaccination clinics are forbidden by law from charging for them. Daycare and school entry checks will inspect your child's vaccination certificate, so missed shots will usually be caught and scheduled for catch-up. If a child misses a shot, you only need to make up the missing doses rather than starting the entire series over, even if the replacement doses come from a different manufacturer.
- Benefit: When children enter daycare or school, preschool institutions and schools must inspect their vaccination certificates. If any child has not received the required national immunization program vaccines according to regulations, the school must report this to the vaccination clinic and assist in urging guardians to complete catch-up vaccinations. Therefore, missed vaccines are generally identified during daycare or school entry inspections. The current schedule in effect is the 2026 edition. The human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine has been incorporated into the national immunization program. The starting age for the DTP (diphtheria, tetanus, and acellular pertussis) vaccine has shifted from 3 months of age to 2 months of age. There are 5 doses in total, administered at 2 months, 4 months, 6 months, 18 months, and 6 years of age. Eligible children in Tibet, Qinghai, Xinjiang, and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps also routinely receive the Japanese encephalitis vaccine starting from March 2026. Multiple vaccines can be given on the same day: currently, all vaccines in the national immunization program can be administered simultaneously according to the immunization schedule or catch-up principles. Two or more injectable vaccines must be administered at different anatomical sites; "mixing two or more vaccines in the same syringe is strictly prohibited." If not given on the same day, an interval of at least 28 days must be maintained between two or more injectable live attenuated vaccines. No interval restrictions apply between inactivated vaccines, oral live attenuated vaccines, and other vaccines. For individuals under 18 years of age, the catch-up principle is to catch up as early as possible: "only uncompleted doses need to be caught up, without restarting the entire course," and switching manufacturers for the same vaccine is permitted to continue the series (Nationwide, 2026 schedule).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:全国人大常委会 (2019). 疫苗管理法（第四十八、四十九条）. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_dde03480953841a5912c864dc29003d0.html>（国家市场监督管理总局网站转载）；国家疾病预防控制局、国家卫生健康委 (2026). 《国家免疫规划疫苗儿童免疫程序及说明（2026 年版）》解读问答. <https://www.ndcpa.gov.cn/jbkzzx/c100013/common/content/content_2073002930292035584.html>
- Notes: The most common misconception is "the child has a weak constitution, so delay vaccination." Official guidance clarifies that a supposed "allergic constitution," a family history of allergic disease, or past food or drug allergies in the child are not valid contraindications. Atopic dermatitis (eczema), urticaria (hives), allergic rhinitis, and bronchial asthma are not reasons to postpone vaccination as long as the condition is stable. Children currently taking anti-allergy medications or using inhaled corticosteroids can also be vaccinated normally. Neonatal physiological jaundice and breast milk jaundice are likewise not contraindications. True contraindications apply to only two scenarios: a prior acute severe allergic reaction to the same vaccine, or a known severe allergy to a specific vaccine component. Children with immunodeficiencies, those undergoing chemotherapy or radiation therapy, and those taking immunosuppressive drugs require individual clinical evaluation; guardians should describe their medical history at the vaccination clinic for a determination. For the first dose of hepatitis B vaccine, see Item 2. Non-immunization-program vaccines paid out-of-pocket incur separate vaccine and service charges, which are unrelated to the free program covered in this item.

### 4. For the first 6 months, feed only breast milk, not even water; from 6 months add complementary foods and keep breastfeeding
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: No money, and it saves the cost of formula. What it takes is time. Feeding takes a large part of every day. The hard part is keeping it up, and not stopping in the first 6 months.
- In plain terms: Start feeding in the 1st hour after the child is born. For the first 6 months, feed only breast milk, not even water. From a full 6 months, add complementary foods, with iron-rich animal foods every day, and keep breastfeeding to 2 years or longer. If there is not enough breast milk, or breastfeeding is not possible, use formula.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization recommends "starting breastfeeding within the 1st hour after birth." For the first 6 months, "exclusive breastfeeding, meaning no other food or liquid, including water." Then "from 6 months of age, start safe and adequate complementary foods, and continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond." The National Health Commission service guide also promotes exclusive breastfeeding from 0 to 6 months. The same guide requires that from a full 6 months "the day's complementary foods must include iron-rich animal foods."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:World Health Organization. Breastfeeding. <https://www.who.int/health-topics/breastfeeding>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 婴幼儿营养喂养评估服务指南（试行）（国卫办妇幼函〔2024〕452 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002872.htm>
- Notes: If breast milk is not enough, or the body does not allow breastfeeding, use formula. There is no need to feel guilty. The gap between breast milk and formula is far smaller than the safe-sleep item. That is the author's judgment. How often to add complementary foods, from that Health Commission service guide: at 6 to 8 months, 1 to 2 times a day; at 9 to 12 months, 2 to 3 times a day. Each day's complementary foods are chosen from 7 food groups, and not fewer than 4. If the child has severe eczema or an egg allergy, whether to avoid peanuts is in item 12 of this section (do not avoid peanuts). If the mother eats vegetarian, or wants the child to eat vegetarian, see item 14 of this section (on a vegetarian diet, supplement vitamin B12 every day).

### 5. Prepare powdered infant formula with water at or above 70 °C, especially for preterm, low-birth-weight, or infants under 2 months old; cool before feeding and discard leftovers
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Free. Takes a few extra minutes each time to let the milk cool down. The hard part is waiting for the boiled water to cool to the right preparation temperature even when the baby is crying from hunger.
- In plain terms: Powdered baby formula is not sterile and can carry bacteria that kill 40% to 80% of infected newborns. Pouring hot water first and letting it cool to around 73.8 °C before adding powder reduces these germs to less than one-hundred-thousandth of their original level in most cases. Hot water degrades some vitamin C, but tested formulas still met safety standards. Cool the bottle before feeding, and throw away any leftovers.
- Benefit: Powdered formula is not sterile and may carry *Cronobacter sakazakii*. Reported case fatality rates in infected newborns range from 40% to 80%. Experimental studies show that pouring hot water into a bottle and waiting until it cools to about 73.8 °C before adding formula powder keeps the liquid above 70 °C in the vast majority of cases. This achieves a reduction of more than 5 log units in these bacteria, cutting them to less than 1/100,000 of the original count. The trade-off with hot water is vitamin C. A 2006 WHO/FAO expert meeting reviewed data showing that using boiling water caused a noticeable loss only in vitamin C, decreasing by 5.6% to 65.6% across 4 formula brands. Manufacturers routinely add surplus vitamin C during production; even the brand with the largest loss still contained 9.0 mg per 100 kcal after preparation, exceeding the Codex Alimentarius minimum threshold of 8 mg. Because only one study was available, the meeting drew no definitive conclusion on this point. High-risk groups: a review by Silano et al. notes that infants aged 2 months and younger are most prone to meningitis following infection, with preterm and immunocompromised infants also facing higher risks. They advocate using 70 °C water, "especially when feeding preterm and low-birth-weight infants."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Beary MA, Daly SE, Baker J, Snyder AB (2025). Assessing Hot Water Reconstitution Instructions and Labeling of Powdered Infant Formula to Ensure Cronobacter spp. Reduction. Journal of Food Protection, 88(9), 100571. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfp.2025.100571>；Sima WG, Legesse T, Girma S, et al. (2025). Emerging microbial risks: Cronobacter sakazakii in powdered infant formula for infants under six months of age in Ethiopia. BMC Microbiology, 26, 307. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12866-025-04380-y>；World Health Organization & FAO (2007). Safe preparation, storage and handling of powdered infant formula: guidelines. <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241595414>；Bundesinstitut für Risikobewertung (2022). Recommendations for the hygienic preparation of powdered infant formula. Updated BfR Opinion No. 009/2022. <https://doi.org/10.17590/20220510-104706>；Agostoni C, Axelsson I, Goulet O, et al. (2004). Preparation and handling of powdered infant formula: a commentary by the ESPGHAN Committee on Nutrition. Journal of Pediatric Gastroenterology and Nutrition, 39(4), 320-322. <https://doi.org/10.1097/00005176-200410000-00002>；Silano M, Paganin P, Davanzo R (2016). Time for the 70 °C water precautionary option in the home dilution of powdered infant formula. Italian Journal of Pediatrics, 42, 17. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s13052-016-0228-9>；Losio MN, Pavoni E, Finazzi G, et al. (2018). Preparation of Powdered Infant Formula: Could Product's Safety Be Improved? Journal of Pediatric Gastroenterology and Nutrition, 67(4), 543-546. <https://doi.org/10.1097/MPG.0000000000002100>
- Notes: Controversy: A 2022 opinion from the German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) suggested that water between 20 and 50 °C is sufficient for healthy, full-term infants. It noted that most manufacturers recommend 40 to 50 °C on the tin. Their rationale is that 70 °C water cools rapidly when poured into a bottle, so the actual bacterial reduction is smaller than calculated models suggest. Hot water also degrades certain nutrients, and feeding without proper cooling risks burns. The European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (ESPGHAN) likewise opposed boiling water in 2004, citing concerns over destroying vitamins and added probiotics. However, WHO and FAO continue to recommend temperatures above 70 °C. Preterm infants, infants with birth weights under 2500 g, those under 2 months old, and immunocompromised infants carry the highest risk; their formula should be prepared at 70 °C, or as advised by a doctor. The German opinion concluded that preparing formula according to tin instructions carries an extremely low infection risk for healthy full-term infants. The 70 °C threshold originates from public health guidelines. Beary et al. state: "public health guidance for caregivers of high-risk infants recommends reconstituting PIF with water heated to at least 70 °C (158 °F) to inactivate the organism." Do not use water that has sat in a thermos for a full day. Never leave prepared formula at room temperature to feed at the next meal.

### 6. Do not feed honey to a child under 1 year of age
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain terms: If the child is under 1 year of age, do not feed honey. Honey water, honey mixed into complementary foods, and pastries that contain honey all count. Honey can carry the bacterium that causes botulism. The bacteria in an adult's gut can hold it down. An infant's cannot.
- Benefit: The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says "do not feed honey to children under 1 year of age," because "honey may contain the bacteria that cause botulism." An adult's gut bacteria can hold down these spores (the dormant form of this bacterium). An infant's cannot.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:US CDC. Preventing Botulism. <https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/prevention/index.html>
- Notes: What must not be fed includes honey water, complementary foods mixed with honey, and pastries that contain honey.

### 7. The vitamin K shot at birth has to be given
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: 0 yuan to a few dozen yuan. This sum is usually already inside the hospital bill for the birth, so you do not pay it separately.
- In plain terms: A newborn has very little vitamin K stored. Without a supplement, bleeding can occur, and the kind that shows up late is often bleeding in the brain. In Tashkent, where this prevention was not generally done, 478 out of every 100000 newborns had this bleeding. With prevention, the estimate is that more than 78% can be cut. For a home birth, or a birth at an irregular facility, ask whether it was given.
- Benefit: A newborn has very little vitamin K stored. Without a supplement, vitamin K deficiency bleeding can occur, and the kind that shows up late often presents as intracranial hemorrhage (bleeding in the brain). The Belgian consensus recommendation is "1 intramuscular injection of 1 or 2 milligrams of vitamin K at birth." Where prevention is not provided systematically, the rate of vitamin K deficiency bleeding is 478 cases per 100000 people. That figure comes from 42000 newborns in Tashkent. The authors estimate that prevention can bring the rate down by "more than 78.5%."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Fiesack S, Smits A, Rayyan M, et al. (2021). Nutrients, 13(11), 4109. <https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13114109>；Tursunov D, Yoshida Y, Yrysov K, et al. (2018). Nagoya Journal of Medical Science, 80(1), 11. <https://doi.org/10.18999/nagjms.80.1.11>
- Notes: At a regular hospital in China, the hospital generally gives this shot as routine. That is the author's experience. No single nationwide official rule was found. Before discharge, look at the discharge summary, or ask. For a home birth, or a birth at an irregular facility, confirm it on your own initiative. The injection meant here goes into the muscle. That Belgian consensus calls the oral route "a slightly worse substitute," used only when the parents refuse the injection: 2 milligrams by mouth at birth, then, for a breastfed child, 1 to 2 milligrams a week for 3 months.

### 8. If an infant under 3 months reaches 38 °C, go to the hospital directly and do not watch at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Registration plus tests, a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: If the child is under 3 months and the temperature reaches 38 °C, go to the hospital directly. Do not watch at home, and do not give a fever medicine first and then wait. The American Academy of Pediatrics set a specific plan for febrile infants who still look all right, so "looking all right" is not a reason to wait at home. The UK guidance also counts a child under 3 months with a fever of 38 °C as high risk of serious illness.
- Benefit: The American Academy of Pediatrics wrote a specific guideline for full-term infants from 8 to 60 days of age who still look all right. That guideline defines fever as "a temperature of 38.0 °C or above." It splits them into 3 bands, 8 to 21 days, 22 to 28 days, and 29 to 60 days, and gives each band its own path. Infants under 8 days of age are outside that guideline. The UK NICE fever guideline covers children under 5 years of age. It states that a child under 3 months with a temperature of 38 °C or above is in the "high-risk group for serious illness."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Pantell RH, Roberts KB, Adams WG, et al. (2021). Evaluation and Management of Well-Appearing Febrile Infants 8 to 60 Days Old. Pediatrics, 148(2), e2021052228. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2021-052228>；NICE (2019, 2021 更新). Fever in under 5s: assessment and initial management (NG143), 1.2.12. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng143/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: At this age, a serious bacterial infection can show up as fever alone. So looking "all right" is not a reason to watch at home. Do not give a fever medicine yourself and then wait and see.

### 9. No matter how tired or angry you are, never shake a baby
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: There is no monetary cost. The real difficulty lies in resisting the urge to shake a baby when you’re exhausted or upset.
- In plain terms: A baby’s head makes up a large proportion of their body size, yet their neck muscles are still very weak. Shaking them forcefully can cause bleeding inside the brain and eyes, potentially leading to death or lifelong disabilities. Gentle rocking while trying to soothe them is not dangerous; the real risk comes from uncontrolled, violent shaking. When you feel yourself losing control, place the baby back in their crib and step away for a few minutes. Letting them cry for a short while is far safer than holding them and shaking them.
- Benefit: Because a baby’s head is proportionally large and their neck muscles are underdeveloped, violent shaking can result in intracranial and retinal hemorrhage — bleeding in both the brain and eyes. This can be fatal or leave lasting impairments. Such harm is distinct from any gentle rocking used to calm them. Medically, injuries caused by shaking or impact are termed “abusive head trauma.” This condition is the leading cause of death from head injuries among children under 2 years old. In the United States between 1999 and 2014, it accounted for nearly 2,250 deaths of children under 5.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Spies EL, Klevens J (2016). Fatal Abusive Head Trauma Among Children Aged <5 Years — United States, 1999–2014. MMWR 65(20):505-509. <https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm6520a1>；Choudhary AK 等 (2018). Consensus statement on abusive head trauma in infants and young children. Pediatric Radiology 48(8):1048-1065. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00247-018-4149-1>
- Notes: When you truly feel you’re about to lose control, the safest action is to return the baby to their crib and leave the room for a few minutes. Allowing them to cry briefly is much safer than holding them and shaking them. This advice is especially vital for parents who care for their children alone and often suffer from sleep deprivation. For guidance on managing your own emotions, please refer to Section 3.

### 10. Choose diapers by three things, not the brand: fit, how often you change them, and whether they failed safety spot-checks
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Ranges from 200–300 yuan to over 1,000 yuan a month, depending on the tier you buy.
- In plain terms: No official agency has ever ranked "the best brand." The only things you can verify are official spot-check bulletins from market regulators and regulatory investigations into specific incidents. Expensive does not mean safe, and imported does not mean compliant. Buy a small trial pack for a week to check for diaper rash or red pressure marks, and only stock up if it works well.
- Benefit: No official agency has ever published a ranking of "the best brand," so this book makes no brand recommendations. Only two categories of information can be verified: product quality spot-check bulletins from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), and regulatory investigations into specific incidents. After public concern arose in June 2026 over formamide in infant diapers, the SAMR led a joint investigation team to look into the issue.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources:市场监管总局 (2026-06-22). 市场监管总局牵头成立联合调查组核查「婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺问题」：「针对媒体反映的"婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺问题"，市场监管总局、工业和信息化部、国家卫生健康委、国家疾控局高度重视，成立联合调查组，核查婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺有关问题，并依法依规处理。有关情况将及时公布。」. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/zj/art/2026/art_78c0425db7604b27bd7a3f36ba319975.html>；中国政府网转载. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202606/content_7072931.htm>；NHS. Nappy rash（2026-08-28 审阅）. <https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/nappy-rash/>
- Notes: When the joint investigation team was formed, it stated only that "relevant information will be released in a timely manner." As of September 30, 2026, no findings from this investigation have appeared on the SAMR official website or in recent national spot-check bulletins. Practical shopping rules: expensive does not mean safe, and imported does not mean compliant. Start with a small pack for a week to see if the child gets diaper rash or red pressure marks, and only stock up if it fits well. These rules are the author's personal experience. The UK National Health Service (NHS) notes that common causes of diaper rash include urine and feces staying against the skin for too long, changing diapers infrequently, and diapers chafing against the skin. Non-absorbent or unbreathable diapers can also cause it. The NHS also recommends ensuring diapers fit properly and are not put on too tightly. See Section 5 for how to search spot-check bulletins.

### 11. Follow the "borrow, buy used, buy new" order for bulky baby gear, buy a new mattress, and verify a used car seat before use
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: No extra spending; the savings usually run into thousands. The hard part is resisting the urge to buy everything all at once.
- In plain terms: Strollers, swaddles, and toys are used only briefly: try to borrow first, buy used if you cannot borrow, and only buy new as a last resort. If you use a secondhand car seat, make sure it was never in a moderate or severe crash, has not been recalled, and still has all its parts; if you cannot verify this, do not use it. In a Scottish study, routinely sleeping on a mattress previously used by another child was linked to a higher risk of sudden infant death, though it is unclear whether the mattress was the actual cause. It is best to buy a new mattress.
- Benefit: Strollers, cribs, swaddles, and toys have very short periods of use, and supply far exceeds demand on the secondhand market. Three safety boundaries must be maintained for used items:

First, car seats. The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration recommends replacing and never reusing a car seat that has been in a moderate or severe crash. A seat may continue to be used only if all five of the following minor-crash criteria are met: the vehicle could be driven away from the crash site; the door nearest the safety seat was undamaged; no vehicle occupants sustained injuries; no airbags deployed; and there is no visible damage to the car seat. A used car seat should only be used if five things can be verified: it has never been in a moderate or severe crash; it has a label with the manufacture date and model number; it has not been recalled; all parts are present; and the manual is still included. The manufacture date and model number are used to check for recalls and whether the seat is expired.

Second, mattresses. A Scottish case-control study compared infants who died from sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) with healthy control infants of the same age, checking what mattress each had slept on. Routinely sleeping on a mattress previously used by another child was associated with an increased risk of SIDS (odds ratio OR 3.07, 95% CI 1.51–6.22). When the used mattress came from another home, the odds ratio was 4.78; when it came from the infant's own home, it was 1.64 (the latter was not statistically significant). The authors noted that existing evidence is insufficient to determine whether the mattress itself was causal.

### 13. Watch for warning signs of newborn jaundice: go to the emergency room immediately if yellowing starts within 24 hours of birth or the baby is hard to wake and will not feed; get checked promptly if yellowing worsens or stool turns pale
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Registration plus a bilirubin test costs well under a few hundred yuan. Infants needing phototherapy (blue light) usually stay in the hospital for about two days. The real hurdle is making the trip even when the baby still looks mostly fine.
- In plain terms: More than 80% of newborns develop some jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes). Go to the hospital right away if yellowing appears within the first 24 hours of birth, or if jaundice comes with unusually deep sleepiness, trouble waking up, refusal to feed, or a noticeably floppy or stiff body. If jaundice keeps getting worse after the first 24 hours, or if the baby's stool turns pale or clay-colored, get it checked promptly, as pale stools can signal a blocked bile duct (biliary atresia). Severe bilirubin buildup can damage the brain and cause lifelong disability.
- Benefit: The 2022 American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) clinical practice guideline states that "more than 80% of newborn infants will have some degree of jaundice." Extremely high bilirubin can lead to acute bilirubin encephalopathy and kernicterus. The guideline describes kernicterus as a "permanent, disabling neurologic condition," with manifestations including choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, upward gaze palsy, enamel dysplasia of deciduous teeth, and sensorineural hearing loss. The guideline lists jaundice appearing within the first 24 hours after birth as a risk factor for developing severe hyperbilirubinemia. The UK National Health Service (NHS) divides advice for parents into two tiers. Tier one calls for calling emergency services or going straight to an emergency department: this applies if the infant has jaundice alongside extreme sleepiness, difficulty waking, refusal to feed, floppiness or stiffness, or seizures, or if jaundice appears within 24 hours of birth. Tier two calls for booking a prompt medical consultation: this applies if, after 24 hours of birth, jaundice fails to improve or worsens, urine is dark yellow or brown, or stool is pale or clay-colored. Global estimates for 2010 indicated that roughly 480,000 newborns developed Rh hemolytic disease or extreme hyperbilirubinemia; 24% of them died, and 13% developed kernicterus. Among infants surviving kernicterus, over 83% had one or more permanent impairments. Pale stool may indicate biliary atresia; performing the Kasai portoenterostomy within 60 days of birth yields significantly better surgical outcomes. In Taiwan, an infant stool color card was integrated into every newborn's child health booklet starting in 2004; the proportion undergoing surgery within 60 days rose from 60% in 2004 to 74.3% in 2005. In another nationwide population-based study in Taiwan, after universal stool card screening was rolled out, mortality among children with biliary atresia dropped from 26.2% to 15.9% (a before-and-after comparison, not a randomized trial).

### 17. Run cold water first then add hot for baby baths, test with your inner wrist or elbow before putting the child in; set on-demand water heaters below 50 °C

- Cost: Free. Adds one to two minutes per bath to test the water temperature. You can buy a bath thermometer if you want extra certainty. The hard part is remembering to test before putting the child in, even when the baby is crying and you are in a rush.
- In plain terms: Touching 65 °C water for just 2 seconds causes third-degree burns in most adults, and an infant's skin is even thinner. Fill the tub with cold water first, mix in hot water, stir well, and test with the inside of your wrist or elbow before placing the baby inside—never bathe them under a running faucet. After one US state lowered the factory preset temperature on water heaters to 49 °C, hospitalizations for hot water scalds among children dropped by more than half.
- Benefit: The US Consumer Product Safety Commission states that for most adults, exposure to 150 °F (about 65 °C) water causes third-degree burns in 2 seconds. Exposure to 140 °F (60 °C) water takes 6 seconds, or 130 °F (about 54 °C) takes 30 seconds to cause burns. Even at 120 °F (49 °C), soaking for 5 minutes can cause third-degree burns. A WHO report notes that infants and young children have thinner skin than adults, meaning scalds occur at lower temperatures and penetrate deeper. The American Academy of Pediatrics advises parents to test bathwater with the inside of the wrist or elbow, aiming for "warm, not hot." They instruct: "Never bathe a baby under running water," because water flowing through pipes fluctuates in temperature, and a sudden change can cause scalds. Washington State enacted legislation in 1983 requiring new water heaters to be preset at 49 °C at the factory. By 1988, the average household hot water temperature fell from 61 °C in 1977 to 50 °C. Hospital admissions for pediatric tap water scalds dropped from 5.5 per year in the 1970s to 2.4 per year. Burn surface area, skin grafts, deaths, and hospital stays also decreased. This is a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled trial. Australia mandates a maximum of 50 °C for bathroom hot water taps. In New South Wales from 1989 to 1996, scald hospitalization rates for children aged 0 to 4 fell by 13%. Severe scalds requiring more than 10 days of hospitalization fell by 30% in the two years following the second phase. In three domestic Chinese hospitalization surveys of pediatric burns, hot liquid scalds accounted for 87.6% to 90.6% of cases, with children under 3 being the most affected.
- Evidence grade: B

- Notes: Grade B was assigned because data come from before-and-after comparisons and medical record statistics rather than randomized controlled trials. Benefit is rated moderate based on practical judgment, as separate domestic figures specifically for bathwater scalds were unavailable. Water heaters fall into two main types. For on-demand tankless heaters (such as gas water heaters), set the output temperature directly below 50 °C. Storage electric water heaters require a different approach: WHO guidelines on preventing Legionella recommend keeping heater tank water at 60 °C and tap water at no less than 50 °C (Legionella is a bacterium that can cause pneumonia). The same guideline notes that Legionella prevention must not increase scalding risks for children, recommending a thermostatic mixing valve at the outlet. If you do not have one installed, rely strictly on running cold water first, mixing in hot, and testing before placing the child in. Never walk away during bath time. The American Academy of Pediatrics reports that more than half of children who drown in bathtubs are under 1 year old (see Section 1, Item 12: Keep children within sight and reach near water). Never hold hot drinks or soup while carrying a child: the WHO notes that in high-income countries, the most common scalds in infants under 1 year old come from hot drinks in cups. If a burn occurs, cool immediately with cold water (see Section 13, Item 14: Cool burns with cold running water).
