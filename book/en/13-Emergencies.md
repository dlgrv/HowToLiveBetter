# 13. Emergencies: What to
 do first

This section outlines what to do, when to do it, and what to avoid. Items regarding first aid and wilderness
 emergencies are calculated based on specific causes of death and survival rates. Items regarding threats and scams are calculated based on money and personal
 liberty. These two types of figures are not interchangeable. These entries are primarily for your own use and for the use of your spouse and
 immediate family; 79.2% of cardiac arrests happen at home, so you are most likely to be saving a family member. Saving
 friends or colleagues is the next priority. There is some benefit to helping strangers, but it is the lowest priority, and
 you risk being extorted or entangled in legal proceedings. Therefore, these items prioritize actions for your own protection and include liability waivers
.

This section covers only actions to take within the first few minutes. Recognizing scams or dealing with extortion—which play
 out over days—is not covered here. For hard rules on anti-fraud and the seven most common scams, see Section
8, Item 3 (Anti-fraud rules). For how to handle extortion involving private photos or videos, see Section 8, Item
 32 (Extortion using compromising material). If money has already been transferred, dial 110 or 96110
 immediately to request a payment stop; see Section 8, Item 2 (Stop payment).

### 1. If someone collapses, is unresponsive, and is not breathing normally, push hard on the chest immediately, and have a bystander call 120 and find an AED
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. A CPR class from the Red Cross or a hospital takes half a day to a day, and one session serves you for years. Even without training, anyone can do it: interlock your hands in the center of the chest between the nipples, and push down hard about 2 times per second without stopping.
- In plain terms: Cardiac arrest often happens at home, and when someone gives immediate chest compressions, a person is more likely to survive and leave the hospital alive. If they do not respond and are not breathing normally, start compressions right away—occasional gasping does not count as normal breathing. Place one hand over the other and push hard on the chest, while having someone nearby call 120 and find an automated external defibrillator (AED).
- Benefit: A meta-analysis combining 79 studies and 142,740 people showed that overall, only 7.6% of patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survived to discharge. Bystander CPR was associated with higher survival to hospital discharge. Stratified by baseline survival across studies, survival to discharge among those receiving bystander CPR ranged from 3.9% to 16.1% (these two figures come from different strata and cannot be treated as a before-and-after change from compressions). While 53% of arrests were witnessed by a bystander, only 32% actually received bystander compressions. In China, the BASIC-OHCA registry recorded 38,227 emergency cases, with 30,282 cases (79.2%) occurring at home; among this group, bystander CPR was performed in 20.3%, and survival to discharge was 1.2%. The proper order is to start chest compressions first, then have a second person call for help and retrieve an AED. Once the device is turned on, follow its voice prompts. The trigger to start compressions is the absence of normal breathing. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines state that in the first few minutes after cardiac arrest, a person may still gasp irregularly, laboriously, or noisily. This does not count as normal breathing. When in doubt, assume it is abnormal. Any abnormal breathing—including gasping, irregular breathing, or no breathing at all—calls for immediate CPR.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Sasson C 等 (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576> ; Zheng J 等 (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1> ; International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025，第 154 页：「If the person is breathing abnormally (gasping, taking irregular breaths or not breathing), start CPR immediately」；第 161 页：「In the first few minutes after cardiac arrest, a person may have agonal breathing (an irregular and laboured breathing pattern, or gasping noisily). This type of breathing is not normal. If there is any doubt about whether breathing is normal, assume it is not」. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Notes: For infants under 1 year old, the technique differs and requires rescue breaths; see item 44 in this section (Infant CPR). Do not waste time pinching the philtrum, searching for relatives, or recording videos. Do not hold back out of fear of cracking ribs. Article 184 of the Civil Code specifies that anyone who voluntarily provides emergency rescue and causes harm to the person being rescued bears no civil liability.

### 2. When an older person falls or someone collapses, squat down to call out to them and dial 120 before rushing to pull them up; walking away from strangers is legal in China, but if you stop, do not move them
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Squatting down to ask and making a phone call takes a few minutes. The hard part is resisting the urge to reach out and pull them up. For strangers, there is an added cost: calling 120 leaves your phone number, which may lead to callback questions or an inquiry. If family members target you, a lawsuit takes months at minimum. And even if you eventually win, you still have to pay your own legal fees.
- In plain terms: This routine is primarily for your own elderly family members and people you know. First squat down and call out to them, checking whether they are breathing normally. If they do not respond and have no normal breathing, follow Item 1 (push hard on the chest). If they are breathing normally, do not try to lift them: they may have fallen because of a stroke or fainting, and forcing them up can make things worse. Ask if they remember how they fell; if they have back pain or signs of a fracture, do not move them at all, and call 120 on the spot.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Health's *Technical Guidelines for Fall Intervention in the Elderly* clearly states two points. First, "Falls are the leading cause of injury-related death among elderly people aged 65 and older in China." Second, "When discovering an elderly person who has fallen, do not rush to help them up; assess the situation first." If they are unconscious, call emergency medical services (120) immediately. If vomiting, turn the head to one side and clear the mouth and nose. If breathing and heartbeat have stopped, perform chest compressions immediately. "If moving is necessary, keep it smooth and as flat as possible." If they are conscious, first ask if they remember how they fell: "If they cannot recall, it may be syncope or a cerebrovascular accident." If there is severe headache, facial drooping, slurred speech, or weakness in the limbs, "immediately helping the elderly person up may worsen cerebral hemorrhage or cerebral ischemia, aggravating their condition." If there are signs of fracture, back pain, or loss of bowel or bladder control, "do not move them casually without relevant professional knowledge." In all these scenarios, call emergency medical services (120) immediately. If the person wants to stand up on their own, assist them to stand slowly, then "let them sit or lie down to rest and observe; only leave after confirming they are fine." Regarding the fear of being extorted, the law provides clear protections. Article 90 of the *Interpretation of the Supreme People's Court on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China* states two points: first, "A party must provide evidence to prove the facts on which its claims are based..."; second, "Before a judgment is rendered, if a party fails to provide evidence or the evidence is insufficient to prove its factual claims, the party bearing the burden of proof shall bear the adverse consequences." Article 1198 of the Civil Code states: "Operators or managers of commercial or public venues such as hotels, shopping malls, banks, stations, airports, stadiums, and entertainment venues, or organizers of mass activities, shall bear tort liability if they fail to fulfill safety obligations and cause harm to others." Article 184 of the Civil Code: "A person who voluntarily undertakes emergency rescue actions that result in harm to the assisted party shall not bear civil liability." The Supreme People's Court published a typical guiding case: a pharmacy operator performed CPR on a person in cardiac arrest, breaking the person's ribs. The assisted person was hospitalized for 18 days and sued for over 9,000 yuan in compensation. The court ruled that the rescuer bore no civil liability.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:卫生部 (2011). 老年人跌倒干预技术指南（「老年人跌倒后的处理」）. 全文 PDF 见广东省疾病预防控制中心 <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>，现场处理部分全文见贵南县人民政府转载 <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十三、一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2023). 人民法院抓实公正与效率践行社会主义核心价值观典型案例（案例十三，齐某某诉孙某某健康权纠纷案，辽宁省康平县人民法院）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>；最高人民法院 (2022 年第二次修正，2022 年 4 月 10 日施行). 关于适用《中华人民共和国民事诉讼法》的解释（第九十条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>；民法典第一千一百九十八条同上最高检转载全文；全国人大常委会 (2023 年修正，2024 年 1 月 1 日施行). 民事诉讼法（第一百五十二、一百六十四条）. 上海市发展和改革委员会转载全文 <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>；国务院 (2006). 诉讼费用交纳办法（国务院令第 481 号，第六、二十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，2026 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第二十六条第一项、第五十条第五项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；德国联邦司法部. Strafgesetzbuch (StGB) § 323c Unterlassene Hilfeleistung; Behinderung von hilfeleistenden Personen. <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__323c.html>
- Notes: Rated B because guidelines detail how to handle the situation, but lack figures on "how many deaths are prevented by following this." Article 184 exempts harm caused during the rescue process, provided your actions are voluntary and the situation is an emergency. It does not cover actions like arbitrarily stuffing someone into a private car to rush them to the hospital; waiting for 120 is safer. Do not hang up after calling 120—follow the dispatcher's instructions. This decision tree is primarily meant for your own family elders and acquaintances. For an unfamiliar person on the street, walking away is not illegal and avoids complications: Chinese law does not mandate rescue. Abroad, rules differ. In Germany, for example, failing to render assistance during an accident or emergency when you are capable of helping without significant danger to yourself is a criminal offense, punishable by up to 1 year in prison or a fine. Abroad, follow local law. The true cost of stopping must be weighed in layers: time, leaving your phone number, being pursued by family members, being sued in court, workplace disruption, and being filmed and uploaded online. See [A stranger has an accident on the street: walk away or stop](../../docs/research/en/Should-You-Stop-To-Help-A-Stranger.md) for details. If you do decide to stop, the single rule you must follow is **do not physically move them**. The reason is medical, not legal: forcefully pulling them up can worsen cerebral hemorrhage or spinal cord injury. One exception: if you operate or manage the venue, or if you organized the activity, walking away may lead to legal liability. The duty of care under Article 1198 of the Civil Code requires you to assume safety responsibilities for people on the premises. For fall prevention itself, see Section 1, Item 13 (train balance and leg strength). For delayed symptoms after a head injury in the elderly, see Item 10 of this section. For compensation when injured while rescuing someone, see Item 39 of this section. For preserving evidence and reporting online harassment, see Section 8, Item 16.

### 3. Call emergency services immediately if a face droops, an arm goes weak, or speech slurs—do not wait or drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Three simple checks you can learn in seconds: ask the person to smile, raise both arms, and speak a full sentence.
- In plain terms: If someone suddenly has a drooping face, weakness in one arm, or slurred speech, call emergency services immediately if even one sign appears. If a stroke can be treated with clot-busting medication, earlier treatment brings a far better chance of recovery. Even if more than 4.5 hours have passed or the onset time is unknown, do not give up on emergency care—let the doctors decide. Note down the exact time symptoms started; paramedics will take the patient to a hospital equipped to dissolve blood clots.
- Benefit: Individual patient data were pooled from 9 randomized trials of intravenous thrombolysis involving 6,756 patients with acute ischemic stroke (a meta-analysis). An ischemic stroke occurs when a blood vessel is blocked; intravenous thrombolysis means administering medication to dissolve the clot and clear the vessel. This study evaluated whether patients had no significant disability (a modified Rankin Scale score of 0–1) at 3 to 6 months. When alteplase was administered within 3 hours of symptom onset, the odds ratio was 1.75 (95% CI 1.35–2.27). The actual proportions were 32.9% in the treated group versus 23.1% in the control group, an absolute difference of 9.8 percentage points. (An odds ratio is not a ratio of probabilities and cannot be interpreted as a 1.75-fold chance of recovery.) When delayed to 3 to 4.5 hours, the odds ratio was 1.26 (95% CI 1.05–1.51). Beyond 4.5 hours, it was 1.15 (95% CI 0.95–1.40), for which this study found no statistically clear difference. This does not mean treatment is impossible past 4.5 hours. The American Heart Association and American Stroke Association 2026 guidelines support extending the thrombolysis window for select patients. Patients with unknown onset time or onset between 4.5 and 9 hours may still be eligible for thrombolysis after screening with specialized neuroimaging. Whether medication can be administered must be assessed by a physician; the earlier one reaches medical care, the better. The 2017 prehospital consensus from the Prehospital Emergency Medicine Branch of the Chinese Stroke Association requires emergency personnel to transport suspected stroke patients "to the nearest certified stroke center or hospital capable of intravenous thrombolysis and/or endovascular treatment in the shortest possible time," and to "prenotify the receiving hospital before the suspected stroke patient arrives." The consensus also notes that acute stroke patients may suffer respiratory impairment due to factors such as "aspiration." It recommends educating the public to recognize stroke using "Stroke 1-2-0": "1 look—1 asymmetrical face, mouth drooping; 2 check—2 arms raised parallel, one-sided weakness; 0 listen—slurred speech, difficulty expressing words. If any of these sudden symptoms occur, dial emergency services (120) immediately." When taking a medical history, emergency personnel note that "asking when symptoms first appeared is of utmost importance."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>；中国卒中学会急救医学分会 (2017). 脑卒中院前急救专家共识. 中华急诊医学杂志, 26(10), 1107-1114. <http://www.cem.org.cn/rhtml/20171002/index.htm>；Prabhakaran S 等 (2026). 2026 Guideline for the Early Management of Patients With Acute Ischemic Stroke. Stroke. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000513>；American Heart Association (2026). Top Things to Know: 2026 Guideline for the Early Management of Patients With AIS，第 3 点. <https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-for-the-early-management-of-patients-with-acute-ischemic-stroke/top-things-to-know>
- Notes: Thrombolysis is more effective the earlier it is given, but emergency care should never be abandoned simply because more than 4.5 hours have elapsed. Call emergency services instead of driving yourself because, under prehospital emergency guidelines, paramedics must transport the patient to a hospital capable of thrombolysis and notify the receiving hospital in advance. Note down the exact time symptoms began, as doctors need it to evaluate treatment options. Do not offer food, water, or medication—an inference drawn by the author from the guideline's mention of "aspiration," since stroke patients can easily choke. Do not wait around to "rest and see."

### 4. Call emergency services for a stroke if the room suddenly spins, you see double, lose vision in one eye, or cannot touch your nose
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Beyond checking for a drooping face, arm weakness, and slurred speech (Item 3), add two more checks. First, have the person stand straight and close their eyes to see if they sway, and ask if their vision is doubled or missing a patch. Second, if you suspect it in yourself, stretch out a finger to touch the tip of your nose, then do it again with your eyes closed.
- In plain terms: Relying only on face droop, arm weakness, and slurred speech misses more than a tenth of strokes, because some strokes begin without facial droop or arm weakness. Adding unsteadiness, double vision, or blind spots drops the missed rate to 4.4%. If the room suddenly spins and you cannot stand, see double, or cannot touch your finger to your nose, call emergency services and note the time just the same.
- Benefit: In 2014, the University of Kentucky Stroke Center reviewed records of 736 acute ischemic stroke cases (strokes caused by blocked blood vessels). Among them, 14.1% of patients had none of the three FAST symptoms (facial droop, arm weakness, or speech difficulty) at onset. Expanding screening to include gait instability, leg weakness, and visual symptoms reduced the proportion missed from 14.1% to 4.4%. A review of 465 emergency department records of ischemic stroke across two US hospitals found that 22% were initially missed. When blood flow to the back of the brain was blocked (posterior circulation stroke), 37% were initially missed, compared with 16% in the anterior circulation. Patients with dizziness were more likely to be missed, with an odds ratio of 1.99 compared to those without dizziness. For those with nausea and vomiting, the odds ratio was 4.02. The International Red Cross 2025 first aid guidelines also list stroke warning signs including "sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes" and "sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance, or lack of coordination."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>；Arch AE, Weisman DC, Coca S, et al. (2016). Missed Ischemic Stroke Diagnosis in the Emergency Department by Emergency Medicine and Neurology Services. Stroke, 47(3), 668-673：「Thirty-seven percent of posterior strokes were initially misdiagnosed compared with 16% of anterior strokes」. <https://doi.org/10.1161/strokeaha.115.010613>；International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (2025). International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025，第 366–367 页：「Sudden trouble in seeing with one or both eyes」「Sudden trouble walking or experiencing dizziness, loss of balance or coordination」. <https://www.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/IFRC%20International%20First%20Aid,%20Resuscitation%20and%20Education%20Guidelines%202025.pdf>
- Notes: When vessels supplying the back of the brain are blocked (posterior circulation stroke), patients often have no facial droop or arm paralysis, presenting only with vertigo, unsteady walking, or double vision, which makes them the most easily missed. Failing the finger-to-nose test or being unable to stand with eyes closed reflects what the Red Cross guidelines describe as balance or coordination problems. However, no movement test can rule out a stroke: if symptoms appear suddenly and do not resolve, call emergency services and note the exact time of onset. The Kentucky study reviewed records from only a single hospital, and the authors noted that further validation is required.

### 5. Treat sudden curtain-like vision loss in one eye as a stroke emergency, even if it clears up in minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Emergency evaluation and vascular imaging take half a day to a full day.
- In plain terms: Sudden loss of vision in one eye often means a blood vessel at the back of the eye is blocked; the longer blood flow is cut off, the less retina can be saved. This is actually a type of stroke, and you need emergency medical care right away even if your sight returns within minutes. When patients with minor strokes or brief warning episodes were evaluated and treated on the same day, their risk of another stroke within 90 days dropped from 10.3% to 2.1%. Go straight to the emergency room—do not wait until the next day.
- Benefit: A 2021 American Heart Association scientific statement on central retinal artery occlusion (a blockage in the main artery supplying the back of the eye) stated: "Acute central retinal artery occlusion is an emergency." Care systems should recognize and triage it as a medical emergency. The longer retinal tissue is deprived of blood, the less can be salvaged. The Oxford EXPRESS study looked at two groups of patients: those with minor stroke and those with brief episodes of cerebral ischemia whose symptoms resolved on their own (transient ischemic attack, or TIA). When the protocol was changed to evaluate and initiate treatment on the same day, the proportion of patients who suffered a recurrent stroke within 90 days dropped from 10.3% (32/310) to 2.1% (6/281). The statement summary also highlighted that central retinal artery occlusion is a form of acute ischemic stroke and serves as a warning sign for subsequent cerebrovascular and cardiovascular events. The UK National Health Service (NHS) lists blurred vision or loss of sight in one or both eyes as a stroke symptom, noting that immediate medical attention is necessary even if symptoms resolve quickly. Anyone who experienced stroke symptoms in the past 24 hours that have since stopped should still call emergency medical services immediately.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources:Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294：「Central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO) is a form of acute ischemic stroke that causes severe visual loss and is a harbinger of further cerebrovascular and cardiovascular events」. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> ；NHS. Symptoms of a stroke：「blurred vision or loss of sight in 1 or both eyes」「Symptoms of a stroke can sometimes stop after a short time, so you may think you're OK. Even if this happens, get medical help straight away」「you've had signs of a stroke within the last 24 hours even if they've now stopped」. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/> ; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>
- Notes: Graded B because the EXPRESS study was not a randomized controlled trial. It compared the same population across two time periods (before and after the practice change), meaning other background factors may have changed over time and cannot be fully separated. Having vision suddenly black out for a few minutes and then return is easily dismissed as fatigue or low blood sugar. The NHS explicitly states that you must seek urgent medical help even if symptoms stop on their own; among the EXPRESS patients who were not treated on the same day, 10.3% suffered another stroke within 90 days. Sudden vision loss in one eye must be taken seriously, even without pain or redness. Do not wait to visit an outpatient eye clinic the next day—go directly to an emergency department with a dedicated stroke center.

### 6. Go to an eye emergency room that day if one eye is painful, red, and swollen, lights have rainbow halos, and you have a headache with nausea
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Having an eye emergency room measure your eye pressure and examine the front chamber of your eye takes about half a day.
- In plain terms: This could be an acute glaucoma attack: fluid inside the eye cannot drain, eye pressure spikes, and the optic nerve connecting the eye to the brain gets crushed. The longer you wait to bring the pressure down, the greater the risk of permanent vision loss. In one hospital study, half of the affected eyes were left with blind spots after one year. If these symptoms appear suddenly, go to an emergency department that same day.
- Benefit: An eye hospital in Wuhan compared patients with acute primary angle-closure glaucoma across two time periods: 54 patients (64 eyes) during the 76-day lockdown in 2020 versus 46 patients (51 eyes) during the same period in 2021, with similar demographics between groups. The time from symptom onset to treatment was 241.84±211.95 hours in the lockdown group versus 121.53±96.12 hours in the control group (P=0.001). Intraocular pressure at presentation was 52.63±12.45 mmHg versus 45.16±9.79 mmHg (P=0.001). The blindness rate was 21.87% versus 7.84%. Glaucomatous optic neuropathy was detected in 20/64 eyes (31.25%) versus 7/51 eyes (13.73%) (P=0.03). A hospital in South Korea reviewed 50 eyes that experienced a single attack and later underwent lens extraction; after 1 year, 25 eyes (50%) were left with varying degrees of visual field defects. A longer duration from symptom onset to intraocular pressure reduction significantly increased the likelihood of persistent defects (P=0.005). Another prospective study had 43 individuals with shallow anterior chambers lie prone in a dark room for 1 hour. Intraocular pressure rose significantly in both eyes (P<0.01), with a median increase of 3.60 mmHg in the dominant eye and 2.70 mmHg in the fellow eye (P<0.05). The NHS notes that glaucoma can sometimes develop suddenly, with symptoms including severe eye pain, redness, rainbow-colored rings around bright lights, blurred vision, nausea, vomiting, and headache; call emergency services or go to an emergency department immediately if these occur suddenly. ### 7. If crushing chest pain, heaviness, or tightness lasts over 15 minutes without relief, or comes with cold sweats, nausea, or shortness of breath, call emergency services—do not tough it out or drive yourself

- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: If you have crushing chest pain, heaviness, or tightness that does not ease after 15 minutes, call emergency services. ### 8. Sudden tearing pain moving from the chest to the back and lower back: call an ambulance and clearly state it feels "like tearing"

- In plain terms: If you feel sudden, severe tearing pain in your chest, back, or abdomen, call an ambulance immediately and explicitly tell them it feels "like tearing." For type A aortic dissection managed with medication alone instead of surgery, hospital mortality reaches 58%. Classic physical signs are often missing, so doctors need to suspect it first. If blood pressure differs greatly between your arms or you cannot feel a pulse on one side, mention that right away too.

### 9. Go to the emergency room immediately for a head CT if you get a sudden "worst headache of your life" that peaks within an hour
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: An emergency room visit itself costs very little. An emergency head CT scan costs a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: When a severe headache peaks within an hour, about 6% are caused by a ruptured blood vessel on the surface of the brain, requiring an immediate trip to the emergency room. If you are 40 or older, have neck pain or stiffness, or someone saw you pass out, a doctor needs to investigate further if even one is true. It also counts if the headache began during physical exertion, exploded instantly, or left you unable to bend your neck forward. Most people who get tested turn out fine, but following these rules in the study did not miss a single bleed.
- Benefit: A prospective cohort study across 10 emergency departments in Canada evaluated 2,131 patients with acute headache peaking within one hour, establishing criteria in advance and tracking outcomes throughout. Among them, 132 (6.2%) had subarachnoid hemorrhage (a ruptured blood vessel on the surface of the brain). Bleeding was confirmed primarily by seeing blood on a head CT scan ("subarachnoid blood on computed tomography scan"), followed by abnormal cerebrospinal fluid on lumbar puncture. The Ottawa SAH Rule includes 6 criteria: age 40 or older, neck pain or stiffness, and witnessed loss of consciousness. The other three are onset during exertion, "thunderclap" peak immediately on onset, and limited neck flexion on physical exam. Meeting any single criterion warrants an evaluation. This rule identified every single patient with bleeding, achieving a sensitivity of 100% (95% CI 97.2%–100.0%). The trade-off was that only 15.3% of patients without hemorrhage were ruled out (specificity 15.3%).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources:Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. - Notes: The key is not simply "how bad it hurts," but "how quickly it reaches its worst." A headache that explodes within seconds to a minute is the true red flag, whereas a migraine that builds gradually is not. Take it even more seriously if accompanied by vomiting, neck stiffness, light sensitivity, or a brief blackout. A specificity of only 15.3% means that the vast majority of people checked under this rule will turn out to be fine. However, a missed diagnosis risks another bleed, making the checkup well worth it. Do not just take a painkiller to suppress the pain and go back to sleep.

### 10. If an older adult develops an unsteady gait, sluggishness, drowsiness, or one-sided weakness weeks to months after a head bump, get a head CT scan
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=老人,60岁 -->
- Cost: 200–300 RMB for a head CT scan.
- In plain terms: After an older adult bumps their head, blood often takes until the third week to pool. The bump is usually minor, and more than half of patients do not recall being injured. Even after surgery, this condition has a recurrence rate of up to 33% and a mortality rate of up to 32%. If an older adult suddenly becomes confused, unsteady on their feet, constantly sleepy, or weak on one side, ask whether they bumped their head in the last two or three months, and get a head CT.
- Benefit: The Chinese Society of Neurosurgery and other organizations published the *Chinese Expert Consensus on the Pharmacological Treatment of Chronic Subdural Hematoma*. Chronic subdural hematoma is a slow accumulation of blood between the brain's covering layers—technically, a chronic space-occupying hematoma between the arachnoid mater and the dura mater. It "usually forms by the third week after head trauma" and occurs predominantly in older adults. Following surgery, the recurrence rate can reach 33%, and the mortality rate can reach 32%. A geriatric review noted that typical clinical presentations "include confusion, cognitive decline, gait disturbance, or drowsiness, which may mimic other geriatric syndromes." It added that the rising incidence is tied to wider use of anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs (such as aspirin and clopidogrel) and a higher tendency to fall in aging populations. Another literature review noted that a clear delay separates the injury from symptom onset, and the trauma is often so minor that it is easily overlooked, with over 50% of patients having no recollection of any head injury.

### 11. Seek medical care promptly if one leg suddenly swells, feels tight, and hurts to the touch; call 120 immediately if sudden shortness of breath or chest pain develops
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: An ultrasound of the leg veins (lower-extremity venous ultrasound) costs 100–200 yuan.
- In plain terms: If one leg suddenly becomes swollen and painful, see a doctor promptly to get it checked. If sudden shortness of breath or chest pain also develops, call 120 immediately: among patients diagnosed with a pulmonary embolism (a blood clot in the lungs), 17.4% die of various causes within 3 months. Traveling by car or plane for over 4 hours roughly doubles the risk, so get up and walk around periodically. If you are concerned about blood clots, ask a doctor before departure whether to wear compression stockings.
- Benefit: The International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER) enrolled 2,454 patients with confirmed acute pulmonary embolism across 52 hospitals in 7 countries. A pulmonary embolism occurs when a blood clot blocks a pulmonary artery. The all-cause mortality rate within 3 months in this cohort was 17.4% (426/2,454). The World Health Organization (WHO) WRIGHT project reported that travel lasting 4 hours or longer "roughly doubles" the risk of developing blood clots in the veins that travel to the lungs (venous thromboembolism). However, the individual absolute risk remains very low, at "approximately 1 in 6,000" after sedentary travel lasting over 4 hours. The WHO also specified that anyone with questions about prevention should consult a doctor before departure. The UK National Health Service (NHS) lists leg pain and swelling in one leg as symptoms of deep vein thrombosis. If this is suspected, book a doctor's appointment promptly; the doctor will arrange a hospital ultrasound within 24 hours. If leg pain and swelling are accompanied by shortness of breath or chest pain, call emergency services (120). The NHS also recommends wearing loose clothing, drinking plenty of water, avoiding alcohol, and walking around whenever possible during flights, train trips, or car rides lasting more than 4 hours. A 2021 Cochrane review pooled 12 randomized trials of airline passengers on flights lasting over 5 hours. Those wearing graduated compression stockings developed fewer asymptomatic deep vein thromboses detected only by screening: odds ratio (OR) 0.10, 95% CI 0.04 to 0.25 (confidence interval). Because this outcome is rare, the odds ratio is close to the relative risk. Clots were detected in 50 out of 2,637 participants: 3 in the stocking group and 47 in the non-stocking group. None of the trials reported any deaths, pulmonary embolisms, or symptomatic blood clots. Therefore, whether compression stockings prevent these truly severe outcomes could not be calculated.

### 12. Press hard on massive bleeding right away, use a tourniquet on limbs if pressing fails, and call emergency services immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A windlass tourniquet costs around $15 to $30; keep one in your car and first-aid kit. Learning the steps—place above the wound, twist until bleeding stops, note the time—takes just a few minutes.
- In plain terms: Press down hard with your hands or clothing immediately while having someone call emergency services. If direct pressure cannot stop limb bleeding, apply a tourniquet; neck and torso bleeding can only be managed with direct pressure. Among battlefield casualties, 90% survived when a tourniquet was applied before shock set in, compared to just 10% when applied after shock, and all 5 casualties who needed one but never received it died. Fasten the tourniquet above the wound, twist until bleeding stops, and note the time. Once it is on, do not loosen it yourself—wait for professional emergency responders.
- Benefit: Across 11 Level 1 trauma centers in Texas over 6 years, 1,026 cases of peripheral vascular injury were evaluated, with 181 patients (17.6%) receiving a prehospital tourniquet. In unadjusted analysis, mortality was 3.9% in the tourniquet group versus 5.2% in the non-tourniquet group. After adjusting for multiple confounders, prehospital tourniquet use remained independently associated with survival, with an adjusted odds ratio (aOR) of 5.86 (95% CI: 1.41 to 24.47). Among 232 wounded soldiers treated at a combat hospital in Baghdad, survival was 90% when tourniquets were applied before shock versus 10% when applied after shock; all 5 casualties who needed a tourniquet but did not receive one died, whereas overall survival among those who received one was 87%. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines specify calling emergency services for all severe bleeding. The recommended sequence is: direct pressure by the injured person, call emergency services immediately, then direct pressure applied by the rescuer using cloth or towels; if limb bleeding cannot be controlled, apply a tourniquet. Once applied, a tourniquet must remain in place until emergency medical personnel take over. The sequence is: direct pressure with clothing or towels while directing a bystander to call emergency dispatch → tourniquet for uncontrollable limb bleeding → do not loosen.

### 13. Wash cat or dog bites and scratches alternately with soapy water and running water for 15 minutes, and get vaccinated the same day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Washing costs nothing. A full course of rabies vaccine costs a few hundred yuan. Category III exposure also requires rabies passive immunization (ready-made antibodies injected directly), costing several hundred to over a thousand yuan. The most expensive mistake is delay.
- In plain terms: Once rabies symptoms appear, the fatality rate is almost 100%, so after a bite, neither washing nor vaccination can be skipped. First, rinse the wound alternately with soapy water and running water for 15 minutes, then get vaccinated that same day—the sooner, the better. Puncture bites or broken skin licked by an animal count as Category III exposure and also require passive immunization (ready-made antibodies). Exposure categories are determined by how contact occurred and how severe it was.
- Benefit: The 2023 guidelines from China's National Disease Control and Prevention Administration and the National Health Commission state: "The fatality rate of rabies is almost 100%." The recommended management is to "thoroughly rinse all bites and scratches alternately with soapy water (or other weakly alkaline cleansers) and running water under moderate pressure for about 15 minutes." Minor nibbles on uncovered skin or scratches without bleeding are classified as Category II exposure, requiring vaccination. Puncturing bites or broken skin licked by an animal are classified as Category III exposure, where "the wound should be managed, rabies passive immunization agents administered, and rabies vaccine injected." Exposures are graded "based on the mode of contact and degree of exposure." The guidelines also specify that "rabies vaccination after first exposure should be started as early as possible." According to the WHO, approximately 59,000 people worldwide die of rabies each year, with 99% of cases transmitted by dogs.

### 14. Cool burns immediately under cool running water for 20 minutes; do not apply toothpaste or soy sauce
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- In plain terms: Children whose burns are cooled under cool running water for a full 20 minutes within 3 hours of injury are less likely to need skin grafts later. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines explicitly state not to use ice water or apply ice, as this worsens tissue injury. The guidelines also note that covering the burn with a clean damp cloth or plastic wrap protects the wound on the way to the hospital. The NHS instructs removing clothing and jewelry near the burn, but leaving anything stuck to the wound untouched, and avoiding any ointments, oils, or butter on the burn.

### 16. If someone has a seizure and collapses, clear the area, turn them on their side, time it, and put nothing in their mouth; do the same for a child having a febrile seizure

- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Move anything that could hurt them out of the way, turn them onto their side with their mouth facing downward, and check a watch to time it. Do not hold them down, and do not put anything into their mouth—that only damages teeth and jaws. ### 19. If a carbon monoxide alarm goes off, or everyone in the room has a headache and nausea, get outside before calling for help

<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

### 22. For dizziness, nausea, lack of sweat, or confusion in severe heat, move to shade immediately, strip clothes, pour water to cool down, do not give water if unconscious, and call emergency services

- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Hot, red skin, slurred speech, and confusion signal heat stroke. Body temperature can surge above 41 °C in 10 to 15 minutes, and severe cases have an extremely high fatality rate. Never give even a single sip of water to someone who is unconscious.
- Benefit: US National Weather Service (NWS): Heat stroke is a "serious medical emergency." The US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) states that during heat stroke, body temperature can rise above 106 °F (approx. 41 °C) within 10 to 15 minutes. Its listed symptoms include "hot, dry skin or profuse sweating."

### 23. Give conscious heat victims cool light salt water or sports drinks in small, frequent sips; do not chug, and avoid alcohol and high-sugar drinks

- In plain terms: Only give fluids if the person is fully conscious. Offer cool, lightly salted water or a sports drink in small, frequent sips—never gulp it down. ### 24. Crawl low in a fire, feel doors before opening, take the stairs, and never turn back once outside

Open doors slowly, and shut them immediately if thick smoke rolls in. Use the stairs rather than the elevator, and once you are out, never turn back for belongings. If your clothes catch fire, stop, drop, and roll, covering your face with your hands.

Guidance from China's Ministry of Emergency Management matches these points: never use elevators during an evacuation; head downstairs if the fire is not blocking the way; cover the mouth and nose with a damp cloth or mask; and evacuate crawling or crouching low.

### 25. If you see someone drowning, shout for help and call 110/120 first, extend a pole or throw a float, and do not enter the water yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting the urge to jump in.
- In plain terms: Call for help and dial 110/120 first, extend a pole or throw something that floats from shore, and do not enter the water yourself. Once the person is pulled out, if they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR immediately. Rescuers trained in CPR should deliver both chest compressions and rescue breaths. If you cannot, do not know how, or are unwilling to provide mouth-to-mouth breaths, perform continuous chest compressions and follow the emergency dispatcher's guidance.
- Benefit: WHO: approximately 300,000 people drown globally each year, with 92% of deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries. Drowning is the 4th leading cause of death among children aged 1 to 4 years, and the 3rd leading cause among children aged 5 to 14 years. WHO-recommended interventions include "safe rescue and resuscitation training," such as learning non-contact rescues without entering the water. Shout loudly for help and call 110/120, then extend a long pole or rope from the shore, or throw a lifebuoy, empty bucket, or other flotation device. The 2025 International Red Cross first aid guidelines specify that rescuers should avoid entering the water whenever possible: reach with a rigid object (such as a pole or branch) from shore or a boat, or throw a rope or floating object. Rescuers should not make direct physical contact with a struggling drowning victim due to the high risk of being pulled under. Rescuers already in the water must understand water conditions and carry a flotation aid. Once the victim is retrieved, if they are unresponsive with abnormal breathing, begin CPR immediately. The 2024 drowning resuscitation guidelines from the American Heart Association and the American Academy of Pediatrics emphasize that drowning cardiac arrest is primarily caused by severe hypoxia. Trained rescuers should combine rescue breathing with chest compressions. Those unable, untrained, or unwilling to deliver rescue breaths should perform continuous chest compressions until emergency responders arrive. Keep the emergency call on speakerphone and follow the dispatcher's instructions.
- Evidence grade: C

### 26. If someone is choking and cannot speak, stand behind anyone over age 1 to give 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts; start CPR if they collapse

### 28. Shivering, slurred speech, and stumbling mean hypothermia: change wet clothes, wrap them up, warm the torso first, and offer warm sweet drinks without alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few dollars for an emergency space blanket, plus a spare set of dry clothes. Learning the warning signs costs nothing.
- In plain terms: Hypothermia sets in when body temperature drops below 35°C (95°F); a person will shiver, stumble, and become confused, but severe hypothermia can actually stop the shivering. Take off wet clothing and wrap them in a sleeping bag, regular blanket, or foil emergency blanket, warming the chest and torso first. If they are awake and can swallow, give them warm, sweet drinks—never alcohol. If they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, perform CPR by pressing hard on the center of the chest, as described in Item 1 of this chapter.
- Benefit: According to the US National Weather Service, hypothermia occurs when core body temperature drops below 95°F (35°C): "feeling cold, sluggish, trouble thinking clearly, see a doctor right away." Management involves removing wet clothing, drying the person, and wrapping them securely against the cold. For someone awake and shivering, allow them to rewarm using a sleeping bag or blankets; reflective materials may also be used. If the person can swallow, offer warm, sweet drinks or high-energy foods. The guidelines also explicitly advise against offering alcohol and rubbing the skin.

### 29. If bitten by a snake, sit still, remove rings and watches, and get to a hospital quickly for antivenom—do not cut, suck, or tie off
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Free. Find out in advance which nearby hospitals stock antivenom. The hardest part is resisting the urge to treat the wound yourself.
- In plain terms: Sit still, immobilize the bitten limb, and take off rings and watches before swelling cuts off circulation. Get to a hospital with antivenom as fast as possible, because antivenom is the most effective treatment to prevent or reverse most toxic effects. ### 33. Pace high-altitude climbs by sleeping elevation, descend if symptoms worsen, and never push through

### 44. If an infant under 1 year is unresponsive and not breathing normally, call emergency services on speakerphone and perform infant CPR: 30 compressions to 2 breaths
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 人群=婴幼儿,怀孕 -->
- Cost: Free. Infant CPR requires hands-on training once, taking half a day to a full day. The hard part is giving rescue breaths covering both the baby's mouth and nose, and overcoming the hesitation to push hard enough.
- In plain terms: If tapping the soles of the feet gets no response and the baby is not breathing normally, turn on speakerphone to call emergency services while keeping your hands at work. Encircle the chest with both hands and press with both thumbs; if your hands cannot encircle the baby, use the heel of one hand. Press about 4 cm deep, about 2 times per second. After every 30 compressions, cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth and deliver 2 rescue breaths. Most infant cardiac arrests stem from breathing trouble, and CPR with rescue breaths saves significantly more lives.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association and the American Academy of Pediatrics released their 2025 Pediatric Basic Life Support guidelines. The guidelines state: for infants and children in cardiac arrest, the primary cause remains respiratory problems. For infants who experience out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, only 6.6% survive to hospital discharge. When lay rescuers encounter an unresponsive infant or child who is not breathing normally and shows no signs of life, they "should immediately begin CPR" without checking for a pulse. Compression depth should be at least one-third of the anterior-posterior diameter of the chest, which for infants is "about 1.5 inches (4 cm)". The rate should be 100 to 120 compressions per minute. For a single rescuer, deliver 30 compressions to 2 breaths. For two rescuers, deliver 15 compressions to 2 breaths. For an infant, compress using the heel of one hand or by encircling the chest with both hands and pressing with both thumbs. If the chest cannot be encircled, use the heel of one hand. The two-finger technique has been removed because it does not achieve sufficient depth. The guidelines also note that for infants and children with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, adding rescue breaths to chest compressions improves survival. A US registry tracked 3,900 children under 18 with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, 59.4% of whom were infants. When bystanders performed CPR, the adjusted survival-to-discharge rate was 13.2%, compared to 9.5% without bystander CPR (adjusted odds ratio OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.25–1.96 [confidence interval]). In infants, CPR with rescue breaths was associated with higher survival, and surviving children achieved better neurological recovery. Compressions-only CPR yielded outcomes similar to no bystander CPR at all. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies also released its 2025 international first aid guidelines. It states: to check if an infant is responsive, pick up the infant or tap the soles of the feet. If you are alone, call emergency services on speakerphone yourself. If you do not have a phone that allows calling while compressing, do CPR for 1 minute first before leaving to call. An educational article by the Capital Institute of Pediatrics, reposted by the Beijing Municipal Health Commission, states: "If only 1 person is present, perform CPR for 2 minutes before calling the 120 ambulance." The compression depth is "4 cm for infants." For infants under 1 year, rescue breathing must be "mouth-to-mouth-and-nose."

### 45. If a child swallows a button battery or more than one strong magnet, head straight to an emergency room with endoscopy—do not wait for it to pass, and do not induce vomiting

### 46. When someone is drunk, put them on their side, stay with them, and never leave them to sleep alone; call 120 if they cannot be woken, breathe slowly or irregularly, convulse, or feel cold to the touch

- Cost: Free. It requires someone to watch over them for several hours, often throughout the entire night. The hard part is that you may have been drinking too and want to go home to sleep, and everyone assumes they will just "sleep it off."
- In plain terms: Someone who passes out drunk can choke to death on their vomit or slowly stop breathing. Turn them onto their side, stay with them constantly, and never leave them alone in a room to sleep. Call 120 if they cannot be woken, their breathing slows down or turns irregular, they have seizures, or their skin feels cold and looks pale or bluish. Do not force coffee down their throat, do not put them in a cold shower, and do not stick fingers down their throat to make them vomit.
- Benefit: The UK National Health Service (NHS) lists symptoms of alcohol poisoning as: confusion, slurred speech, loss of coordination, vomiting, incontinence, pale or bluish skin, slow or irregular breathing, seizures, and unconsciousness (cannot be woken). Call emergency services if you suspect alcohol poisoning, or if the person has seizures, loses consciousness, or stops breathing. What you should do is stay by their side, "because they can choke on their vomit or stop breathing." If they are awake, help them sit up. If they are unconscious, place them on their side in the recovery position and monitor their breathing. If they are still awake, able to swallow, and not vomiting, they can take small sips of water. Keep them warm with a coat or blanket. There are five things you should NOT do: Do not give them more alcohol. Do not give them food, which can cause choking. Do not give them coffee or caffeinated drinks, which worsen dehydration. Do not make them take a cold shower or cold bath, as they may become hypothermic, fall, or pass out in the water. Do not try to make them vomit. An emergency physician's article published on the Shaanxi Provincial Health Commission website notes that a drunk person's gag reflex is impaired, making it easy to inhale vomit into the airway; in severe cases, this causes "direct suffocation and death." Emergency departments frequently receive deceased intoxicated individuals with large amounts of vomit suctioned from their airways. Once they reach the lethargic or comatose stage—pale face, bluish lips, cold clammy skin, dropping body temperature, and shallow breathing—call 120 immediately. At this point, keep them on their side, "do not induce vomiting or feed them food," cover them with a blanket to keep warm, and turn them regularly. If their breathing or heartbeat stops, begin CPR immediately. Public health material from a hospital reposted by the Beijing Municipal Health Commission states: "Never leave a drunk person to sleep alone, lest the patient suffocate with no one around to save them." An article by a pharmacist reposted by the Fujian Medical Products Administration states that an intoxicated person "must lie on their side," and hypothermia can lead to cardiac arrest. According to the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), from 2010 to 2012, an average of 2,221 people aged 15 and older died each year from alcohol poisoning in the United States, equivalent to about 6 deaths per day.

### 48. If an adult tooth is knocked out completely, hold it by the crown, rinse it, put it back in if you can, store it in milk if you cannot, and see a dentist immediately; never reinsert a baby tooth

If it is dirty, gently rinse it with milk or normal saline, put it back into its socket if you can, and bite down on a clean cloth. If it cannot go back in, store it in milk and see a dentist immediately—never wrap it in a dry tissue. ### 49. Go to the ER today if stool turns tarry black and sticky; call 120 if dizzy, sweating cold, or vomiting blood

- Cost: Outpatient registration and blood tests cost several tens to one or two hundred yuan. An endoscopy costs several hundred yuan. If you are still fully conscious, go straight to the emergency room rather than waiting for an outpatient appointment. If you feel dizzy, break out in a cold sweat, or vomit blood, do not drive yourself.
- In plain terms: If your stool turns black, sticky, and foul-smelling, it usually means bleeding in the esophagus, stomach, or duodenum; go to the emergency room the same day. People who only have black stool without vomiting blood are more likely to delay seeking care. Those who delay end up with lower hemoglobin levels and are more likely to need a blood transfusion. If you feel dizzy, break out in a cold sweat, pass out, or vomit blood or dark coffee-ground material, do not drive yourself—call an ambulance.
- Benefit: Six hospitals consecutively enrolled 2,944 emergency patients with upper gastrointestinal bleeding. The upper gastrointestinal tract refers to the esophagus, stomach, or duodenum. Those arriving within 6 hours of symptom onset accounted for 36% (1,068 patients). Those delaying past 48 hours accounted for 20% (576 patients). Patients presenting with black stool were less likely to arrive within 6 hours, with an odds ratio (OR) of 0.22 (95% CI 0.18–0.28). The study concluded that patients with black stool alone without vomiting blood tend to delay seeking care. Delayed arrivals had lower hemoglobin levels and were more likely to require blood transfusions: among those arriving within 6 hours, 27% needed a transfusion (286 patients), compared with 42% of those arriving after 6 hours (791 patients). Among the 1,068 patients arriving within 6 hours, 80 died within 30 days, or 7%. Among those arriving after 6 hours, 121 died, or 6%—a similar rate between the two groups. Hemostatic treatment rates were also similar at 18% versus 20%. In 8 Chinese hospitals, 2,977 inpatients with non-variceal upper gastrointestinal bleeding were admitted. Peptic ulcers accounted for 73.26%; peptic ulcers refer to gastric or duodenal ulcers. In-hospital mortality was 1.71% (51/2,977). Mortality was 0.73% in patients under 60 years old and 8.21% in those aged 80 and older. In patients whose high pressure (systolic blood pressure) dropped to between 70 and 100 mmHg, the adjusted risk of death was higher, with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.67 (95% CI 1.041–6.866). The 2018 Hangzhou guidelines note that the presence of hematemesis and melena essentially establishes a diagnosis of acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding. When bleeding is massive and intestinal motility is rapid, the stool may also appear bloody. Iron supplements, bismuth agents, and animal blood can also turn stool black; bismuth agents are bismuth-containing stomach medications. It takes 24 to 72 hours for hematocrit to reflect the actual amount of blood lost; hematocrit is the proportion of red blood cells in the blood. If a patient is confused, has a pulse exceeding 100 beats per minute, or has a systolic pressure below 90 mmHg, guidelines recommend admission to a resuscitation room. The same applies to cold, clammy limbs or ongoing vomiting of blood or passage of bloody stool. The UK National Health Service (NHS) states that if there is blood in your stool or if your stool is black, sticky, and extremely foul-smelling, you should call emergency services or go to the emergency room. The same applies to vomiting bright red blood or vomiting material that looks like coffee grounds; do not drive yourself. The NHS page on vomiting blood notes that if you have vomited blood and feel faint, dizzy, or have skin that is cold, clammy, or pale, you must also call emergency services or go to the emergency room. The NHS also notes that the two most common causes of stomach ulcers are Helicobacter pylori infection and frequent use of pain relievers such as ibuprofen.
